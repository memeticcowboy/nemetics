# Exploring Life as a Game: Philosophical Analysis of Bernard Suits’ Perspective

Created at 2025/10/29 9:33 AM

Bernard Suits wrote **“Is Life a Game We Are Playing?”**, published in *Ethics* Vol 77(3): 209-213, April 1967. ([PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/SUIILA?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Bernard Suits, Is life a game we are playing?"))

Here are a few reflections on its significance.

***

### What the paper does

- Suits starts by offering a formal definition of “game-playing”: “To play a game is to engage in activity directed toward bringing about a certain state of affairs, using only means permitted by the rules, where the means permitted by the rules are more limited in scope than they would be in the absence of the rules, and where the sole reason for accepting such limitation of means is to make possible such activity.” ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Is-Life-a-Game-We-Are-Playing-Suits/3aa1322e9b9ec561c53018019cafe2004aa68248?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Is Life a Game We Are Playing? - Bernard Suits"))
- He then asks: Could life itself fit this definition of a game we’re (perhaps unconsciously) playing? ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Is-Life-a-Game-We-Are-Playing-Suits/3aa1322e9b9ec561c53018019cafe2004aa68248?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Is Life a Game We Are Playing? - Bernard Suits"))
- He sketches two possible “games” that life might be: one in which death is the end, suicide forbidden; another in which each player seeks pleasure but is constrained from diminishing others’ pleasure. ([Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/340636237/Suits-Is-Life-a-Game-We-Are-Playing?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Suits - Is Life A Game We Are Playing | PDF | Morality"))
- He is careful: he’s *not* claiming definitively that life is a game, but showing that under his definition, the possibility is coherent. ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Is-Life-a-Game-We-Are-Playing-Suits/3aa1322e9b9ec561c53018019cafe2004aa68248?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Is Life a Game We Are Playing? - Bernard Suits"))

***

### Why it matters

- It’s compact (5 pages) yet conceptually rich.
- It revives a philosophical move sometimes lost: start by clarifying your terms (here, “game”), then ask a metaphysical/ontological question (is life a game?), and engage with it.
- It invites a shift in perspective: if you view life as a game under certain rules, what follows for meaning, action, values?
- For your interests — memetic theory, structures of meaning, game metaphors in culture — this ties in interestingly. Suits’ move is a kind of formal memetic mapping: “life = game” as a metaphoric attractor, with rules, means, ends.

***

### Critical engagements (you expected them)

- One question: does the “game” metaphor do justice to the existential seriousness many attribute to life? Suits accepts that many don’t *want* to believe life is a game (he addresses that briefly). ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Is-Life-a-Game-We-Are-Playing-Suits/3aa1322e9b9ec561c53018019cafe2004aa68248?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Is Life a Game We Are Playing? - Bernard Suits")) But is that enough? Conceiving of life as a game could risk trivializing dimensions of suffering, moral urgency, contingency. Perhaps Suits underplays that tension.
- Another: The definition of “game” is very rule- and ends-oriented, but human life arguably involves open-endedness, rule‐breaking, emergent meanings beyond fixed “goals”. If life is a game, what are the rules? How fixed are they? Your work on memetic ecology and emergent structures suggests that “rule‐governed” frames might be too rigid.
- A further point: Suits leaves open that life may *not* be a game, or may be many different games for different people. That pluralism is useful, but then the ambition of “Is life a game we are playing?” becomes diluted: maybe “Some lives are games, others not”. So one might ask: how far do we push the analogy? And does it extend to collective / systemic levels (the hive, the memetic economy) or just individual lives?
