## Insight


**📊 Mapping Academic Citations on Ethics in AI**  
- **Ethics Washing as a Term**: The term "ethics washing" has become a pivotal concept in the field, serving as a reference point for discussions about corporate ethics and the political underpinnings of industry-led initiatives. It emphasizes that framing ethics without political context often leads to superficial solutions that ignore deeper issues of power dynamics.  
- **Critical Data Studies' Emphasis**: Authors leverage the framework to advocate for a power-based analysis over a principles-based one, demonstrating a commitment to unpacking systemic injustices rather than masking them under ethical pretenses.  
- **Politicization Argument's Popularity**: Citing the paper strengthens arguments that suggest a mere ethical facade is often employed to obscure corporate interests, thus reinforcing the theme of ethics as a potential tool for obfuscation.  

**⚖️ Legal and Regulatory Approaches**  
- **Advocacy for Hard Law**: Legal scholars reference the paper to assert that soft law, such as voluntary ethical codes, is insufficient for effective governance of AI technologies, necessitating the establishment of strict regulatory frameworks like the EU AI Act.  
- **Critique of Methodology Solution**: While the diagnosis of ethics washing is acknowledged, there’s a prevailing sentiment that the practical solution recommended in the paper—casuistry—is often overlooked by legal practitioners, who prioritize legislative measures.  
- **Focus on Regulatory Structures**: The emphasis on legislation over methodology signifies a crucial divergence in how AI ethics is viewed within legal circles, reflecting a preference for enforceable regulations instead of ethical reasoning frameworks.  

**💻 Applied AI Ethics and Methodology**  
- **Testing Core Suggestions**: A smaller cohort of researchers in Applied AI Ethics and HCI attempts to adopt the proposed casuistry and exemplar-based reasoning, indicating a cautious but incomplete application of the paper's framework.  
- **Low Implementation of Casuistry**: Despite significant discussion around ethics washing, only a few papers have genuinely applied the proposed methods in practical AI design, which points to a gap between theory and practice.  
- **Observation of Vulnerability**: The hesitance to fully embrace casuistry in technical design creates a weak spot in operationalizing the paper’s recommendations, highlighting challenges in translating ethical theory into real-world applications.  

**📈 Summary of Mapping Insights**  
- **Field Utilization Patterns**: The analysis reveals that while Critical Data Studies robustly engage with the paper’s findings, legal frameworks and technical approaches adopt more ambiguous or superficial interactions with the notion of casuistry.  
- **Tone of Adoption**: The paper enjoys strong affirmative reception in Critical Data Studies yet faces lukewarm support for its solution among legal scholars, and minimal discourse in technical ethics.  
- **Insight on Casuistry’s Viability**: Although theoretically important, the practical application of casuistry to guide ethical AI design is notably underutilized, necessitating further exploration into its integration within broader discussions.  

**🔍 Next Steps and Framework Applications**  
- **Examination of Case Studies**: It’s suggested to explore real-life examples where the paper's theories of "politicized" and "casuistry" approaches have been successfully applied to gauge their effectiveness in AI projects.  
- **Audit Frameworks as Implementation Models**: The Ada Lovelace Institute’s Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) serves as an exemplary case for operationalizing the question-based approach, shifting the focus from compliance to engaging social implications around AI deployment.  
- **Ongoing Trials of Casuistry Approach**: The Queensland Government’s experience with a casuistry framework highlights practical applications, notably scrutinizing AI tools through local historical contexts, which successfully enhances accountability and ethical grounding.  

**✨ Evaluating Effectiveness of New Audits**  
- **Rigorous vs. Superficial Audits**: The contrast between Ethical Washing audits and Politicized/Casuistry audits marks a significant ideological shift towards greater stakeholder engagement and system redesign, albeit with increased complexity.  
- **Challenges Faced in 2025**: The advent of the Productivity Paradox poses challenges as organizations navigating ethics face pressures for rapid compliance over substantive ethical engagement in AI technologies.  
- **Codification of Frameworks**: Institutions like the OECD and Ada Lovelace Institute adopting politicized approaches into future guidelines signals a shift towards embedding deeper ethical considerations into public discourse.  

**🚀 Final Verdict on Practical Applications**  
- **Public Sector Successes**: The 2022 paper's influence is notable in public sector contexts, where auditing practices have effectively integrated political considerations into ethical evaluations of AI.  
- **Trend in Private Sector Compliance**: Conversely, the private sector's preference for automated compliance and AI audits indicates a troubling trend toward Ethics Washing, which circumvents deeper ethical scrutiny called for in the 2022 paper.  
- **Need for Further Inquiry**: A focused effort to examine question-based audit templates or assess how the impending EU AI Act navigates the law vs. ethics debate would be prudent for advancing discussions on meaningful AI governance.
