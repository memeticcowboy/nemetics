### Key Thinkers Concerned with AI's Impact on Epistemic Experiences

Here are some prominent figures and their core ideas:

- **C. Thi Nguyen** (social epistemologist): Building on his influential work distinguishing **epistemic bubbles** (where people lack exposure to alternative views, often accidentally) from **echo chambers** (where distrust of outside sources is actively cultivated), Nguyen and others extend this to AI contexts. Algorithmic recommendation systems and AI-generated content can deepen these structures by personalizing feeds, reinforcing biases, and reducing epistemic agency — the ability to autonomously revise beliefs. This makes escape harder, as AI nudges users toward self-reinforcing loops, exacerbating polarization and distrust in external evidence.
- **Luciano Floridi** (philosopher of information and digital ethics): A leading voice in the philosophy of information, Floridi views AI as introducing an unprecedented "divorce between agency and intelligence." He examines how AI reshapes epistemology through concepts like explicability (combining intelligibility and accountability), the probabilistic nature of AI "knowledge" (likelihood over certainty), and the need for new frameworks to handle AI's role in knowledge production. His work addresses how AI systems challenge traditional justification, data provenance, and human epistemic authority.
- **Mark Coeckelbergh** and others in political epistemology: They connect AI to democracy's epistemic foundations, highlighting risks like **hallucination** (fabricated outputs presented confidently), **bullshit** (indifference to truth), **relativism**, and **epistemic anachronism/incest** (truth trapped in past data loops, stifling future openness). AI can undermine democratic deliberation by eroding shared facts.
- Emerging voices on **epistemic crisis/collapse**: Thinkers like Lauri Korpela, John Wihbey, and authors in journals (e.g., on "AI and Epistemic Risk for Democracy") warn of **hyperreality** from deepfakes and synthetic media, where the volume of AI-generated content overwhelms real evidence, leading to a "fidelity gap" collapse. This threatens collective agreement on reality, echoing post-truth dynamics but amplified by AI's scale.

Other contributors include those discussing **epistemic oppression** via biased AI (e.g., in belief revision), black-box opacity challenging explainability, and the shift toward "epistemic AI" that models humility and self-assessment.
