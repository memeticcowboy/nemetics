# Hard Pause Advocates in AI Development: Organizations, Key Figures, and Proposed Enforcement Mechanisms

Created at 2025/11/14 8:38 AM

## 1. Hard Pause Advocates

### Leading Organizations and Frameworks

**PauseAI** (both US and global branches) represents the most explicit organizational voice calling for comprehensive, indefinite halts to frontier AI development. The organization advocates for an international treaty establishing a moratorium on training runs above GPT-4 capability levels until safety can be demonstrated. PauseAI US, led by organizer Felix de Simone, focuses on dual programming: public protests outside major AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta) and direct lobbying of legislators. The organization explicitly frames its advocacy as moving the Overton window to make moderate AI safety measures more politically feasible, even if a full pause remains aspirational.[^1][^2][^3]

**Future of Life Institute (FLI)**, under the leadership of President Max Tegmark, has emerged as a primary institutional voice for hard pause positions. FLI's comprehensive position statement calls for:[^4][^5]

- A pause on frontier AI experiments until adequate oversight and safety mechanisms exist

- A moratorium on developing artificial superintelligence for at least 15 years

- Mandatory licensing, auditing, and oversight regimes before any continuation of frontier development[^4]

The institute's March 2023 open letter calling for a six-month pause on training systems more powerful than GPT-4 garnered over 31,000 signatures, including prominent AI researchers. However, the letter faced criticism for being insufficient, with AI safety researcher Eliezer Yudkowsky arguing it understated the severity of the situation.[^6][^7][^8]

### Key Individual Advocates

**Eliezer Yudkowsky** represents the most extreme position within the hard pause camp, advocating for complete and immediate shutdown of all AI development. In his March 2023 TIME essay "Pausing AI Developments Isn't Enough. We Need to Shut it All Down," Yudkowsky argued that a six-month pause is woefully inadequate given that solving superintelligence safety could require decades. His position includes:[^7][^8][^9][^10]

- Immediate cessation of all large training runs

- International treaties with enforcement mechanisms, potentially including military intervention against rogue AI development

- Recognition that current trajectories lead to human extinction with near-certainty[^8][^9]

Yudkowsky's 2025 book "If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies" (co-authored with Nate Soares) crystallizes this position, asserting that "if any company or group, anywhere on the planet, builds an artificial superintelligence using anything remotely like current techniques, based on anything remotely like the present understanding of AI, then everyone, everywhere on Earth, will die".[^10]

**Roman Yampolskiy**, University of Louisville computer science professor and pioneer of the term "AI safety," estimates a 99.9% probability of AI-caused extinction within the next century. His 2024 book "AI: Unexplainable, Unpredictable, Uncontrollable" argues that AI control is theoretically impossible and that development should be halted until this fundamental problem can be solved—which he considers unlikely. Yampolskiy's position rests on three risk categories: existential risks (everyone dies), suffering risks (everyone wishes they were dead), and loss of meaning.[^11][^12][^13][^14]

**Geoffrey Hinton** and **Yoshua Bengio**, both Turing Award winners and "godfathers of AI," signed the October 2025 Future of Life Institute statement calling for a prohibition on superintelligence development until there is "broad scientific consensus that it will be done safely and controllably, and strong public buy-in". This represents a significant evolution from their earlier, more cautious positions. Hinton, who won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics for his AI contributions, has stated: "If you take the existential risk seriously, as I now do, it might be quite sensible to just stop developing these things any further".[^15][^16][^17][^18][^19]

### Proposals and Mechanisms

Hard pause advocates generally coalesce around several enforcement mechanisms:

1. **Compute Thresholds**: Training runs exceeding specified FLOP (floating-point operations) limits would be prohibited. The Existential Risk Observatory proposes capping allowed training at GPT-4 levels (approximately 10^25 FLOP). PauseAI includes both compute thresholds and capability benchmarks in their proposal.[^20][^21]

2. **Hardware Tracking and Control**: Proposals call for tracking all high-end GPU clusters capable of frontier training and verification that no unlicensed models are being trained at these locations. Max Tegmark's testimony to the Senate proposed requiring licensure before deployment of systems demonstrating uncontrollable capabilities.[^5][^20]

3. **International Treaty Framework**: Most hard pause advocates recognize that unilateral action is insufficient. The Conditional AI Safety Treaty proposed by the Existential Risk Observatory would establish "if-then commitments" where signatories agree to halt unsafe training if loss-of-control thresholds are approached and alignment remains unsolved. This framework draws on biosafety level precedents and attempts to address coordination problems inherent in competitive AI development.[^22]

4. **Prohibition of Capabilities Research**: Some proposals go beyond pausing training to prohibit publication of research that could dramatically reduce compute requirements for dangerous capabilities, recognizing that algorithmic breakthroughs could circumvent hardware-based controls.[^20]
