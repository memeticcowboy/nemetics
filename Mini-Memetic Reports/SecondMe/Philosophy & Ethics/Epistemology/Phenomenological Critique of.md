# Phenomenological Critique of Debt as a Formative and Metaphysical Structure of Consciousness

Created at 2025/11/23 1:15 PM

Interpret: “Formed by indebtedness, fueled by the self as power over illusions materialized as “real” that we presume debt natural, trained to ignore implications of the deeper metaphysical orientation formed.”<br>https://substack.com/@memeticcowboy/note/c-180441964?r=5a5yhr&utm_medium=ios&utm_source=notes-share-action<br>This is a dense, phenomenological critique of how debt structures consciousness and reality itself. Let me unpack its layers:<br>Core Structure<br>The sentence describes a self-reinforcing trap: indebtedness → subject formation → reification → naturalization → metaphysical blindness.<br>Key Elements<br>1. "Formed by indebtedness"<br>Not merely that we're in debt, but that debt constitutes our subjectivity. Our selves are forged through obligation, duty, and the power relations embedded in owing. This echoes Nietzsche's "mnemotechnics of pain" and Foucault's idea that subjectivity is produced by power.<br>2. "Fueled by the self as power over illusions materialized as 'real'"<br>This is the engine of the trap: the self becomes the mechanism that transforms social constructs (illusions) into concrete reality. We don't just participate in debt; our very identity energizes the process of reification—making the abstract (credit, value, obligation) feel tangible and necessary. The scare quotes around "real" highlight this sleight of hand.<br>3. "that we presume debt natural"<br>Through this reification, debt becomes invisible as a historical and contingent arrangement. It shifts from "a thing we do" to "the way things are"—a classic ideological move. Graeber's Debt: The First 5,000 Years targets this exact naturalization.<br>4. "trained to ignore implications of the deeper metaphysical orientation formed"<br>The final, crucial move: this system doesn't just shape economics—it creates an entire metaphysics where owing/being owed becomes the fundamental relation of existence. We learn not to ask: What kind of being-in-the-world does perpetual indebtedness produce? The implication is that debt creates a specific orientation toward time (future as obligation), self (as human capital), and others (as creditors/debtors), which we are disciplined never to examine.<br>Synthesis<br>The phrase critiques how debt doesn't just describe our condition; it forms the self that then enforces debt as reality, naturalizes it, and forecloses any questioning of the metaphysical prison this creates. It's less about money owed than about a worldview where existence itself becomes a perpetual state of owing—whether to capital, society, or some abstract future.<br>This is essentially an ideology critique in the Marxist-Foucauldian vein: what appears as economic necessity is revealed as a contingent metaphysics that shapes subjectivity while remaining invisible to that very subjectivity.<br>
