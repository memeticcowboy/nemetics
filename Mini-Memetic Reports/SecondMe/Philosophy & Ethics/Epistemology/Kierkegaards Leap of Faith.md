# Kierkegaard's Radical Call to Uncertain Faith

Created at 2025/12/03 7:58 AM

"Go out over 70,000 fathoms with only God to catch you"<br>This vivid metaphor captures Kierkegaard’s view on the nature of faith and his critique of the objective approach to Christianity.

**Context and Meaning**<br>Faith as a Risky Venture:<br>The phrase "Go out over 70,000 fathoms" symbolizes venturing far into the unknown, into deep and vast uncertainty. A fathom is a nautical measure of depth (about 6 feet), so 70,000 fathoms represents an immense depth, emphasizing the profound risk and uncertainty involved in true faith.

**Only God to Catch You:**<br>This highlights that when one takes the leap of faith, there is no safety net of objective proof, certainty, or rational guarantees. The individual must trust entirely in God alone, without relying on empirical evidence or logical demonstration.

**Relation to Kierkegaard’s Critique of the Objective Approach**<br>Objective Approach Seeks Certainty:<br>Kierkegaard criticizes the objective approach for trying to make Christianity a rational system based on evidence and probabilities. This approach demands certainty and avoids the paradox and offense inherent in faith.

**Faith Requires Embracing Uncertainty:**<br>True faith, according to Kierkegaard, is a passionate, subjective commitment that embraces the "objective uncertainty" of Christianity — the paradox of the God-man, the offense of the absurd, and the lack of rational proof.

**The Leap of Faith:**<br>The metaphor illustrates the "leap of faith" — a decisive, personal choice to believe despite uncertainty and the absence of objective proof. It is a solitary, inward act of trust where the individual "floats" over the abyss, relying solely on God’s grace.

**Rejecting the Safety of Rational Proofs:**<br>By urging believers to "go out over 70,000 fathoms," Kierkegaard emphasizes that faith cannot be secured by intellectual deliberation or evidential accumulation. Instead, it demands a radical trust that transcends reason.

**Summary**<br>Kierkegaard’s quote means that genuine Christian faith involves a courageous, personal leap into profound uncertainty, where one abandons the quest for objective proof and rests entirely on God’s support. It is a call to embrace the paradox and risk of faith, rather than seeking the comfort of rational certainty.

## Insight


**⛅ Embracing the Leap of Faith: Kierkegaard’s Perspective on Faith and Uncertainty**  
- **Faith as a Risky Venture**: The phrase "Go out over 70,000 fathoms" symbolizes entering a realm of deep uncertainty, representing the inherent risks associated with true faith. This immense depth signifies the challenge of stepping into the unknown where the believer must confront their own doubts and fears.  
- **Only God as the Safety Net**: This highlights the essential belief that when pursuing faith, one must relinquish reliance on empirical evidence or rational certainty, placing total trust in God's providence. This notion emphasizes that faith is a personal journey devoid of external assurances or guarantees.  
- **Rejecting Rational Proofs**: Kierkegaard calls out the objective approach to Christianity, which seeks logical proofs and certainty. He argues that true faith arises from moving beyond intellectual debate and embracing the uncertainty that comes with trusting in God alone.  

**⚖️ Critique of the Objective Approach**  
- **Seeks Certainty and Objectivity**: Kierkegaard criticizes the objective approach for attempting to create a rational framework around Christianity, which demands a level of certainty that faith does not provide. This approach often leads to disillusionment as it fails to encompass the complexity of genuine belief.  
- **Faith as Subjective Commitment**: True faith necessitates a passionate, personal commitment that recognizes and embraces the "objective uncertainty" inherent in Christianity. It involves accepting paradoxes and the irrational, highlighting how this aspect is crucial to understanding faith itself.  
- **Confronting the Paradox of Faith**: The fundamental paradox of faith, exemplified in the concept of the God-man, illustrates that faith cannot be understood through logic alone and requires a deeper emotional and spiritual resonance with one's beliefs.  

**🚀 The Leap of Faith Explained**  
- **Personal Decision to Believe**: The leap of faith represents a conscious choice to trust in God despite overwhelming uncertainties and the absence of concrete evidence. It necessitates boldness and an inward resolve to commit to belief without guarantees.  
- **Floats Over the Abyss**: The metaphor of floating over an abyss conceives of faith as an act that transcends intellectual reasoning and engages with one's emotional and spiritual conviction. It portrays faith as a journey that demands courage and resilience.  
- **Solitary Act of Trust**: The leap of faith is characterized by its solitary nature, suggesting that true belief is a personal endeavor undertaken by the individual, often in isolation, emphasizing the deep, personal relationship one has with God.  

**🌌 Embracing Uncertainty**  
- **Genuine Faith Must Accept Risk**: Kierkegaard holds that authentic Christian faith cannot exist without the acceptance of risk; it requires one to grapple with doubts and uncertainties rather than seeking comfort in rationality.  
- **Importance of Personal Experience**: The journey of faith is underlined as deeply personal and unique to each individual, thereby asserting that external factors or communal beliefs should not dictate one’s understanding of faith.  
- **Faith Over Empirical Evidence**: This viewpoint challenges believers to prioritize their spiritual experiences and internal convictions over the need for tangible proof or societal validation of their beliefs.  

**📜 Summary Insight**  
Kierkegaard’s perspective reframes faith as a courageous and deeply personal leap into the unknown. It calls individuals to abandon their pursuit of objective proof and instead cultivate a profound trust in God, navigating the paradoxes and uncertainties of belief. This understanding encourages believers to embrace the inherent risks of faith as foundational to their spiritual journeys.
