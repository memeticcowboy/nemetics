# Reconceptualizing Free Will: Insights from Physics

Created at 2025/07/26 11:23 AM

Here is the 8 Core Logic summary for [“The Physics of Free Will: A Radical New Theory”](https://youtu.be/7kvXihDAOi0?si=vVDs2BLqKaVmPm3h)

 based on Professor Jenann Ismael’s discussion:




**1.** 

**Purposes and Goals**


To bridge physics and philosophy in order to reconceptualize free will within a scientifically grounded worldview. Ismael’s goal is to explore how agency, consciousness, and choice can coexist with the physical laws of the universe, especially in light of modern physics.




**2.** 

**Central Questions or Problems**


- Can free will exist in a world governed by deterministic or probabilistic laws of physics?
- How do temporal and informational constraints affect predictability and agency?
- What role does consciousness play in shaping decisions and identity?





**3.** 

**Fundamental Concepts**


- Free Will in Open Systems: Organisms evolve to utilize environmental indeterminacy.
- Past Light Cone & Incompleteness: Events cannot be fully predicted due to relativistic constraints.
- Self-Curation: Individuals are informational structures shaping themselves over time.
- Self-Reference and Paradox: Systems predicting themselves lead to inherent limitations.
- Arrow of Time (Thermodynamics): Causality emerges from entropy, anchoring temporal flow.





**4.** 

**Essential Information**


- Classical determinism is challenged by relativity and complexity theory.
- Predictive incompleteness arises from limited information in the past light cone.
- Consciousness and memory interact dynamically with choice-making.
- Evolution favors exploitation of environmental unpredictability.
- Concepts like Newcomb’s Paradox highlight the tensions in prediction and freedom.





**5.** 

**Points of View or Frames of Reference**


- Philosophical Naturalism: Grounding human freedom in physical law.
- Relativistic Physics: Emphasizing limits of prediction and causation.
- Complex Systems Theory: Seeing free will as emergent, not absolute.
- Phenomenology of Time: Experiencing time as unfolding, not fixed.





**6.** 

**Underlying Assumptions**


- Physics does not account for all aspects of lived experience.
- Deterministic models are useful approximations, not ultimate truths.
- Human identity and agency are structured but also self-revisable.
- Consciousness is an active participant in shaping meaning and behavior.





**7.** 

**Inferences and Conclusions**


- Agency is compatible with a scientific worldview when redefined in light of informational incompleteness and systemic emergence.
- Free will is not absolute control, but situated choice within a causal yet unpredictable world.
- The self is a recursive, curating system that processes, interprets, and enacts meaning.





**8.** 

**Implications and Consequences**


- Reframes debates on free will away from binary opposition with determinism.
- Suggests that meaning, responsibility, and identity are consistent with a physicalist framework.
- Offers a language to integrate scientific insight with existential and ethical reflection.
- Points toward a lived, emergent freedom—conscious beings as nodes of creative navigation within structured uncertainty.
