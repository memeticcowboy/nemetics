# Determinism and Compatibilism

Created at 2024/10/06 5:02 PM

Determinism and compatibilism are two philosophical stances that grapple with the relationship between free will and the causal structure of the universe.

- **Determinism** posits that all events, including human actions, are predetermined by prior causes. In this view, there is no room for genuine choice or randomness.
- **Compatibilism** argues that determinism and free will can coexist. It suggests that even if our actions are determined by prior causes, we can still be considered to have free will if we act in accordance with our own desires and beliefs.

Essentially, compatibilists believe that free will is a matter of having the right kind of internal causes for our actions, rather than being completely uncaused.

### Determinism

- **Pierre-Simon Laplace:** A French mathematician and astronomer, Laplace is often credited with formulating the concept of a Laplacian demon, a hypothetical being that knows the exact positions and velocities of all particles in the universe and can therefore predict the future with perfect accuracy. This idea is closely tied to deterministic views of the universe.
- **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz:** A German philosopher and mathematician, Leibniz developed a monistic view of the universe, arguing that everything is composed of a single substance (monads). This view is often interpreted as deterministic, as the behavior of monads is predetermined by their nature.

### Compatibilism

- **Thomas Hobbes:** An English philosopher, Hobbes argued that free will is compatible with determinism. He believed that freedom is the absence of external restraints, and that even if our actions are determined by internal causes (such as our desires and beliefs), we are still free to act as we choose.
- **Daniel Dennett:** A contemporary American philosopher, Dennett is a prominent advocate of compatibilism. He argues that free will is a matter of having the right kind of internal causes for our actions, and that these causes can be consistent with a deterministic universe.
