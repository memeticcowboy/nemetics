# The Shift from Morality to Integrity in Human Existence

Created at 2025/10/31 12:43 PM

Morality has been replaced by integrity as a basis for human existence, starting from the middle of the 20th century, with the influence of rationalism, empiricism, and positivism.<br><br>The disappearance of morality and ethics from mainstream philosophy, mass media, and everyday life has led to integrity being used as a solution, measured by key performance indicators (KPIs).<br><br>According to Dr. Andrej Drapal, humanity cannot survive without both morality and ethics, and integrity is seen as a "fake morality" that lacks the depth and discussion required for true moral existence, as it is based on measurable concepts rather than constant sense-making and self-authoritative dialogue.<br><br><br>

## Insight


**🔍 The Evolution of Morality to Integrity in Human Existence**

- **📉 The Decline of Morality and Ethics**: The mid-20th century marked a shift where morality and ethics were gradually replaced by concepts such as integrity. This decline is attributed to the rise of rationalism and empiricism, leading to an existential void devoid of these crucial ideals within mainstream philosophy and everyday life. 
  - Key Conclusion: Rationalism and empiricism have diminished the significance of morality, reducing human experience to mechanical determinism rather than ethical discourse and moral ambiguity.
  
- **📊 Integrity as Measurable Standards**: Integrity has emerged as the primary solution replacing morality, articulated through measurable standards known as Key Performance Indicators (KPIs). Organizations utilize KPIs to gauge integrity, prioritizing quantifiable attributes over qualitative moral discussions.
  - Key Conclusion: The focus on KPIs reflects a societal shift towards measurability, where integrity, although practical, lacks the depth necessary for true moral engagement.

- **🔄 The Role of Communication and Dialogue**: Ethical and moral principles are inherently memetic, thriving on communication and constant reinterpretation through dialogue. Without this dialogue, these principles become obsolete.
  - Key Conclusion: The lack of communication diminishes the relevance of both morality and ethics, as they require active engagement and reinterpretation for continued existence.

- **🕊️ The Illusion of 'Fake Morality'**: The notion that integrity acts as a substitute for morality is highlighted as "fake morality." This perception leads to a superficial understanding, where true ethical discourse is evaded in favor of easily digestible, quantifiable metrics.
  - Key Conclusion: Adopting integrity as a stand-in for morality undermines the complexity of ethical discussions, ultimately restricting personal growth and societal development.

- **🚫 The Fear of Self-Authority**: Contemporary individuals often shy away from self-authority and the responsibility of moral reasoning, opting instead for formulaic answers and predefined guidelines that diminish personal agency.
  - Key Conclusion: The reluctance to engage with one's moral compass fosters dependence on external systems, which can hinder true personal and social development.

- **🙅‍♂️ The Dangers of Abstract Humanism**: The trend of seeking abstract humanism disconnected from individual identity leads to a lack of responsibility and authenticity, which is detrimental to societal well-being.
  - Key Conclusion: Overreliance on abstract concepts of humanism detracts from genuine human experiences and ethical obligations, creating a void in personal accountability.

- **⚖️ The Necessity of Authentic Morality**: A clarion call for re-establishing the importance of genuine morality is essential for human sustainability. The absence of a robust moral framework poses a threat to both individuals and societies.
  - Key Conclusion: Humanity's survival is contingent upon reinvigorating moral discussions, as integrity alone cannot provide the foundational ethics needed for coexistence.

- **🗨️ The Call for Practical Engagement**: While theoretical discussions on ethics and morality exist, they often fail to penetrate practical applications in management and daily life. Genuine moral practice is essential for leadership and societal norms.
  - Key Conclusion: Bridging the gap between abstract ethical theory and practical application is critical for fostering a culture that values morality as much as integrity.
