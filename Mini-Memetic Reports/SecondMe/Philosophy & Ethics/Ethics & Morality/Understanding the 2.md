# Understanding the Technocratic Saviors Selfplex: Ethics and Technology in Modern Society

Created at 2025/06/21 7:32 AM

### 🔹 Selfplex Type: *Technocratic Saviors*

- **Core Narrative**: The world’s challenges—moral, social, ecological—can and *should* be solved by elite technical mastery. Rooted in Enlightenment rationalism and Silicon Valley solutionism, this selfplex sees technology as a neutral or benevolent force in need of only better engineering, not ethical transformation.
- **Memetic Immune Triggers**:
    - Appeals to virtue ethics (“soft,” “subjective”)
    - Critiques of system-level technological neutrality
    - Invocations of tradition, spiritual formation, or moral cultivation
- **“They heard…” Biases**:
    - “We must cultivate technomoral wisdom” → *“They think tech innovation is dangerous or immoral”*
    - “Virtue ethics matters in AI design” → *“They don’t trust our technical competence or objectivity”*
- **Symbolic Markers**:
    - Lexicon of efficiency, scale, disruption, optimization
    - Aesthetic of minimalism, data visualization, and techno-futurism
    - Rituals: hackathons, launch demos, TED-style visionary pitches
- **Epistemic Style**: Rational empiricism + predictive modeling; legitimacy comes from system metrics and technical reproducibility.
- **Vulnerabilities**:
    - Moral blindness to unintended consequences
    - Incoherence when “objective” design fails ethical tests (e.g., biased AI)
    - Alienation from publics not aligned with tech culture
- **Persuasion Entry Points**:
    - Framing virtues as “moral UX design”
    - Invoking trusted figures who blend moral and technical authority (e.g. Timnit Gebru, Fei-Fei Li)
    - Showing how technomoral failure leads to lost trust, user attrition, or reputational collapse
