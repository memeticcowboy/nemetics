# 1. 🧬 Posthuman Ethics

### Key Points

### Direct Answer

Posthuman ethics offers a fresh way to think about morality, especially as technology like AI becomes part of our lives. Here’s a breakdown based on your questions, keeping things simple and acknowledging the complexity involved.

#### Main Tenets and Critiques

Posthuman ethics, as seen in Braidotti and Haraway’s work, moves away from the idea that humans are the center of morality. Braidotti focuses on ideas like treating all life equally (species egalitarianism) and seeing ethics as about connections between humans, machines, and nature (relational ethics). Haraway’s ideas, like her cyborg concept, also challenge the separation between humans and technology. They critique classical humanism for putting humans above all else, arguing it ignores the impact on other beings and the environment.

#### Subjectivity and Moral Agency

In a world where humans and machines mix, posthumanism suggests we’re not just individuals but part of a network. Subjectivity becomes shared, not just ours alone—it includes machines. Moral agency, or the ability to make ethical choices, isn’t just human anymore; it can involve AI, especially if we design it to think ethically. This is complex, as it’s still debated how much agency machines can have.

#### Guiding AI Design

For urban infrastructure, posthuman ethics could mean AI helps cities work for both people and nature, like optimizing traffic to reduce pollution. In healthcare, it might mean AI considers how health connects to the environment, not just treating patients in isolation. In warfare, it could push AI to think about the wider impact on ecosystems, not just human lives. These ideas aim for inclusivity and sustainability, though how to implement them is still being explored.

#### AI as a Relational Partner

If we see AI as a partner, not just a tool, new ethical principles emerge. We’d need to ensure AI is accountable, transparent in its decisions, and considers impacts on non-humans, like the environment. This includes making sure AI works with us in a shared network, not just serving us. It’s a debated area, with some seeing AI as having potential moral status, while others see it as just a tool.
