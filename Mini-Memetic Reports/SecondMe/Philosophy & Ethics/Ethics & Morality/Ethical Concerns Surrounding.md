**Ethical Concerns Surrounding Nanomedicine:**

- **Privacy and Surveillance**: Nanomedicine could potentially be used for surveillance at a molecular level, raising concerns about privacy invasion.

- **Equity in Access**: There's a risk that nanomedicine could widen the gap between the rich and poor, with only affluent individuals having access to advanced treatments.

- **Unintended Consequences**: The long-term effects of nanoparticles in the human body are not fully understood, posing risks of unforeseen health issues.

- **Autonomy**: The use of nanomedicine might lead to scenarios where patients' autonomy is compromised, especially if these technologies can alter behavior or cognition.

**Nanotech and Personal Privacy:**

- **Data Collection**: Nanotechnology could enable the collection of incredibly detailed personal health data, which if not properly secured, could lead to privacy breaches.

- **Surveillance**: Nano-devices could be used for surveillance, tracking movements or health conditions without consent, leading to a society where privacy is significantly eroded.

**Skepticism About Nanotech Advancements:**

- **Dual-Use Technology**: While nanotech offers revolutionary benefits, it also has potential for misuse, such as in military applications or for creating harmful substances.

- **Environmental Impact**: The production and disposal of nanomaterials might have unknown environmental impacts, which could be detrimental if not managed properly.

- **Regulation and Oversight**: There might be inadequate regulation to handle the ethical, safety, and environmental issues posed by nanotech, leading to potential harm if advancements outpace laws and ethical guidelines.
