Here are core ethical questions.

Moral Responsibility. Who is accountable for actions, and under what conditions?

Moral Dilemmas. How do we resolve conflicts between competing ethical principles (e.g., truth vs. loyalty, individual rights vs. collective good)?

Moral Relativism vs. Absolutism. Are ethical standards universal, or do they vary by culture, context, or individual?

Here’s a brief overview of some prominent ethical frameworks:

Utilitarianism (John Stuart Mill, Jeremy Bentham): Actions are right if they promote the greatest happiness for the greatest number.

Kantian Ethics (Immanuel Kant): Actions are right if they follow universal moral laws, such as the categorical imperative ("Act only according to that maxim whereby you can, at the same time, will that it should become a universal law").

Virtue Ethics (Aristotle): Focuses on cultivating virtues (e.g., honesty, courage) to achieve eudaimonia (flourishing or a good life).

Care Ethics (Carol Gilligan, Nel Noddings): Emphasizes relationships, empathy, and care as central to moral decision-making.

Social Contract Theory (Thomas Hobbes, John Locke, Jean-Jacques Rousseau): Morality arises from agreements among individuals to live cooperatively.
