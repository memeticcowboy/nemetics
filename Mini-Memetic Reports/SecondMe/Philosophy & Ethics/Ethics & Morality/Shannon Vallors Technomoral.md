### Direct Answer

#### Shannon Vallor’s Concept and Techno-Virtues

Shannon Vallor uses classical virtue ethics, rooted in thinkers like Aristotle, to address modern AI and robotics. She suggests we need "technomoral virtues" like honesty, self-control, and justice to live well with technology. These virtues help us make ethical decisions as AI becomes part of daily life, focusing on character rather than just rules or outcomes.

The proposed techno-virtues for today include honesty, self-control, humility, justice, courage, empathy, care, civility, flexibility, perspective, magnanimity, and technomoral wisdom. Unlike utilitarianism, which focuses on maximizing happiness, or deontology, which follows strict rules, these virtues emphasize developing good habits and character, making them more adaptable to tech changes.

#### Designing AI to Promote Virtues

Designing AI to encourage virtues in users might look like creating hiring platforms that ensure fair treatment, autonomous vehicles that balance safety and fairness, or healthcare AI that respects patient privacy. These systems aim to foster virtues like justice and care, but how to implement this effectively is still debated.

#### Applying Virtue Ethics in Organizations

Organizations can apply virtue ethics to employee-AI relationships by offering ethics training, setting policies that promote virtues like honesty, and designing AI tools that support ethical decisions. For example, AI might give feedback to encourage fair choices. However, challenges like cultural differences and ensuring AI aligns with virtues remain, and it’s a complex area with ongoing discussion.
