	•	00:00 John Green introduces the Crash Course Religions series, promising a fun and engaging discussion.
	•	00:04 Friedrich Nietzsche, a 19th-century philosopher, controversially declared, “God is dead,” indicating his atheistic views.
	•	00:14 Nietzsche criticized Christianity and religion, warning of potential societal consequences from a godless universe.
	•	00:29 Nietzsche questioned the source of moral guidance in a world without God, fearing societal collapse.
	•	00:40 Despite Nietzsche’s alarm, society has continued to function without belief in a deity.
	•	01:13 The term “atheism” stems from the Greek “atheos,” meaning “without a God.”
	•	01:30 Atheism gained traction in the 16th and 17th centuries during the Protestant Reformation, reflecting changing religious beliefs.
	•	01:41 Initially, “atheist” was used as an insult; dictionaries labeled atheism as a “damnable doctrine.”
	•	01:56 Enlightenment thinkers like Voltaire and Diderot popularized non-belief, even amidst personal struggles with faith.
	•	02:12 Early American founders leaned towards deism, believing in a distant creator rather than an active deity.
	•	02:47 19th-century atheism grew through philosophers like Nietzsche and Darwin, challenging traditional religious narratives.
	•	03:24 Studies in 2024 indicated around 28% of Americans identified as religiously unaffiliated, though not all are atheists.
	•	03:35 The unaffiliated category includes agnostics, who claim uncertainty about God’s existence.
	•	04:09 Variations within atheism include explicit atheism, which denies a higher power, and implicit atheism, which passively lacks belief.
	•	04:39 Pascal’s Wager argues that believing in God is a safer bet, a prominent philosophical debate.
	•	05:45 Atheism is often discussed in opposition to Christianity, yet its history and diversity extend beyond that comparison.
	•	06:17 Up to 20% of self-identified Christians may be agnostic regarding the God of the Bible.
	•	06:32 Buddhism, founded without a focus on deities, exemplifies ancient nontheistic traditions.
	•	07:02 In modern times, atheism critiques religion and societal values, as seen in the works of figures like Frederick Douglass.
	•	07:43 The “New Atheism” movement, highlighted by authors like Sam Harris, challenges religious beliefs and advocates for secular ethics.
	•	08:30 Defining religion is complex; atheism’s relation to religion raises questions about belief systems and ethical frameworks.
	•	10:28 Atheism can represent a commitment to ethics without divine judgment or an indifference to religious considerations.
	•	10:45 Atheism exists alongside theism, often complementing rather than simply opposing religious frameworks.
	•	11:09 The debate on whether atheism qualifies as a religion hinges on definitions of moral imperatives and human consciousness.
