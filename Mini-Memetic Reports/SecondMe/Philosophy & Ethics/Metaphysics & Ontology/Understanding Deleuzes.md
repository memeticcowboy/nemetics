# Understanding Deleuze's Concept of the Knot in Relation to Rhizomes and Assemblages

Created at 2025/08/10 6:51 PM

Deleuze never gives “knot” a whole theory of its own, so when people speak of a “Deleuzian knot” they are usually borrowing a metaphor he uses in two places:

1.  the fold (Leibniz-Bergson part of his work)

2.  the assemblage / rhizome (his joint work with Guattari)

Think of the knot as a tight, local tangle inside a much larger, restless fabric.

1.  Outside the knot: an open textile

The world is a vast sheet of moving threads that can slide, stretch, and re-route.

Deleuze calls this the plane of immanence or the rhizome—everything is connected, nothing is fixed.

2.  Inside the knot: a temporary hardening

At certain spots the threads loop back on themselves enough times that a bulge appears.

That bulge now behaves like a thing: it has duration, edges, and effects on other threads.

Yet it is still made only of the same moving threads that continue beyond it.

3.  What the knot does

•  Slows down the global flow just long enough for us to give it a name (“tree,” “law,” “self”).

•  Creates an inside and an outside: the knot appears as an “individual,” while the rest of the fabric becomes “environment.”

•  Preserves difference: no two knots repeat exactly; each is a unique configuration of the same threads.

4.  Everyday picture

Imagine a fishing net lying on the beach.

•  Most of the net lies flat, moving with the tide.

•  At one spot a tangle of seaweed, shells and string forms a fist-sized clump.

•  For a while you can point and say “that’s a knot,” but the tide keeps working on it; sooner or later the clump loosens and the threads spread out again.

So, in Deleuzian language:

A knot is a folded region of the rhizome where differential speeds and forces momentarily crystallize into something we treat as an object or identity, even though it is only a localized intensification of the same ceaseless flow.
