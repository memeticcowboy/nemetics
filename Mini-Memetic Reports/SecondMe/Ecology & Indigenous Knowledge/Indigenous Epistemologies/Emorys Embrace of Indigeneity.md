## Insight


**🌍 Embracing Indigeneity at Emory University**

- **Defining Indigeneity**: Indigeneity is characterized as a quality linking individuals and groups to specific places, emphasizing knowledge and respect for original ways of life. This definition highlights the importance of cultural identity and connection to land, which is crucial for understanding Indigenous perspectives.

- **Significance of Indigenous Land Management**: Indigenous peoples manage half of the world’s land and are responsible for 80% of the world’s biodiversity. This statistic underscores the vital role Indigenous communities play in environmental stewardship and the preservation of biodiversity, making their involvement essential in discussions about sustainability.

- **Growth of Indigenous Populations**: The American Indian/Alaska Native population has seen an 86.5% increase since 2010, attributed to improved methods of measuring complex identities. This growth reflects a broader recognition and validation of Indigenous identities, which is important for fostering inclusive communities.

**🏛️ The Importance of Timing for Emory**

- **Legal Recognition of Tribal Sovereignty**: The U.S. Supreme Court's 2020 decision regarding the Muscogee Creek Nation's territory has significant implications for tribal sovereignty. This legal acknowledgment opens avenues for Emory to engage with Indigenous communities and respect their governance structures.

- **Cultural Relevance**: Emory's current moment is ripe for embracing Indigeneity, as it aligns with broader societal shifts towards recognizing and valuing Indigenous rights and knowledge systems. This cultural relevance can enhance the university's mission and community engagement.

- **Framework for Non-Indigenous Involvement**: Non-Indigenous individuals can engage with Indigeneity by respecting Indigenous protocols and preferences. This approach fosters a collaborative environment where diverse voices can contribute to the understanding and appreciation of Indigenous cultures.

**🤝 Community Engagement and Involvement**

- **Role of the Native American Student Association (NAISA)**: NAISA serves as a cultural, intellectual, and social hub for Native and Indigenous voices at Emory. It emphasizes community building and inclusivity, encouraging collaboration and the sharing of Indigenous knowledge.

- **Promoting Awareness of Indigenous Issues**: NAISA aims to raise awareness about Native cultures and the challenges faced by Indigenous communities. This focus on advocacy is crucial for fostering understanding and support within the broader university community.

- **Invitation to Participate**: Emory encourages all individuals, regardless of their background, to engage with Indigenous studies and communities. This invitation promotes a culture of respect and learning, essential for meaningful involvement.

**📞 Contact for Further Involvement**

- **Beth Michel's Role**: As the Senior Associate Director for the Center for Native & Indigenous Studies, Beth Michel is a key contact for those interested in getting involved. Her position highlights the university's commitment to supporting Indigenous initiatives and fostering connections.

- **Opportunities for Engagement**: Interested individuals are encouraged to reach out to Beth Michel for information on how to participate in Indigenous studies and community activities. This proactive approach can lead to enriching experiences and deeper understanding.

- **Building a Supportive Network**: Engaging with the Center for Native & Indigenous Studies can help create a supportive network for Indigenous and non-Indigenous individuals alike, fostering collaboration and shared learning experiences.
