# Stonehenge's Origins Revealed

Created at 2024/08/15 4:36 PM

A fingerprint on a fragment of Stonehenge reveals the ancient monument's possible origins. The fingerprint, likely from a craftsman, offers insights into the people who constructed Stonehenge. This discovery may help archaeologists better understand the monument's builders and their techniques.

## Insight
Recent findings from a fingerprint on a fragment of Stonehenge suggest a significant shift in our understanding of the monument's origins. This discovery indicates that the Altar Stone likely originated from Scotland rather than Wales, highlighting advanced transport methods and societal organization among its builders. **🔍 Origins of the Altar Stone**
- **Altar Stone's New Provenance**: The Altar Stone, previously believed to be from Wales, is now traced to the Orcadian Basin in Scotland, approximately 466 miles away from Stonehenge.
- **Chemical Fingerprinting Methodology**: Researchers utilized chemical analysis of mineral grains within the Altar Stone to establish its distinct chemical fingerprint, differentiating it from Welsh bedrock.
- **Implications for Historical Understanding**: This finding challenges long-held beliefs about the origins of Stonehenge and suggests a need to reassess the sources of other stones at the site.

**🚢 Transport and Trade Networks**

- **Challenges of Overland Transport**: Transporting such a massive stone overland would have posed significant challenges, reinforcing the idea of advanced logistical capabilities among prehistoric societies.
