## Insight
The Mystic Lake Declaration, drafted during a significant climate change workshop in November 2009, emphasizes the urgent need for indigenous perspectives in global climate discussions. It calls for a moratorium on fossil fuel exploration and advocates for sustainable practices rooted in indigenous knowledge. The declaration aims to elevate the voices of Native peoples at the upcoming Copenhagen climate talks, highlighting their unique experiences and solutions to climate change. If you're interested in supporting indigenous climate initiatives, consider engaging with local organizations or participating in advocacy efforts.

**🌍 Indigenous Perspectives on Climate Change**
- **Inclusion of Indigenous Knowledge**: The declaration stresses the importance of integrating indigenous science and traditional knowledge into climate change solutions, recognizing that indigenous communities are disproportionately affected by environmental changes.
- **Call for Global Solutions**: It advocates for the United States to embrace indigenous perspectives while seeking global solutions to climate change, particularly at international forums like the United Nations.

**🚫 Moratorium on Fossil Fuels**
- **Immediate Action Required**: The declaration calls for a moratorium on new exploration for oil, gas, coal, and uranium, emphasizing the need to phase out fossil fuels to protect indigenous communities.
- **Binding Emission Reduction Targets**: It demands stringent emission reduction targets, proposing a 40% reduction in carbon emissions for developed countries by 2020, and 95% by 2050.

**🌱 Transition to Sustainable Practices**
- **Support for Green Economy**: Participants agreed to support a transition to a green economy, focusing on sustainable jobs and energy solutions that respect indigenous practices.
- **Development of Indigenous Agricultural Economy**: The declaration promotes the development of an indigenous agricultural economy based on traditional food systems and sustainable resource management.

**⚡ Critique of False Solutions**
- **Challenge to Misleading Technologies**: The group criticized solutions like nuclear energy and carbon capture as inadequate, advocating instead for genuine renewable energy developments.
- **Focus on Real Offsets**: It emphasizes that true offsets come from renewable energy that displaces fossil fuel energy, rather than market-based mechanisms.

**📜 Advocacy for International Agreements**
- **Support for Kyoto Protocol**: The declaration urges the U.S. to sign the Kyoto Protocol and the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples.
- **Highlighting Energy Consumption Issues**: It points out the unsustainable energy consumption patterns of industrialized nations, calling for a reevaluation of energy practices.

**🏫 Role of Tribal Colleges**
- **Importance of Education**: The declaration highlights the critical role of tribal colleges in addressing climate change and developing green technologies.
- **Revitalization of Indigenous Languages**: It calls for efforts to revitalize indigenous languages, which hold centuries of ecological knowledge essential for environmental stewardship.

**🤝 Community Engagement**
- **Listening to Community Leaders**: The workshop included a 'listening post' from the White House to gather insights from community leaders on climate impacts.
- **Strengthening Native Voices**: The declaration aims to elevate the visibility of Native peoples in climate discussions, ensuring their voices are heard at international levels.

**🌐 Future Actions and Goals**
- **Distribution at UN Climate Talks**: The Mystic Lake Declaration will be presented at the UN climate talks, aiming to strengthen the representation of indigenous issues.
- **Building Sustainable Communities**: The group is committed to fostering sustainable communities through education and the development of clean energy technologies.
