# Kate Raworth's Doughnut Economics: A Framework for Thriving Within Planetary Boundaries

Created at 2025/04/29 4:23 PM

Here are the **eight key thinking components** that underlie the [TED Talk by economist Kate Raworth](https://www.ted.com/talks/kate_raworth_a_healthy_economy_should_be_designed_to_thrive_not_grow).

***

### 🧭 1. **Purposes and Goals**

**Primary Goal:**<br>To challenge the dominant economic paradigm of endless GDP growth and propose a new model—**Doughnut Economics**—that prioritizes **human and planetary thriving within ecological limits**.

***

### ❓ 2. **Central Questions or Problems**

- Why do modern economies depend so heavily on endless growth?
- What are the social, ecological, and political consequences of this growth addiction?
- How can we redesign economies to be **regenerative and distributive by design**?
- What should progress look like in the 21st century?

***

### 📚 3. **Fundamental Concepts**

- **GDP as flawed metric:** Originated in the 1930s, GDP has become the default economic goal, despite not measuring well-being or sustainability.
- **Growth addiction:** Economies are financially, politically, and socially dependent on continuous growth.
- **Doughnut Economics:** A visual framework where progress means staying within a safe ecological ceiling while meeting a social foundation.
- **Regenerative and Distributive Design:** Systems should reuse resources and share value widely.
- **Planetary Boundaries:** Ecological limits that must not be exceeded for Earth to remain hospitable.

***

### 🧾 4. **Essential Information**

- Rostow’s 5 Stages of Growth framed development as linear, endless economic ascent.
- Modern economies overshoot planetary boundaries and fail to meet basic needs for billions.
- Raworth's doughnut model balances **social justice** (inner circle) and **ecological safety** (outer circle).
- Technological advances offer potential for distributed, sustainable economies.
- Cities and communities are already modeling circular and regenerative design principles.
- Boundaries can enable creativity rather than stifle it.

***

### 🔍 5. **Points of View or Frames of Reference**

- **Historical economic theory:** Especially W.W. Rostow's Cold War-era growth model.
- **Ecological economics:** Focused on resource limits and planetary systems.
- **Critical sociology/psychology:** Cites Bernays and Freud’s influence on consumerism.
- **Design perspective:** Treats economic systems as designable and improvable structures.
- **Cultural-historical lens:** References ancient symbols of balance and cyclical thinking.

***

### ⚙️ 6. **Underlying Assumptions**

- The planet has finite ecological limits that human activity must respect.
- Endless economic growth is neither natural nor sustainable.
- Well-being is not adequately captured by GDP or traditional economic indicators.
- Systems can be redesigned deliberately and ethically.
- Human creativity flourishes within constraints.

***

### 🔗 7. **Inferences and Conclusions**

- Current economic models are unsustainable and unjust by design.
- The obsession with GDP growth is a structural issue rooted in outdated paradigms.
- Economies must be reoriented around well-being, equity, and ecological regeneration.
- Thriving, not growth, should be the guiding principle of 21st-century economics.

***

### 🌍 8. **Implications and Consequences**

- If unaddressed, continued growth addiction will deepen inequality and ecological collapse.
- Adopting doughnut economics could foster global justice and environmental sustainability.
- Shifting from growth to thriving could redefine global development priorities.
- Future economic models must integrate **systemic design thinking**, **technological innovation**, and **cultural transformation**.
