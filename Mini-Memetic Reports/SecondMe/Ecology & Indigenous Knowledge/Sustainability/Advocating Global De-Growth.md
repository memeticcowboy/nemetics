The findings suggest that a structured approach to de-growth can lead to significant improvements in resource equity and environmental health. - **De-growth is essential for ecological balance.**: The article emphasizes that a global reduction in material throughput is necessary to address ecological crises, particularly in wealthier nations.
- **Rich countries must reduce growth to allow poorer nations to develop.**: It argues that wealthier countries should adopt negative growth rates to create space for positive growth in developing regions.
- **A new economic framework is needed.**: The call for an ecological macroeconomics framework highlights the need for a system that supports de-growth while promoting social equity.

- **New accounting tools are required.**: The article suggests developing new metrics that measure qualitative growth rather than just quantitative economic expansion.
- **Legal frameworks must support de-growth.**: Establishing laws that facilitate a transition to a less hyper-productive economy is crucial for sustainability.
- **Cultural and local incentives are vital.**: Encouraging locally tailored governance structures can help communities adapt to de-growth principles effectively.
