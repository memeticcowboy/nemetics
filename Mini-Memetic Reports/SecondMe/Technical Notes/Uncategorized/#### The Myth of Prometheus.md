#### The Myth of Prometheus

The myth of Prometheus, the Titan who stole fire from the gods to give to humanity, is a foundational narrative in Western thought.1 This act is widely regarded as the catalyst for human civilization, as the gift of fire enabled mortals to forge tools, cook food, and develop arts and sciences.1 The stolen flame is a metaphor not just for technology, but for the "spark of human intellect and knowledge" itself.2 It represents the awakening of consciousness and selfhood, granting humanity the ability to reason, plan, and innovate.3

However, the myth’s symbolism is inseparable from the punishment Prometheus endured: bound to a cliff, his liver eternally torn out by an eagle.2 This suffering represents the inherent pain, suffering, and responsibility that come with the gifts of knowledge and consciousness.3 It serves as a cautionary tale about the overreach of human ambition and the grave consequences that can result from seeking power that belongs to the divine.5
