#### Biblical and Etymological Traditions (e.g., Interpreted by Philosophers like Philo of Alexandria or Modern Eco-Theologians)

**Perspective**: In Genesis, Adam (from Hebrew *adama*, meaning soil or earth) symbolizes human selfhood as formed from and returning to soil, emphasizing mortality, humility, and connection to creation. This metaphor underscores humanity's earthy origins: "For dust you are and to dust you will return" (Genesis 3:19). Etymologically, Latin *homo* (human) derives from *humus* (soil), linking selfhood to fertile, grounded materiality. Ancient thinkers like Philo of Alexandria blended this with Platonic ideas, viewing the body as soil-like (malleable, earthly) housing the divine soul. Modern interpretations in eco-theology (e.g., by thinkers like Satish Kumar or in journals like *Philosophical Transactions*) extend this to cultural identities, such as "native soil" for agrarian roots or "blood and soil" (in völkisch philosophy) for national/racial selfhood—though the latter has dark connotations of exclusion and purity.

**Reference to Narrative or** Embodied Self: Primarily **embodied** (soil as physical, created body, emphasizing immediate materiality and finitude), but with narrative self elements in cultural applications (e.g., stories of origin, heritage, or collective identity in traditions like Chinese xiangtu for rural selfhood).
