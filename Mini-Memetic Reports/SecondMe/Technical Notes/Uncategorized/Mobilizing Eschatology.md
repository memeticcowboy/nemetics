# Eschatological Narratives: Drivers of Global Conflict

Created at 2025/09/24 9:56 AM

### 1. Purposes and Goals

The text aims to show how eschatological (end-times) narratives are strategically mobilized to escalate conflict, justify political violence, and frame global struggles as divinely sanctioned or inevitable. It seeks to expose both the instrumental use and cultural power of apocalyptic rhetoric.

***

### 2. Central Questions or Problems

- How do religious end-time beliefs shape strategies of escalation in global conflict?
- In what ways are apocalyptic narratives weaponized for political, economic, or military gain?
- What risks arise when metaphysical worldviews drive geopolitical strategy?

***

### 3. Fundamental Concepts

- **Eschatology**: doctrines of the end times, often involving salvation, judgment, or renewal.
- **Narrative escalation**: rhetorical framing that intensifies conflict through absolute stakes.
- **Strategic communication**: the deliberate deployment of narratives in asymmetric warfare.
- **Petrocapitalism & apocalypse**: linking fossil-fuel exploitation with eschatological justifications.
- **Myth-making**: collective identity sustained through sacred or prophetic stories.

***

### 4. Essential Information

- Historical precedent: medieval, colonial, and modern uses of eschatology in mobilization.
- Present-day cases: Christian Zionism, jihadist movements, political rhetoric in climate debates.
- Structural drivers: capitalism’s crises, resource struggles, ecological collapse.
- Communication tactics: simplification, myth-symbol appeals, framing adversaries as existential threats.

***

### 5. Points of View or Frames of Reference

- **Religious**: believers see conflict as divine will or prophecy fulfillment.
- **Political**: leaders exploit eschatology for mobilization, control, or distraction.
- **Economic**: fossil fuel interests weaponize apocalyptic narratives around energy and collapse.
- **Critical-academic**: the text itself analyzes these narratives as tools of manipulation.

***

### 6. Underlying Assumptions

- Narratives shape material reality (Callon, MacKenzie on performativity).
- Belief systems can override rational cost–benefit reasoning in political decision-making.
- Apocalyptic storytelling has enduring cross-cultural power.
- Escalation is not accidental—it is strategically cultivated.

***

### 7. Inferences and Conclusions

- Eschatological narratives are not marginal—they are central drivers of modern conflict.
- The interplay of religious belief, fossil capitalism, and propaganda creates systemic escalation.
- Strategic communication amplifies these narratives, making them self-fulfilling hyperstitions.
- Left unchecked, these dynamics risk perpetuating cycles of war, exploitation, and ecological collapse.

***

### 8. Implications and Consequences

- **Policy risk**: irrational escalation of global conflicts under religious or apocalyptic justification.
- **Cultural consequence**: reinforcement of polarized worldviews, reducing space for negotiation.
- **Ecological danger**: eschatological justification of extractivism accelerates planetary crisis.
- **Strategic insight**: awareness of eschatological escalation is critical for counter-narratives and peace-building strategies.
