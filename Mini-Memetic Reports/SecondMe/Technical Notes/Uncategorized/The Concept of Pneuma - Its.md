# The Concept of Pneuma: Its Origins, Roles, and Significance in Greek Philosophy and Medicine

Created at 2025/10/10 12:53 PM

The Greeks conceptualized *pneuma*, a term derived from the verb *pneo* which means "to breathe" or "to blow".2

*Pneuma* represented not only "air in motion" but also the vital principle, the soul (*psyche*), and intelligence.1 This concept became a cornerstone of Stoic physics, where

*Pneuma* was believed to provide the universe with both cohesion and its dynamic properties through a principle called *tonos* or "tension".2 In ancient Greek medicine,

Pneuma was the inspired air within the body, with a "vital pneuma" traveling from the lungs to the arteries, highlighting its role in life and health.2 The association of the humor "blood" with air, since both were considered hot and wet, formed a core tenet of Hippocratic medicine.1 This connection between a cosmological element and a physiological principle demonstrates a holistic perspective where the universe and the human body are governed by the same fundamental forces. The health of an individual was seen as a direct reflection of the balance of these elemental humors, with imbalances leading to specific diseases, showing the profound practical significance of air symbolism.
