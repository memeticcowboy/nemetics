#### Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari (Poststructuralism)

**Perspective**: In *A Thousand Plateaus* (1980), Deleuze and Guattari introduce the "rhizome"—an underground, soil-dwelling root structure—as a metaphor for non-hierarchical, decentralized systems, contrasting it with arborescent (tree-like) models of thought and identity. Applied to selfhood, the rhizome represents a fluid, multiplicative self that connects horizontally through networks of relations, resisting fixed centers or linear progressions. Selfhood is not a unified "I" but a "rhizomatic story" of fragments, uncertainties, and becomings, where identity emerges from unpredictable branches and alliances. This critiques traditional, hierarchical notions of self (e.g., Cartesian ego) and aligns with their broader philosophy of immanence and difference. Researchers like Mieke Bal and others have extended this to narrative inquiry, viewing self-construction as an experimental, open-ended process.

**Reference to Embodied or** Narrative Self: Primarily **narrative** (self as a "rhizomatic story" of heterogeneous, branching narratives that defy coherence), though with embodied self undertones (rhizome's physical, soil-embedded growth emphasizes relational, affective connections in the immediate environment).
