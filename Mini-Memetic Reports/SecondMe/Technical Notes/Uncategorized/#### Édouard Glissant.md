#### Édouard Glissant (Postcolonial Theory)

**Perspective**: Influenced by Deleuze and Guattari, Glissant in *Poetics of Relation* (1990) uses the rhizome and soil's opacity as metaphors for a relational, creolized selfhood in postcolonial contexts. Soil represents the complex, untransparent layers of identity—resisting colonial demands for transparency and assimilation. His "right to opacity" posits that parts of the self remain unknowable and protected, like hidden soil ecosystems, fostering diversity and resistance to homogenization. Selfhood is thus a "trembling" network of relations, where identity forms through archipelagic (island-like) connections rather than rooted, singular origins. This draws from Glissant's Martinican background, critiquing imperial narratives while affirming multiplicity.

**Reference to** Embodied Self: Primarily **embodied** (focus on immediate, opaque bodily presence and relational affects, protecting internal differences from scrutiny), with narrative self implications (self as a story of creolization and historical entanglements).
