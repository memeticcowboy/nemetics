# Improving ChatGPT-5 Writing: Tips for More Human-Like Communication

Created at 2025/09/05 3:50 PM

“Rewrite the following text so it sounds like it came from a single, relaxed human who is trying to help a friend—no jargon, no filler, no corporate gloss. Keep every fact, but strip out hype, clichés, and extra words. Aim for an 8th-grade reading level and a warm, conversational tone.”



Hey—here’s the gist of the video.

It talks about how writing with ChatGPT-5 can feel stiff and robotic, and breaks down why that happens. The person behind the video walks through where this style comes from, shows some examples, and gives tips on how to make the writing sound more like a real human.




**So, what’s the problem?**


GPT-5 wasn’t really made to talk to people. It was trained to impress other AIs.

Kristoff Halig, an AI safety researcher, points out that GPT-5 learned by getting feedback from other AIs, not humans. That feedback taught it that longer and more complex writing = better writing.




**A weird example**


Halig gave GPT-5 a bunch of nonsense filled with big, fancy words. The AI gave it an 8 out of 10 for “quality.” So yeah, it learned that complicated = smart, even when it makes no sense to us.




**Why it writes like a robot**


GPT-5 was trained to look for certain patterns—longer, more complicated language usually got better feedback. So when you ask it to write, it keeps checking itself to make sure it sounds “smart.” Problem is, that doesn’t always mean it’s clear or useful for humans.




**When we make it worse**


When people say things like “be more thoughtful” or “explain it more,” they’re actually pushing the AI to overthink it. That often leads to even more bloated writing. Sometimes, you even have to run the answer through another AI just to make it readable again.




**Prompt Example: Before vs After**


Here’s how much difference a better prompt makes.

Original prompt:

Writing a professional email to a marketing agency.

GPT-5 says: “Hi, [first name]. I’ve been following [agency name]’s growth. It’s clear you’re taking on bigger and more complex projects.”

Sounds flat and stiff. No personality. Typical corporate speak.

Rewritten prompt with constraints:

Includes specific stats, no buzzwords, and uses the agency’s name a few times.

GPT-5 says: “Hey [first name]. I noticed [Blank] has been adding new clients quickly. We helped a similar agency cut project delays by 27% while they scaled. Worth a 15-minute chat?”

Now it sounds like an actual person wrote it. Friendly, clear, and to the point.




**3 things that actually help**


1. Be clear and give rules<br>GPT-5 isn’t really a teammate. Think of it more like a tool that needs solid instructions. Set word limits, ban buzzwords, give it structure. The stricter you are, the better it performs.
2. Don’t ask it to “think harder”<br>When you do that, it tries too hard and gets all tangled up in itself. Instead, keep things simple—ask it to write like it’s texting a friend or explaining to a 5th grader.
3. Cut the fluff<br>Don’t pile on style tips like “make it casual” or “be more human.” Just tell it what not to do—like no passive voice, no vague language, and no $10 words. That forces it to stay grounded.





**What’s really going on inside GPT-5**


The model is kind of like a traffic router. It reads your prompt and decides which “mode” to go into—more complex or more direct. If your prompt hints at deep thinking or analysis, it leans into that and gets wordy. But if you keep things focused and simple, it stays grounded.




**The big risk: AI learning from AI**


Here’s a worry: if AIs keep training on other AI-written content, we end up in a loop. Models keep copying each other’s bad habits—like thinking fancy writing means smart writing—and forget how to connect with people. It’s a spiral that could make all AI communication worse over time.




**A little challenge for you**


Try rewriting one of GPT-5’s awkward responses to sound more like you. It’s a great way to see how much better it can get when you take control of the prompt. Don’t just settle for what it gives you—shape it.




**Final note**


If you really want to get good results with GPT-5, the trick is to understand how different it thinks from us. You can totally work with it—but only if you learn how to guide it. Be clear. Be specific. And aim for connection over complexity.
