### Buddhism: The Fire Sermon and Sangha Liberation

**Community Practice for Individual and Collective Freedom**

The Buddha's *Adittapariyaya Sutta* (Fire Sermon) presents a sophisticated understanding of how fire metaphors can guide community practice toward liberation. Rather than condemning fire, the teaching uses fire as a meditation object that reveals the nature of attachment and points toward collective freedom through shared understanding[^1_18][^1_19].

The Fire Sermon describes how "all things are aflame" with the fires of passion, aversion, and delusion, but suggests that recognizing this universal condition creates the foundation for *Sangha* (spiritual community) practice[^1_19]. The community context is crucial: individual practitioners support each other in "putting out the fire" through collective wisdom and mutual encouragement.

**The Sangha as Fire-Tending Community**

Buddhist communities conceptualize themselves as mutual support systems for liberation, with fire serving both as metaphor for the spiritual work and as the energy that binds practitioners together in shared purpose. The *Sangha* represents one of the Three Jewels of refuge, described as offering "refuge from fear and loneliness" where "no matter who we are, no matter what we have done, we can find belonging"[^1_20].

This approach suggests that effective spiritual communities require both individual practice (working with one's own "fires" of attachment) and collective support structures that maintain the energy and commitment necessary for transformation over time[^1_21].
