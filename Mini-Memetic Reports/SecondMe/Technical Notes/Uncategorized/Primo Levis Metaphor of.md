# Primo Levi's Metaphor of Dehumanization in Auschwitz: The Worm in Stripes

Created at 2025/09/21 7:50 PM

The quote you're referencing comes from Primo Levi's *If This Is a Man* (originally titled *Se questo è un uomo*, also known as *Survival in Auschwitz*). The specific passage reads:

**"Anyone who does not have them [clothes] no longer perceives himself as a human being, but rather as a worm: naked, slow, ignoble, prone on the ground."**[luisapiccarretaofficial+3](https://en.luisapiccarretaofficial.org/news/the-works-of-mercy/119)

## Context and Meaning

This powerful metaphor appears in Levi's reflection on the systematic **dehumanization** process in Auschwitz concentration camp. The "stripes" refer to the **striped prison uniforms** that inmates were forced to wear after being stripped of their own clothing, possessions, and identity.[archive+4](https://archive.org/stream/primo_levi_if_this_is_a_man/primo_levi_if_this_is_a_man_djvu.txt)

## The Dehumanization Process

Levi describes how the Nazis deliberately stripped prisoners of everything that constituted their human dignity:[goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/943008-then-for-the-first-time-we-became-aware-that-our)

- **Physical possessions**: "Nothing belongs to us anymore; they have taken away our clothes, our shoes, even our hair"[goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/943008-then-for-the-first-time-we-became-aware-that-our)
- **Identity**: Prisoners were given numbered tattoos and lost their names[litcharts+1](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/survival-in-auschwitz/themes/dehumanization-and-resistance)
- **Basic human needs**: Even drinking water was forbidden in cruel psychological games[archive+1](https://archive.org/stream/primo_levi_if_this_is_a_man/primo_levi_if_this_is_a_man_djvu.txt)

## The Worm Metaphor

The comparison to a **worm** captures the complete degradation prisoners experienced without proper clothing:[brill+3](https://brill.com/display/book/edcoll/9789047419501/Bej.9789004157736.i-296_003.pdf)

- **Naked**: Stripped of protective covering and dignity
- **Slow**: Weakened by starvation and abuse
- **Ignoble**: Reduced from human status to something crawling and despised
- **Prone on the ground**: Physically and psychologically brought low

## Resistance Through Dignity

Levi emphasizes that maintaining **basic human rituals** like washing and caring for one's appearance became acts of resistance against this dehumanization. As fellow prisoner Steinlauf tells him, they must "save at least the skeleton, the scaffolding, the form of civilization" even in the camp's hellish conditions.[wikipedia+1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If_This_Is_a_Man)

The worm metaphor thus illustrates how the Nazis sought to destroy not just bodies, but **human identity itself** - reducing people to the lowest form of life. Yet Levi's very act of bearing witness through his writing represents the ultimate triumph over this attempted "demolition of a man".[ebsco+2](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/if-man-primo-levi)

This image remains one of the most haunting examples of how clothing and basic dignity are fundamental to human identity, and how their systematic removal was central to the Holocaust's machinery of dehumanization.
