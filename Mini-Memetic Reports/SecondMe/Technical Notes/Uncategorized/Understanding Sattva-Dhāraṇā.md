# सत्त्व-धारणा

Created at 2025/09/05 12:29 PM

The Sanskrit term **Sattva-Dhāraṇā** can be broken down into its components: **Sattva** (सत्त्व) and **Dhāraṇā** (धारणा). Below is the translation, definition, and the term written in Sanskrit.

### Translation and Definition:

1. **Sattva** (सत्त्व):
    - **Translation**: Sattva means "purity," "goodness," "truth," or "existence."
    - **Definition**: In Indian philosophy, particularly in Sāṃkhya and Yoga, Sattva is one of the three *guṇas* (qualities of nature), alongside *Rajas* (passion, activity) and *Tamas* (inertia, ignorance). Sattva represents purity, harmony, clarity, and balance, associated with qualities like wisdom, calmness, and spiritual inclination. It is the quality that brings the mind closer to truth and liberation (*mokṣa*) by fostering clarity and equanimity.
2. **Dhāraṇā** (धारणा):
    - **Translation**: Dhāraṇā means "concentration," "holding," or "retention."
    - **Definition**: In Patañjali’s *Yoga Sutras*, Dhāraṇā is the sixth limb of Ashtanga Yoga, referring to the practice of focused concentration where the mind is fixed on a single point, object, or thought. It is a preparatory stage for deeper meditative states like *dhyāna* (meditation) and *samādhi* (absorption), cultivating mental steadiness and clarity.
3. **Sattva-Dhāraṇā Combined**:
    - **Translation**: The term Sattva-Dhāraṇā translates to "concentration on purity" or "the holding of goodness."
    - **Definition**: Sattva-Dhāraṇā likely refers to a meditative or yogic practice where the practitioner focuses their mind (*Dhāraṇā*) on the quality of *Sattva*—purity, harmony, or truth—to cultivate a state of mental clarity and spiritual elevation. It could involve:
        - **Yogic Context**: Concentrating on Sattva as a quality within the mind or as a divine principle to purify mental tendencies and align with higher consciousness. This practice might aim to reduce the influence of *Rajas* (restlessness) and *Tamas* (dullness), fostering a sattvic state conducive to meditation and liberation.
        - **Philosophical Context**: Focusing on the essence of existence or truth (*Sattva* as "being" or "reality") to discern the eternal self (*Ātman*) from the transient material world (*Prakṛti*).
        - **Spiritual Context**: A practice of holding the mind steady on pure, harmonious qualities (e.g., compassion, truth, or divine light) to transform the psyche and move toward spiritual awakening.
    - The term suggests a disciplined focus on cultivating or realizing the purest aspect of consciousness, aligning with the goals of yoga to still the mind’s fluctuations (*citta-vṛtti-nirodha*).
