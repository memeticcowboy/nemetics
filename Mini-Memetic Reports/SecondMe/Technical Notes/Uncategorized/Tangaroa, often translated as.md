Tangaroa, often translated as the god of the sea, embodies the energy of water in all its forms. He is both a creative force and a destructive one, reflecting the dual nature of the ocean's power. A unique aspect of the Maori worldview is the concept of te taha wairua, often translated as "the spiritual plane of existence," which some believe literally means "the dimension of two waters," suggesting that the fundamental dimension of all life takes the form of water. Water is also seen as the "blood of Papatūānuku, the Earth Mother" and the "tears of Ranginui, the Sky Father," linking its spiritual significance directly to the creation story and the land itself.
