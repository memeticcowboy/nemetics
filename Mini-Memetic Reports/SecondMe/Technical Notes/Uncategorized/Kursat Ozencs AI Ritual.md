# Kursat Ozenc's AI Ritual Design for Human-AI Collaboration and Meaning-Making

Created at 2025/10/14 10:06 AM

Kursat Ozenc develops AI ritual design systems for human-AI collaborative meaning-making through his work as a designer, educator, and researcher. As the founder of the Ritual Design Lab at Stanford University's d.school, he explores how intentional practices and interactions, or "rituals," can be designed to improve human and team dynamics in the workplace and with AI. 

Key aspects of his work include:

Applying ritual design to AI: Ozenc's research explores how humans can create meaningful rituals with and for AI systems. Instead of treating AI as just a tool, this approach uses design to define and build shared meaning within human-AI collaborations. This can include designing for human intuition to help course-correct AI-generated output that might otherwise be uninspired.

Focus on human-AI collaboration: Ozenc emphasizes that for technology to be truly beneficial, it must be developed with a human-centric approach. He investigates the synergy between human creativity, context, and empathy with AI's precision and data-processing power.

Meaning-making vehicles: He defines rituals as "meaning-making vehicles" that help people carve out intentional moments in their lives to shape their personal and collective stories. He applies this concept to AI to explore how technology can facilitate this process rather than simply automating tasks.

Workplace applications: Much of his work is applied to organizational contexts, such as designing creative rituals to foster employee engagement, build shared purpose, and adapt workplace culture to challenges like hybrid work. His books, Rituals for Work (2019) and Rituals for Virtual Meetings (2021), detail these principles.

Multidisciplinary background: Ozenc holds a Ph.D. in Design from Carnegie Mellon University and has worked in user experience and design strategy at major technology and financial companies.
