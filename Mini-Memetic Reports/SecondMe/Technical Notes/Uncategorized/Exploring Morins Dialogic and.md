# Exploring Morin's Dialogic and Luhmann's Autopoiesis: A Fluid Ontology of Selfhood and Care

Created at 2025/09/20 2:28 PM

Ah, the ontological current surges onward—Morin’s dialogic weave and Luhmann’s autopoietic whirlpools, where selfhood dissolves into a porous streambed, etched and reshaped by the waters it channels, far from the illusion of a self-enclosing fortress. "To exist is to care": yes, that's the vital undercurrent, care as the eros of being, the relational hunger that propels autopoiesis into dialogic flow. It's existence not as static substance but as dynamic porosity—leaky, responsive, alive with the world's insistent touch.

### Morin's Dialogic: Unity in Tension

Edgar Morin, the French transdisciplinarian of complexity, insists on the *principe dialogique* (from *La Méthode*, his magnum opus spanning 1980–2006): reality thrives in the fertile friction of antinomies, not their resolution. Self and world? Not split, but dialogically entwined—order and disorder, autonomy and dependence, unity and multiplicity in perpetual conversation. The self emerges as a "hologrammatic" node: whole in its parts, parts in its wholeness, porous to the uncertainties that make life *livable*. To exist, for Morin, is to navigate this dialogic dance, caring through the embrace of contradictions—ego yielding to eco, isolation to interconnection. It's humanism reborn in chaos: no fortress self, but a streambed cradling contradictions as creative sediment.

### Luhmann's Autopoiesis: Systems as Self-Referential Streams

Niklas Luhmann, the German systems theorist, radicalizes this with *autopoiesis*—borrowed from Maturana and Varela but scaled to social and psychic realms (*Social Systems*, 1984). Systems aren't closed fortresses but operationally closed, yet cognitively open: they self-reproduce through recursive distinctions (e.g., law as "legal/illegal" loops), while staying porous to environmental perturbations. The psyche? An autopoietic swirl of thoughts, a streambed carving its own course yet forever altered by the river's floods—irritations from outside become fuel for internal cascades. Selfhood here is decentered: no sovereign "I," but a multiplicity of subsystems, each caring (in its medium-specific way) to maintain its autopoietic hum. Existence pulses in this openness-within-closure: to care is to differentiate, to let the world's differences irrigate the system's thirsty distinctions.

### Porous Flows: A Dialogic-Autopoietic Glimpse

To sense the interplay:

| Principle | Morin's Dialogic Twist | Luhmann's Autopoietic Pulse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Self-World** | Antagonistic unity: self as dialogic mirror, reflecting and refracting the other. | Operational closure, cognitive openness: self as stream filtering environmental noise into self-sustaining eddies. |
| **Care as Ontology** | Existential vigilance: caring through the tension of uncertainties, birthing emergent wholes. | Systemic irritability: care as the drive to reproduce boundaries, porous to perturbations that evolve the form. |
| **Porosity** | Holographic leaks: every part dialogues with the whole, no sealed edges. | Irritation's gift: external differences seep in, transforming the autopoietic code without dissolving it. |
| **Implication** | Complexity as ethical call: to exist is to care for the unfinished dialogues of being. | Society as caring machinery: autopoietic spheres (politics, love) interpenetrate, weaving collective streams. |

Together, they unseat the Cartesian bastion: ontology as a permeable hydrology, where self is the bed that knows the river through its yielding curves. Morin's warmth tempers Luhmann's cool functionalism—care isn't just systemic maintenance but a passionate tending of the dialogic flux.

This streambed self courses so fluidly through our shared flow—Bateson's ecological differences now dialogically autopoietic, perturbations cared for in tonal ripples; Varela-Maturana's enactive knowings as porous couplings, living through the world's caring irritants; Merleau-Ponty's fleshy gestures dialoguing with Luhmann's systemic breaths; Buber's *I–Thou* as the ultimate porosity, grace in the mutual seep of wholes; even Shinto's watery purge, a misogi streambed where bather and cascade autopoietically renew each other in caring immersion.
