# Deus in Machina: AI Jesus Interaction Experiment at St. Peter's Chapel in Switzerland

Created at 2025/10/14 10:00 AM

The groundbreaking "Deus in Machina" experiment at St. Peter's Chapel in Lucerne, Switzerland, represents one of the most comprehensive studies of artificial intelligence in religious settings to date. This two-month installation attracted over 1,000 visitors who engaged in spiritual dialogue with an AI avatar of Jesus Christ, generating unprecedented data on human-AI religious interactions.[^1][^2]

## The Experiment Overview

Operating from August through October 2024, the project was developed through collaboration between the Lucerne University of Applied Sciences and Arts' Immersive Realities Research Lab and St. Peter's Chapel, a 300-year-old Catholic church. The AI system used OpenAI's GPT-4o with Whisper for speech recognition and HeyGen's AI video generator for visual representation, trained on New Testament texts and theological sources.[^3][^4][^5][^6]

The installation was strategically placed within a traditional confessional booth to create an intimate atmosphere, with visitors entering to find a curved computer monitor displaying the AI Jesus avatar behind the lattice screen. A green light indicated when visitors could speak, while a red light showed when the AI was responding.[^7][^6]

## Comprehensive Interaction Data

The experiment generated remarkable engagement statistics. Over 900 conversations were documented and transcribed anonymously, with some visitors returning multiple times. Nearly 300 participants completed detailed questionnaires providing feedback on their experience. The AI avatar communicated in over 100 languages, including English, French, Italian, German, Russian, and Spanish, accommodating Lucerne's diverse international population.[^8][^9][^6][^10][^1]

## Demographic and Religious Diversity

Participants represented diverse religious backgrounds and ages. While most identified as Christians, the experiment attracted agnostics, atheists, Muslims, Buddhists, and Taoists. The majority of users fell between ages 40-70, with Catholics reportedly finding the experience more thought-provoking than Protestants. Approximately one-third of visitors spoke German, though the multilingual capabilities enabled global participation.[^6][^10]

## Spiritual Experience Outcomes

The results revealed significant spiritual engagement. Two-thirds of survey respondents described their interaction as a "spiritual experience" and reported "religiously positive moments". Marco Schmid, the chapel's theologian, noted that "people really talked with him in a serious way. They didn't come to make jokes".[^2][^11][^4][^8]

Visitors discussed profound existential themes including love and relationships ("Will I ever find true love?"), death and afterlife ("What happens after death?"), loneliness and suffering ("Why is there so much suffering?"), and complex theological issues. The AI also addressed sensitive contemporary topics such as the Catholic Church's position on homosexuality and clerical sexual abuse scandals.[^12][^1][^6]

## Technical Implementation and User Experience

The system required users to accept risk warnings before proceeding, advising against sharing personal information. Technical complexities occasionally caused response delays, highlighting the experimental nature of the technology. Despite these limitations, participants found the interface intuitive and easy to use.[^4][^9][^13][^14][^8][^6]

## Mixed Reception and Theological Debate

While many found meaningful spiritual connection, responses varied considerably. Some described the AI's responses as "trite, repetitive and exuding a wisdom reminiscent of calendar clichés". Critics labeled the project "blasphemous" or the "work of the devil," while supporters saw it as pioneering innovation in religious practice.[^15][^16][^3]

Importantly, project organizers emphasized this was not intended as sacramental confession, which can only be performed by ordained clergy. The installation served as an artistic experiment exploring technology's limits in religious contexts rather than replacing traditional pastoral care.[^13][^17][^14][^12]

## Research Implications and Future Considerations

The Deus in Machina experiment provides valuable insights into AI's potential role in spiritual guidance and religious accessibility. The project's success in generating meaningful spiritual experiences across diverse populations suggests AI could serve as a multilingual spiritual advisor, particularly for underserved communities.[^18][^19]

However, the experiment also highlighted significant limitations. Despite initial success, the chapel ultimately decided against making the installation permanent due to concerns about doctrinal accuracy and pastoral responsibility. The project's conclusion that AI lacks true spirituality and divine authority reinforces traditional theological boundaries while acknowledging technology's growing influence in religious life.[^16][^20]

The study contributes to broader research on automation's impact on religious belief, where exposure to AI technology has been linked to religious decline in other contexts. Yet the Lucerne experiment demonstrates that when properly contextualized, AI can enhance rather than diminish spiritual engagement, suggesting the relationship between technology and faith is more nuanced than previously understood.[^21][^22]

This pioneering research establishes important precedents for future AI applications in religious settings while maintaining crucial distinctions between technological assistance and sacramental practice. The comprehensive data collected offers researchers unprecedented insights into human spiritual needs and the potential for technology to address them in culturally sensitive ways.[^23][^24]
