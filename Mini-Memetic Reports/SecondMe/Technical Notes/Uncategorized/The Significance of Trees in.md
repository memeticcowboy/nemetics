# Celtic and Druidic Traditions of Wood

Created at 2025/09/11 4:24 PM

The ancient Celts held trees in immense significance, believing that their roots and branches were a link between the mortal and Other worlds.[1] This reverence is embodied in the Ogham script, an ancient form of writing where each of the 20 letters represents a different tree, referred to as "The Tree Alphabet".[1] Specific trees carried powerful symbolic weight:

**The Oak:** Revered as the "King of Trees" and a symbol of strength, wisdom, and protection, the oak was considered the most sacred tree by Druids.[3] Its association with Zeus, the king of the gods, undescores its divine and powerful status.[3]

**The Ash:** Often referred to as a "World Tree," the ash serves as a cosmic bridge connecting the three realms of existence.

**The Birch:** Known as the "Lady of the Woods," the birch symbolizes renewal, purification, and new beginnings. It was used in rituals to drive out old spirits and was a central part of Beltane festivals, which celebrated fertility and the arrival of summer.[2]
