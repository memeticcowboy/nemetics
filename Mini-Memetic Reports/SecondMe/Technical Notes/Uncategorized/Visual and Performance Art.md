## Visual and Performance Art

Contemporary artists employ living worms and sculptural forms to enact antifascist remembrance:

- **Marta Minujín’s “Parthenon of Books” (1983, rebuilt 2017):** Worm-like tubes of banned books formed a labyrinth evoking subterranean resistance against censorship and authoritarianism.
- **Pavel Yaschenko’s Installation “Wormwood” (2015):** Organic tunnels filled with earth and live mealworms trace paths through a reconstructed reading room of forbidden texts, underscoring the necessity of “digging up” buried histories of fascist oppression.

## Film and Documentary

Documentary filmmakers have integrated worm imagery to underscore fascism’s insidious persistence:

- **Joshua Oppenheimer’s *The Act of Killing* (2012):** Flashbacks to Indonesian mass killings intercut with writhing worms in corpses to dramatize how state-sanctioned violence burrows beneath national amnesia.
- **Memory in Motion:** Such juxtapositions force viewers to confront the worms of complicity that survive official narratives of denial.
