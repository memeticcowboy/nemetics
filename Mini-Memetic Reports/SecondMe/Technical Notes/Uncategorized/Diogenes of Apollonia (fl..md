Diogenes of Apollonia (fl. 5th century BCE) developed the pneumatic philosophy further, explicitly identifying **air with divine intelligence**. For Diogenes, the air that enters our bodies through breathing was not separate from the cosmic air that orders the universe through intelligence. The universal air possesses *logos*—rational ordering principle—which manifests both in cosmic regularities (seasons, weather patterns) and in human thought. As he argued, air is "great and powerful, eternal and immortal and it knows many things". This linkage between respiration and cosmic reason would prove foundational for later Stoic psychology and medieval theories of intellect.
