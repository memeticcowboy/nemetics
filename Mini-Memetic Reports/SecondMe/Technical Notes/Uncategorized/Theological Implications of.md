# Theological Implications of the 'Undying Worm' in Mark 9:48 and Isaiah 66:24

Created at 2025/09/21 8:14 PM

**Primary Text (Mark 9:48, Greek and English)**<br>ἐκεῖ ὁ **σκώληξ ἄσβεστος**· καὶ ὁ πῦρ οὐ σβέννυται.<br>“where their **worm does not die** and the fire is not quenched.”

This verse appears in Jesus’ warning about the fate of the unrepentant, drawing on the prophetic imagery of Isaiah 66:24.

## Greek Phrase and Translation

- σκώληξ (skōlēx): “worm,” often understood as maggot
- ἄσβεστος (asbestos): “unquenchable,” “undying”

Combined, **“σκώληξ ἄσβεστος”** literally means “undying worm,” vividly evoking eternal decay.

## Context in Mark’s Gospel

In Mark 9:43–48, Jesus employs hyperbolic imagery to urge radical avoidance of sin:

“If your hand causes you to sin, cut it off…<br>If your foot causes you to sin, cut it off…<br>It is better for you to enter life maimed<br>than with two hands to go to hell,<br>where their worm does not die<br>and the fire is not quenched.”

Here the “undying worm” symbolizes the unending moral and spiritual corruption experienced in Gehenna.

## Origin in Isaiah 66

Jesus’ words echo Isaiah 66:24, which depicts the end-time vision of God’s judgment:

“And they shall go out and look on the corpses of the men<br>who have transgressed against me.<br>For their worm shall not die,<br>their fire shall not be quenched;<br>and they shall be an abhorrence to all flesh.”

By invoking this Old Testament image, the New Testament underscores the permanence of divine judgment and the horror of unrepented sin.

## Theological Significance

- **Eternal Consequence:** The undying worm represents ongoing decay, a metaphor for perpetual suffering rather than annihilation.
- **Moral Urgency:** The vividness of the image serves to shock listeners into self-examination and repentance.
- **Judgment Imagery:** Links prophetic visions of Israel’s judgment with Jesus’ warning to his followers, framing sin as deserving of irreversible consequences.

The phrase **σκώληξ ἄσβεστος** thus remains one of Scripture’s most potent expressions of the reality and permanence of divine judgment.
