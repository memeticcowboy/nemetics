# Medieval Christian Beliefs and Devotion to Guardian Angels: Theology, Hierarchy, and Practices

Created at 2025/10/14 9:20 AM

During the medieval period, the concept of angelic guardianship was **a deeply embedded belief in Christian theology and popular devotion**. Medieval theologians, most notably Thomas Aquinas, systematized the doctrine by building on early Christian teachings and integrating it with the angelic hierarchy. [[1](https://new.flocknote.com/note/33297474), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchy_of_angels), [3](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304681081_The_Guardian_Angel_in_Protestant_England), [4](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1057/9780230316973_13), [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian_angel)]

The theological foundation

- **Scriptural basis:** The belief in guardian angels was rooted in scripture, particularly passages such as Matthew 18:10, where Jesus refers to "their angels" who "always see the face of my Father," and Psalm 91, which promises divine protection through angels.
- **Scholastic development:** The 12th-century theologian Honorius of Autun proposed that every soul was assigned a guardian angel at the moment it was placed in the body. This idea was widely embraced and further developed by scholastic theologians in the High Middle Ages. [[1](https://new.flocknote.com/note/33297474), [3](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304681081_The_Guardian_Angel_in_Protestant_England), [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian_angel), [7](https://academic.oup.com/book/12423/chapter/163016505), [8](https://www.sistersofcarmel.com/angels.php#:~:text=Jesus%20Christ%20stated%20the%20angels%20of%20little,cited%20as%20biblical%20evidence%20of%20guardian%20angels.)] 

The angelic hierarchy

Thomas Aquinas refined the understanding of guardian angels by placing them within a larger, nine-tiered hierarchy of angels adapted from Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite.

- **The lowest choir:** Aquinas asserted that guardian angels belonged to the lowest choir, simply called "Angels". Though the lowest in rank, their position closest to humanity made them direct conduits of God's will to humankind.
- **The flow of enlightenment:** The hierarchy worked as a chain of divine communication, with higher-ranking angels (like the Seraphim, Cherubim, and Archangels) receiving enlightenment directly from God and illuminating the ranks below them. This culminated in the guardian angel, who would then touch the human mind. The function of a guardian angel

The roles of guardian angels were considered active and varied, intervening in many aspects of human life.

- **Protection:** Their primary role was to guide and protect individuals on their journey through life, shielding them from both physical and spiritual harm, including the temptations of demons.
- **Divine communication:** Angels could inspire their human charges through their senses and imaginations, gently steering them toward God's will. Unlike fallen angels (demons), they did not coerce or control a person's will.
- **Mediation:** Angels were seen as mediators who delivered God's grace and delivered special messages.
- **Guiding to salvation:** The guardianship of the angel lasted a person's entire life, continuing their spiritual guidance after death by carrying their soul to its proper place, either to heaven or to judgment.
- **Support for repentance:** Even if a person fell into mortal sin, their guardian angel would not abandon them but would continue to work toward their repentance. [[1](https://new.flocknote.com/note/33297474), [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian_angel), [9](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vfyzc850XKM), [12](https://opusangelorum.org/commonly-asked-questions-on-the-angels-cont/), [13](https://newdailycompass.com/en/holy-guardian-angels-1-1)] 

Broader forms of angelic guardianship

Medieval thinkers also believed that angelic protection extended beyond individuals to encompass larger groups and places.

- **Nations and cities:** Theologians like Thomas Aquinas taught that every country and nation had its own guardian angel appointed by God. This idea reflected older biblical traditions, such as the Book of Daniel, which referred to angelic "princes" of nations.
- **The church:** Angels were also understood to watch over entire communities, including the churches and congregations addressed in the Book of Revelation. [[12](https://opusangelorum.org/commonly-asked-questions-on-the-angels-cont/), [14](https://www.anthonydelgado.net/blog-1/angelology-doctrine-of-angels-guardian-angels-and-the-care-of-god)] 

The development of devotion

The medieval belief in guardian angels had a profound impact on devotional practices.

- **Prayers and Masses:** Prayers to guardian angels became common in late medieval primers and manuscripts. Votive Masses were also offered in their honor.
- **Feast of the Guardian Angels:** By the late 17th century, Pope Clement X made the Feast of the Guardian Angels, celebrated on October 2, a universal observance, cementing its importance in the Catholic liturgical calendar. [[1](https://new.flocknote.com/note/33297474), [3](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304681081_The_Guardian_Angel_in_Protestant_England), [15](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1057/9780230316973_13#:~:text=Like%20the%20saints%2C%20they%20(%20guardian%20angels,in%20late%20medieval%20primers%20and%20manuscript%20collections.), [16](https://aroundtheyear.org/st-michaels-lent/#:~:text=By%20the%20end%20of%20the%20Middle%20Ages,of%20hours%20so%20popular%20among%20the%20laity.), [17](https://brewminate.com/at-the-moment-of-peril-spiritual-and-civic-responses-to-medieval-natural-disasters/)]

**Note:** The widespread veneration of guardian angels in the late Middle Ages was later criticized by Protestant reformers, who saw it as misplaced veneration of a created being instead of God alone.
