## Hillman's Archetypal Psychology and Metal Dispositions

James Hillman's post-Jungian archetypal psychology provides a polytheistic approach to understanding how metal symbolism operates within the broader ecology of archetypal images. His emphasis on the poetic basis of mind shows how archetypal images, including metal symbolism, function as primary organizing principles rather than secondary representations. The concept of character as daimonic calling reveals how individuals embody particular archetypal qualities that shape their engagement with boundaries.

Hillman's critique of developmental psychology emphasizes that archetypal patterns are not phases to be transcended but ongoing organizing principles that require continuous engagement. His understanding of pathology as literalized metaphor shows how psychological problems arise when archetypal boundaries become rigid rather than fluid. Metal symbolism represents both the discriminating power of archetypal consciousness and the protective boundaries that preserve psychological integrity.
