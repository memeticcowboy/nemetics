**Daisy Hildyard’s “Second, Worm-Like Body”**<br>In her essay collection *The Second Body* (2017), Daisy Hildyard argues that each of us possesses two bodies: our **physical, flesh‐and‐blood body** and a **“second, worm-like body”** composed of the ecological and carbon footprints we leave behind. This second body, she writes,<br>“…warms the Pacific Ocean and destroys tiger habitats in Bhutan by virtue of our first body’s acts of living in modernity.”<br>By likening our global externalities to a writhing, subterranean organism, Hildyard makes tangible the diffuse, often invisible effects of our consumption.

**Jeff VanderMeer’s “Luminescent Worm-Ink”**<br>In *Annihilation* (2014), Jeff VanderMeer describes the biologist’s discovery of **fungal inscriptions** scrawled along subterranean chambers in Area X:

> “…fungal writing on the walls… it seemed to sway like sea grass… faintly glowing with what looked like luminescent worm-ink.”<br>This image suggests that nature itself, in its uncanny abundance, **“writes”** back to us—using its own biochemical “ink”—reminding readers that the boundary between human and nonhuman is porous and alive.
