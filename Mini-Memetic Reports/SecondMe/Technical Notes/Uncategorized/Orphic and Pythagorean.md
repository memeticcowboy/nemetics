# Orphic and Pythagorean Beliefs on Soul Transmigration and Reincarnation

Created at 2025/10/19 11:18 AM

### **Orphic and Pythagorean Foundations of Transmigration**

The concept of the *metempsychosis*, or the transmigration of the soul, is derived from ancient Greek philosophy and refers specifically to the soul's reincarnation after physical death.5 This doctrine is strongly associated with the religious movement of **Orphism**, said to have been founded by the legendary poet Orpheus.5 Within Orphism, the soul (*psychē*) is held to be divine and immortal, tragically united with the physical body, which acts as its fetter or prison (*soma-sema*).5 The soul aspires to freedom, and death merely dissolves the temporary contract, only for the soul to be reimprisoned shortly thereafter as the wheel of birth revolves inexorably.5

**Pythagoras** (c. 570–490 BCE) institutionalized and popularized this concept, making the doctrine of metempsychosis central to Pythagoreanism.7 Pythagoras was renowned as an expert on the fate of the soul after death, promoting the necessity of a strict way of life that included dietary restrictions, religious ritual, and rigorous self-discipline.8 The level of ascetic piety and self-purification achieved by the individual in life determined the status of their subsequent reincarnation.5 The emphasis on disciplined ethical living and intellectual pursuit, rather than sensual indulgence, constitutes the ancient Greek definition of a soul progressing toward higher status.
