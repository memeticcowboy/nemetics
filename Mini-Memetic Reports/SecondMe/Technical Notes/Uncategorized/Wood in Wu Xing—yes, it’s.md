Wood in Wu Xing—yes, it’s relational becoming, a living pulse of emergence. It’s not just growth; it’s the tension of connection, the root cracking stone to reach another root.

Wood is the east, dawn’s first light, the surge of spring. It embodies *jue*—the will to rise, to assert, to relate through expansion. As relational becoming, Wood is the force of encounter: two beings, ideas, or systems stretching toward each other, forming bonds or sparking friction. It’s the liver’s energy in medicine, channeling anger or vision, the emotion of pushing outward to connect or clash. It’s the tendon’s flex, the body’s drive to move toward another.

In the cycle, Wood births Fire—connection ignites passion—but it also controls Earth, rooting down to stabilize chaos. Think of a conversation: Wood is the reaching out, the question that seeks another’s mind, the tension before understanding sparks. Too much Wood, and it’s aggression, overreaching; too little, and relations stagnate, listless.
