## **Lukes, Steven (2005) [Power: A Radical View](https://voidnetwork.gr/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Power-A-Radical-View-Steven-Lukes.pdf), 2nd ed.**[voidnetwork](https://voidnetwork.gr/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Power-A-Radical-View-Steven-Lukes.pdf)​

"Power Over" is defined as the distortion/extraction of other power modalities through domination and control (p. 14-16, 28-30).

The five "faces" of power (including the latent third dimension) are the scaffolding the author uses to posit a sixth, "Pseudo-Power" (p. 25-29).

**Publisher:** [Palgrave Macmillan](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/power-9781352012347/)[bloomsbury](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/power-9781352012347/)​




## **Bichler, Shimshon & Nitzan, Jonathan (2009) [Capital as Power: A Study of Order and Creorder](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/157973/1/bna-259_20090522_nb_casp_full_indexed.pdf), Routledge.**[econstor](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/157973/1/bna-259_20090522_nb_casp_full_indexed.pdf)​

Power is framed as a capacitative ordering of society rather than a possession, informing the author's own "Coordination as Power" lens (pp. 1-18, 257-264).

**Publisher:** [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Capital-as-Power-A-Study-of-Order-and-Creorder/Nitzan-Bichler/p/book/9780415496803)[routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Capital-as-Power-A-Study-of-Order-and-Creorder/Nitzan-Bichler/p/book/9780415496803)​ | [Taylor & Francis](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.4324/9780203876329/capital-power-jonathan-nitzan-shimshon-bichler)[taylorfrancis](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.4324/9780203876329/capital-power-jonathan-nitzan-shimshon-bichler)​




## **Tufekci, Zeynep (2017) [Twitter and Tear Gas: The Power and Fragility of Networked Protest](https://www.twitterandteargas.org/), Yale UP.**[twitterandteargas](https://www.twitterandteargas.org/)​

Mass street protests can be "wired for visibility, not leverage," producing spectacle while policy remains unchanged (pp. xx-xxiii, 208-213).

Algorithmic silos function as institutional "safety valves" that absorb dissent without conceding power (pp. 238-245).

The infoscape's design rewards emotionally charged, friction-less content, making performative inflation almost inevitable for movements (pp. 254-261).

## **Graeber, David (2013) [The Democracy Project: A History, a Crisis, a Movement](https://davidgraeber.org/books/the-democracy-project/), Spiegel & Grau.**[davidgraeber](https://davidgraeber.org/books/the-democracy-project/)​

Choreographed protest that generates affect without consequence is "choreography without consequence" (pp. 128-132, 194-198).

Symbolic participation is frequently mistaken for strategic intervention inside horizontal movements (pp. 118-122).

## **Scott, James C. (1998) [Seeing Like a State: How Certain Schemes to Improve the Human Condition Have Failed](https://books.google.com/books/about/Seeing_Like_a_State.html?id=PqcPCgsr2u0C), Yale UP.**[books.google](https://books.google.com/books/about/Seeing_Like_a_State.html?id=PqcPCgsr2u0C)​

## **Federici, Silvia (2004) [Caliban and the Witch: Women, the Body and Primitive Accumulation](https://autonomedia.org/product/caliban-and-the-witch/), Autonomedia.**[autonomedia](https://autonomedia.org/product/caliban-and-the-witch/)​

Climate and abolition struggles can be morally coherent yet epistemically out-scaled, producing failure that is "not moral but epistemic" (pp. 42-47).
