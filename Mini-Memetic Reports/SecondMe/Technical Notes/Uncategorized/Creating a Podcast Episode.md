# Creating a Podcast Episode Guide for App Design Using Open-Source Repositories

Created at 2025/01/19 6:23 AM

Alright, I gave this a try. There's a million and one ways to do this, so let me walk you through what I did.

Step 1: Make the episode guide
I'm experimenting with stylizing my podcasts. I can't say that I've had much success, but I've heard of others having such luck (e.g. the final episode by u/Optimal-Fix1216). One thing they mention is using an episode guide as a source. I headed over to chatGPT and prompted it with this template.

Create an official episode guide. In this podcast, we look at open-source repositories and decide how they might be useful for designing a new app. In this episode, we are creating a <<your app idea here>>.

Act as professional-quality web designers and developers. Establish theming for the app. Discuss data structure needs. Talk about the architecture, e.g. the components that will be reused.

The app will be able to...
<<list of app requirements here>>

Here are the repos we will be using...
<<list of repos to use here>>

Step 2: Editing the result
The resulting text that comes back should be a pretty useful starting point. If it's not, try revising your original template, continuing the conversation with ChatGPT, or manually editing it in Notepad.

Step 3: Your sources
Take that finished episode guide and drop it into NotebookLM as a pasted source. Then also provide the website links to any GitHub repo or documentation page that you feel is relevant.

My results were impressive. Maybe TOO impressive. It was a tad bit overwhelming. If I were to do this again (i.e. not so hastily), I would break this down into a series of podcasts. Starting with the basics of what is the app (product design), who are the clients (UX researcher), what is the brand (UX designer), how is it structured (software architect), etc...

But honestly, I don't feel like this is a great use of this technology. Iterative creative design doesn't play well with long-form content. It's fun though!
