# From Divine Halos to Surveillance Symbols: Societal Legitimization and Its Dark Side

Created at 2025/10/13 8:57 PM

The transition from "halos (sanctity markers)" to "surveillance infrastructure legitimacy symbols" is a theoretical shift in how society perceives and accepts authoritative oversight. This idea links the religious and aesthetic history of the halo, which symbolizes divine approval, to modern surveillance, suggesting that technological and state surveillance is increasingly viewed as a necessary, legitimate, and even benevolent force. The classical halo: Divine sanction

**The traditional halo is a symbol of sacredness and divine favor used in religious art.**

- **Symbolic heritage:** In religious iconography across cultures, a circular or radiant halo signifies a figure's holiness, virtue, or divine power.
- **Aesthetic legitimacy:** The use of the halo establishes a figure's moral authority and sanctified status by representing divine approval through visual shorthand.
- **Power and authority:** While it became a staple of Christian art, the halo's use can be traced back to earlier pagan civilizations, where it also indicated power and worldly dignity. [[4](https://www.holyart.com/blog/religious-items/the-halo-origins-and-meaning/), [6](https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20210623-the-halo-a-symbol-that-spread-around-the-world), [7](https://medium.com/illumination/how-the-halo-effect-shapes-the-success-of-tech-products-28f635076dde), [8](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355751601_An_Analysis_of_The_Halo_Image_Use_On_Non-Human_Beings_And_Its_Place_In_Art), [9](https://fiveable.me/key-terms/art-prehistoric-to-middle-ages/halos#:~:text=Halos%20are%20circular%20or%20oval%20radiance%20or,culture%2C%20halos%20emphasize%20the%20spiritual%20authority%20and)]

The transition to modern surveillance

**This theoretical transition to surveillance infrastructure as a symbol of legitimacy involves a psychological and societal shift.**

- **From divine to omniscient:** The concept of a divine, all-seeing power—the "God's-eye view" of surveillance—is secularized and repurposed by the modern state or corporation. The watchful eye moves from the heavens to the security camera, presenting its omnipresence as necessary for order.
- **Aesthetic of security:** Modern surveillance equipment, like CCTV cameras and drones, is designed and marketed to be aesthetically clean and minimalist. This design suggests efficiency, reliability, and benevolent intent, creating a "halo effect" similar to how sleek tech products are perceived as trustworthy. Facial recognition systems are also presented as a means of enhancing security in a manner that preserves the "holiness" of a space.
- **Perceived security as sanctity:** Surveillance reframes safety and control as sacred values. For example, the presence of security cameras is intended to evoke a sense of safety and order, giving the infrastructure a sacred or protective role in a community.
- **Behavioral control and morality:** Much like the divine halo implies a higher moral order, surveillance systems can impose a form of "surveillance spirituality". The knowledge of being watched encourages conformity to social norms, equating surveillance with moral behavior.
- **The panopticon and divine gaze:** The panopticon, a theoretical model of institutional control in which inmates cannot tell if they are being watched, is a key concept here. The psychological effect of the panopticon mirrors the religious idea of God's constant gaze, reinforcing self-discipline and obedience. The dark side of the transition

**While the shift suggests an acceptance of surveillance, there is a counter-narrative of resistance and concern.**

- **Erosion of privacy:** Critics argue that the legitimization of surveillance directly undermines religious liberty, intellectual privacy, and civil liberties, which are foundational to democratic societies.
- **Instrument of oppression:** Surveillance technologies are sometimes used to target and repress religious and ethnic minorities, weaponizing this new "sanctity" against marginalized groups. In this context, the symbol of legitimacy is subverted into a tool of oppression.
- **False sense of security:** The supposed sanctity and protection of surveillance may be an illusion. A sense of safety might be gained at the cost of personal freedom and privacy, without delivering on the promise of perfect security.
