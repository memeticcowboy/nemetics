# Luce Irigaray's Elemental Philosophy of Breath and Its Ontological and Ethical Dimensions

Created at 2025/10/11 10:04 AM

### Irigaray's Elemental Philosophy of Breath

Luce Irigaray (1930-) has developed the most systematic contemporary philosophy of breath and elemental air. Irigaray's project explicitly challenges Western philosophy's neglect of the elemental conditions that make thought possible, particularly its forgetting of air as the medium of existence.

In *The Forgetting of Air in* *Martin Heidegger*, Irigaray argues that Heidegger's emphasis on earth and dwelling obscures the more fundamental role of air as the condition of all existence. We cannot dwell anywhere without first breathing, and breath connects us to the atmospheric commons that makes individual existence possible. Irigaray's critique extends beyond Heidegger to Western philosophy generally: the tradition has been "forgetting to breathe" in its pursuit of abstract concepts.

Irigaray's philosophy of breathing develops both ontological and ethical dimensions. Ontologically, breathing reveals existence as fundamentally relational—"I can breathe in my own way, but the air will never be simply mine". This creates what she calls "being-there and being-with" (*être-là* and *être-avec*) as inseparable dimensions of existence. Ethically, awareness of breathing cultivates responsibility for the atmospheric commons and attention to the breath of others.

The gendered dimensions of Irigaray's breath philosophy emerge through her analysis of pregnancy and birth. In pregnancy, two beings share breath within one body, creating a unique form of atmospheric communion that challenges masculine models of separate, self-contained subjects. Birth represents the first autonomous breathing, the moment of atmospheric independence, yet this independence remains grounded in continued dependence on shared air.

Irigaray's work bridges Eastern and Western approaches to breath. Drawing particularly on yoga and *pranayama* practices, she argues for cultivation of breath awareness as a path toward embodied spirituality that does not abandon matter for spirit but finds spirit through deepened attention to elemental existence.
