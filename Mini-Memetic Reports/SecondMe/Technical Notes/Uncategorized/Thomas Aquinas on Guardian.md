# Thomas Aquinas on Guardian Angels: Functions, Rationale, and Theological Significance

Created at 2025/10/14 9:18 AM

In Question 113 of the *Summa Theologiae*, Thomas Aquinas constructs a systematic framework for understanding the protective functions of guardian angels. He asserts that every human being is assigned a personal guardian angel from birth to provide guidance and protection throughout life's journey. [[1](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1113.htm), [2](https://www.catholictheology.info/summa-theologica/summa-part1.php?q=135)]

The rationale for angelic guardianship

Aquinas argues that human beings need guardian angels for two main reasons:

- **Human weakness**: Man has the ability to avoid evil through free will, but this ability is not sufficient on its own. Human affection is often weak due to the passions of the soul, and universal knowledge of the law is often misapplied to particular actions. Therefore, angels are needed to regulate and move humans toward good.
- **Divine Providence**: Angels are an extension of divine providence, which regulates lower things through higher things. Just as God governs corporeal beings through spiritual substances, he governs humanity through angels, which move us toward the good. Key aspects of angelic protection

According to Aquinas, the functions of guardian angels include:

- **Spiritual warfare**: Angels, who operate contrary to demons, strengthen us against temptation and fight off evil spirits that seek to tempt and harm us.
- **Guiding the intellect**: Angels propose intelligible truths to the human mind under the guise of sensible things. They strengthen the human intellect and help us develop prudence by passing on God's precepts.
- **Influencing the senses and will**: While angels cannot directly control our will, they can influence it indirectly through our senses and imagination. They do this by presenting good thoughts and arousing our conscience to gently guide us toward God's will.
- **Ward off physical and spiritual harm**: Angels serve to protect humanity from both physical and spiritual dangers.
- **Bearing souls to heaven**: At the end of earthly life, a guardian angel's mission is complete. For a saved soul, the angel joyfully leads it to heaven. For a lost soul, the angel steps aside in alignment with God's justice. Important distinctions and context

Aquinas's teaching also clarifies specific aspects of angelic guardianship:

- **The lowest order**: The office of guardian angels belongs to the lowest of the nine orders of heavenly spirits. However, these angels still continuously behold the beatific vision of God.
- **The beginning and end of guardianship**: A guardian angel is assigned to each individual from birth and stays with them throughout their life. For a developing child still in the womb, Aquinas suggests the mother's guardian angel provides protection.
- **The fate of the unsaved**: The protective function of a guardian angel does not guarantee salvation. However, even those who are ultimately lost, including infidels and the Antichrist, are not deprived of all exterior angelic help. This assistance, although it does not lead to salvation, protects them from certain evils they would otherwise suffer.
- **No grief in angels**: The angels, enjoying the constant joy of the beatific vision, do not grieve over the sins and misfortunes of their charges. Instead, they will for the fulfillment of divine justice.
