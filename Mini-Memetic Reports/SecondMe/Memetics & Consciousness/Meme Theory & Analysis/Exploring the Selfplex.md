# Exploring Consciousness: Blackmore's Selfplex Theory

Created at 2024/11/21 5:32 PM

In "Dismantling the Selfplex: Meme Machines and the Nature of Consciousness," Susan Blackmore explores the concept of the 'self' as a construct formed by a complex of memes, termed the 'selfplex.' She posits that this selfplex serves to protect and propagate its constituent memes, leading individuals to perceive a false sense of self with consciousness and free will. Blackmore suggests that practices like meditation and mindfulness can help dismantle the selfplex, potentially revealing a transformed state of consciousness. <br>

## Insight


**🧠 Dismantling the Selfplex: Understanding Consciousness Through Memes**

- **The Selfplex as a Memeplex**: The selfplex is described as a complex of memes that collectively form an individual's sense of self. This construct serves to protect and propagate its constituent memes, suggesting that the self is not an inherent entity but rather a collection of ideas and beliefs shaped by cultural influences. The selfplex thrives on the interaction of memes, which compete for mental and cultural space, leading to a false perception of individuality.

- **Illusion of Consciousness and Free Will**: Blackmore argues that the perception of consciousness and free will is an illusion created by the selfplex. This false sense of self does not contribute to evolutionary fitness and may even hinder it. Instead, the selfplex serves the purpose of meme propagation, leading individuals to believe they possess an inner self that makes decisions and holds opinions, which is fundamentally misleading.

- **Meditation as a Tool for Dismantling the Selfplex**: Practices such as meditation and mindfulness are proposed as effective methods for dismantling the selfplex. By engaging in these practices, individuals can "weed out" the memes that contribute to the false self, potentially leading to a transformed state of consciousness. This transformation allows for a clearer perception of reality, free from the distortions imposed by the selfplex.

- **Evolutionary Perspective on Memes**: The evolutionary explanation for the existence of the selfplex is framed not in terms of genetic advantage but rather in the context of memes. Memes, as replicators, shape human behavior and culture, and the selfplex acts as a mechanism to enhance the survival and propagation of these memes. This perspective shifts the focus from individual survival to the survival of ideas and cultural constructs.

- **The Role of Emotion in Protecting the Selfplex**: Emotional responses are closely tied to the selfplex, as individuals often defend their beliefs and identities with strong emotional language. This behavior reinforces the illusion of a stable self and distracts from the underlying meme-driven nature of consciousness. The emotional investment in the selfplex can lead to suffering and delusion, as individuals cling to their constructed identities.

- **Critique of Dennett's Perspective**: Blackmore contrasts her views with those of philosopher Daniel Dennett, who describes the self as a "benign user illusion." She argues that this illusion is not benign but rather a source of human suffering. Additionally, while Dennett posits that consciousness is a complex of memes, Blackmore suggests that memes obscure consciousness rather than constitute it, opening up avenues for empirical research into the nature of consciousness.

- **First-Person Research and Empirical Questions**: The exploration of consciousness and the selfplex invites empirical investigation through first-person research methods. By studying individuals who have engaged in meditation and mindfulness, researchers can gain insights into the effects of dismantling the selfplex and the nature of transformed consciousness.

- **Potential Outcomes of Dismantling the Selfplex**: The ultimate goal of dismantling the selfplex through meditation and mindfulness is to transcend the dualistic perception of self and other. While consciousness may be transformed, it does not cease to exist. This suggests a deeper understanding of consciousness that is not reliant on the selfplex, potentially leading to a more authentic experience of reality.
