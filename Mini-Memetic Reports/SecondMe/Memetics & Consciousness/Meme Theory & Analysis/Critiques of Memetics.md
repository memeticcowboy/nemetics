# Critiques of Memetics

Created at 2025/07/26 5:52 PM

By treating memes as a second, independent replicator, memetics separates cultural fitness from biological fitness. Gene-culture coevolution frameworks, in contrast, examine how genetic and cultural processes interact dynamically. This rigid separation hinders integration with established evidence on how culture and biology co-shape human evolution

Blackmore posits imitation as the sole mechanism for meme replication, yet evidence shows animals and humans copy and transform information through diverse cognitive processes. By focusing narrowly on imitation, memetics ignores the roles of innovation, reasoning, and context-sensitive learning, limiting its explanatory power for complex cultural phenomena

Some commentators view radical memetics as mechanistic and reductionist, portraying human thought and behavior as mere vehicles for viral ideas. This perspective has been critiqued as anti-humanist, ignoring economic, environmental, and political forces in cultural change, and as presenting a narrow materialism that alienates social scientists and theologians.
