* The image illustrates a layered conceptual framework that critiques the framing of "labor-assisting technology" by exploring the complexities of human-technology interaction. The four layers—object, process, substrate, and frame—provide a multi-dimensional lens for understanding not only what technology does, but also how it shapes human cognition and societal structures.

This simplistic framing fails to account for the deeper implications of technology’s impact on labor and human agency. * The middle layers elaborate on the idea that technology not only aids in productivity but actively reshapes how users engage with their work and interpret their responsibilities. * The innermost layer critically examines the language surrounding technology, particularly the word "assistance." It suggests that this term can obscure the complex power dynamics at play, wherein technology may serve capital interests more than it serves the user, transforming the concept of labor into a framework of control that benefits specific stakeholders.

* The "return arrows" and seams between layers indicate that sensemaking in this context requires iterative reflection. Returning to examine the questions posed within each layer can foster a deeper understanding of how technology modifies relationships and responsibilities, ultimately necessitating a more conscious approach to technological integration in labor contexts.
