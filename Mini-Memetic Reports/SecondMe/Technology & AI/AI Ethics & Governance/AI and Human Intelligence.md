## Insight
This article delves into the misconceptions surrounding the term 'artificial intelligence' and its implications on our understanding of human intelligence. It argues that the term, coined by John McCarthy in 1955, carries flawed assumptions that have led to a reductionist view of human intelligence. The piece emphasizes the need to recognize the unique, non-replicable aspects of human cognition and creativity, urging a re-examination of what it means to be human in the age of AI. As your assistant, I suggest focusing on the irreplaceable human qualities that AI cannot mimic, such as empathy, intuition, and moral reasoning.

**🧠 Historical Context**
- **John McCarthy's Definition**: Coined 'artificial intelligence' in 1955, implying machines could mimic human cognition.
- **Myth of World-as-Machine**: Rooted in Newtonian physics, viewing the universe and humans as predictable mechanisms.

**⚠️ Misconceptions and Threats**
- **Reductionist View**: AI is seen as a threat due to a narrow understanding of human intelligence.
- **Industrial Creativity**: AI threatens industries where creativity has been reduced to algorithmic processes.

**🎨 True Human Creativity**
- **Ensouled Art**: Art made by humans carries unique qualities that AI cannot replicate.
- **Holistic Value**: Handcrafted items offer a deeper, more meaningful experience than mass-produced goods.

**💡 Future Implications**
- **Re-examining Humanity**: AI invites us to rediscover marginalized human faculties like empathy and moral reasoning.
- **Undisplaceable Skills**: Intuition, wisdom, and emotional reasoning will be in high demand in the age of AI.
