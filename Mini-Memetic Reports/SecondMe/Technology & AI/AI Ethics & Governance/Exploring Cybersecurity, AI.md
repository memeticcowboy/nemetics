## Introduction to the Caveat Live Event

- The Caveat Live event is a recording of the Caveat podcast at the FBI and KU Cybersecurity Conference, where Ben Yellen discusses cybersecurity topics with guests.
- Dr. [Perry Alexander](https://app.getrecall.ai/item/f21b86b5-ed52-49b0-8f76-530e678b5a83), the AT&T Foundation Distinguished Professor of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at the [University of Kansas](https://app.getrecall.ai/item/ae1685f0-5340-4c31-8e88-7d6839c8f647), talks about his research on trusted systems and formal methods.
- Dr. Alexander discusses his work with the [National Security Agency](https://app.getrecall.ai/item/05ef835c-d0a0-431a-bcea-dac15a8cfe8f) and the importance of partnerships between government entities and research universities, including his involvement in the development of a company called Inveri, which offers a product to verify the integrity of [Linux](https://app.getrecall.ai/item/07e5bee0-905b-4fe1-8e38-8191f48b704d) kernels.
- The speaker emphasizes the importance of building relationships and networks in their field, citing their experience working with the NSA and developing products based on their intellectual property.

## Cybersecurity and AI Challenges

- The integration of AI and machine learning into formal methods is discussed, including using large language models to generate proofs and the potential for modifying certification processes.
- Concerns around AI, such as hallucinations and inherent biases, are raised, and the speaker notes that verifying AI systems is very difficult due to the lack of understanding of how they work.
- The biggest challenge in embedded security is seen as keeping up with the adversary, with the speaker expressing worry about the ability to regulate and develop policy around rapidly changing technology.

## Advice for Students

- Advice for students includes pursuing a diverse education, learning to solve problems, and getting out of their discipline to interact with people from different fields, with the speaker citing their own background in music as an example of the benefits of interdisciplinary exploration.
- Dr. [Perry Alexander](https://app.getrecall.ai/item/f21b86b5-ed52-49b0-8f76-530e678b5a83) discusses the discipline of learning and its application to various fields, including music and computing, emphasizing the importance of practice and incremental improvement.

## Ethics in Cybersecurity and AI

- John Simons, a professor of philosophy and director of the Center for Cyber Social Dynamics, highlights the need for ethics in cybersecurity and artificial intelligence, as security involves values and what we care about.
- Simons notes that while progress has been made in addressing technical aspects of AI ethics, such as privacy and bias, more work is needed to consider the social impacts of design choices in tech and to strike a balance between taking ethics into consideration and technical solutions.
- The speaker discusses the differences between humans and AI, highlighting that AI is an abstract object with superior cognitive capacities, but lacks the ability to form close personal relationships like humans do.
- The speaker argues that AI can optimize on one kind of value, but cannot distinguish or choose between different kinds of values, which is a core technical obstacle to AI navigating complex relationships.
- The speaker suggests that policymakers can make decisions to regulate AI and its impact on society, citing examples such as restricting access to certain websites or introducing age restrictions on devices, and that people can distinguish between using AI as a cognitive enhancer and forming close personal relationships with humans.
- The discussion revolves around human relationships with AI and the concept of agency, with philosophers having a high standard for genuine agency.
- Professor John Simons criticizes the traditional philosophical conception of agency, arguing that social agency can be broken down into components and exhibited by machines or artifacts.
- The conversation also touches on the topic of blame and praise, with the idea that AI itself is not blameworthy, but rather the makers and deployers of AI can be held accountable.
