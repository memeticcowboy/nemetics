“This type of reasoning poses challenges for LLMs. Given their probabilistic nature, it seems unlikely they can achieve true deductive closure, which could be a significant limitation for organisations wanting to deploy LLMs in real-world production environments where certainty is required for key aspects of their operations.

However, not all reasoning is deductive. Inductive reasoning, for instance, involves drawing general conclusions from specific observations, leading to probable but not certain outcomes. LLMs excel at statistical generalisation and syllogism. While we may not fully understand how LLMs compress and generalise information, it’s clear they do so effectively, which is why they are so useful.”

This quote is part of a larger post on whether LLMs can truly reason or are "merely faking it".

This is an important question from a product management perspective as it influences use case choices and how we communicate to users their probabilistic nature.

If we need certainty, we should look to deterministic rules-based logic, rather than probabilistic GenAI. 

[](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ACoAACVVf8MB-N-gpVungTKP0c1T5En2jdu2D7A)[Peter White](https://www.linkedin.com/in/petewht/) wrote a superb post capturing this vein inside his formula ‘How to build AI features that customers actually love’:

• Map it out: Start with a state machine or flow diagram
• Find AI sweet spots: find where AI would add value. If a task is predictable, stick to deterministic logic.
• Use AI sparingly: only deploy AI for ambiguous problems that traditional logic can't solve.
