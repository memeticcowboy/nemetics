# Transhuman Doom: Pessimism and Risks in the Transhumanist Movement

Created at 2025/08/23 9:48 AM

**Kimi**

Yes—there are absolutely transhuman doomers.

Thinkers and activists who identify with transhumanism but simultaneously warn that the very technologies they champion could wipe out humanity or make life worse, not better, are often called “transhuman doomers” or simply “transhumanist pessimists.”

1. AI-centric transhuman doomers

Eliezer Yudkowsky is the textbook example.  In the early 2000s he was an exuberant transhumanist who co-founded the Singularity Institute to hurry the arrival of super-intelligence.  After running the alignment-math himself, he flipped: he now assigns a ≥ 99 % probability (“p(doom)”) that unaligned AI will kill everyone.  His institute (now MIRI) is famous in both AI-safety and transhumanist circles for white-papers describing how even a “friendly” seed AI could quickly tile the universe with paper-clips if its goals drift a nanometer from ours .

2. Singularitarian doomers

Nick Bostrom’s Superintelligence (2014) is a transhumanist manifesto that spends most of its pages on extinction scenarios—fast take-off, decisive strategic advantage, and “the treacherous turn.”  Bostrom still calls himself a transhumanist, but his work is the go-to citation for people who believe the Singularity is more likely to be an existential catastrophe than a rapture .

3. TESCREAL skeptics

The acronym TESCREAL bundles together Transhumanism, Extropianism, Singularitarianism, Cosmism, Rationalism, Effective Altruism, and Longtermism.  Critics inside that cluster increasingly warn that the whole bundle could “lock in” dystopian outcomes—e.g., immortal digital minds with no place for biological humans .

4. Quora & forum culture

On discussion boards the label “doomer” is sometimes used self-deprecatingly by transhumanists who believe that radical life-extension, mind-uploading, or brain-computer interfaces will only magnify inequality, create ungovernable posthumans, or give a tiny elite god-like power over the rest of us .

In short, transhumanism has its own internal pessimists—people who accept the destination (radical enhancement or post-human futures) but fear the journey will end in extinction or dystopia.
