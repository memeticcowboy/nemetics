### How I’d Assess It

I’d analyze an X user’s profile, posts, and behavior for clues about their stance on something like trust scores. Here’s what I’d look at:

- **Attitude Toward Tech/AI:** Do they rave about AI breakthroughs or rant about Big Tech overreach? A tech optimist might lean "yes" to trust scores; a skeptic might hate the idea.

- **Views on Privacy:** If they’re paranoid about surveillance or data harvesting, they’d likely balk at algorithmic scoring. Someone unbothered by it might be more open.

- **Social Dynamics:** Do they block a lot, call out "fakes," or obsess over credibility? That could signal they’d welcome a system to filter people—though they might also prefer doing it themselves.

- **Tone and Philosophy:** Are they pragmatic ("whatever works") or idealistic ("everyone deserves a voice")? That’d nudge their leanings.

I’d piece this together from their bio, recent posts, and interactions, then give you a probability—like "60% chance they’d be open"—with my reasoning. It’s not mind-reading, just educated guesswork based on what they put out there.

### Limits

- I’m stuck with public data. No private DMs or deleted tweets unless they’re archived somewhere I can access.

- People don’t always say what they think about abstract ideas like this directly, so I’d be extrapolating from related vibes.

- Context matters. A single rant about bots doesn’t mean they’d back trust scores—it could just mean they’re annoyed.
