## Insight
This paper investigates the challenges faced by AI ethics professionals in tech companies and their strategies for institutionalizing ethical practices. Despite some progress, the findings reveal a reliance on informal tactics and limited authority, leading to a state of 'minimum viable ethics.' The study emphasizes the need for regulatory intervention to enhance the impact of AI ethics on product development. For those involved in AI ethics, consider advocating for stronger regulatory frameworks to support your initiatives and drive meaningful change.

**📜 Institutionalizing AI Ethics**
- **AI ethics professionals are agile and opportunistic.**: They create standardized processes and tools despite limited authority within their organizations.
- **Negotiation with product teams is challenging.**: Ethics professionals often lack ownership over products, making it difficult to influence decisions.
- **Internal coalitions can enhance influence.**: Building alliances across departments helps legitimize ethics initiatives and secure resources.

**⚖️ Product Impact Challenges**
- **AI ethics is often reduced to compliance.**: The focus on regulatory responses limits the scope of ethical considerations in product development.
- **Ethics initiatives are reactive rather than proactive.**: Many ethics efforts are only prioritized when crises arise, leading to suboptimal decision-making.
- **Organizational pressures hinder ethical practices.**: The rush to innovate often sidelines ethical considerations, impacting the quality of AI products.

**📊 Regulatory Intervention**
- **Future regulations could bridge ethical gaps.**: Proposed regulations may provide a framework for more robust ethical practices in AI.
- **Specific standards for ethics are needed.**: Policymakers should collaborate with various stakeholders to establish clear ethical guidelines.
- **Consequences for noncompliance can drive change.**: Legal mandates can transform ethical practices from optional to essential within organizations.

**🔍 Organizational Change Dynamics**
- **Ethics professionals act as institutional entrepreneurs.**: They seek to transform organizational practices by aligning ethics with business priorities.
- **Existing narratives can be leveraged for change.**: Framing ethical considerations as business risks or opportunities can facilitate acceptance.
- **Sustainable change requires ongoing effort.**: Ethics initiatives must be integrated into standard practices to ensure long-term impact.

**🛠️ Technical Tools and Infrastructure**
- **Reusable tools enhance scalability.**: Developing common tools for ethical assessments can streamline processes across teams.
- **Integration into ML infrastructure is key.**: Embedding ethics tools within existing systems promotes wider adoption and consistency.
- **Collaboration is essential for effective implementation.**: AI ethics professionals must coordinate with product teams to ensure tools are utilized effectively.

**💡 Insights from Interviews**
- **Participants highlight the novelty of their work.**: Many AI ethics challenges are unique and not fully addressed by existing academic literature.
- **Coordination between research and product is crucial.**: Ethics professionals need to bridge the gap between theoretical research and practical application.
- **Access to data and models is often limited.**: Ethics teams face challenges in obtaining necessary information to assess AI impacts.

**📈 Measuring Impact**
- **Impact evaluation is often informal.**: Many ethics teams lack structured methods to assess the effectiveness of their interventions.
- **Meaningful changes are infrequent.**: AI ethics professionals report limited success in influencing product decisions.
- **Organizational buy-in is critical.**: Support from leadership can significantly enhance the effectiveness of ethics initiatives.

**🔗 Future Directions for AI Ethics**
- **Collaboration with external entities is vital.**: Engaging with academic researchers and independent auditors can strengthen internal ethics efforts.
- **Investment in applied research is necessary.**: More resources should be directed towards addressing practical ethical challenges in AI.
- **Empowering third-party auditors can enhance accountability.**: Independent oversight can help ensure that ethical standards are upheld in AI development.
