The term **wetware cognitive computing** refers to computational systems that integrate biological components—particularly living neurons—into their architecture. This approach seeks to harness the inherent processing capabilities of biological neural networks, potentially leading to more efficient and adaptable computing paradigms.

Recent advancements in this field have demonstrated the feasibility of such systems. For instance, in late 2021, researchers successfully integrated human brain cells into digital platforms, enabling these biological components to perform goal-directed tasks. Notably, these human neurons learned to play a simulated game of Pong more rapidly than traditional machine learning algorithms, albeit achieving a lower overall skill level. This experiment provided empirical evidence of the superior information-processing capacities of human neurons compared to those of other species, such as mice.

The emerging field of **organoid intelligence (OI)** exemplifies the convergence of biological and computational sciences. OI involves the use of lab-grown brain organoids—three-dimensional cultures of human brain cells—as biological hardware for computing purposes. Researchers posit that such organoids could offer faster, more efficient, and more powerful computing capabilities than traditional silicon-based systems, while consuming significantly less energy. Although these organoids are not yet capable of replicating human cognitive functions, they hold promise for advancing our understanding of brain development, learning, and memory, and for developing treatments for neurological disorders.

These innovations suggest a paradigm shift in computing, moving from purely silicon-based architectures to hybrid systems that incorporate biological elements. Such systems could emulate the brain's ability to process vast amounts of data in real-time, adapt to new information, and perform complex tasks with remarkable energy efficiency. However, the integration of living tissues into computational frameworks raises ethical considerations, particularly concerning the potential for consciousness in biological components and the implications of their use in technology.

In summary, **wetware cognitive computing** represents a frontier in computational science, merging the adaptability and efficiency of biological neural networks with the precision and scalability of traditional computing systems. While still in the experimental stages, this interdisciplinary approach holds the potential to revolutionize our understanding and application of both biological and artificial intelligence.
