# Electric Cars as an Example Of DODO Loop

Created at 2025/09/03 12:22 PM

- The electric car (E-car) industry illustrates the ownership issue within the DODO loop. Early attempts to popularize electric cars faced significant challenges, including technological limitations, infrastructure deficits, and cultural resistance tied to existing ownership and debt models.
- Past failures in electric car adoption were often attributed to the technology or market readiness, but underlying ownership dynamics played a crucial role. Consumers and producers were locked into traditional ownership models tied to fossil fuel economies, debt structures, and performance expectations that limited innovation and adoption.
- The ownership of cars was not just about possession but about the obligations and survival scripts tied to them—loans, maintenance costs, social status, and infrastructure compatibility. These factors created resistance to change and reinforced the DODO loop's entrapment.
- However, with different owners—such as companies with new visions, governments with supportive policies, or communities embracing stewardship models—the electric car industry has seen renewed success.
- Innovations in financing (e.g., leasing, subscription models), infrastructure development (charging networks), and cultural shifts toward sustainability have helped reframe ownership from a cage into a more flexible, stewardship-oriented relationship.
- This transformation shows that ownership issues in the DODO loop are not fixed but can evolve with systemic changes and different actors who break survival scripts and foster new narratives around possession and responsibility.
- The electric car example demonstrates that while "we tried this before, it didn't work" may be true historically, it does not preclude future success under different ownership conditions and systemic frameworks.
- This aligns with the broader understanding that ownership can shift from obligation to stewardship, enabling projects to succeed where they previously failed.
