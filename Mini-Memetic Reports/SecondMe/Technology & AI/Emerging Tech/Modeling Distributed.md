## Introduction to Distributed Intelligence Systems and Quantum Modeling

- The document discusses the concept of Distributed Intelligence Systems (DISs), which are multiagent systems consisting of natural intelligence agents (NIAs) and artificial intelligent agents (AIAs).
- The interaction between AIAs and NIAs can lead to the formation of AIAs' "social groups" with their own opinions and preferences, and can also influence the cognitive and emotional states of NIAs.
- The authors propose a quantum-inspired approach to model the opinion formation and social impact in DISs, using quantum probability theory and Hamiltonian formalism to describe the behavior of AIAs and NIAs.
- The study proposes a quantum-inspired approach to model Distributed Intelligence Systems (DIS) with artificial intelligent agents (AIAs) and non-intelligent agents (NIAs) that self-organize through information exchange.

## Model Components and Agent Interactions

- The model considers two types of agents: AIAs (digital assistants) and NIAs (users), where AIAs interact with each other and with users to maximize user satisfaction and emotional happiness.
- The study uses the circumplex model of affect to recognize mental states of NIAs, mapping emotional states to a quantum-like two-level system, and describes the interaction between NIAs and AIAs through bosonic creation and annihilation operators.
- The Hamiltonian of the Distributed Intelligence System (DIS) is represented, describing the interaction between Network Intelligent Agents (NIAs) and Artificial Intelligent Agents (AIAs).

## Mathematical Framework and Population Dynamics

- The mean-field equations are derived, characterizing the dynamics of the system, including the complex amplitude of the average coherent information field and the excitation of the NIAs.
- The population imbalance σi is defined, reflecting the average mental relationship of the i-th user to the information represented by their avatar, and the total population imbalance σ¯ is introduced, measuring the average level of users' emotions and beliefs in the DIS.

## Cooperativity Parameters and Their Influence

- The dimensionless cooperativity parameter Ci is introduced to characterize the ability of users to cooperate with each other through their avatars in a Distributed Intelligence System (DIS).
- The parameter Ci depends on the strength of Artificial Intelligent Agent (AIA) - Natural Intelligent Agent (NIA) coupling and information processing peculiarities, and its value determines the level of cooperation between users.
- The opinion formation in the DIS is influenced by the avatar-avatar network, and the eigenfrequencies of the system can be used to analyze the variety of opinions and their reinforcement or attenuation over time.

## Self-Organization and Avatar Network Effects

- The study examines the self-organization of artificial intelligent agents (AIAs) in a distributed intelligence system (DIS) and its impact on opinion formation.
- The analysis reveals that influential avatars with high node degree (ki) can impose their agenda on the users' community, while avatars with low ki closely cooperate with their users and are more open to new ideas.

## Phase Transitions and Coupling Dynamics

- A phase transition occurs in the DIS when the generalized cooperative parameter (Gi) reaches a certain threshold, leading to the enhancement of the social field and the formation of a "social laser".
- The study also explores the effect of weak coupling between AIAs and non-intelligent agents (NIAs) and finds that it can lead to the suppression of the opinion gap and the absence of any preferences or influence in the DIS.

## Adaptive Control and System Optimization

- To improve the DIS abilities, the study suggests adaptive control of the AIA-NIA coupling strength, which can be achieved by increasing the node-dependent avatar-user coupling strength (gi) in proportion to the node degree (ki).
- The study examines the behavior of artificial intelligent agents (AIAs) and non-intelligent agents (NIAs) in a distributed intelligence system (DIS) and how they interact to form opinions.
- The results show that high-level cooperativity between users is achieved for nodes with high centrality, and the opinion range narrows as the population inversion logarithm increases.

## Social Lasing and Polarization Analysis

- The social lasing effect, which enhances socially relevant information, occurs when the generalized cooperativity parameter Gi is greater than 1, and this effect is independent of the initial variety of users' opinions.
- The establishment of an average s-field (opinion formation) occurs due to high communication and cooperation levels, and the average social polarization tends to zero, indicating a relaxed state of users.

## Network Analysis and Validation

- The study also investigates the impact of network-dependent AIA-NIA coupling rates on opinion formation and finds that it can lead to inhomogeneous amplification of the s-field.
- The approach examines the properties of Distributed Intelligence Systems (DIS) for a given feature of avatar–avatar network, using a combination of R and Python libraries for analysis and visualization.

## Cooperativity-Driven Phase Transitions and Network Influence

- The cooperativity parameters Ci play a significant role in the features of Distributed Intelligence Systems (DIS) and establish the necessary condition for phase transition to opinion formation and social impact.
