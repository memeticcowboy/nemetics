# Exploring AI's Influence on Language Change in Unscripted Spoken English

Created at 2025/09/01 2:35 PM

### 1. Purposes and Goals

The study aims to determine whether lexical shifts in human spoken language are influenced directly by Large Language Models (LLMs), beyond simple tool use. It explores if AI-associated word patterns are seeping into natural human communication.

### 2. Central Questions or Problems

- Are human speakers increasingly adopting LLM-associated lexical patterns?
- Is this a natural language change or an AI-driven misalignment effect?
- What are the broader implications for alignment, ethics, and language research?

### 3. Fundamental Concepts

- **Language change**: shaped by social, cultural, cognitive, and technological factors.
- **LLM misalignment**: divergences between model outputs and human norms.
- **Seep-in effect**: AI language patterns entering human language through exposure.
- **Human-authorship indeterminacy**: growing difficulty in distinguishing AI vs. human text.

### 4. Essential Information

- Dataset: 22.1 million words from unscripted science/tech podcasts (pre-2022 vs. post-2022).
- Findings: Moderate but significant uptick in AI-associated words (e.g., *align, surpass, boast*).
- Contrasts: Baseline synonyms showed no consistent directional change.
- Implication: Spoken language convergence with AI is weaker than in writing but still notable.

### 5. Points of View or Frames of Reference

- **Linguistic**: normal processes of language change vs. AI-driven acceleration.
- **Technological**: historical parallels with printing press, phone, internet.
- **Ethical**: concerns about alignment, fairness, and seep-in of unintended values.

### 6. Underlying Assumptions

- Lexical patterns in unscripted podcasts are reliable indicators of natural human language use.
- LLM overuse patterns can be meaningfully compared to human lexical shifts.
- Increased usage of specific words reflects systemic change, not random fluctuation.

### 7. Inferences and Conclusions

- Human language shows selective convergence with LLM-preferred vocabulary.
- LLMs may amplify pre-existing lexical trends, not just create new ones.
- Evidence of seep-in supports concerns that misalignment can subtly alter norms of human communication.

### 8. Implications and Consequences

- **Linguistic**: possibility of a homogenized AI-influenced register.
- **Methodological**: challenges to studying language when authorship is indeterminate.
- **Societal**: AI-driven seep-in could extend beyond word choice to beliefs, values, and social norms.
- **Ethical**: raises urgency in alignment debates, since linguistic misalignment mirrors moral misalignment.
