# Soft Robots with Self-Amputation and Fusion Abilities

## Insight
Roboticists at Yale University's Faboratory have developed innovative soft robots capable of self-amputation and fusion, mimicking certain animal behaviors. These robots use a unique bicontinuous thermoplastic foam and sticky polymer for their joints, allowing them to detach and reattach limbs or fuse together to overcome obstacles. This advancement could lead to future robots with radical shape-shifting abilities. If you're interested in robotics, this breakthrough is a fascinating development to follow.

**🦾 Soft Robot Capabilities**
- **Self-Amputation**: Soft robots can detach a limb to escape from traps, similar to certain reptiles.
- **Fusion**: Multiple robots can fuse together to form a single unit, enabling them to cross gaps or obstacles.

**🔬 Innovative Joint Material**
- **Bicontinuous Thermoplastic Foam**: This material allows the joints to be melted and reformed, providing flexibility.
- **Sticky Polymer**: Used in conjunction with the foam, it enables the joints to stick together and be pulled apart as needed.

**📜 Research Publication**
- **Advanced Materials Paper**: The research is detailed in a paper titled 'Self-Amputating and Interfusing Machines'.
- **Future Implications**: The techniques could lead to robots capable of radical shape-shifting through autotomy and interfusion.

**📹 Demonstration Videos**
- **Self-Amputation Demo**: A video shows a robot detaching its leg to escape from a trap.
- **Fusion Demo**: Another video demonstrates three robots fusing together to cross a gap.

**🧩 Comparison with Existing Systems**
- **Mechanical Connections and Magnets**: Existing systems are rigid, unlike the flexible joints developed by Yale's team.
- **Modular Robotics**: While modular robotics isn't new, the innovation lies in the flexible joint material.
