Evolution discovers this bottleneck spontaneously — not as a decoration, but because any non-compressed solution wastes resources and is outcompeted.<br>No choice: if the mapping is compressible, the system converges to a bow-tie. Expansion (diversification of outputs) rides on top of prior compression; it cannot reverse or compensate for the information that was thrown away at the waist.<br>This creates a fundamental arrow: systems can become more phenotypically diverse over time, but only by building ever-more elaborate recombinations on a conserved, ever-more-compressed core. With only a handful of building blocks (e.g., ~12 core metabolites, a few dozen signaling modules), the only way to adapt to new challenges or generate new phenotypes is to discover new combinatorial uses, new regulatory wiring around the core, or (rarely) add a new core element by making a previously irrelevant distinction relevant.<br>This is why evolution is creative under constraint: the bottleneck turns saturation into crises that demand orthogonal extensions — new dimensions, new distinctions — rather than endless shuffling of existing ones.
