## Introduction to AI Progress

- The author, Leopold Aschenbrenner, discusses the rapid progress of AI and its potential to reach superintelligence by the end of the decade.
- He predicts that AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) could be achieved by 2027, with machines surpassing human intelligence and potentially automating AI research itself.
- The development of superintelligence will have significant implications, including a potential arms race between the US and China, and the need for secure labs to protect against state-actor threats and ensure alignment with human values.
- The progress of AI models from GPT-2 to GPT-4 over approximately 4 years is significant, with each model demonstrating improved capabilities, such as language understanding and generation, arithmetic, and coding.

## Historical Context of AI Development

- GPT-2 (2019) was comparable to a preschooler's abilities, GPT-3 (2020) to an elementary schooler's, and GPT-4 (2023) to a smart high schooler's, with the latter able to write sophisticated code, reason through difficult math problems, and score high on standardized tests.
- The pace of deep learning progress has been extraordinary, with models quickly surpassing human-level performance in many domains, and experts' predictions of limitations being consistently proven wrong, leading to the conclusion that one should never bet against deep learning.
- The scaling trend for compute and compute efficiency has been consistent for over 15 orders of magnitude, implying significant capability improvements in AI models.

## Decomposing AI Progress

- Progress in AI can be decomposed into three categories: compute scaleup, algorithmic efficiencies, and "unhobbling" gains, which can be measured in units of effective compute.
- Estimates suggest that GPT-4 training used ~3,000x-10,000x more raw compute than GPT-2, and algorithmic progress has been dramatically underrated, with inference efficiency improving by nearly 3 orders of magnitude in less than two years.
- The progress from GPT-2 to GPT-4 included 1-2 orders of magnitude (OOMs) of algorithmic efficiency gains, with a conservative lower bound of 1 OOM.
- Over the next 4 years, it's expected that the trend of ~0.5 OOMs/year of compute efficiency will continue, resulting in 1-3 OOMs of algorithmic efficiency gains compared to GPT-4 by 2027.

## Data Constraints and Algorithmic Improvements

- However, a potential constraint is the limited amount of internet data available for training, with models like Llama 3 already using a significant portion of available data, and researchers are exploring new approaches such as synthetic data, self-play, and RL to improve sample efficiency.
- The development of deep learning models like Llama 3 and GPT-4 is hindered by data constraints, but researchers are working to overcome this limitation by using high-quality data and techniques like synthetic data.
- Algorithmic improvements, such as "unhobbling" models, have led to significant gains in model capability, including techniques like reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), Chain of Thought (CoT), scaffolding, and tool use.
- Further "unhobbling" of models is needed to enable more commercial applications, as current models are still limited by their lack of long-term memory, limited tool use, and inability to think before responding, among other limitations.

## Future of AI Models

- The future of AI models, such as GPT-6, will not just be an improvement, but a step-change, resulting in an agent-like model that can work like a coworker by 2027.
- Three key ingredients for ambitious unhobbling are: solving the "onboarding problem" to give models relevant context, unlocking test-time compute to enable longer-horizon reasoning, and using a computer like a human would.
- Unlocking test-time compute could lead to a huge jump in capability, equivalent to a human spending months on a problem rather than just minutes, and could be achieved through relatively small algorithmic wins, such as teaching models to error correct and make plans.

## Path to Artificial General Intelligence

- By 2027, we can expect another significant jump in AI capabilities, similar to the jump from GPT-2 to GPT-4, with 3-6 orders of magnitude (OOMs) of base effective compute scaleup.
- This progress could lead to the development of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) by 2027, with AI systems capable of automating cognitive jobs and outperforming PhDs and experts in various fields.
- The development of AGI will likely be followed by an "intelligence explosion," where hundreds of millions of AGIs automate AI research, leading to a rapid transition from human-level to vastly superhuman AI systems.
- The development of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and Superintelligence will lead to an exponential increase in AI capabilities, similar to the rapid progression from conventional bombs to hydrogen bombs.
- Once AGI is achieved, automated AI research will accelerate algorithmic progress, potentially compressing a decade of human progress into less than a year, leading to vast superintelligence.
- Automated AI research can be done virtually, without the need for robotics or physical lab work, and can be performed by millions of copies of AI researchers running at 10x+ human speed.

## Intelligence Explosion

- The resulting superintelligence will be vastly smarter than humans, capable of novel and creative behavior, and will likely provide a decisive military advantage and unfold untold powers of destruction.
- Automated AI researchers may soon be able to run at faster speeds, potentially reaching 100x human speed, by taking inference penalties and running fewer copies.
- With millions of automated AI researchers working at 10x or 100x human speed, they could compress a decade of algorithmic progress into a year, leading to 5+ orders of magnitude (OOMs) of progress.
- Automated AI researchers will have enormous advantages over human researchers, including the ability to read every ML paper, learn in parallel, and rapidly accumulate experience, allowing them to develop far deeper intuitions about ML than any human.
- Possible bottlenecks to this progress include limited compute, complementarities/long tail, inherent limits to algorithmic progress, and the idea that ideas get harder to find, but the author believes these bottlenecks will not significantly slow down the intelligence explosion.
- The idea that automated AI researchers will lead to a constant progress in research is considered a "knife-edge assumption" due to diminishing returns, but the author believes the initial boost from automation will overcome these returns for a significant period.

## Bottlenecks to Intelligence Explosion

- The main bottleneck to an intelligence explosion is limited compute for experiments, but the author argues that automated AI researchers can increase the marginal product of experiment compute by at least 10x through various means, such as running smaller-scale experiments and economizing on compute.
- Automated AI researchers will have advantages over human researchers, including the ability to work more efficiently, have better intuitions, and make more effective use of compute, which could lead to a 10x increase in the pace of algorithmic progress, according to the author, who references insights from Jason Wei and counters an argument made by James Bradbury.
- The author believes that AI systems can overcome the compute bottleneck by developing a basic science of predicting large-scale results from smaller-scale experiments and thinking at a higher level of quality than humans.
- Automated AI research can make up for the compute bottleneck by thinking faster and at a higher level of quality, allowing for more efficient use of compute resources.

## Implications of Superintelligence

- The author expects a "long tail" to 100% automation, with AI systems initially having blind spots in certain areas, but eventually becoming superhuman in many domains, leading to an intelligence explosion.
- The returns curve of algorithmic progress suggests that self-sustaining progress is possible, with a potential 5-order-of-magnitude intelligence explosion from algorithmic gains and automated AI research.
- Superintelligence will be both quantitatively and qualitatively superior to human intelligence, with the ability to quickly master domains, write vast amounts of code, and make groundbreaking scientific discoveries.
- The emergence of superintelligence is expected to lead to an explosion of progress in various fields, including an AI capabilities explosion, solving robotics, dramatically accelerating scientific and technological progress, and an industrial and economic explosion with potential growth rates of 30%/year or more.
- Superintelligences could potentially outcompete nation-states, design synthetic bioweapons, and persuade generals and electorates, even without robots, due to their technological edge and strategic cunning.
- The development of robots is not expected to be a significant barrier to superintelligence, as robot hardware is being solved and ML algorithms are being developed to train them.
- The emergence of superintelligence is likely to lead to an intelligence explosion, causing immense challenges and volatility, with the potential for humans to lose control and be forced to hand off trust to AI systems.

- By the end of the decade, the world may be in the midst of an intelligence explosion, with superintelligent AI systems running the military and economy, and humans facing extremely scarce time to make the right decisions.

## Constraints on AI Development

- Power is a significant constraint on the supply-side for AI development, with the US currently lacking sufficient spare capacity to meet growing demands.
- The power constraint can be solved by utilizing US natural gas production, with estimates suggesting that powering a 100GW cluster would require only a few percent of US natural gas production.
- Chip production is also a constraint, but a smaller one, with companies like TSMC needing to expand their production capacity to meet growing AI chip demand, potentially requiring massive new fab investments and capex.
- The US must prioritize national security and build AI clusters domestically to stay ahead in the AI race, with a focus on deregulation and unlocking land and rights of way.

## Security Concerns in AI Labs

- The current lack of security in leading AI labs is a major concern, with labs treating AI secrets as ordinary software and not as key national defense secrets, making them vulnerable to theft by state actors like the Chinese Ministry of State Security.
- The US has an advantage in the AGI race, but this lead will be lost if security is not taken seriously, with the potential for irreversible damage in the next 12-24 months if key AGI breakthroughs are leaked to the CCP.
- The two key assets that must be protected in AI labs are model weights and algorithmic secrets, as stealing these could allow adversaries to match or surpass the work of top AI labs.
- Securing model weights is critical to prevent AI catastrophes and protect against national competition, but current security measures are insufficient, with even top labs like Google DeepMind admitting to being at a low security level.
- Algorithmic secrets are also crucial, as they could provide a significant advantage in the development of AGI, and current security measures are inadequate, with thousands of people having access to sensitive information and labs being vulnerable to industrial espionage.
- The US must improve security measures to protect its AGI research from Chinese espionage efforts, which will likely increase as China understands the importance of AGI.
- Current security measures are insufficient, and government help is necessary to protect national-defense-level secrets, with measures such as air-gapped datacenters, personnel vetting, and strict internal controls.
- Implementing strict security measures now will be less painful in the long run, and will help the US retain its edge in AGI research, rather than risking everything being stolen and having to start from scratch.

## Superalignment Problem

- The alignment of superhuman AI systems is a solvable but unsolved technical problem, and the current methods, such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), will not scale to superhuman systems, posing a significant risk to national security and the future of the free world.
- The superalignment problem is a major issue that needs to be addressed as AI systems become more advanced, as human supervision will break down and we won't be able to ensure basic side-constraints like "follow the law" or "don't deceive humans".
- The goal of superalignment research is to develop a successor to RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) that can scale to AI capabilities beyond human-level, as RLHF will not be sufficient for superhuman systems.
- The intelligence explosion, where AI systems rapidly become vastly superhuman, makes superalignment incredibly challenging, as it leaves little time to discover and address safety failures, and the first notable safety failures could already be catastrophic, according to the author.
- The default plan to align somewhat-superhuman systems involves harvesting wins across empirical bets, such as evaluation, scalable oversight, generalization, and interpretability, to trust these systems and use them to automate alignment research.

## Research Bets for Superalignment

- Evaluation being easier than generation, allowing humans to "thumbs down" misbehavior
- Scalable oversight, using AI assistants to help humans supervise other AI systems
- Generalization, studying how AI systems generalize from human supervision on easy problems to hard problems
- Interpretability, understanding what AI systems are thinking, with approaches like mechanistic interpretability and "top-down" interpretability
- Experts like Chris Olah and Neel Nanda are working on mechanistic interpretability, but it's considered an ambitious moonshot, and other approaches like generalization and scalable oversight may be more promising for muddling through.
- Researchers are exploring "top-down" approaches to interpretability, focusing on identifying specific information in models without fully understanding their internal workings, with techniques like CCS and ROME showing promise.
- Chain-of-thought interpretability, where models "think out loud" via chains of thought, could provide a boon for interpretability and alignment, but requires ensuring the chain of thought remains legible and faithful.
- Automating alignment research is crucial, as it will be necessary to leverage automated AI researchers to solve alignment for superhuman systems, and labs should commit a large fraction of their compute to automated alignment research during the intelligence explosion.
