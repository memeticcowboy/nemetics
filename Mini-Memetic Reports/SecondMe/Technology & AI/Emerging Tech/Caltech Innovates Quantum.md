# Caltech Innovates Quantum Memory Using Sound

Created at 2025/11/19 7:19 PM

A team of Caltech scientists, led by Mohammad Mirhosseini, has developed a hybrid approach to store quantum information by translating electrical information into sound, allowing quantum states to survive in storage for up to 30 times longer.


The approach uses a mechanical oscillator, similar to a miniature tuning fork, to store quantum information, which offers advantages such as compact devices and reduced energy leakage.


This method has the potential to be scalable and could lead to the development of more efficient quantum memories for superconducting qubits, with the team already exploring ways to increase the interaction rate for faster data input and output.

## Insight


**🔊 Harnessing Sound for Quantum Memory Storage**  
A Caltech team led by Mohammad Mirhosseini has pioneered a hybrid method for storing quantum information by converting electrical signals into sound, enhancing stability and duration of quantum states up to 30 times longer.

---

**🔬 Innovative Hybrid Approach**  
- **Breaking Conventional Barriers**: This new method overcomes limitations in existing quantum memory technologies by utilizing mechanical oscillators (miniature tuning forks) that store quantum states more effectively than traditional superconducting qubits.
- **Enhanced Storage Capability**: The team achieved retention of quantum states for 30 times longer than previous bests, improving data reliability. This is critical for quantum computing, where maintaining the integrity of quantum data is essential during computations.
- **Combining Electricity with Sound**: By translating electrical information to sound waves at gigahertz frequencies, the oscillators can interact with electrical signals, thus providing a more robust mechanism for storing quantum information.

---

**⚙️ Technical Advantages of Mechanical Oscillators**  
- **Compact Device Design**: Mechanical oscillators enable the creation of smaller, more efficient devices compared to traditional electromagnetic methods, paving the way for tighter integration on chips.
- **Reduced Energy Leakage**: Unlike electromagnetic waves, mechanical vibrations do not radiate energy into free space, which helps in preserving quantum information and minimizes unwanted energy exchanges.
- **Scalability Potential**: The design facilitates the potential for numerous oscillators to exist within a single chip, a key factor for future quantum computing scalability.

---

**💡 Future Development Directions**  
- **Increasing Interaction Rates**: Researchers aim to enhance interaction rates between electromagnetic and acoustic waves. The current system could be improved by a factor of three to ten, enabling faster data input and output.
- **Applying Learnings to Quantum Technologies**: Successful execution of this approach can be a stepping stone towards realizing more efficient quantum memories, crucial for the progression of quantum computing applications.
- **Exploring New Configurations**: The team is investigating various configurations that optimize both the acoustic and quantum properties to maximize the benefits of this hybrid system.
