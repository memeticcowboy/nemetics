## Insight

* The critique of treating human choices as reversible actions raises critical questions about the nature of iterative processes; while they allow for flexibility, the assumption that practitioners can repeatedly apply the same methods without consequence overlooks the finite nature of personal and organizational resources.
* The document asserts that the PROVEN framework, while effective in a carrier-substrate context—like software development or business process improvement—fails when applied to practitioner activation due to lack of acknowledgment of the thermodynamic realities underlying human behavior and decisions.
* The distinction made by the NEMA document about the A-phase serves as a vital critique, asserting that distinguishing between activation and articulation is essential; without this, organizations risk misunderstanding the true nature of the pressures faced during the iterative process, leading to ineffective adaptation strategies.
* The warning against "memetic parasites" elucidates the dangers posed by methodologies that appear efficient but neglect the reality of human limitations; practitioners should be vigilant in identifying frameworks that may gloss over the irreversible costs associated with rapid iteration, ensuring a balance between innovation and sustainability.
