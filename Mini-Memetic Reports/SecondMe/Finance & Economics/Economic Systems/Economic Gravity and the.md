**Section 2 — Economic Gravity**

Now here’s where the trail turns steep.<br>In the main essay I said "*AI under [debt-capitalism](https://app.me.bot/public/RXHOYVE7EKYAKTPQ) ain’t optimizing for human flourishing, it’s kneeling before the hungry god of engagement."*<br>That line hits because it’s true enough to sting and clean enough to spread like wildfire.<br>But as with most truths told from horseback, the corners got rounded while the hooves were flying.<br>Market dynamics ain’t a single river — they’re a whole delta of branching currents.<br>Calm-water niches exist (Headspace, meditation apps, parental-control ecosystems).<br>But the main tributary — the ad-driven AI frontier — is still an outrage-fed floodplain.<br>So here’s the deeper cut.

**2.1 — Multi-Agent Competitive Pressure**<br>In markets where:

- revenue ∝ engagement
- engagement ∝ emotional arousal

…there is a multi-agent reinforcement learning dynamic where firms are agents in a competitive environment.^1<br>Thus:<br>Claim: Outrage-optimized agents spread because they yield higher short-term retention, unless counterbalanced by regulatory penalties.^2<br>This is consistent with memetic theory from AI Obsolescence Theater, where you describe survival dynamics as D.O.D.O.:<br>Debt. Ownership. Deadlines. Othering.^3<br>This is economic gravity — not metaphysical Id forces. Empirical models of outrage virality confirm that anger-driven content achieves 20–30% higher sharing rates than neutral equivalents, creating a feedback loop where provocative strategies dominate population-level dynamics in simulated social networks.^4

**2.2 — Proposed Model (for future testing)**<br>Simulation:

- 100 firms
- Strategy = {calm, neutral, provocative}
- Reward = engagement – λ·reg_penalty

Expectation:

- Provocative dominates when λ < threshold
- Calm dominates only when λ > threshold

This creates a predictable policy lever (see Section 5). In multi-agent RL environments modeling social dilemmas, defection (analogous to provocative strategies) emerges as the Nash equilibrium without punishment mechanisms, invading cooperative populations at rates up to 80% under low-cost exploitation conditions.^5 Such dynamics mirror attention markets, where unregulated competition leads to supra-competitive equilibria favoring arousal-maximizing behaviors.^6
