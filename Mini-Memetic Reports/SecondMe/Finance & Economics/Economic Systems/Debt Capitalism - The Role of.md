# Debt Capitalism

Created at 2025/11/19 8:54 AM

**Grok:** Yes, the concept of "debt capitalism" (sometimes stylized as "debt-capitalism") refers to a form of contemporary capitalism where debt serves as the primary mechanism of social control, economic production, and subjectivation, extending creditor-debtor relations across society and replacing traditional capital-labor antagonisms. It critiques how financialization under neoliberalism turns individuals, households, and states into perpetual debtors, enabling governance through austerity, credit, and unpayable obligations.

### Key Thinker: Maurizio Lazzarato

The term is most prominently developed by French philosopher and social theorist **Maurizio Lazzarato** in his 2011 book *The Making of the Indebted Man* (originally *La Fabrique de l'homme endetté*) and expanded in *Governing by Debt* (2015). Lazzarato argues that debt capitalism emerges from the hegemony of finance capital, which subsumes all social relations under a "capitalism of flows" without distinction between finance and the "real" economy. 

https://www.theoryculturesociety.org/blog/review-maurizio-lazzarato-governing-by-debt

Key elements include:

- **Debt as governmentality**: Drawing on Michel Foucault's biopolitics, debt produces neoliberal subjects (*homo economicus*) who internalize responsibility for economic failures, viewing themselves as human capital to be invested in via loans (e.g., student debt in the U.S., where two-thirds of graduates are indebted).
- **Ontological indebtedness**: Debt is infinite and inexpiable, an existential condition that captures subjectivity through apparatuses like credit scoring, taxation, and sovereign debt crises.
- **Historical continuity**: Influenced by Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari's axiomatic view of capitalism, Lazzarato sees finance's dominance as inherent (echoing Lenin's analysis of imperialism), with Fordism as a brief industrial exception.

Lazzarato's work has influenced discussions in autonomist Marxism and post-structuralism, emphasizing refusal of work and exodus from debt relations.

### Other Thinkers and Related Concepts

Several economists, anthropologists, and philosophers have engaged with debt's role in capitalism, often overlapping with Lazzarato's framework:

- **David Graeber**: In *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* (2011), the anthropologist historicizes debt as predating capitalism but critiques its modern weaponization under neoliberalism, where it enforces inequality and moral guilt (echoing Walter Benjamin's 1921 fragment "Capitalism as Religion," which equates capitalism to a cult of Schuld—German for both "guilt" and "debt"). Graeber discussed these ideas with economist **Thomas Piketty** in a 2014 dialogue on debt-fueled inequality.
- **Thomas Piketty**: While focused on wealth inequality in *Capital in the Twenty-First Century* (2013), Piketty links rising private and public debt to capitalist accumulation, arguing it exacerbates r > g (return on capital outpacing growth).
- **Isabelle Guérin et al.**: In *A Women's History of Debt* (forthcoming contexts), they explore "capitalism's sexual division of debt," showing how gendered kinship and sexuality intersect with debt in the Global South.
- **Yuki Tada**: An economist applying Hyman Minsky's financial instability hypothesis to "the paradox of debt," where rising leverage drives capitalist growth but sows supercycles of crisis.
- **Broader influences**: Jacques Lacan (via discourses of the capitalist in austerity), Friedrich Hayek (neoliberal debt ideologies), and Carl Schmitt (taxation as sovereign debt politics) appear in related analyses.

This concept has gained traction in critiques of post-2008 austerity, higher education commodification, and racialized debt (e.g., U.S. student loans as extensions of slavery's legacy). For deeper reading, Lazzarato's texts are foundational.
