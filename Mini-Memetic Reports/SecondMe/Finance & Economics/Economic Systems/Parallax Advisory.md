**Executive Briefing: Escaping the Debt Spiral**
Subtitle: What Every Organization Must Confront About the Global Economic System Before It’s Too Late
**The Situation**
Global debt is rising—dramatically. Japan’s public debt is now 235% of its GDP. The U.S. is at 123%, Italy at 137%. Developing nations, many of them shackled by debt in foreign currencies, face even greater vulnerability.
Governments and institutions continue borrowing just to maintain stability. But this isn’t a short-term imbalance—it’s the result of how money itself is created. Every dollar, yen, or euro enters the world as a loan—with interest attached. That interest compounds over time, and the only way to keep the system running is to issue more debt.
This isn’t mismanagement—it’s structural. And it’s not sustainable.
**Why This Matters to You**
If you’re building a budget, a business model, or a long-term strategy on the assumption that the current economic model will stabilize, you’re operating on a myth.
- Inflation isn’t temporary—it’s a pressure valve for a system designed to overheat.
- Policy responses are reactive—not strategic—and increasingly shaped by bond markets, not democratic mandates.
- Your organization’s future is linked to a system that masks fragility behind complexity and optimism.
- Leadership teams are making plans for the next quarter, while the economic architecture is fracturing beneath them.
