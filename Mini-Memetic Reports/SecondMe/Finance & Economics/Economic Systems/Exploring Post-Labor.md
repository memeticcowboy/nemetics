# Exploring Post-Labor Economics: Automation, Income Shifts, and New Rights Frameworks

Created at 2025/06/20 6:16 PM

[Study Notes: Post-Labor Economics in 8 Minutes](https://youtu.be/BctIQs_nHg4?si=MfyAOvEk62BHH8Nc) - David Shapiro

Main Ideas

	•	Post-Labor Economics: Refers to the significant detachment of GDP growth from wage employment, driven by automation.

	•	Great Decoupling: A term used to describe how increased productivity through automation does not translate into job creation.

	•	Labor Substitution: Transition from human labor to machine labor when machines are more efficient (better, faster, cheaper, safer).

Key Concepts

	•	Economic Agency Paradox: The scenario where replacing a vast workforce with AI leads to lower operating costs but results in decreased consumer purchasing power due to widespread job loss.

	•	Aggregate Demand and Income Sources:

	•	Wages: Currently provide 60-80% of income but are declining.

	•	Property Income: Comes from assets like stocks and rental properties.

	•	Transfers: Government benefits (e.g., Social Security, SNAP) make up a fraction of income but can create dependency on the state.

	•	Property-Based Income Streams:

	•	Universal Basic Income (UBI): A foundation income provided by the government.

	•	Wealth Funds: Government or community-managed funds providing dividends to citizens.

	•	Collective Property: Shared ownership models (e.g., credit unions, DAOs) that manage resources collectively.

	•	Private Wealth: Traditional forms of wealth like stocks and real estate.

	•	Residual Wages: Anticipated remaining employment income, which could be around 20%.

Four Pillars of Civic Society

	•	Civilians: The general population and their rights.

	•	The State: Governmental institutions that should serve the people, though increasingly influenced by corporate interests.

	•	Labor Rights: Essential for maintaining economic power; erosion of these rights is a significant concern with increasing automation.

Algorithmic Rights

	•	Replacement for Labor Rights: As jobs disappear, algorithmic rights can protect individual freedoms and property rights in a technology-driven world.

	•	Key Components:

	•	Data Sovereignty: Control over personal data.

	•	Algorithmic Auditability: Ensuring algorithms operate transparently.

	•	Participatory Algorithmic Governance: Involving citizens in decision-making regarding algorithms.

	•	Algorithmic Dividends: Payments stemming from communal or algorithmically-managed resources.

Technological Foundations

	•	Blockchain Technology: Proposed as a cornerstone for the new social contract due to its:

	•	Democratic Nature: Promotes equal access.

	•	Decentralization: Reduces centralized control.

	•	Unstoppable: Resistant to shutdown.

	•	Permissionless: Open for development without government intervention.

Summary

Post-labor economics outlines a future where automation reduces the need for human labor, leading to significant shifts in income sources and societal power dynamics. With the decline of traditional labor rights, new frameworks like algorithmic rights and decentralized technologies such as blockchain may emerge to foster a more equitable society amidst these changes.
