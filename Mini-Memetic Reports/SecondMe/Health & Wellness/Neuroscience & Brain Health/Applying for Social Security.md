# Applying for Social Security Disability Benefits for Crohn’s Disease in Oregon

Created at 2026/01/14 12:13 PM

Getting on Social Security disability benefits (primarily SSDI or SSI) for Crohn’s disease in Oregon is possible but often challenging, especially since you’ve had the condition for 20 years and it’s worsening, making conventional work unsustainable. Crohn’s is recognized by the Social Security Administration (SSA) as a potentially qualifying condition under the Blue Book Listing 5.06 for Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD), which includes Crohn’s.<br>The process is the same nationwide (handled federally by the SSA, with initial medical decisions made by Oregon’s Disability Determination Services), but approval rates vary. Nationally, many claims like yours are denied initially and require appeals, and experiences show Crohn’s cases can be tough due to the need for strong documentation of severity.<br>Key Eligibility Requirements<br>To qualify, your Crohn’s must prevent substantial gainful activity (SGA — earning roughly $1,690/month or more in 2026 for non-blind individuals) and be expected to last at least 12 months (which is usually straightforward for chronic Crohn’s).<br>You can qualify in one of these ways:<br>	•	Meeting (or equaling) Listing 5.06 — This provides automatic approval if your medical records show documented IBD (via endoscopy, biopsy, imaging, etc.) plus severe complications despite treatment. Criteria include (simplified):<br>	◦	Option A — Bowel obstructions requiring hospitalization/surgery at least twice in a 12-month period (60+ days apart).<br>	◦	Option B — At least two of these in a 12-month period (60+ days apart): severe anemia (hemoglobin <10.0 g/dL), low albumin (≤3.0 g/dL), tender abdominal mass with pain, draining perianal abscess/fistula, or need for daily supplemental nutrition (via feeding tube or IV, not oral/nasal).<br>	◦	Option C — Repeated complications (e.g., abscesses, perforations, infections) averaging 3+ times/year, each lasting 2+ weeks, plus marked limitations in daily activities, social functioning, or concentration/persistence/pace.<br>	•	If you don’t meet the listing — Approval can still happen via a medical-vocational allowance, where the SSA assesses your residual functional capacity (RFC) — what work you can still do (e.g., considering frequent bathroom needs, pain, fatigue, absences). If you’re over age 50 and limited to sedentary/unskilled work (or can’t do any full-time job), approval odds improve.<br>	•	Significant weight loss from Crohn’s might qualify under Listing 5.08 instead.<br>With 20 years of history and worsening symptoms impacting work, gather extensive records: treatment notes, endoscopy/biopsy results, hospitalizations, lab tests (anemia/albumin), doctor statements on limitations (e.g., frequent breaks, absences), and any related issues (e.g., arthritis, fatigue).<br>Difficulty and Approval Rates in Oregon<br>The process is often difficult for Crohn’s/IBD claims because symptoms are episodic and “invisible,” making it hard to prove consistent severity without objective evidence. Initial denials are common nationally (only ~26% approval for Crohn’s primary claims in older data), and many require appeals.<br>In Oregon specifically:<br>	•	Initial applications — Oregon performs well, with approval rates around 44-48% in recent years (often above national average of ~35-39%).<br>	•	Reconsideration (first appeal) — Lower, around 16% (similar to national ~13-15%).<br>	•	Hearing level (before an Administrative Law Judge, after reconsideration denial) — Stronger odds, with Oregon often in the 59-71% range in recent data (among the higher states nationally).<br>Many Crohn’s applicants get denied initially/reconsideration but win at the hearing (which can take 12-18+ months total). Persistence pays off — and hiring a disability attorney (who works on contingency, taking ~25% of backpay if you win, capped by law) significantly boosts chances (they handle evidence, appeals, and hearings).<br>Steps to Apply in Oregon<br>	1	Apply online at ssa.gov, by phone (1-800-772-1213), or at a local SSA office.<br>	2	Expect 3-6+ months for initial decision (Oregon wait times are average/slightly longer).<br>	3	If denied, appeal quickly (60 days) — most success comes later.<br>	4	Consider free help from Oregon disability advocates or attorneys (search “Oregon SSDI attorney” or contact NOSSCR.org).<br>I’m sorry you’re dealing with this flare-up and work challenges after so long — it’s understandably concerning for quality of life. Many with severe Crohn’s do get approved, especially with solid documentation of how it prevents reliable work. If your symptoms include frequent/unpredictable bathroom needs, pain, or malnutrition, emphasize those in your application.
