# Compounds Identified by the Interventions Testing Program That Extend Lifespan in Mice

Created at 2024/09/12 1:27 PM

The video explores the compounds that have been shown to extend lifespan in mice according to the Interventions Testing Program (ITP). The ITP is a rigorous program that has been investigating longevity molecules and their effects on mice for over 20 years.

Key Points

 * The ITP is a rigorous peer-reviewed program for investigating longevity molecules and their effects on mice.

 * It has been running for over 20 years and has tested dozens of different compounds.

 * Over the 20 years, the ITP has identified nine molecules that have extended lifespan in mice.

Molecules That Have Extended Lifespan in Mice

 * Acarbose: A diabetes drug that blocks glucose absorption. It extended median lifespan in male mice by 22% and by only 5% in female mice. Maximum lifespan increased by 11% in males and 9% in females.

 * Aspirin: A non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID). It extended median survival in male mice by 8%. However, in humans, daily intake of aspirin has been linked to a higher risk of all-cause mortality, primarily because of increased cancer-related deaths.

 * Canagliflozin: A diabetes drug that supports kidney function and cardiovascular function. It extended median survival of male mice by 14%.

 * Captopril: An angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor used to treat high blood pressure. It extended median lifespan of female mice by 4-5%.

 * Glycine: An amino acid that supports collagen turnover, glutathione production, creatine synthesis, and heme synthesis. It increased maximum lifespan in both male and female mice by 4-6%.

 * Nordihydroguaiaretic acid (NDGA): A lignan found in creosote bush or greasewood. It resulted in a 10% increase in lifespan in both sexes.

 * Protandim: A herbal supplement that contains a patented mix of five herbal ingredients. It resulted in a 7% increase in median survival of male mice.

 * Rapamycin: A drug used for organ transplantation rejection. It has been shown to have life extension effects in animals for decades. In the ITP studies, it extended lifespan by 14% in females and 9% in males.

 * 17α-estradiol: A non-feminizing estrogen. It increased median male lifespan by 19% and by 11% when fed late in life.

Practical Takeaways

 * Most supplements do not have life extension effects, even in animals.

 * Of the nine molecules that have been shown to extend lifespan in mice, only rapamycin has been shown to have significant life extension effects in humans.

 * Rapamycin is a promising drug that may be able to extend lifespan in humans, but more clinical trials are needed.

 * Other compounds that may have life extension effects in humans include acarbose, canagliflozin, and 17α-estradiol. However, more research is needed to confirm these effects.
