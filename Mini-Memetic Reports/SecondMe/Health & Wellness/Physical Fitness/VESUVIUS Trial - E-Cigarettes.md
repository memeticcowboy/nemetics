# VESUVIUS Trial: E-Cigarettes Improve Vascular Health

Created at 2025/08/16 8:43 AM

Clinical Evidence of Health Improvements

Cardiovascular Benefits

The VESUVIUS trial, published in the Journal of the American College of Cardiology in 2019, provides the most robust clinical evidence of rapid cardiovascular improvements. This randomized controlled trial of 114 chronic smokers found that within just one month of switching from cigarettes to e-cigarettes, participants experienced:

	•	1.5% improvement in vascular function (flow-mediated dilation), statistically significant at p < 0.0001

	•	Reduced arterial stiffness and improved blood vessel health

	•	Greater benefits in female participants across all cardiovascular measurements

	•	No difference between nicotine-containing and nicotine-free e-cigarettes, suggesting the benefits stem from avoiding combustion toxins rather than nicotine cessation

The study noted that these vascular improvements approached levels seen in healthy non-smokers and could translate to a 13% lower risk of cardiovascular events based on previous meta-analyses.

## Insight


**💔 Cardiovascular Benefits of Switching from Tobacco to E-Cigarettes: Insights from the VESUVIUS Trial**

- **Significant Vascular Improvements**: The VESUVIUS trial demonstrated a 1.5% improvement in vascular function (flow-mediated dilation) within one month of chronic smokers switching from tobacco cigarettes to e-cigarettes. This improvement was statistically significant (p < 0.0001) and suggests that e-cigarettes may offer rapid cardiovascular benefits.
  
- **Reduced Arterial Stiffness**: Participants in the trial also experienced reduced arterial stiffness, indicating improved blood vessel health. This is crucial as arterial stiffness is a known risk factor for cardiovascular diseases.

- **Gender-Specific Benefits**: Female participants showed greater cardiovascular improvements compared to males across all measurements. This highlights the potential for targeted smoking cessation strategies that consider gender differences in health outcomes.

**🚭 Study Design and Methodology**
- **Randomized Controlled Trial**: The study involved 114 chronic smokers who were randomized to switch to either nicotine-containing or nicotine-free e-cigarettes for one month. A parallel group continued smoking traditional cigarettes, allowing for a comparative analysis of vascular health outcomes.

- **Assessment Techniques**: Vascular function was assessed using flow-mediated dilation (FMD) and pulse wave velocity (PWV), both validated predictors of cardiovascular risk. Compliance with e-cigarette use was monitored through carbon monoxide levels.

- **Statistical Analysis**: The study employed a propensity score analysis to adjust for differences between randomized and preference arms, ensuring robust results that account for potential biases.

**📊 Key Findings on Vascular Health**
- **Approaching Non-Smoker Levels**: The vascular improvements observed in smokers switching to e-cigarettes approached levels seen in healthy non-smokers, suggesting that e-cigarettes may significantly reduce cardiovascular risks associated with traditional smoking.

- **No Difference Between E-Cigarette Types**: The study found no significant difference in vascular effects between nicotine-containing and nicotine-free e-cigarettes, indicating that the benefits are likely due to the absence of combustion toxins rather than nicotine cessation.

- **Potential for Reduced Cardiovascular Events**: Based on previous meta-analyses, the improvements in FMD could translate to a 13% lower risk of cardiovascular events, emphasizing the importance of switching to e-cigarettes as a harm reduction strategy.

**👩‍⚕️ Implications for Public Health**
- **Harm Reduction Strategy**: The findings support the notion that switching from tobacco to e-cigarettes can be considered a harm reduction measure, particularly for chronic smokers looking to improve their cardiovascular health.

- **Targeted Interventions for Women**: Given the greater benefits observed in female participants, public health initiatives could focus on encouraging women to switch to e-cigarettes as a means of reducing smoking-related health risks.

- **Encouraging Compliance**: The study highlights the importance of compliance in achieving health benefits. Strategies to minimize dual use of e-cigarettes and traditional cigarettes could enhance the effectiveness of smoking cessation efforts.

**🔍 Future Research Directions**
- **Long-Term Effects**: Further research is needed to assess the long-term cardiovascular and non-cardiovascular effects of e-cigarette use, as well as the impact of nicotine on vascular health over extended periods.

- **Diverse E-Cigarette Devices**: Future studies should explore the effects of various e-cigarette devices and formulations to determine the most effective options for smokers transitioning from traditional cigarettes.

- **Broader Population Studies**: Expanding research to include diverse populations will help generalize findings and inform public health policies aimed at reducing smoking prevalence and improving cardiovascular health outcomes.

**📈 Conclusion**
- The VESUVIUS trial provides compelling evidence that switching from tobacco cigarettes to e-cigarettes can lead to significant early improvements in vascular health, particularly for female smokers. This research underscores the potential of e-cigarettes as a less harmful alternative to traditional smoking, warranting further investigation into their long-term health impacts.
