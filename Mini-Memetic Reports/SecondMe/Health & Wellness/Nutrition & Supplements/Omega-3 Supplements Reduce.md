# Omega-3 Supplements Reduce Aggression by 28%: 2024 Study Insights

Created at 2025/01/19 7:03 PM

## Summary

A 2024 meta-analysis of 29 randomized controlled trials involving 3,918 participants found that omega-3 fatty acid supplements can reduce aggression by up to 28%. The study, conducted by researchers from the University of Pennsylvania, showed modest but noticeable short-term effects across various demographics and types of aggression.

**🧠 The Impact of Omega-3 Fatty Acids on Aggression Reduction**

- **Omega-3 Supplementation and Behavior**: Omega-3 fatty acids, commonly found in fish oil, have been shown to influence behavior positively. The 2024 meta-analysis indicates that these supplements can lead to a reduction in aggression levels by up to 28%, suggesting a significant behavioral impact across various demographics.

- **Types of Aggression Addressed**: The study highlights that omega-3 supplementation is effective in reducing both reactive aggression (impulsive responses to provocation) and proactive aggression (premeditated actions). This dual effect broadens the potential applications of omega-3 in behavioral interventions.

- **Demographic Versatility**: The trials included participants from diverse age groups, ranging from children to older adults. This suggests that omega-3 supplementation could be beneficial across different life stages, making it a versatile option for aggression management.

- **Short-Term Effects Observed**: The analysis revealed modest but noticeable short-term effects of omega-3 supplementation on aggression. While the results are promising, further long-term studies are necessary to fully understand the implications.
