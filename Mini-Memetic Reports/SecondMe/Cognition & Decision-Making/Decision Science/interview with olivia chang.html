Out here on the frontier of human choice, the landscape’s changing fast. AI ain’t just some newfangled tool—it’s starting to shape the very way we think, choose, and act. Some say it frees us, others reckon it’s leading us down a path where our decisions ain’t really ours anymore. So the question stands: is AI helping us make better choices, or is it quietly takin’ the reins?

In this nine-part series, we’re sittin’ down with a whole cast of thinkers—executives, ethicists, philosophers, and more—each bringing a different view on what happens when AI steps into the decision-making saddle.

Next up, we’re hearing from Olivia Chang, a high-powered executive who knows a thing or two about decision fatigue. With a workload heavier than a pack mule, she turned to AI to lighten the burden. For Chang, AI ain’t just about efficiency—it’s a way to keep her mind clear for the choices that really matter. She argues that AI can help professionals focus on big-picture thinkin’ while offloading’ the smaller, routine decisions that wear folks down. But she warns that there’s a fine line between convenience and dependence, and if we ain’t careful, we might just find ourselves following AI’s lead without even realizing it.

Memetic Cowboy (MC): Well now, Ms. Chang, I appreciate you taking a moment to sit down and share your story. Folks say you’re one of the sharpest minds in the boardroom, but you’ve been handin’ off more of your decisions to AI these days. What led you down that trail?

Olivia Chang (OC): It started with exhaustion, plain and simple. As an executive, I make hundreds of decisions every day—hiring, resource allocation, strategic planning. It got to the point where even simple choices, like what to eat for lunch, felt overwhelming. That’s classic decision fatigue—when your cognitive resources run low, your choices get worse. Research shows that even judges are more likely to deny parole later in the day simply because they’re mentally drained [1]. I knew I was making suboptimal calls, and I needed a way to offload some of that burden.

MC: So this wasn’t just about efficiency—it was about survival. But I gotta ask, did it trouble you, handing off big decisions? Weren’t you worried about losing your grip on the reins?

OC: At first, yes. There’s always that fear—‘Am I giving up too much control?’ But the truth is, AI doesn’t take away all the decision-making; it just streamlines it. Instead of agonizing over a hundred tiny variables, I let AI filter the noise and present me with the best options. It’s like having a highly competent assistant who never gets tired or emotional.

And the result? Clarity. AI helps me focus my mental energy on the decisions that actually matter—the creative, strategic, high-stakes choices that define leadership. Research shows that delegating routine decisions to AI preserves cognitive resources for deeper thinking, allowing professionals to operate at their best [2]."

MC: Alright, so it ain’t about surrender—it’s about freein’ up your mind for the real heavy liftin’. But do you ever feel like you’re losin’ a sense of ownership? Like the choices don’t quite feel like yours anymore?

OC: That’s an interesting point. There is a psychological shift when you start outsourcing decisions. At first, I felt a bit detached—almost like I was watching my life play out from the outside. But over time, I realized that what I gained was perspective. When you’re overwhelmed, you react instinctively, often falling back on biases. AI helps me step back and make more rational, well-informed choices [3].

Of course, it’s important to set clear boundaries. I use AI for data-driven decisions—financial modeling, hiring analytics, scheduling—but I don’t let it dictate personal choices or high-level vision. That’s where human judgment, emotion, and instinct still matter.

MC: Boundaries, huh? Now that’s the rub, ain’t it? Where do you draw the line between convenience and dependence? What’s fine to hand off, and what’s gotta stay human?

OC: For me, the line is simple: AI is great for optimizing, but it shouldn’t replace intuition where human nuance is key. Scheduling meetings? AI. Predicting market trends? AI. Deciding how to handle a delicate personnel issue? That’s still on me.

This is backed by research—people tend to trust AI for structured, analytical tasks but resist it for emotionally complex decisions [3]. And there’s a good reason for that. AI struggles with things like ethical gray areas, conflicting values, and the subconscious factors that drive human intuition [4].

MC: You’re making AI sound like a trusty saddlebag—useful, but it ain’t riding the horse for ya. But what about the folks who say too much outsourcing makes folks soft? That it dulls our decision-making edge over time?

OC: There’s truth to that concern. Studies show that frequent AI reliance correlates with weaker critical thinking skills, especially among younger professionals [5]. The more we let AI handle decisions, the less we engage in deep, reflective thought. That’s why I think AI should be a partner, not a replacement—it should help us think better, not less.

MC: That’s a fine balance to strike. But tell me, do you think this AI decision-making shift is just for high-powered folks like yourself, or could it help the average person too?

OC: It’s already happening at every level of society. From AI-curated news feeds to algorithmic financial planning, people are outsourcing decisions all the time—often without realizing it. The key is to do it consciously. If we’re not careful, AI could nudge us toward a world where we don’t own our choices—we just follow recommendations without questioning them [6].

MC: And that’s a mighty fine point to end on. Ms. Chang, I thank ya kindly for sharing your insights. You’ve shown us that decision-makin’ ain’t just about the choices we make—it’s about who does the choosin’. And that’s a trail we best keep our eyes on. Much obliged for riding this trail with me. You’ve given us plenty to chew on—how AI can ease the burden of decision-making, but also how it might shape our choices in ways we don’t even notice. Seems to me the trick ain’t just in what we delegate, but in staying sharp enough to know when to take the reins back. Mighty fine wisdom, that.

But if today’s talk was about makin’ room for the choices that matter, next time, we’re heading into a whole different kind of dust storm. We’ll be sittin’ down with Max Holloway, a philosopher who reckons we ain’t really makin’ choices at all. If our thoughts and actions are just the echoes of subconscious patterns, then maybe AI ain’t taking nothing from us—it’s just showing us what was always there. So saddle up, folks. Next stop, we’re ridin’ straight into the heart of free will—if such a thing even exists." So stick around. The trail ahead’s only getting stranger.
