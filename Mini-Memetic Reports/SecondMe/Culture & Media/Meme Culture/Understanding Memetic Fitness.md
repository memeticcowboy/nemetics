# Understanding Memetic Fitness: Factors Influencing Meme Survival and Spread

Created at 2025/02/28 12:47 PM

### **Elaborating on Memetic Fitness Factors**

Memetic fitness refers to a meme's ability to survive, spread, and influence minds within a given cultural and informational ecosystem. This fitness depends on both internal attributes of the meme and external environmental factors that determine whether it will be assimilated, retained, expressed, and transmitted.

***

## **1. The Four-Stage Replication Model**

A meme's success can be mathematically represented as:

F(m)=A(m)∗R(m)∗E(m)∗T(m)F(m) = A(m) * R(m) * E(m) * T(m)

Where:

- **Assimilation (A)**: The likelihood that an individual understands and accepts the meme.
- **Retention (R)**: The likelihood that the meme remains in memory or stored in some medium.
- **Expression (E)**: The likelihood that the individual expresses or acts upon the meme.
- **Transmission (T)**: The likelihood that the meme is passed on to others.

If any of these factors are zero, the meme fails to replicate. For example:

- A complex or incoherent meme may have low assimilation.
- A meme that is uninteresting or irrelevant may not be retained.
- A meme that is socially unacceptable may not be expressed.
- A meme without a suitable transmission channel (e.g., social media, word of mouth) may not spread.

***

## **2. Criteria Affecting Memetic Fitness**

Memetic fitness is determined by several interacting factors, divided into objective, subjective, intersubjective, and meme-centered criteria.

### **A. Objective Criteria (Predictive Power)**

Objective fitness relates to how well a meme aligns with observable reality and predicts future events.

- **Scientific Memes**: Memes that help explain phenomena (e.g., "evolution by natural selection") tend to survive if they consistently produce accurate predictions.
- **Conspiratorial Memes**: Even if incorrect, conspiracy theories thrive by providing a seemingly coherent explanation for unpredictable events.

Memes with high predictive power tend to be retained longer and gain credibility.

### **B. Subjective Criteria (Individual Acceptance)**

These factors influence whether an individual accepts a meme based on cognitive and emotional alignment:

- **Coherence**: The meme must fit within an individual's existing mental models.
- **Novelty**: People are drawn to new and surprising ideas.
- **Simplicity**: Simple memes spread more easily (e.g., "Just Do It" vs. complex philosophical arguments).
- **Individual Utility**: If a meme offers personal benefit (e.g., productivity hacks), people are more likely to retain and transmit it.

### **C. Intersubjective Criteria (Social Transmission)**

Some memes spread because they are highly communicable between individuals:

- **Salience**: The meme must be noticeable and memorable.
- **Expressivity**: Easily articulated memes (e.g., catchy slogans, memes with visuals) spread faster.
- **Infectiveness**: Emotional impact—memes that evoke strong feelings (fear, humor, outrage) have a higher transmission rate.

### **D. Meme-Centered Criteria (Self-Sustaining Properties)**

Certain internal features of memes make them self-perpetuating:

- **Self-justification**: The meme provides its own defense (e.g., "If you question this idea, you're part of the problem").
- **Self-reinforcement**: The meme builds networks of interconnected beliefs that make it harder to reject (e.g., religious or ideological belief systems).
- **Intolerance**: Some memes discourage competing ideas, strengthening their hold (e.g., "All dissenters are enemies").
- **Proselytism**: Some memes encourage active spreading (e.g., "Share this to 10 people or bad luck follows").

***

## **3. Additional Factors Enhancing Memetic Fitness**

Beyond the core selection criteria, additional dynamics enhance meme survivability and virality.

### **A. Fecundity, Copying-Fidelity, and Longevity**

- **Fecundity**: A meme’s ability to generate many copies quickly (e.g., viral tweets, chain emails).
- **Copying-Fidelity**: The degree to which a meme remains intact across replications (e.g., religious scriptures tend to be carefully preserved, while internet memes mutate rapidly).
- **Longevity**: Memes that persist across generations (e.g., national anthems, folktales) have high cultural endurance.

### **B. Ease of Reproduction**

- Simple, easy-to-understand memes replicate better than complex ones.
- Short, visual, and symbolic memes (e.g., emojis, GIFs) have a higher reproduction rate.

### **C. Adaptability (Meme Flexibility)**

- A meme that can be adapted to different contexts spreads more easily.
- Example: The "Keep Calm and Carry On" meme has been modified countless times (e.g., "Keep Calm and Drink Coffee").

### **D. Exploiting Human Motivators**

Memes leverage fundamental human emotions to spread:

- **Fear**: "If you don’t believe this, bad things will happen" (e.g., urban legends, scare tactics).
- **Hope & Reward**: "If you share this, good things will happen" (e.g., lottery scams, prosperity gospel).
- **Tribalism**: "We are the good guys; they are the bad guys" (e.g., political and religious dogma).

### **E. "Hooks" (Intrinsic Rewards)**

Memes that provide immediate psychological satisfaction are more likely to spread:

- **Humor**: Funny memes travel faster because laughter encourages sharing.
- **Novelty**: Unusual or surprising memes capture attention.
- **Feelings of Superiority**: Memes that make people feel smart or morally superior are retained longer.
- **Usefulness**: Practical memes (e.g., "Life hacks") are more likely to be adopted.

### **F. Fitness of Channel and Network**

A meme’s survival depends on the transmission infrastructure:

- **Information Capacity**: A meme’s complexity must match the medium (e.g., Twitter favors short memes).
- **Signal-to-Noise Ratio**: If a meme is drowned in noise, it may fail to spread.
- **Network Effects**: Platforms with high user engagement (e.g., TikTok, Reddit) amplify meme reach.

### **G. Plausibility and Credibility**

- Memes that align with people's expectations are more likely to be accepted.
- A meme that "feels true" can be more powerful than one that is objectively correct (e.g., fake news, pseudoscience).

### **H. Dogmatism and Viral Resistance**

- Some memes gain strength by discouraging questioning (e.g., cult doctrines, political extremism).
- High dogmatism can create self-replicating belief systems that resist counter-memes.

***

## **4. Implications: Memes as Independent Replicators**

Memes operate **independently of morality, truth, or human well-being**:

- **Survival over accuracy**: A false but emotionally compelling meme can outcompete a rational but dry one.
- **Viral contagion**: Harmful memes (e.g., conspiracy theories, disinformation) can propagate if they exploit cognitive biases.
- **Memetic warfare**: Governments, corporations, and ideologies use memes strategically to manipulate public opinion.

### **Conclusion**

Memetic fitness is driven by cognitive, social, and technological factors that determine how well an idea replicates and persists. Some memes survive because they are useful, while others survive purely due to their viral properties. As digital and AI technologies evolve, memetic engineering will become an even more powerful tool for shaping thought, behavior, and society.
