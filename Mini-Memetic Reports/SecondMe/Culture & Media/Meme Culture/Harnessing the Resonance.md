# Harnessing the Resonance Model for Effective Communication

The Resonance Model

Description
When you are seeking to persuade or communicate something, rather than tell them what they need to know, try and draw it out of them. For example:

Find and used similar experiences they have already had.
Talk about the sensory elements of experience (speech, vision, hearing, etc.)
Use metaphor and analogy.
Use questioning methods, particularly Socratic questioning.
Ask them to extend their current thinking outwards.
In particular, seek to use their models ways of thinking rather than trying to impose your models and ways of thinking.

Example
So the last Volvo you drove lasted a long time. Well remember that comfortable reliability and imagine if you could have a car that lasted twice as long and that half as many problems. How would that feel now?

Discussion
When inferring meaning and developing new learning, we build on existing models, basing new understanding on current edifices rather than constructing new ideas from scratch. The Resonance communication model takes the practical step of suggesting that communications should align with this learning principle and, rather than taking the more difficult route of seeking to transfer a carbon copy of our own understanding.

Experiences are not stored as symbols and so symbolic cueing is not the best way to evoke them. The close linkage with feelings means that more emotional evocative methods will be more successful.

The Resonance Model is also known as the Evoked Recall model.

**🗣️ The Resonance Model: Enhancing Communication through Connection**

- **Understanding the Resonance Model**: The Resonance Model emphasizes the importance of drawing out information from others rather than simply telling them what they need to know. This approach fosters a more engaging and effective communication process.
  - By utilizing similar past experiences, communicators can create a relatable context that resonates with the audience.
  - Engaging sensory elements (sight, sound, etc.) enhances the emotional connection, making the communication more impactful.
  - The model encourages the use of metaphors and analogies to simplify complex ideas and make them more relatable.

- **Effective Communication Techniques**: The model suggests various techniques to enhance communication effectiveness.
  - Socratic questioning is highlighted as a method to stimulate critical thinking and encourage deeper understanding.
  - Asking individuals to extend their current thinking helps them explore new ideas while feeling in control of the conversation.
  - Utilizing the audience's existing mental models rather than imposing new ones fosters a collaborative learning environment.

- **Emotional Engagement in Communication**: The Resonance Model underscores the significance of emotional connection in communication.
  - Experiences are better recalled through emotional cues rather than symbolic representations, making emotional engagement crucial.
  - By tapping into feelings, communicators can evoke stronger responses and facilitate better understanding.
  - The model suggests that emotional methods are more successful in conveying messages and persuading audiences.

- **Practical Application Example**: A practical example illustrates the model's effectiveness in real-life scenarios.
  - The example of recalling a reliable car experience (like a Volvo) helps the audience visualize and emotionally connect with the idea of an even better vehicle.
  - This technique not only makes the message relatable but also encourages the audience to imagine the benefits, enhancing persuasion.
  - Such examples demonstrate how effective communication can lead to a deeper understanding and acceptance of new ideas.

- **Building on Existing Knowledge**: The model advocates for building new understanding on existing knowledge frameworks.
  - New ideas should be constructed upon current knowledge rather than starting from scratch, making learning more accessible.
  - This approach aligns with cognitive learning principles, facilitating smoother transitions to new concepts.
  - By leveraging existing models, communicators can enhance the audience's ability to grasp and retain new information.

- **The Role of Rapport in Communication**: Building rapport is essential for effective communication as per the Resonance Model.
  - Establishing a connection with the audience creates a safe space for open dialogue and exploration of ideas.
  - Rapport enhances trust, making the audience more receptive to new information and perspectives.
  - The model suggests that effective communication is not just about the message but also about the relationship between the communicator and the audience.

- **Theoretical Foundations**: The Resonance Model is rooted in established communication theories and practices.

  - The model is also known as the Evoked Recall model, highlighting its focus on memory and emotional connection.
