**🎨 Prefigurative Community Building: Art as a Tool for Social Change**

- **Embodiment of Future Values**: Prefigurative community building emphasizes living out the ideals of a desired future society in the present. This approach encourages individuals and groups to adopt values such as inclusivity, sustainability, and grassroots participation, creating a foundation for a more equitable future.

- **Community Building through Art**: The creation of intentional communities that reflect desired social structures is central to this concept. Public art collaboratives serve as a means to reclaim public spaces, allowing communities to express their identities and stories collectively, rather than through commodified art forms.

- **Grassroots Activism**: Engaging in bottom-up, participatory approaches to social change is vital. By fostering collaboration and mutual respect, communities can activate art as a living commons, resisting the commodification of culture and space.

**🤝 Steps to Start a Public Art Collaborative**

- **Root in Relationship, Not Aesthetic**: The first step involves forming a diverse group of individuals who share a common goal of storytelling and community engagement. This process prioritizes the narratives that need to be told over the visual aesthetics of the art itself.

- **Set Collaborative Agreements**: Establishing clear agreements on decision-making processes and conflict resolution is crucial. This ensures that the collaborative effort embodies the principles of Power With, promoting shared authorship and collective responsibility.

- **Identify and Reclaim the Site**: Choosing meaningful locations for public art projects is essential. Collaborating with local residents to understand the significance of these spaces can inspire the medium and message of the artwork.

**🎉 Co-Creation and Community Engagement**

- **Build a Participatory Design Process**: Engaging community members through story circles and oral history sessions allows for a rich exchange of memories and ideas. This participatory approach ensures that the art reflects the community's collective identity and aspirations.

- **Co-create the Work—No Spectators**: The process of creating the artwork should be as communal as the planning phase. Involving community members in the actual making of the art fosters a sense of ownership and connection to the project.

- **Host a Public Unveiling as a Community Event**: Rather than a formal unveiling, hosting a celebratory event encourages community participation and storytelling. This approach transforms the unveiling into a collective celebration of the community's voice and creativity.

**🔄 Sustaining Community Art Projects**

- **Leave an Open Thread**: Designing projects to remain open-ended allows for future contributions and adaptations. This ongoing engagement keeps the art alive and relevant, reflecting the evolving stories of the community.

- **Power With, Not Power Over**: Emphasizing collective decision-making and accessibility ensures that the process is inclusive. This approach actively challenges traditional power dynamics in art creation, fostering a more equitable environment for all participants.

**🌍 Real-World Examples of Power With Public Art Projects**

- **Trust Your Struggle Collective**: This decentralized group focuses on co-creating murals that address social justice issues, emphasizing deep relationships and collective authorship in their projects.

- **Spiral Q**: Based in Philadelphia, Spiral Q utilizes community workshops and puppet-making to amplify marginalized voices, deeply rooted in mutual empowerment and community engagement.

**🖌️ Conclusion: Art as a Radical Act**

- **Every Wall Is a Canvas**: Public art created through collaborative efforts is not merely about beautification; it serves as a powerful tool for reclaiming voice and memory. By engaging in collective creation, communities can resist commodification and assert their narratives in public spaces.

- **Imagining a Better Future**: The act of creating art together is a radical expression of hope and resistance. It allows communities to envision and work towards the world they desire, transforming public spaces into platforms for joy, grief, and collective memory.
