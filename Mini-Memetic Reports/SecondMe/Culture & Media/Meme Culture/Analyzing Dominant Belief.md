# Analyzing Dominant Belief Systems on X: Trends in Populism, Conspiracies, and Financial Ideologies

Created at 2025/05/19 6:27 AM

Determining the "strongest belief system" based on X posts is tricky since the platform hosts a chaotic mix of ideologies, and no single belief system dominates universally. However, certain trends stand out due to their volume, engagement, and persistence, reflecting what captures attention right now. Based on recent activity and the nature of X’s algorithm, which amplifies emotionally charged and polarizing content, here’s what seems to hold significant sway:
1. **Populist and Anti-Establishment Sentiments**: A strong undercurrent on X revolves around distrust in institutions—governments, media, corporations, and global organizations. This manifests in posts railing against "elites," "globalists," or "the system." These ideas often blend with nationalist or populist rhetoric, resonating across both left and right. For example, posts echoing distrust in electoral processes or corporate overreach (like Big Tech or Big Pharma) consistently rack up high engagement, especially when tied to figures like Elon Musk or Donald Trump. This aligns with studies noting X’s role in amplifying narratives of systemic unfairness.[](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Believability-of-USA-Today-by-Year_fig1_355858181)
2. **Conspiracy-Driven Narratives**: Conspiracy theories, particularly those tied to political events, maintain a robust presence. Beliefs around election fraud, "deep state" machinations, or global cabals (e.g., QAnon-adjacent ideas) persist, especially among right-leaning users. A 2023 study found that negative emotional valence, like outrage over alleged voter fraud, drives retweet frequency, suggesting these ideas spread because they hit emotional triggers. QAnon’s core tenets, like distrust in a "Satan-worshipping elite," still linger, with 28% of Republicans in a 2021 survey agreeing with some of its claims.[](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-023-02106-x)[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QAnon)
3. **Right-Wing Political Ideology**: Since mid-2024, X has seen a tilt toward right-wing voices, boosted by algorithmic changes post-July 13, 2024, when Musk endorsed Trump. Posts from Republican-leaning accounts have gained disproportionate visibility, with view counts spiking significantly compared to Democrat-leaning ones. This suggests a belief system rooted in conservative values—pro-Trump, anti-immigration, and skeptical of progressive policies—has a structural edge on the platform. The Center for Countering Digital Hate noted Musk’s pro-Trump posts alone garnered 1.7 billion views, dwarfing other political ads.[](https://www.euronews.com/next/2024/11/06/musks-x-sees-record-usage-during-us-election-yet-study-claims-he-boosted-the-algorithm)[](https://www.theregister.com/2024/11/20/x_marks_the_spot_for/)
4. **Crypto and Financial Freedom**: Belief in decentralized finance, cryptocurrencies, and economic self-sovereignty is another loud voice. Posts hyping Bitcoin, meme coins, or DeFi as hedges against fiat currency or government control are pervasive, often tied to libertarian ideals. These gain traction in communities skeptical of central banks and traditional finance, amplified by influencers with massive followings.
5. **Self-Optimization and Hustle Culture**: On a less political note, X is flooded with posts pushing personal growth, fitness, and financial success. This belief system—rooted in self-reliance, discipline, and "grinding"—is less ideological but hugely visible, driven by motivational influencers and crypto bros. It’s less about community and more about individual triumph, resonating with younger, male-dominated audiences.
**Why These Stand Out**: X’s algorithm favors engagement over nuance, so belief systems that provoke strong emotions—anger, fear, or aspiration—rise to the top. The platform’s shift under Musk has also dialed up visibility for right-leaning and anti-establishment content, as seen in studies showing a 138% view increase for Musk’s posts post-endorsement. Negative or outrage-driven posts, like those fueling conspiracy theories, spread faster due to content bias.[](https://www.techpolicy.press/new-research-points-to-possible-algorithmic-bias-on-x/)[](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-023-02106-x)
**Caveats**: No single belief system "wins" on X—it’s a fractured space where echo chambers thrive. Left-leaning beliefs (e.g., climate activism, social justice) still exist but seem less viral, possibly due to algorithmic bias or user migration to platforms like Bluesky. Also, X isn’t a perfect mirror of society; it’s skewed toward vocal minorities and outrage porn, so its trends overrepresent certain voices.[](https://www.theregister.com/2024/11/20/x_marks_the_spot_for/)
