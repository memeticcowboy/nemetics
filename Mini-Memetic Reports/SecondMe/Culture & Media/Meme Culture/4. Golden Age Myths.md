**4. Golden Age Myths (Idealized Past Eras)**

Example A (Right-Wing): “1950s family values and national pride”
Example B (Left-Wing): “Pre-colonial harmony and communal living”

**R –** 
**Recognize:**

Golden age myths evoke nostalgia, loss, and moral superiority of a past era—usually a selective reconstruction filtered through current needs.

**E –** 
**Examine:**

Unpack what was omitted. The 1950s had segregation, gender roles, and nuclear anxiety. Pre-colonial life was diverse, sometimes violent, not uniformly idyllic.

**V –** 
**Validate:**

Compare myth to data. Was well-being higher? For whom? Is the remembered past a composite of curated memory?

**E –** 
**Expose:**

Show how golden age myths encode desires for simplicity, order, or justice. They often project present dissatisfaction backward into idealized memory.

**A –** 
**Alternatives:**

Replace nostalgia with reflective synthesis: What values were worth preserving, and what must be transformed? Can we evolve the spirit of the age, not its surface?

**L –** 
**Literate:**

Teach myth-detection in nostalgic language. Help people interrogate their own longing and its memetic roots.
