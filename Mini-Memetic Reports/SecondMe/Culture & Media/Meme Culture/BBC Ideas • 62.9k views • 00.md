	•	Role of Metaphors: Metaphors significantly influence our beliefs, attitudes, and actions, often without our awareness. They can simplify complex ideas, typically framed as “X equals Y.”
	•	Literary Examples: In The Great Gatsby, the metaphor of rowers battling a current intertwines themes of time, nostalgia, and the past.
	•	Philosophical Perspectives: Philosopher Thomas Hobbes criticized metaphors, equating their use to deception. However, they are essential for conveying abstract concepts, like heartbreak.
	•	Familiar Metaphors: Common metaphors, such as equating time with money, reflect societal values. Linguist George Lakoff identified such comparisons as “orientational metaphors.”
	•	Political Implications: The language we use can shape public perception and influence social issues, framing what is accepted as common sense.
	•	Impact on Health: A 2019 study found that military metaphors in discussing cancer treatment could foster fatalism and blame among patients.
	•	Crime Metaphors Study: A 2011 Stanford study demonstrated that framing crime as a “wild beast” led to support for stricter policing measures compared to framing it as a “virus.”
	•	Conclusion: The choice of metaphors has significant implications for societal attitudes and responses, highlighting their power in shaping our understanding of various issues
