# Understanding the 4-H Pledge: History, Variations, and Significance

Created at 2025/01/16 11:04 AM

[via Bob-RJ/X](https://x.com/BurkhartRj/status/1879966865872838922)

The 4-H pledge is:

> I pledge my head to clearer thinking,<br>my heart to greater loyalty,<br>my hands to larger service,<br>and my health to better living,<br>for my club, my community, my country, and my world.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-H#cite_note-24)

Otis E. Hall wrote the original pledge of [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas "Kansas") in 1918. Some [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California") 4-H clubs add either "As a true 4-H member" or "As a loyal 4-H member" at the beginning of the pledge. [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota "Minnesota") and [Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine "Maine") 4-H clubs add "for my family" to the last line of the pledge.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-H#cite_note-25) Initially, the pledge ended in "and my country," but in 1973, "and my world" was added.

<br>It is a common practice to involve hand motions to accompany these spoken words. While reciting the first line of the pledge, the speaker points to their head with both of their hands. As the speaker recites the second line, they place their right hand over their heart much like during the [Pledge of Allegiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pledge_of_Allegiance_(United_States) "Pledge of Allegiance (United States)"). For the third line, the speaker presents their hands, palm side up, before them. For the fourth line, the speaker motions to their body down their sides. For the final line, the speaker usually places their right hand out for the club, left hand for the community, brings them together for the country, and then brings their hands upwards in a circle representing the world.
