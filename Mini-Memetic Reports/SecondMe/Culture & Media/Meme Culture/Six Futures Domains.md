# Six Futures Domains: NeuroScape-Navigation Wheel

Created at 2025/07/14 7:37 AM

**🧭** 

**The Six Futures Domains (Labeled 1–6)**



**1️⃣** 

**None**

 **–**


Futures that are neither preferable, possible, nor probable. These might be dystopian dead ends or discarded what-ifs. Symbolized with broken geometric forms and lack of directionality.


**2️⃣** 

**Preferable**

 **–**


Aspirational visions: what we want to happen. These futures reflect values, desires, or visions of a better world, though they might not be realistic or likely without intervention.


**3️⃣** 

**Probable**

 **–**


Based on trends, trajectories, and extrapolation. These are the most likely to occur given current patterns. Often the default path if no interventions occur.


**4️⃣** 

**Possible**

 **–**


Futures that are imaginable, even if not likely or desired. This domain is the playground of speculation, foresight, and #MemeticEngineering.


**5️⃣** 

**(implied but not explicitly numbered)**

 **–**


Combinations of the above states:

- Both Preferable & Probable
- Both Possible & Probable
- Both Preferable & Possible<br>These hybrid zones are strategic sweet spots, often where #ScenarioPlanning and #Backcasting are most useful.



**6️⃣** 

**All**

 **–**


A comprehensive zone that integrates all dimensions: preferable, probable, possible, and even “none” as outliers. This represents the systems-level synthesis, likely for strategic navigation and decision support.




**🧠** 

**Memetic Markers**


The inclusion of iconography (e.g., flags, geometric cues, cognitive symbols) acts as attention-directing memes—anchoring abstract futures to culturally or emotionally salient cues.


**Example:**


The South African flag pin appears three times to mark the overlap areas:

- Preferable & Probable
- Preferable & Possible
- Possible & Probable<br>This suggests an emphasis on futures that are ethically desirable AND realistically actionable—a very SMART-meme-aligned choice.





**🧠** 

**NeuroScape-Navigation**


The term hints at:

- Cognitive-emotional landscape mapping
- Scenario awareness as neural navigation
- Possibly interactive futures simulation via thought experiments, prompts, or embodiment practices.


This aligns with frameworks like:

- OODA Loops (Observe-Orient-Decide-Act)
- Futures Wheels
- Implications mapping
- And even Extended Mind models of cognition (#TranshumanFutures)





**🔄** 

**Application**


This wheel could be used in:

- Strategic foresight workshops
- Memetic sensemaking
- Scenario simulation
- Education for anticipatory intelligence
- Ethical AI alignment via mapping value-congruent futures





**🔮 Synthesis**


This is not just a diagram—it’s a strategic cognition engine.

It helps navigate the chaoscape of emergent futures by labeling and tracking:

- What’s imaginable but not likely (Possible)
- What’s likely but not necessarily desirable (Probable)
- What’s desirable, but might not happen without intention (Preferable)
- What futures lie in the overlap of vision and viability


🧠 In that sense, it’s a memetic cartography tool—helping individuals, groups, or organizations map where they are—and where they could go—across temporal and ideological terrain.

## Insight


*  The "Alt-Futures Implications Wheel (NeuroScape-Navigation)" serves as a strategic tool for navigating potential future scenarios. It categorizes futures into six domains: None, Preferable, Probable, Possible, combinations of these, and All, providing a framework for strategic foresight and decision-making.
*  The wheel incorporates "Memetic Markers," such as the South African flag, to anchor abstract futures to culturally relevant cues. The flag's presence in the "Preferable & Probable," "Preferable & Possible," and "Possible & Probable" zones suggests a focus on ethically desirable and realistically actionable futures, aligning with SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) goals.
*  The term "NeuroScape-Navigation" implies a cognitive and emotional mapping of future scenarios, suggesting that understanding and navigating futures involves both rational analysis and emotional intelligence. This concept aligns with frameworks like OODA Loops and Futures Wheels, which emphasize the importance of observation, orientation, decision-making, and action in uncertain environments.
*  The wheel's application extends to various fields, including strategic foresight workshops, memetic sensemaking, scenario simulation, and ethical AI alignment. By mapping value-congruent futures, the wheel can help individuals and organizations make informed decisions that align with their values and goals.
*  The "None" category represents dystopian or discarded futures, highlighting the importance of considering undesirable outcomes in foresight exercises. This category serves as a reminder that not all possible futures are desirable and that proactive measures may be needed to avoid negative scenarios.
*  The "All" category represents a comprehensive, systems-level synthesis of all dimensions of the future, including preferable, probable, possible, and even "none" as outliers. This category emphasizes the interconnectedness of different future scenarios and the need for a holistic approach to strategic navigation and decision support.
*  The wheel's design encourages users to consider the interplay between different future domains, such as the overlap between preferable and probable futures. These hybrid zones represent strategic sweet spots where scenario planning and backcasting can be most effective in shaping desired outcomes.
*  The wheel can be used as a "memetic cartography tool" to help individuals, groups, or organizations map their current position and potential future trajectories across temporal and ideological landscapes. This mapping process can facilitate a deeper understanding of the challenges and opportunities that lie ahead, enabling more effective strategic planning and decision-making.
