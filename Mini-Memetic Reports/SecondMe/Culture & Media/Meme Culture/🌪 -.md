**2. Crisis Narratives (Explaining Social Problems)**
Example A (Right-Wing): “Crime and immigration are destroying society”\
Example B (Left-Wing): “Capitalism is inherently producing inequality and ecological collapse”
**R –** \
**Recognize:**
Identify tropes of chaos, loss of control, enemy infiltration, or system decay. Crisis myths often invoke urgency and moral breakdown.
**E –** \
**Examine:**
Trace the timeline: When did the “crisis” begin? What structural factors played roles? Are the crises ongoing or selectively highlighted?
**V –** \
**Validate:**
Cross-check data. Are immigration rates correlated with crime? Is inequality increasing, and by which metrics? Are ecological indicators worsening?
**E –** \
**Expose:**
Uncover how fear operates memetically. Right-leaning versions often use crisis to justify surveillance or exclusion; left-leaning ones to promote systemic overhaul. Both frame urgency to accelerate action.
**A –** \
**Alternatives:**
Offer complex systemic models: Economic dislocation + media panic + institutional decay = multifaceted cause. Not a single villain, but entangled roots.
**L –** \
**Literate:**
Help readers see how crisis memes override nuance—teaching how emotional contagion shapes policy and tribal alignment.
