**1. Foundation Myths (National Origins)**

Example A (Right-Wing): The Founding Fathers as divine architects of freedom
Example B (Left-Wing): Indigenous or working-class movements as the “true” origin of national integrity

**R –** 
**Recognize:**

Look for mythic markers: sacred origin stories, archetypal heroes, divine sanction, purity of vision. Founding myths often feature heroic struggle, moral clarity, and intentional destiny.

**E –** 
**Examine:**

Analyze historical complexity. Was the founding moment as unified as portrayed? Which groups were included/excluded? What tensions were hidden beneath the myth?

**V –** 
**Validate:**

Fact-check symbolic claims: Did the Founders fight for universal freedom, or for property and racialized hierarchies? Was the labor or indigenous origin story inclusive or selective?

**E –** 
**Expose:**

Reveal ideological undercurrents: Right-leaning versions often justify current political structures as “original intent,” while left-leaning versions may anchor radical legitimacy in “real people’s” origins.

**A –** 
**Alternatives:**

Offer plural narratives: Consider multi-foundational views—layering settler, enslaved, immigrant, and indigenous experiences as parallel origin codes.

**L –** 
**Literate:**

Teach how to recognize founding myths as memetic tools of legitimation—used to sanctify current policies by appealing to ancestral echoes.
