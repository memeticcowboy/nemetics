# Exploring Personal Identity Through the Cultural Watershed Metaphor

Created at 2025/04/10 3:15 PM

[X/Change w/ Bob-RJ](https://x.com/BurkhartRj/status/1910074448583287076)<br>**Identity within Cultural Watershed**<br><br>A geographical watershed is defined as an area of land that drains into a single river or body of water, representing a system where diverse sources (e.g., streams, rivers) converge to form a unified flow. In the metaphorical sense, "cultural watershed" applies this concept to personal identity, suggesting that various cultural factors converge to shape an individual's unique identity and life course.

Research suggests this metaphor was introduced by Running-Grass, director of the Three Circles Center for Multicultural Environmental Education, as a tool for educational activities. It is detailed in resources such as [Three Circles: Cultural Watershed](https://threecircles.org/cultural-watershed/) and [Cultural Watersheds: Diagramming One’s Own Experience of Culture](http://www.socialstudies.org/sites/default/files/publications/yl/1202/120203.html), which describe its use in helping students reflect on their cultural identities.

#### Detailed Analysis of the Metaphor

The "cultural watershed" metaphor works by likening the formation of personal identity to the dynamics of a watershed:

- **Cultural Elements as Streams**: Personal identity is seen as composed of various cultural factors, such as:
    - Family origin and structure.
    - Ethnicity and race.
    - Socioeconomic status.
    - Gender and sexual orientation.
    - Spirituality, religion, and language.
    - Age, abilities, and handicapping conditions.
- These factors are compared to streams and rivers that feed into a watershed, each contributing to the overall flow. For instance, [Cultural Watersheds: Diagramming One’s Own Experience of Culture](http://www.socialstudies.org/sites/default/files/publications/yl/1202/120203.html) explains: "The goal of this assignment is for you to create a mural in which a watershed (its land forms and the movement of water upon it) is used as a metaphor for your cultural identity (in all its various aspects) and the course of your life."
- **Convergence and Interaction**: Just as a watershed collects water from diverse sources and directs it towards a common point, an individual's cultural identity is formed by the convergence of these diverse cultural influences. The metaphor highlights how these elements interact, shaping the individual's life path. For example, [Three Circles: Cultural Watershed](https://threecircles.org/cultural-watershed/) notes: "The activity also opens the space for critical dialogue on how people have experienced various and intersecting oppressions and how those have shaped their personal identities."
- **Dynamic and Reflective Nature**: The watershed metaphor emphasizes that identity is not static but dynamic, constantly shaped by the interaction of cultural factors, much like how water in a watershed is influenced by the landscape it flows through. [ERIC - EJ631618 - Cultural Watersheds: Diagramming One's Own Experience of Culture.](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ631618) states: "In-service teachers created murals in which a watershed (its landforms and the movement of water upon it) is used as a metaphor for one's cultural identity and life course," underscoring its use for reflection.

#### Reasons for Using the Watershed Metaphor

The choice of the watershed as a metaphor is deliberate, with several reasons identified:

- **Ubiquity**: A watershed is an environmental phenomenon that is always present, though often unnoticed, much like cultural influences on our lives. [Cultural Watersheds: Diagramming One’s Own Experience of Culture](http://www.socialstudies.org/sites/default/files/publications/yl/1202/120203.html) explains: "First, the watershed is an environmental phenomenon that is all around us—although we might not be aware of it. Likewise, our cultural roots provide the base upon which we build our lives—although it is usually not easy to see the sources of our own behavior."
- **Interconnectedness**: It highlights how different cultural elements are interconnected, fostering a holistic view of identity, similar to how water from different sources flows together in a watershed.
- **Educational Tool**: It serves as a tool for self-reflection and cultural awareness, particularly in educational settings. Students are encouraged to create visual representations, such as murals, to articulate how their cultural background has influenced their personal growth and worldview.
