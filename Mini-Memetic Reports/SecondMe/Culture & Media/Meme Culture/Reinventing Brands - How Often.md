The author, Andrej Drapal, begins by asking: "How often should we rebrand? How often should we reinvent the brand that we are managing?"

He explains the challenge of change: "Brands interact with the environment as a complex memetic entity that can be defined as the identity of a brand." He notes that "Change happens by necessity" and warns that if change is unrecognized, "the discrepancy between identity definition and reality grows."

The author then questions if constant re-formulation is necessary: "If change is constant, does that mean that brand managers must re-formulate the brand's identity monthly, daily, and hourly?"

He compares brand evolution to biological evolution: "There is only one way of reinvention. The way taught by evolutionary biology." He argues that reinvention "happens both evolutionary and revolutionary."

## Insight
Andrej Drapal explores the frequency and necessity of rebranding in a rapidly changing environment. He emphasizes that brands, like living entities, must adapt continuously to avoid discrepancies between their identity and reality. Drapal draws parallels between brand evolution and biological evolution, suggesting that reinvention should be both evolutionary and revolutionary. For brand managers, understanding this dynamic can help in making informed decisions about when and how to rebrand effectively.

**🔄 The Challenge of Change**
- **Brands as Complex Memetic Entities**: Brands interact with their environment as complex memetic entities, meaning their identity is a sum of all interactions.
- **Necessity of Change**: Change is inevitable and necessary. Unrecognized change leads to a growing discrepancy between a brand's identity and reality, risking devaluation.
- **Adapting to the Environment**: Brands must adapt to their changing environment to survive, similar to living organisms.

**🔁 Constant Re-formulation**
- **Frequency of Rebranding**: The frequency of rebranding should be aligned with the rate of change in the environment. Constant re-formulation is impractical.
- **Logical Approach to Rebranding**: Higher frequency of rebranding requires different methods. It's impractical to rebrand second by second.
- **Balancing Act**: Brand managers must find a balance between too frequent and too infrequent rebranding.

**🧬 Evolution of Brands**
- **Evolutionary and Revolutionary Change**: Reinvention should follow the principles of evolutionary biology, involving both gradual (evolutionary) and radical (revolutionary) changes.
- **Obsolete Methods**: Old methods of reinvention are obsolete. New tools and approaches are needed.
- **Radical Perception Shift**: A radical shift in how brands are perceived is necessary for sustainable reinvention.

**🌱 Life Within Brands**
- **Brands as Living Entities**: Brands evolve and become more organized over time, unlike inanimate objects like stones.
- **Constant Re-interpretation**: Life within brands involves constant sense-making and re-interpretation.
- **Natural Reinvention**: Brands naturally reinvent themselves through usage and discourse. Brand management should adapt to this natural process.
