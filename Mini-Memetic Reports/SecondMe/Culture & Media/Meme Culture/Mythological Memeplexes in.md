## Insight
This article delves into the concept of 'mythological memeplexes' and their influence on morality. It argues that societal pressures and memeplexes shape moral standards more than inherent truths. Key outcomes include the recognition of societal influence on ethical behavior, the role of memeplexes in moral constructs, and the impact of compassion and cooperation on societal norms. If you're interested in understanding how societal pressures shape your moral compass, this article offers valuable insights.

**🌍 Societal Influence on Morality**
- **Morality as Societal Control**: Morality is seen as an enforcing arm of societal norms, directing human choices through social pressure.
- **Fear of Censure**: Individuals often avoid pursuing personal desires due to fear of societal censure, even if such fears are based on outdated standards.
- **Moral Tenets and Obedience**: The proclamation of moral tenets increases societal pressure to conform, reinforcing obedience to these standards.

**🧠 Memeplexes in Ethical Behavior**
- **Memeplexes as Mythical Entities**: Memeplexes are described as powerful, mythical entities that influence moral behavior, existing on a meta-plane rather than as inherent truths.
- **Punishment for Challenging Memeplexes**: Challenging these memeplexes can lead to societal punishment, as they are deeply ingrained in laws and norms.
- **Relation to Global Brain**: Memeplexes are compared to the 'Global Brain,' indicating their pervasive influence on collective human consciousness.

**❤️ Compassion and Cooperation**
- **Compassion as a Memeplex**: Compassion is identified as a significant memeplex that influences moral behavior and societal norms.
- **Cooperation as a Strategy**: Cooperation, linked to compassion, is highlighted as a successful strategy for societal interaction, supported by concepts like 'Tit for Tat' and the 'Golden Rule.'
- **Expansion of Compassion**: The article questions the future influence of compassion in an increasingly interconnected society, pondering its limits and potential expansion.
