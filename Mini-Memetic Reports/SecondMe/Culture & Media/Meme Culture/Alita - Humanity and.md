# Alita: Humanity and Resilience in Cyberpunk

Created at 2025/10/25 11:10 AM

[image via x/Delerat7](https://x.com/delerat7/status/1956698251266064570?s=46&t=7Z-E-ACnGlzdI-iyUwNCrQ)<br><br>Overview of Battle Angel Alita (Gunnm)<br>Battle Angel Alita, originally titled Gunnm in Japan, is a cyberpunk manga by Yukito Kishiro (1990–1995, with sequels like Last Order). Set in a dystopian future, it follows Alita (Gally in the original), a amnesiac cyborg rebuilt from scrap by cyberneticist Daisuke Ido in the lawless Iron City below the elite sky city Zalem. Alita discovers her lost martial art, Panzer Kunst, becomes a bounty hunter, explores her origins, and confronts themes of identity, humanity, and societal inequality.<br>The story spans brutal arena fights, conspiracies involving Zalem, and Alita’s quests for purpose amid cycles of violence and rebirth.<br>Key Moral of the Story<br>Humanity is defined by choice, resilience, and the pursuit of meaning—not by flesh, origins, or societal status.<br>Substantiation from the Narrative:<br>	1	Identity Beyond the Body:<br>	◦	Alita starts as a disembodied head with no memories, yet her actions—choosing compassion (e.g., sparing enemies), love (relationships with Hugo and Figure), and growth—make her “human.” Kishiro contrasts this with purely mechanical beings or corrupt humans in Zalem, showing that soul emerges from will, not hardware.<br>	◦	Example: Alita’s multiple body upgrades and brain transfers (e.g., berserker body, imaginos body in Last Order) emphasize that selfhood persists through change. She rejects being a “weapon” from her Martian past, forging her own path.<br>	2	Resilience Against Despair:<br>	◦	Iron City’s underclass dreams of Zalem but faces exploitation (e.g., factory workers turned into cyborgs, bounty hunting as survival). Alita rises from amnesia and defeat (losing to Makaku, Zapan) through unyielding spirit, symbolizing hope in a deterministic world.<br>	◦	Hugo’s tragic arc—obsessed with reaching Zalem, only to be killed by its defenses—highlights false dreams; Alita learns to find purpose in the scrapyard, not escape.<br>	3	Critique of Hierarchy and Free Will:<br>	◦	Zalem’s elite hoard technology and discard “undesirables” to Iron City, mirroring class divides. Alita’s rebellion (challenging Motorball champions, uncovering Zalem’s secrets) asserts that worth isn’t granted by birth or position.<br>	◦	In Last Order, themes expand to cosmic scales (nanotech immortality, AI gods), but the core remains: even immortals like Nova stagnate without empathy and choice—Alita’s humanity shines in her refusal to abandon connections.<br>	4	Cycle of Violence and Redemption:<br>	◦	Panzer Kunst teaches adaptation in endless battle, but Alita seeks to break cycles (e.g., mentoring others, questioning vengeance). The moral warns that unchecked power corrupts (Desty Nova’s experiments), but empathy redeems.<br>Kishiro draws from philosophy (e.g., Nietzsche’s Übermensch in Alita’s evolution) and Buddhism (rebirth, suffering). The open-ended sequels reinforce that meaning is ongoing struggle, not destination.<br>In essence, Alita’s journey teaches: In a broken world, what makes you human is how you fight to become more—despite everything trying to strip it away. This resonates in cyberpunk as a beacon against nihilism.<br>

## Insight


* The artwork draws heavily from the themes of *Battle Angel Alita*, showcasing the blend of human and machine that defines the genre. The character's cybernetic enhancements symbolize the struggle for identity and agency against societal constructs, much like Alita's journey from amnesia to self-discovery amidst the chaos of Iron City.  
* The portrayal of the cyborg here emphasizes a strong, empowered figure, echoing Alita's evolution as a warrior. This highlights the narrative's idea that one's humanity is not determined by physical form but by choices, resilience, and moral courage, as seen when Alita chooses compassion over mere survival.  
* The visual style reflects the manga's cyberpunk aesthetic, characterized by high-tech, low-life themes—an art form that captures the contrast between the affluent sky city of Zalem and the impoverished Iron City. This juxtaposition is crucial in understanding the societal critiques present in Yukon Kishiro's work.  
* The inclusion of intricate mechanical elements and the character's confident stance may also serve as a commentary on the power dynamics within the story, illustrating how characters like Alita challenge the oppressive structures of Zalem through their actions, underlining the moral that one's worth transcends social hierarchy.  
* This art piece resonates with a wider trend in contemporary visual culture of embracing and exploring cybernetic themes, reflecting ongoing conversations about humanity and technology, identity, and the choices we make, aligning neatly with the philosophical underpinnings of the original *Battle Angel Alita* narrative.
