Yes—what you’re describing is a textbook case of cognitive-immunological failure viewed through a memetic lens.<br>The mind’s normal “forget / update / doubt” routines are hijacked the same way a virus hijacks a cell’s replication machinery. The clinical name in bias-catalogues is continued-influence effect or belief perseverance, but “memetic infection” is more vivid and more general: the meme has installed a do-not-delete flag that outranks new evidence or even the host’s own goals.<br>Parasitic vs. mutualistic symbiosis<br>Most successful memes sit on a spectrum:<br>1.  Parasitic (fitness cost to host, fitness gain for meme).<br>– Doomsday cults, QAnon, extreme self-loathing loops.<br>– Host pays in time, money, health, relationships; meme gets copied.<br>2.  Commensal (little host cost, meme still spreads).<br>– Trivia, catchy jingles, neutral fashion trends.<br>3.  Mutualistic (both sides win).<br>– “Wash your hands”: meme spreads, host stays alive.<br>– Scientific method itself: a meta-meme that improves the host’s map of reality.<br>A “positive feedback loop” is therefore only healthy if the meme’s reproductive incentive is aligned with the host’s long-term utility. The trick is that short-term reward can masquerade as alignment while slowly drifting into parasitism (e.g., hustle-culture memes that feel empowering but end in burnout).<br>Immune response & cognitive “ hygiene ”<br>We do have evolved counter-measures:<br>• Source-tracking (“who told me this?”)<br>• Contradiction-detection (“does this clash with something else I believe?”)<br>• Reputation logic (“is the source usually right?”)<br>• Analytic override (slow, System-2 deliberation).<br>Infectious memes evolve to disable those modules:<br>– “Trust no one but us.”<br>– “Thinking too hard is sinful / arrogant / ‘analysis-paralysis’.”<br>– “Any doubt you feel proves the meme is true (cognitive-dissonance flip).”<br>So the same way a parasite can suppress the host’s immune system, a meme can suppress the host’s epistemic immune system.<br>Endlessly positive?<br>Only if the meme’s success metric is tightly coupled to the host’s. A genuine “ever-positive” loop would be something like:<br>Host utility = U<br>Meme fitness = F<br>dU/dF > 0 and dF/dU > 0 for all future time.<br>In practice that condition is fragile: once the meme can spread without improving host welfare (or by exploiting a new audience), mutation will push it toward parasitism. Evolution at the meme level doesn’t care about your happiness; it cares about copy-count.<br>Take-away<br>Treat memes like you would gut bacteria: cultivate the strains that synthesise vitamins you need, and periodically take antibiotics when a harmful bloom appears. The “antibiotics” are scepticism, falsification, and exposure to rival memes that keep any single strain from achieving monoculture.<br>[x/Change w/Peter](https://x.com/innov8tor3/status/1981348388655202476?s=46&t=7Z-E-ACnGlzdI-iyUwNCrQ)<br>
