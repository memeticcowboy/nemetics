### 🧠 **Memetic Definition of "Decision"**:

**A memetic decision is a culturally resonant act of pattern commitment**—a moment where an individual or collective adopts, amplifies, or resists a meme (cultural signal) in a way that affects narrative propagation and social structure.

***

### 🪶 Key Components:

- **Meaning Saturation**: Unlike data-optimized decisions, memetic decisions carry symbolic weight. They evoke identity, values, and mythic structures. (e.g., “coming out” is not just a personal choice—it alters the memetic fabric of social identity.)
- **Resonance over Optimization**: Success is measured not in accuracy or utility, but in cultural impact, adoption, and symbolic alignment—how it echoes across time, space, and psyche.
- **Field Sensitivity**: Decisions interact with cultural attractor fields—zones where certain ideas stick, replicate, or repel. Memetic choices shift the gravity wells of these fields (i.e., trends, taboos, sacred symbols).
- **Agency-Modulation**: While classical decisions imply rational actors, memetic decisions often emerge from **memetic saturation points**—where memes act through people as much as people act on memes.
