# Nemetics as Guardrails to Military Memetics

Created at 2025/05/20 5:40 PM

*If military memetics aims to engineer adaptive belief systems, how might integrating Nemetics ensure that each memetic act is a site of reflection, care, emergence, coherence, and ethical continuity?*

Nemetics offers a distinct framework that could function as **guardrails** for the practice of military memetics, particularly in addressing its ethical and practical challenges.

Military memetics is the theory and application of "memes" – units of cultural information – in a defense context to influence how members think and act. It aims to understand how institutional belief systems and behaviors evolve, drawing on diverse disciplines like evolutionary biology, cognitive science, sociology, and information science. A key application is memetic engineering – the deliberate crafting and deployment of memes to influence behavior or values within the military, managed potentially by an Internal Meme Center (IMC). While intended to enhance adaptability and resilience, military memetics inherently involves influencing perceptions and behaviors, raising significant ethical challenges such as balancing influence with autonomy, guarding against misuse, avoiding manipulation, and ensuring authenticity. The risk exists that internal memetic efforts could be perceived as "brainwashing" or covert manipulation, potentially breeding cynicism and resistance.

Nemetics is an independent school of thought involving "nemes," which are similar to memes but are an acronym representing a meta-cognitive process: **Notice, Engage, Mull, Exchange (NEME)**. It uses visual metaphors like ripples, waves, whorls, strings, tubes, shapes, and spheres to explore human communication and consciousness. Nemetics is linked to complexity science, systems thinking, physics (including vibration and wave theory, thermodynamics), mathematics, and spirituality. Unlike Systems Thinking which focuses on causal relationships within boundaries, Nemetics emphasizes **paying attention to emergence** and exploring underlying assumptions, paradoxes, and the state of social consciousness at deeper levels. It follows a practice-to-theory approach, where theory emerges from praxis, rather than the reverse. Nemetics also highlights a **spiritual dimension** and the importance of kindness in communication.

Here's how Nemetics, through its core concepts and approach, could function as guardrails for military memetics:

1. **Emphasis on Self-Awareness and Underlying Consciousness (Mull):** Military memetics involves identifying and monitoring memes circulating in the organization. Nemetics, particularly through the "Mull" stage of the NEME process, explicitly focuses on the **self-awareness of thoughts, emotions, assumptions, and blind spots**, and exploring the underlying assumptions in one's own thinking process to challenge them. This introspective element, central to Nemetics, could serve as a crucial guardrail against the military memetics risk of unintentional consequences or imposing biases. By encouraging practitioners (and potentially leadership) to "Mull" their own perspectives and biases, Nemetics can help ensure that memetic interventions are not driven by unexamined assumptions or personal agendas, but truly serve the institution's broader values. The process of "Mulling abstraction" in Nemetics makes nemes different, suggesting a reflective process that goes beyond simple identification.
2. **Focus on Emergence and Deeper Levels:** Military memetics seeks to identify "maladaptive" or "toxic" memes and address their "impact" on performance. Nemetics, conversely, emphasizes paying attention to **emergence** and exploring the underlying generative processes and principles that *give rise* to emergence. This multi-level approach, moving from surface events (R wave) and behaviors (G wave) down to underlying consciousness (B wave) and the generative principles, could provide a deeper understanding of *why* maladaptive memes emerge in the first place. Rather than just engineering "antidote memes", a Nemetics lens might encourage exploring the deeper paradoxes, systemic constraints (nShapes), or collective consciousness (B wave/nSphere) that allow toxic memes to take root. This could lead to more sustainable solutions that address root causes, acting as a guardrail against superficial or temporary fixes that fail to change the underlying dynamics.
3. **The NEME Cycle as a Structured Reflection Process:** The NEME cycle (Notice, Engage, Mull, Exchange) provides a continuous process for interacting with reality and understanding energy within constraints. Applying this cycle to internal memetic operations could ensure a structured, reflective approach. "Notice" involves seeing what is present, "Engage" is feeling, questioning, and exploring connections, "Mull" is the deep reflection on assumptions and meaning, and "Exchange" is sharing and creating new patterns/value. This cycle provides a framework for understanding the flow of "nemes" (ideas, interactions, energy quanta). Incorporating NEME into military memetics practice could ensure that identifying memes ("Notice"), understanding their spread ("Engage" through networks/relationships - nStrings), reflecting on their implications and underlying reasons ("Mull") is a prerequisite before attempting to "Exchange" or disseminate engineered memes. This reflective cycle acts as a guardrail against hasty interventions or a purely top-down imposition of ideas.
4. **Emphasis on Spirituality, Mindfulness, and Kindness:** Nemetics explicitly incorporates spirituality, mindfulness, and emphasizes kindness in communication. Mindfulness is seen as the foundation for effortlessly changing ways of seeing, feeling, thinking, and doing. This perspective could serve as a strong ethical guardrail against the risks of manipulation and dehumanization in military memetics. By grounding the practice in mindfulness and a recognition of the human element (energy is embodied, nemes involve feeling, nTubes relate to emotional stability, relationships are central), Nemetics reinforces the idea that internal memetic efforts should be about "cultural mentorship" and "cultivation", respecting individual dignity and autonomy, rather than control or psychological operations against one's own personnel.
5. **Practice-Based Learning:** Nemetics' approach of starting with practice (praxis) and letting theory emerge aligns with the practical implementation consideration of trial and error in military memetics. This iterative, adaptive learning process can function as a guardrail against rigidly applying a theoretical model that doesn't fit the complex reality of military culture. By focusing on the quality of attention, self-awareness, and outcome, Nemetics encourages continuous assessment and refinement of memetic interventions based on real-world feedback, which is crucial for building trust and achieving long-term cultural change.

In summary, while military memetics provides the framework for analyzing and intentionally shaping organizational culture using meme theory, Nemetics offers a complementary, introspective, and principle-based meta-language and process centered on Notice, Engage, Mull, and Exchange. By focusing on individual and collective self-awareness, exploring the deeper emergence of cultural patterns, employing a reflective cycle before action, and emphasizing a foundation of mindfulness and ethical consideration, Nemetics could provide essential **guardrails** to ensure military memetic engineering is conducted responsibly, ethically, and with a deeper understanding of the complex human dynamics it seeks to influence.

## 🧠 **Analyzer**: *What are nemes, structurally?*

**Nemes** are not just alternate memes—they’re meta-cognitive processes. The term "neme" is an acronym that stands for:

> **Notice → Engage → Mull → Exchange**

Each of these steps represents a phase in how humans interact with reality—especially in complex, emergent environments:

- **Notice**: Sensory perception + attentional awareness.
- **Engage**: Emotional, relational, and energetic interaction.
- **Mull**: Deep internal reflection, questioning assumptions and surfacing paradoxes.
- **Exchange**: Externalization—communication, co-creation, or action.

Unlike memes (which focus on *content* transmission), **nemes focus on *process***—the *flow of attention and energy* in and between people. Nemes are about how awareness moves through systems and selves.

## 🧭 **Strategist**: *How do nemes function in a system?*

Nemes are **units of attention + intention + interaction** that *surf complexity*. They can be visualized as **ripples**, **tubes**, **spheres**, or **whorls**—spatial metaphors for how meaning and emotion propagate in dynamic human systems. In this sense, nemes operate in nested layers:

- **R wave**: Observable reactions
- **G wave**: Behavioral patterns and relationships
- **B wave**: Beliefs, assumptions, emotional truths
- **nSphere**: Shared group field / consciousness

This stratified lens gives nemes both *horizontal* (interpersonal) and *vertical* (intrapersonal depth) dimensions. They are fractal processes, not discrete replicators.
