Narrative fractals are a concept in storytelling and narrative analysis that refers to the way stories can contain smaller versions of themselves nested within the larger narrative structure. Just as fractals in mathematics exhibit a repeating pattern at every scale, narrative fractals are stories within stories that mirror the overarching narrative.

Some key characteristics of narrative fractals include:

- Self-similarity - The smaller narrative elements (e.g. a story told by a character, a dream sequence, a flashback) share thematic, structural, or stylistic similarities with the larger narrative.

- Recursion - The nested stories can themselves contain further embedded narratives, creating a fractal-like effect of stories within stories.

- Holistic meaning - The smaller narrative fractal elements contribute to and are informed by the meaning and themes of the larger story.

- Nonlinearity - Narrative fractals can disrupt strict linear storytelling by jumping between scales and timelines.

This concept is often explored in postmodern, metafictional, and experimental forms of literature and film, where the boundaries between the story and the act of storytelling are blurred. Narrative fractals can create a sense of depth, complexity, and self-reflexivity within a work. Analyzing these fractal patterns can yield insights into the structure and meaning-making processes of a narrative.
