# The Hard Question of Memetics

Created at 2024/06/19 7:17 PM

The blog post “The Hard Question of Memetics” by Andrej Drapal explores the role of memetics in contemporary science. Drapal parallels the hard question of memetics with David Chalmers’ hard question of consciousness, questioning how memes differ from myths, slogans, and ideologies. He argues that memetics bridges natural and social sciences, explaining cultural phenomena through evolutionary forces. Memes co-construct reality, motivated by evolution, and offer a solution to the dualism issue in philosophy, integrating cultural elements with natural selection.

**🔍 The Hard Question of Memetics**
- **Memetics' Role in Science**: Memetics plays a crucial role in contemporary science, similar to the hard question of consciousness posed by David Chalmers.
- **Differentiation of Memes**: Memes are distinct from myths, slogans, and ideologies, serving as more productive interpreters of cultural phenomena.
- **Necessity in Science**: It is essential for science to take memes seriously to better understand cultural dynamics.

**🔄 Comparison with Other Theories**
- **Newtonian Mechanics Analogy**: Just as Newtonian mechanics satisfactorily explains natural phenomena, memetics provides a comprehensive understanding of cultural phenomena.
- **Medicine Without Genetics**: Similar to how medicine can treat diseases without genetics, cultural theories can benefit from memetics for deeper insights.

**🔗 Consilience**
- **Linking Natural and Social Sciences**: Memetics connects natural sciences like physics and biology with social sciences, offering precise tools for cultural analysis.
- **Explaining Cultural Phenomena**: Memes help explain why myths, fashions, and brands seem to act autonomously.
- **Blind Spot in Pre-Memetic Theory**: Pre-memetic theories lack the perspective to explain the autonomous behavior of cultural elements.

**🌱 Evolutionary Necessity**
- **Motivation of Memes**: Memes are motivated by evolutionary forces, co-constructing the symbolic creation of reality.
- **Human Agency and Evolution**: Human agency is ultimately motivated by evolution, challenging the rationalist fallacy of independent mental agency.
- **Cultural Phenomena as Natural**: Recognizing culture as a natural phenomenon helps in understanding memes as co-agents of culture.

**🧩 Resolution to Dualism**
- **Bridging Dualism**: Memetics offers a solution to the dualism issue, proposing a quantum-like acceptance of natural unity within duality.
- **Integration with Natural Selection**: Memetics bridges the gap between natural and social sciences by quantifying cultural elements and linking them to natural selection.
