### 1. Purposes and Goals

The primary purpose of the *Story Circles Toolkit* is to provide a structured framework for facilitating *Story Circles*, a storytelling process that builds trust, empathy, and community among participants. The toolkit aims to:

- Enable facilitators to conduct Story Circles in various settings (e.g., in-person, remote, community events, or classrooms).

- Foster relationship-building, cultural humility, and dialogue through shared personal or imaginative stories.

- Support experiential learning and community engagement, particularly in the context of diversity, equity, and inclusion.

- Encourage the documentation of stories for oral history projects or community-based initiatives, such as those archived in OSU’s Multicultural and Queer Archives.

- Inspire action and co-creation of projects (e.g., the Healing Cards Initiative) based on themes emerging from stories.

### 3. Fundamental Concepts

The key concepts underpinning the *Story Circles* process include:

- **Storytelling**: A universal human practice that involves sharing narratives with a beginning, middle, and end to convey experiences, emotions, or imagination.

- **Community-Building**: Creating spaces where trust, empathy, and mutual understanding are fostered through shared stories.

- **Cultural Humility**: An approach that emphasizes openness, curiosity, and respect for diverse perspectives and experiences.

- **Empathy**: The ability to understand and share the feelings of others, cultivated through active listening and storytelling.

- **Equity in Sharing**: Ensuring all participants have equal time and attention to share their stories without judgment or interruption.

- **Reflection and Dialogue**: The process of collectively analyzing stories to identify common themes, differences, and insights that spark further discussion or action.

- **Indigenous and Theatrical Roots**: The practice’s origins in indigenous traditions and its development by theatermakers for community organizing and performance.

- **Co-Creation**: Using stories as a foundation for collaborative projects or initiatives that address community needs or promote healing.

### 4. Essential Information

The toolkit provides critical information to support the implementation and understanding of Story Circles:

- **Process Overview**: Story Circles involve small groups (6–10 people) sharing stories on a common theme, guided by a facilitator who ensures equitable participation and adherence to community guidelines.

- **Formats**: Story Circles can be conducted in-person or remotely (e.g., via Zoom breakout rooms), with specific recommendations for group size (4–8 for remote, 6–10 for in-person) and spatial arrangements to minimize sound interference.

- **Facilitator Role**: Facilitators manage time, ensure guidelines are followed, and create a safe space. They are trained through demonstration and practice to model storytelling and maintain neutrality.

- **Community Guidelines**: Adapted from adrienne maree brown’s *Emergent Strategy*, these include principles like “listen from the inside out,” “make space, take space,” and “assume best intent, attend to impact.”

- **Story Prompts**: Examples include stories about joy, belonging, mentors, hope, or breaking barriers, with flexibility to create group-specific prompts.

- **Documentation**: Stories can be recorded (with consent) for oral histories or community projects, with scribes capturing narratives and ensuring accuracy with storytellers’ approval.

- **OSU Context**: The toolkit was adapted for OSU’s experiential learning programs, with examples like the Trans Story Circles and Healing Cards Initiative demonstrating its application.

- **Timing**: A typical Story Circle session lasts about 80 minutes, including introductions, storytelling (3 minutes per person), reflection, and large-group discussion.
