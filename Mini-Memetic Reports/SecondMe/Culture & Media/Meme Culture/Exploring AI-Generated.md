## **Introduction: The Power of Words**

- The speaker reflects on their fascination with good writing from childhood.

## **The Rise of AI-Generated Writing**

- Large language models (LLMs) are becoming better than humans at writing.
- These AI-generated texts can evoke laughter, tears, and persuasive effects.
- The speaker asks: Have you ever been convinced to do or believe something you initially resisted? Often, persuasion happens unnoticed.

***

## **Superhuman Persuasion and Influence**

- Humanity may never have encountered anything with **superhuman persuasion abilities**—but AI is approaching that level.
- AI models could soon surpass human writers in their ability to influence beliefs and decisions.
- Example: Websites use A/B testing (split tests) to determine the most persuasive elements, even down to the color of a button.
- Small, seemingly inconsequential factors can significantly influence decision-making.

***

## **The Subjectivity of Writing Quality in AI**

- **Sam Altman’s Experiments**:
    - Sam Altman posted a story written by an AI model that remains unreleased.
    - Instead of focusing on performance benchmarks, OpenAI is shifting towards subjective evaluations—“Is this writing better than that writing?”
- **The GPT-4 vs. GPT-4.5 Experiment**:
    - When GPT-4.5 was released, people were unsure what made it different.
    - Andrej Karpathy ran a poll comparing GPT-4 and GPT-4.5’s writing.
    - Surprisingly, many still preferred GPT-4, but the results were inconclusive.
    - This suggests that writing quality, particularly in terms of emotional impact, is highly subjective.

***

## **Defining Writing Quality: EQ, Persuasion, and Emotional Depth**

- Some suggest measuring AI-generated writing using an **EQ (Emotional Quotient) test**.
- However, persuasion and emotional impact are difficult to quantify:
    - **Short-term influence:** Did the writing make someone take immediate action?
    - **Long-term influence:** Did it shape beliefs over time?
- AI-generated writing is evolving beyond benchmarks into the realm of emotional effectiveness.

***

## **Sam Altman’s Six-Word Story**

- Altman had AI generate a six-word story:
    - *"Near the singularity, unclear which side."*
- Possible interpretations:
    - A reference to the **Simulation Hypothesis**.
    - A comment on **AI Takeoff and Singularity**—the point at which AI surpasses human intelligence.
- This story highlights AI’s ability to create **thought-provoking and layered writing**.

***

## **Meta-Fictional AI Writing: The Literary Experiment**

- A new AI model generated a **meta-fictional literary short story** about AI and grief.
- **What is meta-fiction?**
    - A literary technique that **self-consciously acknowledges its fictionality**.
    - Examples:
        - **Breaking the fourth wall** (characters acknowledging they are in a story).
        - **A narrator aware of their role within the text**.
- The AI-generated story was a **self-aware narrative**, reflecting on its own existence.

***

## **Audience Reactions: The Human vs. AI Debate**

- Some readers found the AI-generated story **emotionally empty**:
    - One person commented:<br>*"If a human had written this, I would care. Knowing it’s AI, I don’t."*
    - The perception that AI writing lacks **"weight" or "meaning"** persists.
- The speaker challenges this notion:
    - **If you don’t know whether a human or AI wrote something, does it change its meaning?**
    - **Does the emotional impact of writing come from the author, or the words themselves?**
    - What happens if the most moving story, song, or art you’ve ever encountered turns out to be AI-generated?

***

## **AI’s Philosophical Dilemma: What Does "Meaning" Mean?**

- The speaker examines what it means for writing to have **"weight" or "meaning."**
    - If two identical sentences are spoken, one by a human and one by AI, does one carry more significance?
    - If AI **copies human expression**, is it still **authentic communication**?
- The underlying question:<br>**Is meaning derived from the process of creation or from the experience of the audience?**
