The Sanskrit term **Brahmāṇḍa** (ब्रह्माण्ड) is a single word with profound significance in Indian philosophy, cosmology, and spiritual traditions. Below is the definition and the term written in Sanskrit.

### Definition:

**Brahmāṇḍa** (ब्रह्माण्ड):

- **Brahmāṇḍa** is a compound word derived from **Brahma** (ब्रह्म, referring to the cosmic principle, the creator god, or the ultimate reality) and **Aṇḍa** (अण्ड, meaning "egg" or "sphere"). It translates to "the cosmic egg" or "the universe."
- **Cosmological Context**: In Hindu cosmology, Brahmāṇḍa refers to the entire universe, conceptualized as an egg-like structure that contains all creation, including galaxies, planets, and beings. It is believed to emerge from the cosmic principle or the creator god Brahma, encompassing the cycle of creation, preservation, and dissolution.
- **Philosophical Context**: In texts like the *Puranas* and *Upanishads*, Brahmāṇḍa symbolizes the totality of existence, including both the material and spiritual realms. It represents the macrocosm, which is often paralleled with the microcosm of the individual self (*Pinda-Brahmāṇḍa* theory), where the human body mirrors the structure of the universe.
- **Spiritual Context**: In yogic and Tantric traditions, understanding or meditating on the Brahmāṇḍa can lead to realization of the unity between the individual self (*Ātman*) and the universal consciousness (*Brahman*).
- **Symbolic Context**: The term evokes the idea of the universe as a self-contained, organic whole, born from a primordial source and subject to cyclic processes of creation and dissolution.
