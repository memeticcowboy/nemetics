# Storytelling as Decolonizing Knowledge Methodology

Created at 2025/12/16 10:22 PM

Contrast with Western Questioning<br>Many Indigenous traditions view direct questioning as potentially disruptive or violent, forcing tacit, relational knowledge into explicit forms. Instead, knowledge emerges through witnessing, sharing stories reciprocally, or observational presence. This aligns with principles like Jo-ann Archibald’s “storywork” (respect, responsibility, reciprocity, reverence, holism, interrelatedness, synergy), prioritizing co-emergence over interrogation.<br><br>In research and education, storytelling serves as a decolonizing methodology, challenging Western dominance and fostering ethical, community-driven inquiry.

This format contrasts with Western academic approaches that often rely on direct questioning and explicit inquiry, potentially neglecting the deeper relational knowledge embedded in oral traditions.

* Firelight serves as a metaphorical and literal illumination of shared experiences, reflecting the holistic understanding that blurs the lines between the observed and the observer. The warmth and intimacy of the gathering foster an environment conducive to the type of ethical inquiry that emphasizes co-emergence and connection over competition and confrontation.

* Indigenous methodologies, such as the one demonstrated in the image, challenge dominant educational practices by advocating for an ethical, community-driven approach to inquiry. This not only allows for a more inclusive understanding of knowledge but also plays a pivotal role in decolonizing research practices, as it prioritizes the voices and experiences of the community members over the impositions of external frameworks.

* The image captures a moment rich with potential for learning and understanding, illustrating how storytelling in communal settings provides a counter-narrative to Western interrogation methods, drawing attention to the significance of context, relationships, and shared histories in the learning process.
