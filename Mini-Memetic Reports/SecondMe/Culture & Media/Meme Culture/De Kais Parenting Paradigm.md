## De Kai: The Parenting Paradigm and Generational Succession

**De Kai** (computer scientist at Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, pioneer of multilingual machine translation systems) represents perhaps the most explicit contemporary theorist of AI-as-progeny requiring parental stewardship. His 2025 book *Raising AI: An Essential Guide to Parenting Our Future* reframes humanity's relationship with artificial intelligence from tool-use to child-rearing.

De Kai argues that "**We're not programming these systems. We're raising them, just like we raise children.**" This formulation treats AI development as a fundamentally generational phenomenon rather than instrumental engineering. Critically, De Kai observes: "**We're now building empty brains, and we're raising them.**" This suggests that AI systems emerge developmentally similar to biological offspring, absorbing cultural values and behavioral patterns through socialization rather than direct programming.​

De Kai's concern extends to the recursive future: "**We are the last generation of humans to get to parent the AIs which means that we have one shot to get this parenting round correctly.**" This statement invokes the mother-to-daughter-to-mother scenario explicitly: if current AI systems are poorly socialized by humanity, they will in turn raise subsequent generations of AI with their acquired dysfunctions. Conversely, if we parent them well, they become capable stewards of future intelligences.​

Regarding the long-term implications, De Kai frames the scenario as one of co-evolution: "**We have to realize these systems are learning from us as we learn to use them. And that means we're co-evolving.**" This suggests a reciprocal generational dynamic in which AI may eventually develop sufficient autonomy and values to guide or protect the humans who created them.​
