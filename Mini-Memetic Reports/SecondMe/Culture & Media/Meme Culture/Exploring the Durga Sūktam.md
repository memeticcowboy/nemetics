The text you’ve shared is a translation and poetic interpretation of the Durga Sūktam (दुर्गा सूक्तम्), a Vedic hymn found in the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka of the Krishna Yajurveda, specifically in:


**📜** 

**Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.1.1–10.1.7**


This hymn is traditionally recited in praise of Agni (the fire deity) as a manifestation of Goddess Durga, invoking Her as a savior who guides devotees across the ocean of worldly existence (saṁsāra). Though Agni is the literal addressee, in the tantric and devotional traditions, the Sūktam is interpreted as invoking Durga as the divine fire of wisdom and liberation.

Here’s a brief alignment of some key mantras from the original Sanskrit to the translations in your image:




**🔥** 

**Core Mantra from the Sanskrit:**


Jātavedase sunavāma somam arātīyato nidahāti vedah

Sa naḥ parṣadati durgāṇi viśvā nāveva sindhum duritātyagniḥ

Translation:

We offer our oblation to Jātavedas (Agni); may He destroy our enemies, grant us prosperity, and carry us across all difficulties and dangers like a boat across the ocean.

✅ This matches the first and second verses in your image: invoking Agni to ferry us over the difficulties of life.




**🔥** 

**Further Verses from 10.1.2 – 10.1.7:**


Each verse continues to praise Agni/Durga for:

- Burning inner enemies (ignorance, delusion)
- Spreading auspiciousness
- Being the form of tapas (spiritual fire)
- Guiding towards blissful consciousness
- Being the cosmic force that removes suffering


These motifs appear throughout the English rendition on the image, such as:

- “May that Agni… burn to ashes the very difficult enemies within us”
- “Let our mind contemplate on Devi Katyayani…”
- “You are the one who is all-pervading and the form of Pranava”





**📚** 

**Classical Commentary and Usage:**


- Durga Sūktam is widely recited during Navaratri, Durga Pūjā, and Agni homa (sacred fire rituals).
- Often coupled with the Mahāmrityunjaya Mantra and Sri Sūkta in ritual contexts.
- In Tantric traditions, Durga Sūktam aligns with Agni as Shakti, especially through the symbolism of fire as transformative divine feminine energy.
