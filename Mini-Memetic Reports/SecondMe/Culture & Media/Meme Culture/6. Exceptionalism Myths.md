**6. Exceptionalism Myths (Unique National Character)**

Example A (Right-Wing): “America is the greatest force for freedom the world has ever known”
Example B (Left-Wing): “Our nation has a unique moral responsibility because of its sins”

**R –** 
**Recognize:**

Exceptionalism frames the nation as chosen, unique, or cosmically significant—whether in virtue or guilt.

**E –** 
**Examine:**

Interrogate origin: When did the nation begin seeing itself this way? What external validations supported this view? How has it been reinforced?

**V –** 
**Validate:**

Measure claims against comparative data. Does the U.S. lead in freedom indices? Has it been unusually interventionist?

**E –** 
**Expose:**

Reveal the functional role: Exceptionalism grants moral license—whether for intervention or reparative leadership. It’s a memetic license to act.

**A –** 
**Alternatives:**

Frame nations as participants in shared evolution, not cosmic outliers. Focus on interdependence, mutual learning, and humility.

**L –** 
**Literate:**

Help readers detect exceptionalism in language (e.g. “beacon,” “city on a hill,” “leading the world”) and question its function.
