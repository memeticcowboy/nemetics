**1. Ontological Anti-Realism**


- Shift: The Threadplex isn’t “discovered” in the wild like an ancient ruin; it’s made through braiding, naming, and applying the concept.
- Effect:<br>
    - There’s no “Threadplex map” that exists independently of its creators’ purposes.
    - Without an observer/practitioner who understands its mechanics and declares “this is a threadplex,” it’s just background weave in The Threadplex (capital-T).





**2. Pragmatic Instrumentalism**


- Shift: The label “threadplex” is provisional and survives only as long as it’s useful in producing insight, emotional leverage, or collective coordination.
- Effect:<br>
    - Encourages constant evaluation: “Is this threadplex still serving our aims?”
    - Prevents ossification — if it loses function, it dissolves into unlabeled background.
    - Ties its existence to agency outcomes rather than theoretical permanence.





**3. Ethical Veto on Imposition**


- Shift: To label someone’s weave as a threadplex without their participation is to interfere with their attentional ecology — akin to installing a memeform without consent.
- Effect:<br>
    - Threadplex construction must be co-creative — with, not to.
    - Aligns with We-Sphere ethics: naming becomes a shared act, embedded in consent, trust, and mutual authorship.
    - Builds guardrails against “weaponized threadplexing” for manipulation.





**4. Philosophical Positioning**


- Nelson Goodman → “Worldmaking” applies here: Threadplexes are world-versions, crafted for specific utility.
- Richard Rorty → Contingent vocabularies: The label is adopted when it helps, abandoned when it doesn’t.
- Donna Haraway → Response-ability: Naming must remain answerable to those entangled in the weave.
- Post-structural pragmatism → Naming is never neutral; this stance limits it to ethical, co-authored contexts.

It makes three nested scopes of agency explicit:




**1. The Threadplex (capital-T)**


- Purpose: Not to be “mapped” as a static structure, but to be imagined and steered toward the We-Sphere we actually want to inhabit.
- Guiding Question:<br>“What do we want in our We-Sphere?”
- Implication: This is collective authorship — the point isn’t to uncover “what’s there,” but to decide what belongs and co-weave it through nested participatory threadplexes.
- Working basis: An unprovable but useful speculative construct — a directional star rather than a map — that orients practice toward the hope of aligning local and global weaves.






**2. Local / Collective Weaving**


- Purpose: To shape the weave of our shared narrative-affect space (We-Sphere) so it aligns with agreed values and desired futures.
- Guiding Question:<br>“How do We want to weave our threadplex?”
- Implication: Requires co-threading — weaving together diverse threads (elemental, memetic, experiential) in ways that preserve consent, resonance, and generativity.





**3. Individual I-Tube**


- Purpose: To reconfigure the practitioner’s personal internalization weave so that their own threads support the chosen We-Sphere.
- Guiding Question:<br>“How might I change the Threadplexes constituting my I-Tube to support this We-Sphere?”
- Implication: The I-Tube becomes the loom for personal reweaving — adjusting resonance, composting knots, and aligning new threads to collective intent.





**Core Principle Across All Levels**


- Any “understanding” of The Threadplex is instrumental — only insofar as it informs what to weave, how to weave, and how to reweave.
- Avoids both:<br>
    - Treating threadplexes as “natural phenomena” that exist to be cataloged (ontological anti-realism).
    - Deploying them unilaterally as tools of control (ethical veto).
