The theory presented in this [Quora post](https://www.quora.com/What-are-narrative-fractals/answer/Michael-Josefowicz?ch=15&oid=102141&share=61f7c2cc&target_type=answer ) describes a complex conceptual framework related to information processing, learning, and communication. Let me break down and elaborate on some key aspects, particularly focusing on the concept of "narrative fractals" mentioned at the end:

1. Fundamental elements:
The author proposes three fundamental elements: Emotion (Ewaves), Cognition (Cwaves), and Place (Bplace). These seem to represent different aspects of how information is processed and experienced.

2. Information processing model:
The Bplace is described as the smallest input-output model, with ECwaves as input and ECwaves as output. This suggests a cyclical process of information transformation.

3. Learning process:
The author introduces #NFME (Notice, Focus, Mull, Engage) as a condensed representation of the learning process.

4. Information carriers:
"Lumenes" and "Memes" are described as artifacts moving through Bplace, with Lumenes being emotion-heavy (Ewave) and Memes being cognition-heavy (Cwave).

5. Narrative Fractals:
This is where the author's speculation becomes particularly intriguing. They describe narrative fractals as "strings of #NFME" that resonate across multiple Bplaces on a global scale. The author suggests these fractals often condense into single words representing broad, complex concepts like "Peace," "Justice," "Fairness," and "Love."
