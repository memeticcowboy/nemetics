## Insight
This article explores the concept of 'memetic resonance' as a reframing of Rupert Sheldrake's morphic resonance within memetic theory. It aims to bridge Sheldrake's ideas with established cultural evolution theories, providing a scientifically grounded interpretation of cultural patterns and their transmission. The findings suggest that memetic resonance can explain cultural phenomena while remaining open to empirical investigation. If you're interested in understanding how cultural patterns spread, this framework could offer valuable insights into social learning and collective memory.

- **Morphic resonance redefined as memetic resonance**: The paper proposes that Sheldrake's morphic resonance can be understood as memetic resonance, which focuses on how cultural patterns resonate across populations and generations.
- **Bridging controversial and established theories**: By integrating Sheldrake's ideas with memetic theory, the paper aims to provide a more scientifically acceptable framework for understanding cultural evolution.
- **Addressing skepticism towards morphic resonance**: The reframing seeks to counter the pseudoscience label often associated with morphic resonance by grounding it in established scientific principles.

**🌍 Memetic Transmission and Cultural Evolution**
- **Cultural patterns influence behavior**: Memetic resonance explains how the prevalence of certain memes increases the likelihood of their adoption, thereby influencing cognition and behavior.
- **Memetic memory as a cultural repository**: The concept of memetic memory is introduced, representing the cumulative pool of memes accessible through cultural artifacts and shared knowledge.
- **Natural laws as habits**: The paper suggests that natural laws can be viewed as memetic entrenchment, where certain memes become more resistant to change through repeated use.
