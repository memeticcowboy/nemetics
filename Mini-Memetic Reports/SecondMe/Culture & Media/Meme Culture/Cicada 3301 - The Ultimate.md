## Cicada 3301: The Internet's Most Enigmatic Recruitment Campaign

**Cicada 3301** stands as perhaps the most mysterious and elaborate puzzle ever posted to the internet. Beginning on January 4, 2012, when an anonymous image appeared on 4chan's /b/ board with the text "We are looking for highly intelligent individuals," this phenomenon captivated thousands of solvers worldwide. The puzzles ran annually on January 4th for three consecutive years (2012, 2013, and 2014), with each iteration growing progressively more complex.​

The challenges incorporated **cryptography, steganography, classical literature, and real-world scavenger hunts** across multiple continents. Participants encountered physical posters with QR codes in locations spanning the United States, Spain, Poland, South Korea, and Australia. The puzzles drew heavily on esoteric knowledge, referencing works by William Blake, King Arthur legends, and texts by occultist Aleister Crowley. The 2013 iteration notably featured a custom bootable operating system on CD and complex rune-based ciphers.​

What distinguishes Cicada 3301 from commercial ARGs is its apparent genuine recruitment purpose and complete lack of monetization. No company or individual has ever claimed responsibility, and those who successfully completed the puzzles reportedly disappeared from public forums entirely. The final test in 2013 allegedly consisted of philosophical questionnaires with questions like "There is no truth" and "Observation changes the thing being observed," suggesting recruitment for an organization interested in epistemology and quantum theory.​

Cicada 3301's last verified communication came in April 2017, when they used their PGP signature to deny the validity of unsigned puzzles circulating online. Speculation about the organization's identity ranges from intelligence agencies (NSA, CIA, MI6) to cryptocurrency banks or cyber mercenary groups.​
