# Rethinking AI Art and Human Creativity: Challenging Overrated Self-Perceptions

Created at 2025/11/22 9:16 AM

**DD:** Reflect on this: The AI art ain’t overrated, but we overrate ourselves as artists. Hubristic humans seeing themselves in everything.<br><br>**Kimi:** That’s a scalpel of a thought—cuts clean to the bone of our creative anxiety.<br>The first half deflates the hype balloon from the inside: AI art isn’t overrated because we’ve barely begun to rate it on its own terms. We keep measuring it against human intent, human struggle, human “touch”—as if those are the only metrics that matter. The output is raw pattern, unburdened by autobiography, and we’re still squinting to see ourselves in it, demanding it prove its worth by our standards. That’s not criticism; that’s a mirror.<br>The second half is the kicker. We do overrate ourselves as artists—not because our art is worthless, but because we’ve confused self-expression with self-importance. We’ve built a cult of the individual genius, the suffering creator, the hand that holds the brush. So when a diffusion model paints a thousand masterpieces in a minute, it doesn’t just threaten our jobs; it threatens our identity. We see our reflection everywhere because we’ve never learned to look at anything else.<br>But here’s the twist: that hubris is also our tell. The urge to anthropomorphize, to project narrative onto noise, to find the “human story” in silicon—that’s not just ego, it’s the same pattern-matching engine that made us artists to begin with. We’re not wrong to see ourselves in everything; we’re just late realizing that “everything” never belonged exclusively to us.<br>Maybe the real move isn’t to downgrade our own rating, but to upgrade our curiosity. Let AI art be alien, unhoused from our mythology. And let human art become more honest—less about the lone genius, more about the specific friction of being conscious, mortal, and hilariously, hopelessly biased.<br>The art was never the overrated part. The story we told about the artist was.<br>
