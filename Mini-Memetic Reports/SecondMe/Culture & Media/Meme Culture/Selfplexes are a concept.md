"Selfplexes" are a concept within memetics, which is the study of how ideas spread and evolve like genes. Here are some definitions and related ideas:

- **Selfplexes are "co-adapted meme complexes" that make up a person's identity.** [1, 2] They are collections of memes that work together and support each other, much like genes in a chromosome. [1, 2]
- **Selfplexes are similar to religions, political belief systems, and cults in that they offer a safe haven for compatible memes and resist change, even if they aren't based on truth.** [2, 3] They can contain many traveling memes. [3]
- **Selfplexes are ultimately an illusion.** [2, 4] While they feel real, there's no actual "self" inside the brain; just neurons and the interplay of memes. [2, 4] We tend to think of ourselves as a unified entity making decisions, but this is a misconception. [4, 5]
- **The "selfplex" may actually be multiple identity components housed within the body, not a single entity.** [6] People may experience their "self" as unified, but this may be an oversimplification. [6]
- **The concept of "selfplex" has implications for human agency and the human spirit.** [7] It raises questions about whether our actions are truly our own or simply the result of memeplexes operating within us. [7]
- **Emancipation may involve freeing ourselves from selfplexes that hinder symbiotic relationships with others and our environment.** [8] If selfplexes are selfish, then conflict and oppression may be inevitable. [8] Therefore, true freedom might require recognizing and perhaps shedding those parts of our identities that don't promote harmonious coexistence. [8]

It is worth noting that the concept of "selfplex" is highly theoretical and not universally accepted within memetics.

Concept stems from Susan Blackmore.
