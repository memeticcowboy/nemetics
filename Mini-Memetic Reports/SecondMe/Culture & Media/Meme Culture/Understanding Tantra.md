# तन्त्र

Created at 2025/07/23 9:15 AM

The Sanskrit term **Tantra** (तन्त्र) is a single word with profound significance in Indian spiritual, philosophical, and cultural traditions. Below is the definition and the term written in Sanskrit.

### Definition:

**Tantra** (तन्त्र):

- **Tantra** literally means "loom," "weaving," "system," or "framework," derived from the root **tan** (to stretch or extend). It refers to a diverse set of esoteric texts, practices, and philosophies in Hinduism and Buddhism that provide a systematic approach to spiritual liberation (*mokṣa*) or worldly goals (*siddhis*).
- **Spiritual Context**: Tantra encompasses rituals, meditation, mantras, yantras (sacred diagrams), and energy practices (e.g., kundalini yoga) aimed at transforming consciousness and realizing the unity of the individual self with the divine. Unlike purely ascetic traditions, Tantra often embraces the material world as an expression of divine consciousness, integrating the physical, mental, and spiritual.
- **Philosophical Context**: In traditions like Kashmir Shaivism, Tantra emphasizes non-dualism, viewing the universe as a manifestation of divine energy (*Shakti*) inseparable from pure consciousness (*Shiva*). It contrasts with dualistic philosophies like Sāṃkhya by seeing the material and spiritual as interconnected.
- **Practical Context**: Tantric practices include mantra recitation, visualization, ritual worship (*pūjā*), and yogic techniques to awaken spiritual energy. Tantra is often divided into paths like *Vāmācāra* (left-hand path, involving unconventional practices) and *Dakṣiṇācāra* (right-hand path, focusing on orthodox rituals).
- **Cultural Context**: Tantra has influenced Indian art, dance, and literature, and its principles are found in texts like the *Tantras* (scriptures) and traditions like Shakta, Shaiva, and Buddhist Tantra. It is sometimes misunderstood due to its association with esoteric or sensual practices, but its core is spiritual transformation.

The exact meaning depends on the context (e.g., Hindu Tantra, Buddhist Tantra, or specific practices).
