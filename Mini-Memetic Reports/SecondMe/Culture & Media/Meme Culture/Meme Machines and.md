## Insight
This paper by S. Blackmore explores the potential for artificial systems to develop human-like consciousness through memeplexes. Key outcomes include the understanding of consciousness as a subjective experience, the role of memes in shaping consciousness, and the potential for artificial systems to develop consciousness through imitation. If you're interested in the intersection of artificial intelligence and human consciousness, this paper provides valuable insights into how imitation and memetic evolution could lead to new forms of consciousness. Let's dive deeper into the main themes and key points discussed.

**🧠 Nature of Consciousness**
- **Subjectivity and Self**: Central to human consciousness is the notion of subjectivity and an experiencing self.
- **Self as a Story**: The self is argued to be more of a story or myth rather than a persistent entity with free will and consciousness.

**🧬 Role of Memes in Consciousness**
- **Definition of Memes**: Memes are information passed from one person to another by imitation.
- **Memeplexes and Selfplex**: The idea of a persisting self is considered a memeplex, which Blackmore calls the 'selfplex.'
- **Origin of Human Consciousness**: This selfplex is proposed as the origin of ordinary human consciousness.

**🤖 Artificial Systems and Imitation**
- **Imitation Capability**: Only systems capable of imitation, and thus capable of sustaining memetic evolution, can develop a form of consciousness.
- **Types of Systems**: Human-imitating systems could develop human-like consciousness, while system-imitating systems might develop a novel form of consciousness distinct from human experience.

**🔍 Types of Artificial Consciousness**
- **Human-like Consciousness**: Systems that imitate humans could potentially possess human-like consciousness.
