# वाच्

Created at 2025/07/26 4:52 PM

The Sanskrit term **Vāc** (वाच्) is a single word with profound significance in Indian philosophy, mythology, and spiritual traditions. Below is the definition and the term written in Sanskrit.

### Definition:

**Vāc** (वाच्):

- **Vāc** means "speech," "voice," or "word." In Vedic and Hindu traditions, it represents the power of sound and articulation, considered a creative and divine force. Vāc is not merely ordinary speech but the cosmic principle of sound that underlies creation and communication.
- **Mythological Context**: In the *Rigveda* (e.g., Vāc Sukta, RV 10.125), Vāc is personified as a goddess, embodying the creative energy of speech. She is described as the source of all existence, through whom the universe is manifested. Vāc is associated with the primordial vibration that gives rise to reality.
- **Philosophical Context**: In Vedanta and other philosophies, Vāc is linked to the concept of *Śabda* (sound) as a means of knowledge and revelation, particularly through the *Vedas*. It represents the expressive aspect of consciousness and is connected to the idea of *nāda* (cosmic sound) or *Om*.
- **Yogic and Tantric Context**: In Tantric traditions, Vāc is associated with the power of mantras, where sacred sounds are used to invoke divine energies or transform consciousness. It is also linked to the stages of speech (*parā*, *paśyantī*, *madhyamā*, *vaikharī*), representing the progression from subtle to manifest sound.
- **Symbolic Context**: Vāc symbolizes the creative and transformative power of words, sound, and intention, emphasizing their role in shaping reality and connecting the human with the divine.
