# लोह-धारणाने

Created at 2025/07/27 8:06 PM

The Sanskrit term **Loha-Dhāraṇāne** can be broken down into its components: **Loha** (लोह) and **Dhāraṇāne** (धारणाने). Below is the definition and the term written in Sanskrit.

### Definition:

1. **Loha** (लोह): This term means "metal" or "iron" in Sanskrit. It is often used to refer to metals in general or specifically to iron in Vedic and classical texts. In a broader context, Loha can symbolize strength, durability, or material substance, and in some spiritual or alchemical traditions, metals are associated with transformation or purification processes.
2. **Dhāraṇāne** (धारणाने): This term is derived from **Dhāraṇā** (धारणा), which means "concentration," "holding," or "retention." In the context of yoga, particularly in Patañjali’s *Yoga Sutras*, Dhāraṇā is the sixth limb of Ashtanga Yoga, referring to the practice of focused concentration where the mind is fixed on a single point or object. The suffix **-āne** in the locative case suggests "in the act of concentration" or "in the state of holding." Thus, Dhāraṇāne implies the process or state of concentrated focus.
3. **Loha-Dhāraṇāne Combined**: The term Loha-Dhāraṇāne can be interpreted as "concentration on metal" or "the act of focusing on metal." It likely refers to a meditative or spiritual practice where the practitioner focuses their mind (*Dhāraṇā*) on the essence, form, or symbolic qualities of metal (*Loha*). In a yogic context, this could involve meditating on the qualities of metal (e.g., strength, stability, or transformation) to cultivate mental clarity or spiritual insight. In an alchemical or Tantric context, it might relate to practices involving metals, such as *rasāyana* (alchemy), where metals are purified or transformed, symbolizing inner purification or spiritual awakening. Alternatively, it could poetically suggest steadfast focus as unyielding as metal.
