# McKenna's Novelty Theory: Time and Complexity

Created at 2025/07/04 7:16 AM

[Study Notes: The End of Time - Novelty Theory by Terence McKenna](https://youtu.be/hL0yfxDe6jE?si=d9CuZo_M0PNuH8qa)

Main Ideas

	•	Novelty and Complexity:

	•	Novelty is characterized as complex and not inherently good or bad. It reflects a rise in contradictions and rapid changes in society.

	•	As novelty increases, society will face extreme and bizarre circumstances, prompting discussions and explanations regarding these shifts.

	•	Transformation Period:

	•	McKenna predicts that by 2012, significant advancements will occur, including:

	•	Artificial life and human cloning.

	•	Possible contact with extraterrestrials.

	•	The potential for human immortality.

	•	Concurrently, there will be societal issues like brutality, genocide, and famine due to inadequate systems.

	•	Historical Context:

	•	The collapse of the Soviet Union and the rise of the internet are cited as radical changes that many people don’t fully appreciate.

	•	Humans are adaptable, having survived various historical challenges, which will be crucial as society faces upcoming tests.

	•	Crisis of Tradition:

	•	The breakdown of traditional norms is alarming to some, but this transformation is viewed as an essential evolution rather than resistance to change.

Key Concepts

	•	Novelty Theory:

	•	A framework developed by McKenna since the 1970s, viewing time as a process of increasing complexity.

	•	Complexification:

	•	The idea that the universe is moving toward greater complexity over time, which is contrary to the second law of thermodynamics (which suggests decay into disorder).

	•	Asymptotic Curve:

	•	Concept illustrating that the rate of complexification increases over time, leading to a point where complexity dominates reality.

	•	Omega Point:

	•	A theoretical moment in time when the rate of novelty reaches an overwhelming state, leading to a transformative event in human history.

Important Terms

	•	Transcendental Object:

	•	An ultimate goal or culmination of processes that humanity is approaching, often linked with spiritual or transformative experiences.

Examples and Predictions

	•	Mayan Calendar:

	•	McKenna draws parallels between his predictions and the Mayan calendar’s countdown to a significant event, suggesting a shared understanding of an impending transformation around 2012.

Summary of Complex Concepts

	•	McKenna believes that as humanity approaches the future, the pace of change will accelerate, leading to unprecedented levels of complexity and novelty. He emphasizes that this transformation is not just a human phenomenon but affects all life on Earth, suggesting a collective evolutionary leap rather than an isolated event.
