## Insight
Ah, the fascinating world of future studies! This flowchart is like a treasure map guiding us through the labyrinth of methodologies used to explore the unknown. Let's dive into some key insights from this intriguing diagram.

- Future-oriented thinking is at the heart of scenario planning. If a phenomenon isn't future-oriented, it might be a counterfactual or a nowcast, which focuses on what could have happened or what's happening now, respectively. This distinction helps in understanding whether we're dealing with potential futures or current realities.
- Considering the external context is crucial in future studies. If a phenomenon isn't about the external context, it might involve option evaluation or management dilemmas. This highlights the importance of understanding the broader environment in which future scenarios might unfold, ensuring that predictions are grounded in reality.
- Narrative descriptions play a significant role in making future scenarios relatable and understandable. Without a narrative, we might be looking at trend extrapolation or model simulations, which are more data-driven and less story-based. Narratives help in visualizing and communicating complex future possibilities in a more engaging way.
- Plausibility is a key criterion in distinguishing between realistic scenarios and pure fiction. If a scenario isn't plausibly possible, it might fall into the realm of science fiction or future fantasy. This helps in setting boundaries between what could realistically happen and what is purely imaginative.
- Systematized sets of scenarios provide a structured approach to future studies. If a phenomenon isn't part of a systematized set, it might be a prediction or a 'what-if' scenario. Systematization ensures that scenarios are comprehensive and cover a range of possibilities, enhancing their utility in planning and decision-making.
