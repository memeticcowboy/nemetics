# Exploring the Interconnections of Greek and Indian Philosophies in Thomas McEvilley's 'The Shape of Ancient Thought'

Created at 2025/07/15 8:20 PM

[This video features Thomas McEvilley](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8HAiTfOSP_w&t=1s) discussing his book **"The Shape of Ancient Thought"**, an influential empirical study examining the interconnections between **Greek and Indian philosophies** and broader **East-West cultural exchange** in antiquity.

**Key Points:**

- McEvilley argues that **Greek and Indian civilizations were never fully isolated**, instead **deeply influenced each other** through sustained contact beginning in the 6th century BCE, facilitated by their shared inclusion in the Persian Empire, their mutual inheritance from Bronze Age Mesopotamian cultures, and robust trade relations.
- He presents **artistic and religious motifs** (such as the hero-king archetype, tree goddesses, bull-men, and fertility icons) that appeared in **both the ancient Near East, India (Indus Valley), and Greece**, demonstrating the **diffusion of cultural and spiritual ideas** rather than their independent development.
- A central thesis is that **pre-Socratic Greek philosophy** was significantly shaped by **Indian philosophical doctrines** encountered via Persian intermediaries, challenging the notion that Western philosophical traditions arose in isolation.
- McEvilley explains that **colonial and nationalist agendas** promoted a narrative of “pure” Greek origin to justify claims of Western superiority, while evidence of deep influence and exchange was ignored or suppressed.
- He also explores later periods of exchange, notably after **Alexander the Great's conquest**, where **Greek colonies in Bactria (Afghanistan) and India** became centers of **intellectual and artistic fusion,** including the likely Greek origins of the first Buddha sculptures in Gandharan art.
- **Fundamental philosophical concepts**—for example, reincarnation and spiritual ascent—are shown to exist in both Indian and Greek pre-Socratic thought, contradicting stereotypes about the supposed rationality of Greece versus the mysticism of India.
- McEvilley concludes that the perceived **East-West dichotomy is largely a product of colonial-era ideology**, and real historical evidence points to **centuries of mutually enriching exchange** that formed the roots of both Asian and Western civilizations.

The video thus highlights a **major scholarly re-evaluation** of the foundations of Western thought, emphasizing **cultural interdependence over isolation** and challenging the colonial narratives that have shaped modern academic interpretations.
