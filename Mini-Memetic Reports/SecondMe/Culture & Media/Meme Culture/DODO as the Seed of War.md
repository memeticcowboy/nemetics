**DODO as the Seed of War**


1. Debt → Pretext for Expansion<br>

    - Personal, cultural, and national debts create existential pressure.
    - Nations fight to secure resources or repay imagined “owed” balances.
    - Example: Colonial expansion often justified as paying off internal scarcity—feeding an empire’s debt hunger.

2. Ownership → Territorialization<br>

    - Once debt shapes desire, ownership becomes sacred.
    - Land, water, and identity are clutched like lifelines.
    - What you own defines your security—and anything threatening it becomes an enemy.
    - “Ours” versus “theirs” becomes a moral frame.

4. Deadlines → Escalation of Violence<br>

    - Urgency pushes action toward preemptive strikes and rigid timelines.
    - Wars have always been sold under the pressure of time: “Act now, before they do.”
    - Deadlines convert fear into immediate aggression, locking leaders into cycles of escalation.

6. Othering → Moral Justification<br>

    - For war to gain collective consent, the enemy must become Other.
    - Once othered, empathy collapses, and atrocity becomes permissible.
    - Internal DODO becomes externalized as militarized DODO—war as the grid’s ultimate enforcement.
