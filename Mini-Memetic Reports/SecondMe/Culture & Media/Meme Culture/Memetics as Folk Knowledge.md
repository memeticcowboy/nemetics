**Memetics as Folk Knowledge?**


Because traditional academic treatments of memetics:

- Abstract memes into sterile, quantified models (e.g., Dawkins’ gene-analogy or Blackmore’s replicators).
- Presume a need for expert validation before ideas can be considered “real.”
- Often disengage from the communal and situated contexts where ideas actually propagate.


Folk knowledge, by contrast:

- Lives in stories, proverbs, customs, everyday language.
- Transmits through interpersonal resonance, ritual, repetition.
- Doesn’t require citation to have legitimacy within a community.


So to make memetics accessible and generative outside academia, you have to:

1. De-emphasize jargon.
2. Focus on lived examples.
3. Enable communities to notice and shape their own memetic patterns.
4. Provide simple tools—ideally AI-augmented—that don’t feel like learning an entire discipline.
