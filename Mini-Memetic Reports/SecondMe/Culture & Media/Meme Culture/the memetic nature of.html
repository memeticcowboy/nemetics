Clearing the Dust from the Corral

It finds clarity in the moment ideology poses as kin.

Ideologies (whether political, religious, or philosophical) often masquerade as essential frameworks for meaning and identity. Out here on the memetic frontier, these creeds don’t just ride with us, they ride us. Each claim to show the way yet often fences the range to ensure its own survival. From a memetic lens, these frameworks echo self-replicating code—evolving less for truth than for transmissibility [1].

Once seeded, ideology grows stubborn. Confirmation bias, cognitive dissonance, and belief perseverance act like thorns and burrs, we don’t just carry the creed, we defend it like it’s our own hide.

Susan Blackmore calls memes universal replicators, thriving through fidelity, fecundity, and longevity [2]. Ideologies act like meme herds: those that breed better believers and fend off questions stampede further.

Susan Blackmore expanded on this framework by examining memes as universal replicators characterized by high-fidelity replication, fecundity, and longevity. She argues that memes have evolved to become increasingly effective replicators, though perhaps not yet as refined as genes . This evolutionary pressure helps explain why certain ideological systems become more rigid and absolutist over time - those variants that most effectively ensure their own reproduction tend to persist and spread.

So, can we wrangle these ideologies without getting tied down?

To escape the herd-think trap, we need more than skepticism—we need memetic literacy. This means learning to recognize the shape, signal, and survival instinct of an idea. Which ones embed in the nervous system? Which ones ask to be repeated? Which hitch a ride through humor, shame, or tribal flattery? Memetic literacy arms the rider with pattern-sense, not just content awareness. It makes visible the hooks beneath the headline, the loops within belief, and the brands itching to burn.

One of its most vital practices? Meta-ideological awareness (MIA). It offers a different mount. Instead of riding with blinders, MIA invites us to see how saddles get stitched in the first place. It lets us hold belief like a tool, not a tattoo. When done right, it turns ideology from a master of the mind into a companion of the journey—something you ride with, not something that rides you.

Memeplex Mechanics: How Ideologies Hitch a Ride

Richard Dawkins framed memes as cultural genes, replicating via mimicry, not merit [4]. What spreads best isn’t what’s most real—but what hooks attention. An ideology with a slogan outruns a nuanced thought 10-to-1.

Memeplexes are the interwoven meme systems you’ll find reinforcing themselves like gospel choirs. Susan Blackmore notes that memes evolve by reinforcing internal cohesion and selecting for durability [2]. Some familiar defenses:

  • Consistency Armor: Every piece props up the next. Tug one thread and the whole coat unravels (think: scripture reinforces authority which reinforces scripture).

  • Doubt Immunity: "Questioning means betrayal." So dissent gets treated like disloyalty.

  • Villain Scripts: Outsiders become caricatures—good vs evil, us vs them [5].

Locked tight, these memeplexes resist novelty. And a range without mutation dies in drought.

But memeplexes don’t just live in minds, they build We-Spheres. A We-Sphere is the shared atmospheric bubble where stories, norms, and emotions resonate across bodies. Think rituals, inside jokes, flags waved at rallies. Memeplexes generate these collective habitats by looping belief into group identity, shaping not just what we think—but who we belong to.

That’s why prying loose a single memeform from your mind can feel like betrayal: you’re not just questioning an idea, you’re rattling the bonds of the campfire that warmed you.

Mental Bear Traps: Why We Cling to the Brand

Why do ideologies cling like burrs? It feels the resonance between belief and body. Psychological traps make ideology sticky:

  • Cognitive Dissonance: We twist facts to protect old beliefs [6]. “Fake news” serves as shield against inner doubt [6]..

  • Belief Perseverance: Even when debunked, ideas persist—especially when they stroke identity [7].

  • Backfire Effect: When challenged, we double down. Facts become threats, not revelations [8]..

These aren’t bugs—they’re features. They shield the selfplex: a stitched-together coat of memory, ritual, and belief loops that feels like a person.

I think of them as I-Tubes.

I-Tube refers to the individual's perceptual sheath and the lens that filters the world before it becomes thought. It twists the prairie to match our gaze. When fogged by ideology, the I-Tube turns into a tunnel, guiding only what agrees and blurring what doesn't. Cleared through breath and wonder, it becomes a tool of discernment rather than distortion.

Mirage Maps: When Ideologies Pretend to Know Everything

Totalizing ideologies promise the whole map of human life, but they’re mirages in the desert of reality. Karl Popper, in The Open Society and Its Enemies (1945), roasted them as closed systems—dogmatic setups that squash debate for the sake of certainty. History backs him up:

  • Capitalism: Sparks innovation, yet often deepens inequality (Piketty, 2014) [9].

  • Communism: Preaches equality but calcifies into control (Kornai, 1992) [10].

  • Liberalism: Uplifts autonomy yet may fray communal threads (Putnam, 2000) [11].

No single framework fits the shifting terrain. Even new ideologies like Dugin’s Fourth Political Theory replicate old traps—trading one cage for another [12]. MIA avoids that bait. Instead of new dogmas, it scouts adaptable routes.

Ride Light: Dewey, Watts, and Feyerabend on Living Without the Brand

Watts rode thought like river current. He called fixed beliefs fences on flowing ground [13]. Instead of gripping truths, he let them pass like scenery.

Dewey, meanwhile, packed a pragmatic saddlebag—belief as tool, not treasure. He judged beliefs by their consequences, mistrusting fixed truths. Instead, he anchored knowledge in lived inquiry [14].

Paul Feyerabend would ride even looser. In Against Method, he lit dynamite under methodological orthodoxy, claiming, “Anything goes” [17]. But he didn’t mean chaos—he meant freedom: the kind that emerges when no method gets crowned king. Where Dewey treats belief as tool, Feyerabend treats method as mood—something tested by what it does to the soul, the field, the possibility-space of imagination.

His anarchic stance fits memetic ecology like a hand-stitched glove: ideas survive not because they obey, but because they bloom, adapt, and disturb just enough to wake us up.

All three nudge us to ride light, testing ideas against experience rather than swearing eternal loyalty. Watts dissolves the illusion of solidity; Dewey encourages adaptive use; Feyerabend tears down the temple of method to let imagination breathe. Rather than treating beliefs as fixed truths to be defended, these thinkers urge us to see knowledge as a living process—less Episteme, more Praxis.

Practicing Meta-Ideological Awareness (MIA)

Meta-ideological awareness (MIA) is the art of staying atop the ideological herd, not penned in by it. It fends off hollow patterns by naming the reins before they slip into the skin. Here’s how:

  1. Scout the Range: Track diverse views. Map where fence lines rise (Haidt, 2012) [16].

  2. Spot the Reins: Notice when discomfort signals tethered belief.

  3. Ride Easy: Use ideologies for traction—not for identity. Swap saddles when the horse changes.

Integral Life frames MIA as capacity to reflect on worldview architecture—not just content [15]. It means walking the trail while knowing the map floats.

Conclusion: Freedom Beyond the Fence

It feeds the cycle with stories that loosen their grip.

Ideologies give warmth—but cling too long and they singe. Meta-ideological awareness doesn’t leave you cold. It teaches how to sit by every fire without forgetting the stars.
