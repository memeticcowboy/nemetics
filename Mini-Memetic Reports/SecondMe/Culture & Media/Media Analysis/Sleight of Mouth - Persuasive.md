# Sleight of Mouth: Persuasive Language Techniques

Created at 2024/07/08 8:37 PM

Robert Dilts developed the Sleight of Mouth patterns as a system of persuasive language techniques for reframing beliefs and changing minds. The patterns were modeled based on the persuasion skills of Richard Bandler, co-founder of Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP)[1][3].

There are 14 main Sleight of Mouth patterns, which can be grouped into four basic types:

1. Change meaning
2. Change cause  
3. Comparisons
4. Change logical level

Some key patterns include:

- Hierarchy of Criteria: Appealing to higher-level values or priorities
- Consequence: Pointing out potential results of a belief
- Redefine: Giving a new meaning or interpretation to a statement
- Chunk Up/Down: Shifting to more general or specific levels
- Counter Example: Providing an exception that challenges the belief
- Metaphor/Analogy: Using comparisons to reframe the situation
- Intent: Focusing on the positive intention behind a behavior

The patterns provide ways to respond to stated beliefs and reframe them, allowing one to be more persuasive in conversations and debates[2][3]. Mastering these techniques can help in changing limiting beliefs, both in oneself and others.

Dilts systematized these patterns into specific categories and developed a mind map to make them easier to learn and apply in real-time communication[3]. While powerful, the techniques require practice to use effectively and ethically in facilitating positive changes in thinking and behavior.
