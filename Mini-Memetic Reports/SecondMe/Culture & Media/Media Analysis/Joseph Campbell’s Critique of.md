# Joseph Campbell’s Critique of Yellow Journalism as Modern Mythmaking Manipulation

Created at 2025/11/15 1:04 PM

Key places where Campbell explicitly talks about yellow journalism:<br><br>1.  The Power of Myth (1988 book/interviews with Bill Moyers, Chapter 6 – “The Gift of the Goddess” section) : Campbell recounts how Hearst, wanting a war to sell papers, sent artist Frederic Remington to Cuba. When Remington wired back that there was no war and he wanted to come home, Hearst famously replied:<br><br>**“Please remain. You furnish the pictures and I’ll furnish the war.”**<br><br>Campbell’s point: the press lord had become a secular priest who could deliberately manufacture a national myth (the noble crusade to “free” Cuba) for profit and power. In Campbellian terms, Hearst hijacked the hero’s journey archetype and turned it into a commercial and political weapon. Instead of genuine myth serving the psyche and society, you get counterfeit myth serving circulation numbers.<br><br>2.  In lectures (especially late 1970s – mid 1980s) : He often contrasted the traditional role of the storyteller/shaman (who aligns the tribe with deeper truths) with the modern journalist who can now “create the war that the artist draws.” He saw yellow journalism as the moment when the printing press fully revealed its potential to become the new “sorcerer” — capable of conjuring collective hallucinations on a mass scale.<br><br>3.  Broader Campbellian framing:  Campbell viewed all media as the modern equivalent of tribal mythology. When it’s responsible, it orients people toward meaning; when it’s irresponsible (yellow), it becomes what he called “counterfeit mythology” — emotionally charged stories that feel profound but actually manipulate for ulterior motives (profit, power, ideology). In his terminology, yellow journalism is the dark side of the “shamanism of the press.”<br><br>So while he never analyzed clickbait Twitter or Substack gossip columns directly, his Hearst example is almost always the one he reached for when warning about the demonic potential of mass media. If he were alive in 2025, he would almost certainly say that algorithmic outrage feeds and anonymous engagement-farming accounts are simply yellow journalism reborn — same archetypal corruption, new technological skin.<br><br>In short: Joseph Campbell saw yellow journalism as the original sin of modern mythmaking: the moment the storyteller stopped serving the soul of society and started selling war, fear, and sensation instead.
