# Modern Examples of DoubleThink in Politics, Media, and Business

Created at 2024/05/18 12:21 PM

Modern examples of DoubleThink often involve cognitive dissonance, where individuals hold two contradictory beliefs simultaneously. Here are some additional examples and elaborations:

1. Nationalism and globalism: Political leaders may promote nationalistic policies, emphasizing the importance of national sovereignty and border control, while simultaneously engaging in global trade agreements and multinational alliances that seemingly undermine those very principles.

2. War and peace: Governments might justify military interventions or wars as necessary for maintaining peace and stability, essentially framing war as a means to achieve peace. This contradiction is often accompanied by propaganda that portrays the enemy as a threat to peace, even if the conflict itself is causing significant harm and destruction.

3. Privacy and surveillance: Authorities may claim to respect citizens' privacy rights while engaging in mass surveillance programs or collecting personal data without adequate safeguards. The public is expected to accept the notion that their privacy is being protected, even as evidence suggests otherwise.

4. Equality and discrimination: Institutions or individuals might profess a commitment to equality and diversity while simultaneously engaging in or tolerating discriminatory practices. For instance, a company may claim to value diversity in its hiring processes but have a workforce that is notably homogeneous.

5. Health and unhealthy products: Corporations, particularly in the food and beverage industry, may market products as healthy or beneficial while their offerings are actually detrimental to consumers' well-being. For example, a sugary breakfast cereal might be advertised as a nutritious start to the day, despite its high sugar content and low nutritional value.

6. Environmental responsibility and consumerism: Companies may present themselves as environmentally conscious while engaging in practices that contribute to pollution, deforestation, or other ecological harms. Consumers are encouraged to buy "green" products or support "eco-friendly" initiatives, even as the overall impact of consumerism on the environment remains significant.

These examples demonstrate how DoubleThink can manifest in various aspects of modern life, from politics and media to business and personal choices. By promoting contradictory beliefs or actions, those in power can manipulate public opinion, maintain control, and avoid accountability for their inconsistencies.​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​
