Noam Chomsky and Edward S. Herman, in their book* [Manufacturing Consent: The Political Economy of the Mass Media (1988),](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_Consent)* outlined a **propaganda model** that describes how media filters information to serve the interests of powerful elites. They identified **five filters** that shape news content, effectively "manufacturing" public consent for policies and ideologies that benefit those in power. These filters are:

1. **Ownership**: Media outlets are typically owned by large corporations or conglomerates with vested interests in maintaining the status quo. These owners influence editorial decisions to align with their economic and political goals.
2. **Advertising**: Media relies heavily on advertising revenue. Advertisers favor content that aligns with their commercial interests, pressuring media to avoid controversial or anti-corporate narratives that might alienate sponsors or audiences.
3. **Sourcing**: Media outlets depend on official sources (e.g., government, corporate spokespeople) for information, as these are seen as "credible" and provide steady content. This reliance filters out perspectives that challenge official narratives, marginalizing dissenting voices.
4. **Flak**: Powerful groups (e.g., governments, corporations) produce "flak"—criticism, lawsuits, or lobbying—to discipline media that deviate from approved narratives. Fear of flak encourages self-censorship among journalists and editors.
5. **Ideology** (originally "anti-communism," later broadened): A dominant ideological framework (e.g., free-market capitalism, anti-terrorism) shapes media narratives. Stories are filtered to reinforce this ideology, sidelining alternative worldviews as fringe or illegitimate.
