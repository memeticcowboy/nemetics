**Synopsis of "How to Combat the Growing Synthetic Content in Disinformation Campaigns" in the Context of Synthetic Influence Campaigns**
The article, authored by a co-owner of nDemand Consulting, discusses the rising threat of synthetic content—digitally manipulated text, images, audio, and videos, including deepfakes—used in disinformation campaigns, as highlighted by a 2021 FBI report. Synthetic influence campaigns, often orchestrated by foreign actors from countries like China and Russia, leverage AI and machine learning to create fake online profiles, fraudulent articles, and fabricated identities to manipulate public opinion and sow unrest. Examples include fake journalist profiles with synthetically generated images and backstories, some of which have been published by media outlets, and disinformation campaigns in regions like Angola and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, where synthetic content has disrupted crisis-related messaging and fueled social unrest.
The FBI anticipates a significant increase in such campaigns within 12–18 months, with an emerging threat called "business identity compromise" (BIC), where synthetic content is used to manipulate corporate identities for unauthorized access to systems and data, potentially causing reputational and operational damage. To counter these threats, businesses are advised to adopt robust defensive measures: fact-checking information, verifying source credentials, securing websites by hiding source code, using enterprise antivirus software, operating within secure VPNs, and implementing multifactor authentication. Employee training on recognizing synthetic content and reporting phishing attempts is also critical. By limiting publicly available employee data and staying vigilant, businesses can mitigate the risks posed by synthetic influence campaigns, which combine AI-driven content manipulation with traditional social engineering tactics like phishing to achieve significant impact.

**🛡️ Combatting Synthetic Influence Campaigns: Understanding the Threats and Solutions**

- **AI-Driven Disinformation**: Synthetic content, including deepfakes and fake personas, is increasingly used to manipulate public discourse. This technology allows malicious actors to create convincing narratives that can mislead the public and disrupt societal norms. The use of AI-generated media can lead to significant misinformation, as seen in various global contexts.

- **Cybersecurity Threats**: The rise of Business Identity Compromise (BIC) represents a new frontier in cybersecurity risks. This tactic involves using synthetic content to impersonate corporate identities, leading to unauthorized access to sensitive systems and data. The potential for reputational and operational damage is substantial, making it a critical area for businesses to address.

- **Geopolitical Manipulation**: State-sponsored actors, particularly from countries like China and Russia, exploit synthetic content to destabilize societies. By creating fake online profiles and disseminating fraudulent information, these actors can incite unrest and manipulate public opinion, particularly in vulnerable regions such as Angola and the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

- **Crisis Messaging Disruption**: Synthetic influence campaigns have been shown to disrupt crisis-related messaging, complicating communication during emergencies. This manipulation can lead to confusion and hinder effective responses, exacerbating the impact of crises on affected populations.

- **Anticipated Growth of Threats**: The FBI predicts a significant increase in synthetic influence campaigns within the next 12–18 months. This forecast underscores the urgency for organizations to prepare for and counteract these evolving threats.

- **Defensive Measures for Businesses**: To combat synthetic influence campaigns, businesses should implement robust defensive strategies. These include fact-checking information, verifying source credentials, and securing websites. Utilizing enterprise antivirus software and operating within secure VPNs are also essential steps.

- **Employee Training and Awareness**: Training employees to recognize synthetic content and report phishing attempts is crucial. By fostering a culture of vigilance and awareness, organizations can better protect themselves against the risks posed by disinformation campaigns.

- **Limiting Public Data Exposure**: Reducing the amount of publicly available employee data can mitigate risks associated with synthetic influence campaigns. By controlling the information that is accessible, businesses can decrease the likelihood of being targeted by malicious actors.

By understanding these key themes and implementing strategic measures, organizations can better navigate the challenges posed by synthetic influence campaigns and safeguard their integrity in an increasingly digital world.
