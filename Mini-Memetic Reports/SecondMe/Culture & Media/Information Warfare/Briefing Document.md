## Briefing Document: Information Warfare as a Future Weapon of Mass-Disruption in Africa

This briefing document reviews the main themes and critical insights from Rianne van Vuuren's article, "Information Warfare as Future Weapon of Mass-disruption, Africa 2030s Scenarios" (Journal of Futures Studies, 2018). The paper explores the evolving nature of information warfare (IW) and its potential to disrupt African societies by the 2030s.

**Key Themes:**

1. **Information Warfare: A Multifaceted Threat**

- Van Vuuren defines IW as "actions focused on destabilizing or manipulating the core information networks of a state or entity in society with the aim to influence the ability and will to project power as well as efforts to counter similar attacks by an opposing entity and/or state."
- IW encompasses three distinct manifestations:
- **Netwar:** Conflict waged at the societal level using networked forms of organization.
- **Cyber warfare:** Power-related conflict occurring in cyberspace.
- **Psychological operations:** Conflict aimed at influencing people's minds and beliefs.

1. **Driving Forces Shaping the Future of Information Warfare in Africa**

- An environmental scan within the Technological, War/Conflict, Economical, Political, and Social (TWEPS) framework reveals three overarching trends:
- **Rapid technological innovation:** Leading to both advancements and inequalities.
- **Increased global integration and the rise of networks:** Empowering both state and non-state actors.
- **Accelerated societal transformation:** Creating a constantly evolving landscape.
- Two Delphi studies involving security and multidisciplinary experts identified key driving forces:
- **Shifting global power dynamics:** From developed to developing countries.
- **Increasing societal integration and polarization:** Leading to systemic stresses.
- **ICT's crucial role in society:** Creating vulnerabilities and opportunities.

1. **Plausible Information Warfare Scenarios for Africa in the 2030s**

- Four scenarios are developed based on the level of ICT embeddedness and societal integration/polarization:
- **"Shango Rejuvenated":** High ICT embeddedness, high integration. IW is a preferred tool for power projection and consolidation.
- **"Gaunab Rising":** High ICT embeddedness, high polarization. IW is rife and expanding at all societal levels.
- **"Inkanyamba Reduced":** Low ICT embeddedness, high polarization. Cyberwarfare is limited, but cognitive aspects of IW (netwar and psychological operations) are prevalent.
- **"Tsunigoab Revived":** Low ICT embeddedness, high integration. IW is present but at lower levels.

1. **Propositions for the Future of Information Warfare**

- IW will be a significant national security threat by the 2030s.
- Multi-lateral measures will prove ineffective in controlling IW.
- Societal polarization will exacerbate IW's threat.
- Innovative, networked actions will constantly reshape IW's manifestation.
- IW poses a potential threat to individual freedom from both state and non-state actors.
- IW will become deeply ingrained in society as virtual and real worlds merge.

**Important Facts and Quotes:**

- "Information in all its manifestations – from data to wisdom – has been central to institutional power since the dawn of humankind."
- "The African Union’s Agenda 2063 foresee a peaceful and secure Africa an integrated, prosperous and peaceful Africa, driven by its own citizens and representing a dynamic force in the international arena."
- "Information warfare will become a growing option for power projection."
- "Symbolic, information-related phenomena are increasingly impacting behaviour."
- "Social media is a significant part of communication and this is expected to grow in the future."

**Conclusion:**

Van Vuuren's research underscores the growing importance of understanding and addressing the multifaceted threat of information warfare. The article's scenarios provide valuable frameworks for anticipating and mitigating the disruptive potential of IW in Africa and beyond. As we move towards an increasingly interconnected and digitally dependent future, proactive strategies for building resilience and fostering international collaboration are crucial to navigating the complex landscape of information warfare.
