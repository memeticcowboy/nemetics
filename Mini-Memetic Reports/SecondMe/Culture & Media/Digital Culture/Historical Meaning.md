Historical Meaning
In ancient Greek, “Oikos” referred to a household or family. This wasn’t just the people living together—it included:
	•	The house itself
	•	The estate or land
	•	All members, such as family, slaves, and sometimes livestock
The oikos was a cornerstone of Greek society, functioning as the basic unit of social, economic, and political life. Each household was like a small, self-sustaining community, central to how ancient Greeks organized their world.
Modern Uses
Today, “Oikos” has been adapted into various fields beyond its original context:
	•	Ecology: In environmental science, “oikos” relates to a habitat or ecological niche. It’s the root of words like “oikosystem” or “ecosystem,” which describe how organisms interact with their surroundings.
	•	Philosophy and Sociology: Scholars sometimes use “oikos” to explore domestic or familial structures, especially when studying ancient societies and their social frameworks.
