# SIMPLIFY Communication: Clear, Concise, Impactful Messaging

Created at 2025/08/31 10:53 AM

- SIMPLIFY Communication Template<br>
    - S — Streamline Ideas<br>
        - Distill into a one-sentence anchor message
        - Eliminate jargon; use everyday language
        - State what is most important here (guiding principle)
        - Acknowledge: This is a simplification of a deeper system
    - 
    - I — Identify the Core Context<br>
        - Use a simple metaphor/analogy (audience-friendly)
        - Define boundaries: relevant vs. noise
        - Explicitly name system type (machine, organism, ecosystem)
        - State main assumptions and uncertainties
    - 
    - M — Minimize Perceived Layers<br>
        - Collapse details into 2–3 themes
        - Use visuals or bullets to reduce overload
        - Show which complexities are being set aside
    - 
    - P — Present the Core Message Early<br>
        - State the decision/action in one line
        - Immediately follow with “why this matters”
        - Clarify what changes vs. what does not (stability anchors)
    - 
    - L — Link to Pain and Value<br>
        - Describe the current pain point or risk
        - Show the consequence of ignoring it
        - Connect solution to value delivered
        - Identify small move → big effect leverage point
    - 
    - I — Illustrate Audience Wins<br>
        - Translate system benefits into personal gains (WIIFM)
        - Role-specific framing: Execs, Teams, Customers
        - Show cross-system benefits (how parts reinforce the whole)
    - 
    - F — Focus on Engagement and Feedback<br>
        - Invite early questions and reactions
        - Share how feedback will be used
        - Define review cadence and checkpoints
        - Highlight safe-to-fail pilots before scaling
    - 
    - Y — Your Target Takeaway<br>
        - End with a sticky phrase or heuristic
        - Reinforce the core logic in plain speech
        - One line stakeholders should repeat afterward
    -

## Insight


*  The "SIMPLIFY Communication Template" is structured as a mind map, which is a visual tool used to organize information hierarchically, showing relationships among pieces of the whole. This format is particularly useful for brainstorming, planning, and summarizing information in a way that is easy to understand and remember.
*  The template emphasizes streamlining ideas by distilling them into a one-sentence anchor message and eliminating jargon. This approach aligns with the communication strategies advocated by experts like Chip and Dan Heath in their book "Made to Stick," where they highlight the importance of simplicity and clarity in making ideas memorable and impactful.
*  Identifying the core context involves using metaphors and analogies to make complex systems more accessible. This technique is rooted in cognitive psychology, which suggests that people understand new information better when it is linked to familiar concepts. For example, comparing a business strategy to a sports team's game plan can help stakeholders grasp the strategy more intuitively.
*  Minimizing perceived layers by collapsing details into themes and using visuals is a nod to cognitive load theory, which posits that our working memory has limited capacity. By reducing the amount of information presented at once, the template aims to prevent cognitive overload and improve comprehension.
*  Presenting the core message early and linking it to pain points and value are strategies that tap into the audience's immediate interests and motivations. This approach is consistent with persuasive communication principles, which suggest that highlighting the benefits and addressing the concerns of the audience can increase engagement and buy-in.
*  The template includes a focus on engagement and feedback, highlighting the importance of inviting questions and sharing how feedback will be used. This reflects the principles of participatory communication, which emphasizes the active involvement of stakeholders in shaping the message and the process.
*  The final step, "Your Target Takeaway," focuses on ending with a sticky phrase or heuristic. This is designed to ensure that the core message is not only understood but also remembered and repeated by stakeholders, reinforcing the communication's impact.
*  The reference to "Change w/Bob-RJ" links to a tweet by RJ Burkhart, suggesting that this communication template is part of a broader discussion or initiative related to change management and organizational communication. This connection implies that the template is intended to facilitate smoother transitions and better understanding during periods of change.
