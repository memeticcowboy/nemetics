Introduction
In this video, Doug Fall explores a unique form of the English language known as E Prime. This linguistic construct was developed to enhance clarity in communication by eliminating the verb “to be” and its various forms. Fall argues that adopting E Prime can help writers, speakers, and particularly actors convey their ideas more clearly and avoid misunderstandings.
Overview of E Prime
	•	Definition: E Prime is a form of English that eliminates all forms of the verb “to be” (including is, are, was, were, am, etc.).
	•	Purpose: The aim is to enhance clarity, reduce misconceptions, and present statements in a way that reflects personal observations rather than asserting absolute truths.
Historical Context
	•	Origins:
	•	The concept of eliminating “isness” from language originates from Alfred Korzybski’s work in the early 20th century, particularly his book Science and Sanity.
	•	D. David Bourland Jr. later formalized E Prime, advocating for its use to improve clarity in scientific and political discourse.
Key Principles of E Prime
	•	Elimination of “isness”:
	•	The removal of “to be” statements fosters a more observational approach rather than definitive claims about identity or predication.
	•	For instance, instead of saying “John is grumpy,” one might say “John appears grumpy in the office,” which maintains a level of subjectivity and reduces assumptions.
	•	Subjectivity and Clarity:
	•	E Prime encourages the speaker or writer to include themselves in the observation, utilizing phrases such as “it seems to me,” “it appears,” or “I think.”
	•	This personalizes statements and acknowledges the observer’s perspective, which enhances clarity.
Practical Applications of E Prime
	1.	In Scientific Writing:
	•	E Prime can eliminate ambiguities associated with scientific claims. For example, instead of saying “the photon is a particle,” one might say “the photon registers as a particle under certain conditions.”
	2.	In Political Discourse:
	•	Political statements often use absolutes that can mislead or polarize. Through E Prime, one might say, “I believe X has questionable ethics” rather than stating “X is corrupt,” providing a more nuanced viewpoint.
	3.	In Personal Communication:
	•	Reflecting on personal experiences rather than making broad statements can foster better understanding and reduce conflict. For instance, instead of “all men are pigs,” one could say “many men I have encountered demonstrate entitlement that disrespects women.”
Examples Demonstrating E Prime
	•	Identity Statements:
	•	Instead of saying, “I am an actor,” which implies exclusivity, one might say, “The last time I saw Doug, he acted in a show,” making the statement verifiable and less absolute.
	•	Generalizations:
	•	Rather than stating “lawyers are corrupt,” one could say “in my experience, I have often felt that lawyers prioritize their interests over clients’ needs,” which allows for personal interpretation and reduces blanket accusations.
	•	Metaphorical Language:
	•	The video emphasizes that common phrases often lead to confusion. For instance, saying “it is raining” can be rephrased as “I observe rain falling,” directly involving the observer in the statement.
The Impact of Language on Thought
Fall discusses how language shapes our thoughts and perceptions. He suggests that using E Prime can help clarify personal narratives and reduce self-limiting beliefs. Statements like “I am depressed” carry a heavy psychological load, while rephrasing them to “I feel down about my current situation” can alleviate some of that weight.
The Dangers of “To Be” Language
Fall cautions against the hypnotic nature of “to be” statements:
	•	Manipulation: Historical figures, like Hitler, used definitive statements to dehumanize groups, illustrating how language can manipulate mass opinion.
	•	Advertising: Advertisers use similar tactics to create persuasive slogans, often employing “to be” language to assert claims that may not hold up under scrutiny.
Conclusion and Call to Action
In conclusion, Doug Fall advocates for the use of E Prime as a tool for enhancing clarity and reducing misunderstanding in communication. He encourages viewers, especially those in the acting profession, to practice writing and speaking in E Prime to improve their clarity and effectiveness.
