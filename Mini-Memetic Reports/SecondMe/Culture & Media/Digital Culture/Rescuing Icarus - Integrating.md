# Rescuing Icarus: Integrating Playfulness and Creativity into American Air Power Culture

### Summary of Jason Trew's "Rescuing Icarus" (2018)

"Rescuing Icarus" is a key chapter in Jason Trew's 2018 doctoral dissertation, *“No One Comes Close”: US Airmen and the Cultural Origins of American Air Power, 1918–1945*, submitted to Auburn University. Drawing on the Greek myth of Icarus (symbolizing reckless ambition) and Daedalus (embodying prudent craftsmanship), Trew reframes "airmindedness"—a multifaceted cultural, psychological, and strategic awareness of aviation's impacts—as a dialectical force essential to American air power's development. Rather than dismissing Icarus as mere hubris, Trew "rescues" him as a metaphor for the playful, creative daring (*metis*, or cunning intelligence) that complements technological restraint (*techne*), enabling adaptive innovation in warfare and society.

#### Core Concepts and Historical Context

- **Airmindedness Defined**: Encompassing practical (e.g., commerce, defense logistics), romantic/spiritual (e.g., flight as transcendent "miracle" or "gospel," evoking awe and superhuman evolution), and cultural dimensions (e.g., art, dance, and literature inspired by aerial perspectives of omniscience and hierarchy). Interwar examples include Billy Mitchell's prophetic yet court-martialed visions in *Winged Defense* (1925), Henry H. Arnold's emphasis on thrill and independence, and cultural phenomena like Lindbergh's mythic status or aviation's role in nationalism and colonial control.
- **Interwar Evolution (1918–1939)**: Post-WWI, airmindedness balanced ecstatic bursts (e.g., barnstorming, transatlantic flights) with pragmatic corrections (e.g., Air Mail Scandal, 1930–31). It fostered doctrines at the Air Corps Tactical School (ACTS) prioritizing air superiority, surprise, and flexibility, while media and public enthusiasm mythologized flight as soul-liberating, masking vulnerabilities like economic crashes.

#### WWII Theories and Strategic Applications

Trew applies this to WWII air power theories, critiquing overly rigid models:

- **Industrial Web Theory**: Targeted economic disruptions (e.g., AWPD-1 plan, 1941) via cumulative, adaptive strikes—embodying *metis* over brute force (*bia*).
- **Strategic Paralysis (John Warden)**: Five-ring model (leadership to fielded forces) in *The Air Campaign* (1988) and Instant Thunder (1990), emphasizing precision, psyops, and minimal destruction for geopolitical leverage. Critiques highlight anomalies like Vietnam's failure to account for tactical innovations (e.g., Elwood Quesada, George Kenney).

#### Doctrinal Critiques and the "Icarian Solution"

Post-WWII U.S. Air Force doctrines (e.g., AFM 1-1, 1992) narrowed airmindedness to operational tactics, sidelining its inspirational and psychological roots—a "strategic paralysis" in thought. Scholarly biases in Air University papers reinforce this, ignoring affective elements like joy and awe. Trew proposes an "Icarian Solution": Integrate Icarus's boldness with Daedalus's caution through narrative, mythology, and playfulness to tackle "wicked problems." This revives cultural inheritance (e.g., "Aim High" motto), fosters innovation via metaphors (war as art/sport), and equips airmen for complex, flux-filled environments by blending material and mental forces.

#### Broader Implications

Ultimately, Trew advocates an "Odyssean" model of poly-*metis*—tactician, storyteller, adapter—for perpetual strategic renewal. By embracing incomplete theories and synthesis over analysis, airmindedness counters tech-centric dominance, ensuring air power's evolution amid technological cycles. The chapter synthesizes history, theory, and culture to argue that American air power's origins lie not just in machines, but in the imaginative daring of its pioneers.

### Play and Playfulness in the Context of Air-Mindedness

- **Contrast Between Daedalus and Icarus**<br>The myth of Daedalus and Icarus is used to illustrate two contrasting approaches: Daedalus represents rationality and pragmatism, while Icarus embodies playfulness, creativity, and high spirits. Icarus’ playfulness, though leading to his downfall, symbolizes imagination, innovation, and daring art.
- **Playfulness as a Missing Dimension**<br>Modern Air Force discourse and the concept of air-mindedness have largely emphasized the Daedalus model—practical, technical, and strategic thinking—while neglecting the playful, imaginative, and creative aspects represented by Icarus. This omission is seen as a loss because playfulness fosters innovation and inspiration.
- **Playfulness and Defense Professionals**<br>The serious nature of military work often leads defense professionals to disparage play and artistry. However, the document argues that playfulness is a fundamental moral of the Icarus myth and should be embraced rather than dismissed.
- **Creative Tension Between Rational and Playful**<br>Air-mindedness should be redefined to hold the rational (Daedalus) and playful (Icarus) dimensions in creative tension. This means combining practical skills with imaginative and philosophical thinking to foster innovation and strategic advantage.
- **Historical and Cultural Significance of Playfulness**<br>Early aviation enthusiasm included a sense of spiritual rebirth and exhilaration, where flying was not just a technical skill but an emotionally wondrous and inspiring experience. This spirit of adventure and playfulness was integral to the original concept of air-mindedness.
- **Playfulness in Strategy and Innovation**<br>The document references ancient history and scholarship linking military strategy with playfulness, suggesting that a playful approach to strategy can be beneficial. It cites examples where strategic thinking is described as “playful,” encouraging creativity and innovation.
- **Symbolic and Inspirational Role of Play**<br>Icarus’ daring and creativity symbolize the inspirational and artistic side of flight, which has been celebrated in poetry, literature, and Air Force culture (e.g., the poem *High Flight* and the Air Force Academy magazine named *Icarus*).

### Summary

Play and playfulness are essential but often overlooked components of air-mindedness. They represent creativity, innovation, and the inspirational spirit that complements the practical and strategic aspects of aviation and airpower. Embracing playfulness alongside rationality can lead to a more dynamic and effective approach to airpower and military strategy.
