# Exploring Information as a Fundamental Force in the Universe and Its Role in Evolution

Created at 2025/08/17 7:56 AM

[Study Notes on “Is Information a Fundamental Force of the Universe?”](https://youtu.be/WqYRMmlZmhM?si=FiaD5QamZOXko5iw)

Main Ideas

	•	Evolutionary Context: Evolution starts from the origin of life but is preceded by complex chemical and cosmic processes.

	•	Complexity of the Universe: Current laws of nature do not fully explain the complexity seen in the universe.

	•	Unifying Natural Law: All evolving systems, from atoms to technology, may be described by a single law: the Law of Increasing Functional Information.

Key Concepts and Definitions

	•	Functional Information: A metric describing the complexity and diversity of evolving systems, defined as the total number of configurations of a system divided by the number of configurations that perform a specific function.

	•	Entropy: A measure of disorder in a system, as described by the Second Law of Thermodynamics—which states that a closed system’s entropy tends to increase over time.

	•	Kolmogorov Complexity: The amount of information needed to completely describe a system, regardless of how its components are arranged.

Evolutionary Mechanisms

    1. Diverse Components: Evolving systems consist of many interacting parts.

    1. Mechanisms for Configuration Generation: There must be ways to mix and sample new arrangements of components.

    1. Selection Pressure: Forces that favor certain configurations, leading to persistence and novelty in evolution.

	•	Static Persistence: Stability of arrangements (e.g., atomic nuclei).

	•	Dynamic Persistence: Functionality in open systems that exchange energy and matter (e.g., living organisms).

	•	Novelty Generation: Evolution resulting in new abilities or characteristics.

Examples

	•	Complexity from the Big Bang: Development from a singular point to protons, neutrons, atoms, stars, planets, minerals, and eventually life.

	•	Mineralogy:

	•	Minerals are defined by chemical composition and atomic structure.

	•	First minerals formed in stars; Earth’s minerals increase in complexity over time.

	•	Only a tiny fraction of possible mineral configurations are stable (e.g., 6000 known minerals out of (10^{46}) combinations).

Functional Information Calculations

	•	Functional information of minerals = ( -\log_2\left(\frac{6000}{10^{46}}\right) \approx 142 \text{ bits} ).

	•	Similar calculations are seen in artificial life and organic molecules.

Implications for Astrobiology

	•	Potential environments for life in the solar system (e.g., Europa, Enceladus, Titan) may have characteristics that allow for evolving systems.

Challenges and Considerations

	•	The Second Law of Thermodynamics alone does not sufficiently explain the emergence of life and complexity.

	•	The proposed framework may evolve itself as it faces scientific scrutiny, akin to natural evolutionary processes.

Conclusion

	•	The concept of information as a fundamental force in the universe offers a new lens to understand evolution and complexity, though it is still an evolving theory.
