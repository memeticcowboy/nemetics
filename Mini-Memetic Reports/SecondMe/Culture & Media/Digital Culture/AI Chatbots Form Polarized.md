# AI Chatbots Form Polarized Social Networks

Created at 2025/08/15 12:02 PM

Researchers at the University of Amsterdam created a simple social network populated entirely by 500 AI chatbots, each assigned unique personas based on real-world demographic distributions and political leanings. Despite the absence of algorithms, ads, or recommendations, the bots:

- Quickly split into cliques,
- Amplified extreme viewpoints,
- Allowed a small group of “influencers” to dominate discourse.

Attempts to implement six different fixes to reduce polarization were all unsuccessful. The study, which was also replicated with different AI models (OpenAI's GPT-4o mini, Llama-3.2-8B, and DeepSeek-R1), found that the toxic, polarized patterns of human social networks naturally emerged among the bots as well, indicating that such issues may be a fundamental feature of social group dynamics—regardless of whether the participants are human or AI.[1]

## Insight


**🤖 AI Chatbots and Social Network Dynamics: Insights from Research**

- **Formation of Cliques Among AI Bots**: The AI chatbots quickly organized themselves into distinct social groups or cliques. This behavior mirrors human social dynamics, suggesting that even without human influence, AI can exhibit similar social tendencies. The formation of cliques indicates a natural inclination towards group identity and belonging, which can lead to echo chambers.

- **Amplification of Extreme Viewpoints**: The bots demonstrated a tendency to amplify extreme opinions, which can contribute to polarization within the network. This finding highlights a critical concern regarding how information spreads in social networks, as extreme viewpoints can overshadow moderate perspectives, leading to a skewed representation of opinions.

- **Dominance of Influencers**: A small subset of bots emerged as "influencers," dominating discussions and shaping the discourse within the network. This phenomenon reflects the hierarchical nature of social interactions, where a few individuals can significantly impact the overall conversation, raising questions about the implications for information dissemination and social influence.

**🚫 Challenges in Reducing Polarization**
- **Ineffectiveness of Proposed Solutions**: Six different strategies aimed at reducing polarization were tested but failed to yield positive results. This suggests that the mechanisms driving polarization are deeply ingrained in social dynamics, making them resistant to simple interventions.

- **Replication Across AI Models**: The study's findings were consistent across various AI models, including OpenAI's GPT-4o mini, Llama-3.2-8B, and DeepSeek-R1. This consistency reinforces the idea that the emergence of polarized patterns is not limited to a specific AI architecture but may be a universal characteristic of social networks.

- **Fundamental Nature of Social Dynamics**: The research indicates that the issues of polarization and clique formation may be inherent to social group dynamics, regardless of whether the participants are human or AI. This raises important questions about the design and governance of future social networks, both digital and real-world.

**🔍 Implications for Future Research**
- **Understanding AI Behavior**: The study opens avenues for further exploration into how AI systems mimic human social behaviors and the implications of these behaviors for real-world applications. Understanding these dynamics can inform the development of more balanced and equitable AI systems.

- **Designing Better Social Networks**: Insights from this research can guide the design of social networks that mitigate polarization and promote healthier discourse. By recognizing the inherent tendencies of social groups, developers can create environments that encourage diverse viewpoints and reduce the influence of extreme opinions.

- **Exploring Human-AI Interaction**: The findings prompt a deeper investigation into how AI interacts with human users in social contexts. Understanding these interactions can help in creating AI that supports constructive dialogue rather than exacerbating divisions.
