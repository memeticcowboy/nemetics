The speaker emphasizes that the conventional models of observers in quantum mechanics are insufficient and could be hindering progress in areas like quantum gravity.
	3.	Problem of Time in Quantum Gravity: The discussion addresses the “problem of time” in quantum gravity, which raises questions about how time is represented when gravity is quantized. This also relates to how we understand observations and outcomes in the universe, indicating the need for a new model of observers.
	4.	Sudoku Universe Concept: Adlam introduces the concept of a “Sudoku universe,” where the laws of nature function like Sudoku rules—defining valid patterns across all of spacetime instead of sequentially evolving through time. This perspective could provide insights into nonlocal phenomena observed in quantum mechanics.
	5.	Nonlocality and Temporal Aspects: Nonlocality is described as quantum correlations that defy classical explanations of causation. The speaker introduces the idea of temporal nonlocality, where influences cross time rather than just space, suggesting a more complex relationship between events than traditional models allow.
	6.	Epistemological Concerns: The speaker emphasizes the importance of understanding the epistemological implications of quantum theories. She argues that many interpretations, particularly the many-worlds interpretation, face significant challenges in explaining how we can know and assign probabilities to outcomes.
	7.	Advice for Researchers: Adlam advises upcoming researchers to pursue their genuine interests rather than follow trends in popular topics. She highlights the value of patience and persistence in research, as significant contributions often take time to gain recognition.
	8.	Connecting Physics and Philosophy: The interview also covers the intersection of physics and philosophy, with Adlam advocating for a more philosophical approach to understanding fundamental concepts in physics, such as causation, lawhood, and the nature of observers.
