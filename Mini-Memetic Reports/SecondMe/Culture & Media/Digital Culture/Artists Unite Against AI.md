# Artists Unite Against AI Copyright Infringement: Legal and Legislative Developments

Created at 2025/09/02 11:54 AM

## Artists Fighting Back Against AI Training on Their Work

### Major Copyright Lawsuits Gaining Ground

Artists have mobilized through **at least 16 major lawsuits** against virtually every major AI company, with courts beginning to rule in favor of creators. In *Andersen v. Stability AI*, a federal judge ruled that artists can pursue claims that AI companies including Stability AI, Midjourney, DeviantArt, and Runway **directly infringe copyright** by training models on copyrighted works without permission.[^11][^12][^13][^14]

Visual artists Sarah Andersen, Kelly McKernan, and Karla Ortiz have successfully advanced their class-action lawsuit, with the court finding that allegations of copying copyrighted works to train AI systems were "sufficient to plead direct infringement".[^13]

### Mass Artist Mobilization

**Over 20,000 creators** have signed public statements opposing unlicensed use of copyrighted works for AI training. The campaign, founded by former AI executive Ed Newton-Rex who quit Stability AI in protest, includes renowned artists like Amoako Boafo, Kennedy Yanko, Shantell Martin, and Hans Haacke, along with major authors and musicians including all five members of Radiohead.[^15]

The statement declares: "The unlicensed use of creative works for training generative AI is a major, unjust threat to the livelihoods of the people behind those works, and must not be permitted".[^15]

### Creative Industry Union Victories

**SAG-AFTRA has achieved groundbreaking AI protections** through multiple contract negotiations. The union's 2024 video game strike, lasting nearly 11 months, resulted in industry-leading AI protections requiring **explicit written consent** for digital replicas and **fair compensation** for AI use of performers' likenesses.[^16][^17][^18]

The new video game contract mandates that companies like Activision, Electronic Arts, and others must obtain performer consent before creating visual or vocal replicas, with automatic consent suspension during future strikes—preventing AI replacement of striking workers.[^17][^19]

SAG-AFTRA's commercial contract negotiations have gone further, **prohibiting AI training on covered work without union approval** and requiring specific costs when AI-generated "synthetic performers" are used.[^20]

### Legislative Wins for Creator Protection

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed **AB 1836 and AB 2602** in 2024, providing comprehensive AI protections for both living and deceased performers, including voice-over and motion capture work. The bills require "reasonable specificity" for AI use and grant rights to performers' estates.[^21]
