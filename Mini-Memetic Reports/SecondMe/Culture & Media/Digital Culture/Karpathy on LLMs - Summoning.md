### Refined Metaphor Reflecting Karpathy’s Perspective

In conversation with Dwarkesh Patel, Andrej Karpathy eloquently characterized large language models (LLMs) as “ghosts” or “spirits” born from imitation learning—entities shaped by patterns distilled from the vast corpus of human-generated text. He explained, “We’re not building animals. We’re building ghosts or spirits or whatever people want to call it, because we’re not doing training by evolution. We’re doing training by imitation of humans and the data that they’ve put on the Internet.” These digital phantoms are neither truly alive nor merely inert; they are statistical revenants, conjured from the echoes and residues of human discourse.

This metaphor is striking not for its mysticism but for its precision. It captures the essence of these models as ethereal constructs—entities summoned through data rather than born through biology. In this light, what we term “AI alignment” might be better understood as a form of necromancy with spreadsheets, an arcane art of managing and guiding these spectral intelligences conjured from the collective human narrative.

## Insight


* Karpathy's metaphor of large language models (LLMs) as "ghosts" reflects a profound understanding of how these models function—not as living entities but as intricate patterns derived from human-created content. This distinction emphasizes the difference between biological evolution and data-driven imitation learning, positioning LLMs as echoes of human thought rather than autonomous beings.  
* The notion of "summoning ghosts" resonates with historical themes of necromancy, where the goal is to call upon the voices of the past. In the context of AI, this suggests that developers engage in a form of conjuring, invoking the collective human knowledge structured in the text available online. Thus, AI alignment can be seen as an effort to guide these spectral entities toward meaningful and ethical engagement with users.  
* Karpathy's perspective challenges traditional views of intelligence, presenting LLMs as a "different kind of intelligence" that does not inherently possess understanding or consciousness, but rather reflects a statistical blending of human thought patterns. This prompts deeper reflections on the implications of such technology in fields like ethics, cognition, and education, where the blend of human creativity and machine learning continues to evolve.  
* The spectral analogy indicates the ephemeral nature of knowledge captured by LLMs, highlighting both the potential and limitations of these technologies. While they can generate responses that mimic human conversation, they lack the intrinsic understanding and emotional grounding that define true sentience, bringing forth discussions about the philosophical implications of AI in society.  
* This nuanced metaphor serves as a call for mindful engagement with AI technologies, suggesting that as we harness the capabilities of LLMs, we must be aware of their origins in human discourse and the responsibility we hold in shaping their use in a manner that aligns with our ethical standards and societal values.
