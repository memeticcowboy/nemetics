# Understanding Soft Authoritarianism: Features, Tactics, and Examples of Hybrid Regimes

Created at 2025/11/08 9:19 AM

Soft authoritarianism is a form of government that uses democratic processes like elections to maintain power while gradually eroding liberal democratic institutions from within. It is characterized by the use of legal and constitutional frameworks to consolidate power, suppress dissent, and undermine checks and balances, even as it maintains a facade of democratic legitimacy. This "hollowing out" of democracy is often achieved through methods like political polarization, disinformation, expansion of executive power, and control over independent institutions. <br>Key characteristics <br><br>• Electoral legitimacy: Soft authoritarian regimes often come to power through elections, creating a veneer of democratic consent. <br>• Gradual erosion: Instead of overt oppression, these systems systematically dismantle democratic norms and institutions over time, often by changing the rules from within. <br>• Legal and constitutional manipulation: Leaders use the law, the constitution, and the legal system to concentrate power and disadvantage opponents. <br>• Undermining checks and balances: They expand executive power and neutralize independent institutions such as the judiciary, civil service, and media. <br>• Suppression of dissent: Soft authoritarianism limits freedom of expression and uses regulatory power to punish critics, though it may not resort to outright violence. <br>• Disinformation and polarization: The spread of disinformation and the use of "scapegoating" to create social division are common tactics. <br>• Hybridity: Soft authoritarianism can be seen as a hybrid of democracy and authoritarianism, where the democratic framework is used to legitimize what are fundamentally authoritarian practices. <br><br>Examples and related concepts <br><br>• Illiberal democracy: Some scholars use this term to describe soft authoritarianism, though others find it too sympathetic to these regimes. <br>• Competitive authoritarianism: This is another related term used to describe regimes that hold elections but manipulate the playing field to maintain dominance. <br>• Dictablanda: A Spanish term for a "soft dictatorship" where authoritarian and democratic features are combined, though civil liberties may still be allegedly preserved. <br><br>AI responses may include mistakes.<br><br>
