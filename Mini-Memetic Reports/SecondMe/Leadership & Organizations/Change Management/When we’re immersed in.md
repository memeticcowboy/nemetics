When we’re immersed in complexity it’s harder for us to predict what could happen next.
Prediction is one of the brains primary, survival functions - to predict what could happen next meant we’d have a better chance of knowing where danger or opportunity lurked. 
But todays complexity means the brain has too many factors to consider when trying to figure out what could happen next. 
A recent study around the ‘AI Adoption Gap’ highlighted some aspects of AI adoption in organisations that I suspect many leaders did not predict: [Writer 2025 AI Survey]
=> More than 1 in 3 executives feel Gen-AI has been a massive disappointment so far.
=> Some employees admit to sabotaging their companies AI strategy, including 41% of Gen Z and Millennial workers.
=> Around 1 in 3 employees on average refuse to use AI tools or ditch AI-related training.
=> More than 1 in 3 employees said that AI-produced work that is not on par with their own.
=> Most executives say Gen-AI adoption is creating conflict and friction within their organisation.
=> Around 2/3 of business leaders say technology has led to division between IT teams and other lines of business. 
=> 42% of executives say it’s tearing their companies apart.
This may be unexpected to leaders who thought they were providing a valuable tool to their teams.
Leaders who bought into the massive hype that AI has generated, which is understandable, considering the huge leaps it’s made even just over the last few months.

	How will leaders reduce the conflict between teams?
We’re grappling with issues never before encountered and we’re poor at predicting outcomes in an increasingly complex world.
