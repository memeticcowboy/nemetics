# Span of Control (Two-Pizza Rule)

Created at 2025/06/08 11:08 AM

Keep in mind the concept of "span of control" and the "two-pizza rule" can apply to community coordination, particularly when it comes to organizing and managing teams of volunteers or community members.<br><br>**Heuristic:** Span of Control (Two-Pizza Rule)

**Description:** A manager should only directly supervise a limited number of people (classic management science suggests ~5–7). Jeff Bezos’s “two-pizza team” is a modern twist: no team (and by implication, no manager’s span) should be so large it needs more than two pizzas to feed. Keeping spans of control small maintains effective leadership and cohesive teams.

**Use Case:** Companies often limit how many direct reports a supervisor has, to ensure they can give sufficient attention and feedback to each. For example, a customer support lead might only manage 6 agents; if the team grows to 12, they’ll create another lead position. This prevents communication breakdown and manager burnout. Bezos applied this to keep teams autonomous and agile – a large project at Amazon is broken into many small, pizza-sized teams rather than one giant department .

**Supporting Quote:**  “Each team is small enough to be fed by two pizzas, typically 5-7 people.” – Amazon’s team guideline . Small teams mean a manager’s span is naturally limited, which improves accountability and clarity.- 

**Related Concept:** Law of Diminishing Returns (in communication) – as team size grows linearly, communication channels grow combinatorially. Beyond a certain point, adding people (or direct reports) yields less productivity and more confusion – hence the virtue of a limited span of control
