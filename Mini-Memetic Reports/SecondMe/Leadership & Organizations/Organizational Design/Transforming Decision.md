# Transforming Decision Intelligence: From Experimentation to Operational Excellence

Created at 2025/08/15 1:08 PM

1.	Strategic Shift

• Moving from high-level experimentation at scale toward full “decision intelligence” (DI) operations and global deployment

• Emphasis on turning digital “experiments” into operational processes, accelerating digital decision-making across functions

	2.	Market Momentum & Demand

• “The demand is actually catching up with us”—customers racing from proof-of-concepts (“Aera Learns”) to live deployments

• Fear of Missing Out (FOMO) evolving into a “Certainty of Missing Out” unless AI agents are adopted

• Clients no longer asking “why” but “how”—they want turnkey capability, not just analytics

	3.	Expanding Decision Coverage

• Beyond traditional supply-chain use cases into cross-practice: finance, procurement, demand creation, even waste-management micro-decisions (Dell example)

• 80% of operational/supply-chain decisions must be digitized to reduce risk

	4.	Platform & Technology

• “DI as an alternative to offshoring”—digitize processes instead of outsourcing, keeping full process control

• On-premises (“return to on-prem”) and hybrid deployment options in response to client security concerns

• Agentic AI: NLQ-style interfaces progressing toward agents that simulate, recommend, learn, and write back into systems

	5.	Decision Intelligence Agents

• Reframing Aera as a “DI Agent” or “agentic” platform, though current agent capabilities resemble advanced natural-language queries more than agentic agents and not yet anything like our idea of AI agents for DI

• Plan for agents to not only generate decisions but to “simulate increases,” write back results, learn from outcomes, and orchestrate across a DI network

	6.	Architecture & Experience

• The predict a "collapse of the traditional enterprise-software stack" especially the – System of Differentiation into:

– System of Record at the base with the

– System of Intelligence (the DI layer) over the top, and on top of the agentic orchestration

• One unified workspace where end-users, decision architects, and managers operate together.

Retention of system-of-differentiation functionality, but reimagined: manual decision interfaces give way to digital execution frameworks (e.g., self-driving-car analogy)

• make me think of the idea of a "Decision Control Room" model replacing individual operator intervention with ambient, agentic orchestration

	7.	Personas & UX

• Knowledge Worker (end user): receives recommended actions in an inbox—Accept, Modify, Dismiss—and writes back to systems

• Decision Architect (what we call a decision engineer): configures and fine-tunes decision “skills” i.e. models)

• Manager/Operator: oversees performance, real-time visibility, and learning metrics, i.e. monitoring and governance.

	8.	Other

• Clients demand rapid sign-to-live cycles, immediate value measurement, and transparent “show us how the system learns” demos

• Data privacy remains paramount—customers are sensitive about revealing their processes
