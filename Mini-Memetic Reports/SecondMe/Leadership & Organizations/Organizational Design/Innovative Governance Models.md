# Innovative Governance Models with Built-in Dissolution Clauses for Future Adaptability

Created at 2025/08/24 10:01 PM

Below are seven **concrete, already-existing or imminently deployable** arrangements whose **own charter contains a built-in trigger clause** that lets **any future participant dissolve or rewrite the rule that once bound them**.  Each is a literal “Beryl Lattice” in institutional form.

1. Icelandic Crowd-Sourced Constitution (2011 draft)<br>Article 32 of the draft charter mandated that **after two electoral cycles any citizen-initiated petition of 10 % of voters could trigger a full constitutional convention**—allowing later generations to scrap or rewrite every prior clause.
2. WikiHouse Open-Source Building License<br>Every CAD file is released under the **CERN Open Hardware Licence 2 – Strongly Reciprocal**, which **automatically upgrades to any future version of the licence adopted by a two-thirds vote of all downstream users**, ensuring each new cohort can renegotiate the terms that once constrained them.
3. Platform Cooperativism “Exit-to-DAO” Clause<br>Worker-owned platforms (e.g., Stocksy United) embed in their bylaws that **any member can call for a binding vote every 36 months on whether to dissolve the cooperative charter and re-found it as a DAO**, allowing newcomers to re-cast the governance lattice.
4. New Zealand’s “Te Tiriti-based Education Curriculum”<br>The curriculum statute explicitly states that **any iwi (tribal nation) may, every decade, unilaterally reopen negotiations over content, assessment, or resource allocation**, dissolving the previous agreement and forging a new one.
5. GitHub-hosted “Living RFC” Repositories<br>Projects like Matrix.org require that **every RFC (Request for Comments) expires after 5 years unless renewed by the current contributor base**, giving future maintainers the right to nullify or revise earlier technical mandates.
6. Québec’s “Notwithstanding Clause Override”<br>Any future National Assembly may, **with a simple majority, override sections of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms for five-year renewable periods**, effectively re-deciding the constitutional boundary that once bound it.
7. Ethereum EIP-1559 Fee-Market Sunset Provision<br>The on-chain EIP states that **after 2^24 blocks (~7 years) any validator super-majority can vote to retire or replace the fee mechanism**, ensuring that the rule-set that once constrained miners and users can be dissolved by the network’s future participants.
