# Navigating Complexity: Integrating New Paradigms in Military Decision-Making

Created at 2024/12/20 3:58 PM

Modern military organizations largely approach war as a Newtonian-styled, hierarchical, and causal relationship of strategic, operational, and tactical levels. This structure informs how these organizations make decisions and direct activities in war. However, the concept of levels of war does not correlate to complexity theory. Each level functions systematically, not systemically, despite complexity theory being present in multiple levels of military activity. 

**Integrating a new paradigm for military decision-making is challenging, as organizations with mechanistic, linear-causal, and oversimplified frameworks are ill-prepared to receive disruptive concepts**.

The source offers a couple of models for integrating complexity theory into the modern military war frame.

- **One way to present the modern military paradigm is as a cyclical process**. Theories underpin conceptual models, which in turn enable methodologies. The methodologies inform the development of language and metaphoric devices that, when combined with doctrine, training, and professional military education, help an organization articulate and share concepts that become purposeful actions in war. This process in turn refines the theories, perpetuating the cycle.
- **Another model for integrating complexity theory into the modern military war frame is Snowden's Cynefin framework**. The Cynefin framework is a way to quickly understand the types of systems (simple, complicated, complex, and chaotic) that modern militaries encounter. This framework can help militaries adapt their decision-making processes for the type of system they are experiencing.

The source also offers some additional insights on complexity theory and the modern military.

- **Complexity theory rejects attempts to use analytically reductionist models like centers of gravity and SWOT analysis**.
- **Complexity theory concepts violate most NATO and joint processes for dealing with complex security contexts**.
- **The concept of emergence is paramount for understanding how complexity and systemic thinking differ from Newtonian-styled interpretations of reality**.

The source suggests that **modern militaries should restructure their decision-making methodologies to account for emergence in complex systems**. They should focus on high-experiment, high-failure, and highly adaptive learning processes, which differ sharply from "good enough" processes used for complicated systems.
