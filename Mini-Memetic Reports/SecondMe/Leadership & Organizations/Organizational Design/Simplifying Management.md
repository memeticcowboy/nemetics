# Simplifying Management Decisions: The Case for Reducing KPIs

Created at 2025/08/31 8:21 AM

“Dumb It Down” (DID) style post, meaning:

➡️ take a complex systemic decision and explain it simply, clearly, and persuasively to stakeholders, without losing the integrity of the reasoning.

Here’s a structured example of how such a DID post could look for a hypothetical complex management decision (say, choosing to reduce the number of KPIs in a company dashboard):




**Dumb It Down (DID) Post Example**


Title: Why We’re Cutting KPIs (and Why That’s Good News)

1. The Core Message (1–2 sentences, no jargon):

We’re reducing the number of KPIs we track because having too many makes it harder to focus. By keeping only the most important ones, we’ll save time, reduce confusion, and get better results.

2. The Problem (paint it simply):

Right now, our dashboard is overloaded. Managers and teams are spending more time looking at numbers than acting on them. It’s like trying to steer a car with 20 steering wheels — no one knows which one really moves the car.

3. The Decision (state it clearly):

We’re going from 20 KPIs down to 6. These 6 cover what really matters: customer happiness, financial health, and team performance.

4. The Reason (give the simple systemic logic):

Complex systems (like our company) respond best when we focus on the right signals, not all signals. By simplifying, we’re actually making the system work better. Think of it like decluttering your home — fewer things to trip over means more space to live.

5. The Benefit (show the stakeholder win):

- Managers: less noise, more clarity.
- Teams: clear priorities, less reporting burden.
- Company: faster reactions to what matters most.


6. The Assurance (close the loop):

This doesn’t mean we’re ignoring everything else — we’ll still keep an eye on the other numbers, but only the key ones will guide our daily actions.

7. The Takeaway (one-line summary):

Fewer KPIs = more focus, better results.


🔑 DID Formula you can re-use:

- One-line core message (what + why)
- The problem (in a relatable metaphor)
- The decision (plain language)
- The reason (systemic logic simplified)
- The benefit (who gains what)
- The assurance (address fears)
- The takeaway (memorable line)
