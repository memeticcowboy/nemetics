The stages from Dator’s Four Futures Framework—Continuation, Limits and Discipline, Decline and Collapse, and Transformation—can indeed occur simultaneously but in different areas or layers of society, sectors, or systems.

For example:

	1.	**Continuation**: In some sectors, like technology or certain economies, growth might still appear robust and expanding without significant disruptions. For instance, tech innovations continue to drive growth in the software industry, embodying the “Continuation” scenario.
	2.	**Limits and Discipline**: In parallel, other sectors or regions might be approaching their ecological or social limits, leading to more emphasis on sustainability and disciplined resource management. Countries with an energy crisis may need to adapt to resource constraints, showcasing “Limits and Discipline.”
	3.	**Decline and Collapse**: Simultaneously, other sectors could be in a state of decline. For example, traditional fossil fuel industries might face decline due to shifts in policy, social attitudes, or depleted resources—falling into the “Decline and Collapse” scenario.
	4.	**Transformation**: Meanwhile, transformation might occur in places that adopt disruptive technologies or new societal structures. For example, countries that embrace green technology and energy transformation might be fundamentally altering their energy systems, thus fitting into the “Transformation” scenario.

These scenarios can coexist across different geographical regions, industries, or even aspects of society (e.g., culture, economy, environment), because systems are interconnected but operate at varying stages of development and adaptation. The coexistence emphasizes the non-linearity of societal change—different elements evolve at different speeds, requiring an adaptive and multifaceted approach.

Your exploration of backcasting techniques complements this framework because it provides a structured way to envision how we might progress from a desired future back to the present, recognizing that different sectors could face different trajectories. Backcasting helps identify steps to reach transformative goals while acknowledging the complex landscape of continuation, discipline, collapse, and transformation unfolding simultaneously.
