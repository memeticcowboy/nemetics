# Excitant's Six-Step Decision Process Explained

Created at 2025/08/15 2:36 PM

The webpage explains the Excitant six-step decision process for effective team and organizational decisions. Here’s a summary:

Distinction between “decision making” and “decision taking”: The page emphasizes these are different—decision making is the process of constructing and analyzing options, while decision taking is the commitment to action.

Benefits of an explicit decision process:

Clarifies where individuals or teams are in the process

Identifies points where decisions can go wrong

Improves communication and collective understanding

The Six Steps:

Decision Awareness: Recognizing that a decision is needed.

Decision Framing/Diagnosis: Defining or diagnosing the problem/situation to ensure everyone understands it the same way.

Decision Making: Analyzing options and discussing implications and assumptions before deciding.

Decision Taking/Commitment: Commitment to a particular choice, ensuring consensus and clarity of action.

Decision Action: Executing the decision—consistent and coherent action is necessary.

Decision Learning: Reviewing both the outcome and the process to learn and improve future decision-making.

Key insights:

Many failures come from skipping steps or poor alignment among team members.

Framing/diagnosis is critical—misdiagnosis leads to inappropriate solutions.

Consensus and clear communication are essential before moving to action.

“Decision learning” involves reflecting not only on the result but the whole process itself.

The process aims to improve quality of conversation, team alignment, action, and learning in organizational decisions

## Insight


**🧠 The Excitant Six-Step Decision Process for Effective Team Decisions**

- **🔍 Distinction Between Decision Making and Decision Taking**: Understanding the difference between decision making (the analytical process of evaluating options) and decision taking (the commitment to act) is crucial for effective decision-making. This distinction helps teams recognize the importance of both constructing options and committing to a chosen course of action.

- **💡 Benefits of an Explicit Decision Process**: Implementing a structured decision process clarifies the current status of decisions, identifies potential pitfalls, and enhances communication among team members. This clarity fosters a collective understanding, ensuring that everyone is aligned and aware of their roles in the decision-making journey.

- **🚦 Step 1: Decision Awareness**: Recognizing the need for a decision is the first step. This awareness often arises from external signals, such as market changes or project setbacks. Ensuring that all team members are on the same page regarding the necessity of a decision is vital to avoid premature actions.

- **🛠️ Step 2: Decision Framing/Diagnosis**: Properly diagnosing the problem is essential. Misalignment in understanding the issue can lead to inappropriate solutions. Teams must explicitly discuss their frames of reference to ensure everyone is addressing the same problem, thus avoiding the "diagnosis trap."

- **📊 Step 3: Decision Making**: This complex stage involves analyzing options and discussing implications. It requires careful consideration of who should be involved, what information is needed, and the assumptions being made. Different decision-making styles among team members must be acknowledged to prevent misunderstandings and ensure comprehensive analysis.

- **🤝 Step 4: Decision Taking/Commitment**: This step marks the transition from discussion to action. Achieving consensus and clarity on the decision is crucial. Techniques such as rich voting systems can help gauge team members' readiness and commitment, ensuring that everyone is aligned before moving forward.

- **🚀 Step 5: Decision Action**: Effective execution of the decision is paramount. All team members must consistently act on the agreed-upon course of action. Without coherent and unified action, the decision lacks substance, and the intended outcomes may not be realized.

- **📚 Step 6: Decision Learning**: This final step emphasizes the importance of reflecting on both the decision's content and the process. Evaluating what worked and what didn’t provides valuable insights for future decisions. Learning from both the outcomes and the decision-making process itself is essential for continuous improvement.

**🔄 Next Steps for Implementation**: To enhance decision-making quality, teams should start incorporating the language of the six-step decision process into their meetings. This practice will foster better discussions and lead to more effective decision-making outcomes.
