**3. Fundamental Concepts:**

- **Algorithmic Amplification:** The process by which platform algorithms increase the visibility and reach of certain content.
- **Filter Bubbles:** Echo chambers created by algorithmic personalization, potentially limiting exposure to diverse perspectives (presented as a concept to be debated).
- **Content Moderation:** Traditional approaches to managing online content, often focused on removing harmful content.
- **Design-Focused Interventions:** Reforming platform design and architecture as a means of addressing negative impacts.
- **Regulatory Frameworks:** Governmental and institutional rules and guidelines for platform governance.
- **Algorithmic Audits:** Systematic examinations of algorithms to assess their impact and fairness.
- **Transparency:** Making algorithmic processes and their effects more understandable and visible.
- **Friction and Design:** Deliberately introducing obstacles or "seams" into digital systems to encourage reflection, slow down interactions, and promote governance.
- **Nutrition Labels for Algorithms:** A metaphor for standardized metrics to measure and disclose algorithmic amplification, drawing parallels to food nutrition labels.
- **Governance Seams:** Points of intervention and control at the interfaces of systems, where friction can be introduced for governance purposes (e.g., captchas).
- **Orwellian Engagement:** Superficial and shallow online engagement patterns driven by platform design.
- **Middleware:** User-created or third-party tools that add layers of functionality or friction on top of existing platforms.

- **Call for Co-creation:** Developing metrics and transparency mechanisms should involve civil society and marginalized groups.
