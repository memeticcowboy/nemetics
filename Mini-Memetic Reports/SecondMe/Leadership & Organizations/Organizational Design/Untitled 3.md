# The RCISAction framework and Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) AI development

Created at 2024/09/29 1:35 PM

The RCISAction framework and Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) AI development intersect well in their focus on continuous improvement and adaptability, driven by human expertise and oversight.

**1. Feedback Loop and Continuous Improvement:**
The RCISAction framework, which emphasizes recurring cycles of Review, Change, Implement, and Sustain, naturally aligns with HITL development processes that incorporate ongoing human feedback for refining AI models. In both systems, the role of humans is to monitor outcomes, identify gaps, and make necessary changes, ensuring a loop of continuous improvement. By having a human touch embedded within AI decision-making, biases can be reduced, and more context-aware decision-making is fostered, mirroring the ‘Review’ and ‘Change’ phases of RCISAction.

**2. Human Agency in Decision Processes:**
Human-in-the-Loop AI incorporates human decision-makers to directly influence and validate AI behaviors. In RCISAction, human agency is essential to guide actions after assessment. Humans play a central role in setting goals and making critical decisions based on reviewed outcomes. This human-centered control helps to safeguard the ethical use of AI and maintain alignment with strategic goals, directly supporting the core principles of the RCISAction framework.

**3. Scalability and Flexibility Through Human Insight:**
RCISAction aims for adaptable improvement, which requires a flexible approach to change. HITL processes introduce human adaptability in developing AI, making it possible to shift the focus of an AI system based on new information. This approach reinforces the RCISAction framework by ensuring that, even in automated environments, changes are implemented flexibly and sustain positive outcomes, driven by nuanced human insight.

In essence, RCISAction’s structured but iterative nature aligns with HITL's role in AI development to ensure continuous feedback, human adaptability, and ethically driven decision-making. Together, these frameworks create a holistic approach to building systems that not only evolve continuously but do so while maintaining human alignment and ethical standards.

Here’s how:

**1. Feedback and Reinforcement Learning (Review & Change):**
After the initial training of a language model on a large dataset, human input is used extensively for reviewing and improving its performance. Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) is a key method used in LLM development. Human trainers rate or rank model responses, which helps adjust and fine-tune the model, ensuring it aligns more closely with desired outcomes. This reflects the "Review" and "Change" phases in RCISAction, where human evaluators assess outcomes and inform changes.

**2. Human Agency in Decision-Making (Implement):**
Humans are actively involved in making critical decisions regarding what the model should learn and prioritize. During training, developers decide what data should be included, how biases should be minimized, and what adjustments need to be made based on user feedback. This human oversight mirrors the decision-making and goal-setting aspects of RCISAction, ensuring the model operates within specific ethical and functional guidelines.

**3. Iterative Improvement and Model Updates (Sustain):**
After deployment, LLMs like ChatGPT continue to be updated based on new data, usage insights, and direct user feedback. These continuous improvements are part of the sustaining process in RCISAction, where models evolve to meet new requirements and improve user interactions over time.

However, **current LLM development processes do differ in some areas**:

- **Scale and Complexity**: The training process for LLMs involves massive computational resources and an overwhelming amount of data, making the process less directly interactive compared to the RCISAction framework, which emphasizes human-driven, more manual iteration.

Continuous user feedback is collected, but the level of direct human interaction is not as intensive as in frameworks like RCISAction, where continuous human intervention is more consistent throughout the process.
