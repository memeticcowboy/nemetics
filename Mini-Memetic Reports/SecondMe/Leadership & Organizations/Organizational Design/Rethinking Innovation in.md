## Insight


* The concept of "promissory materialism" in science fiction suggests a reliance on future discoveries and technologies that are yet to be realized, highlighting a fundamental aspect of human imagination and progress. This notion can be traced back to philosophers like Karl Popper, who emphasized the importance of future possibilities in scientific theory development, encouraging a forward-thinking approach in both science and the arts.

* The resistance to innovation among career soldiers reflects a broader tension present in various fields where established competencies can become threatened by new methodologies. This phenomenon is discussed extensively in literature on organizational behavior, revealing the psychological and sociopolitical dimensions at play when individuals or institutions grapple with disruptions to their familiar practices.

* The "ends-based rationality" referenced in decision-making contexts underscores a critical challenge in institutions: the tendency to assume that knowing the outcomes of past events enables accurate predictions for future planning. This can lead to a misalignment with changing circumstances, as highlighted by scholars who critique such deterministic approaches, emphasizing a need for adaptive and flexible strategies rather than rigid adherence to historical models.

* The unique challenges faced by military forces operating in the space domain illustrate the complexities of modern warfare, characterized by evolving technologies and strategies. The discussion around the fragility and exotic nature of space warfare serves as a reminder of the need for innovative thinking and approaches, especially as resources and priorities shift within military and governmental structures. This concern resonates with contemporary discussions on the ethics and logistics of space exploration and conflict.
