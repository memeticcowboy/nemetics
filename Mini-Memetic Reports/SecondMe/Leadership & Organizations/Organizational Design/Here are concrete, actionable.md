Here are concrete, actionable examples of using the AEIOU framework to overcome anxiety when starting contextual inquiry, with enhancements tailored to early-stage UX research and best practices from sources like IDEO and Nielsen Norman Group.<br><br>### AEIOU Framework: Guided Example<br><br>A structured note-taking approach using AEIOU turns anxious, open-ended observation into organized, confidence-boosting insights. Here’s how to apply it as a beginner working with a familiar tool or context (like a productivity app), along with sample entries and strategies for gradual skill building.<br><br>#### Activities<br>- What users are doing: “User opens the Notion app, creates a new to-do list, and sorts tasks by dragging cards”.<br>- Think-aloud prompt: “Can you tell me what you’re trying to achieve right now?”<br>- Sample note: “Completing daily task planning, prefers drag-and-drop over keyboard shortcuts”.<br><br>#### Environments<br>- Contexts and setups: “Working at a dining table with laptop, phone nearby, moderate background noise from TV”.<br>- Note: “Digital setup, home workspace, distractions present but user works around them with headphones”.<br><br>#### Interactions<br>- Engagements: “User interacts mostly with the app UI, occasionally checks calendar on phone, messages a coworker via Slack”.<br>- Sample: “Switches to Slack during a task to confirm meeting time, mentions 'I get interrupted often'”.<br><br>#### Objects<br>- Tangibles/tools: “Uses physical sticky notes to jot down reminders, keeps a water bottle on desk for breaks”.<br>- Note: “Concurrent use of analog (sticky notes) and digital (Notion, Slack); switches between tools fluidly”.<br><br>#### Users<br>- Emotions, workarounds, goals: “Appears focused but mentions feeling overwhelmed by unfinished tasks; smiles after ticking off an item”.<br>- Prompt: “How did you feel about organizing tasks today?”<br>- Note: “Stated goal is ‘just not to forget anything,’ feels less anxious after organizing visually”.<br><br><br><br>***### Anxiety-Reducing Strategies with AEIOU***<br><br>***- Start with a personal pilot: Record yourself using a familiar app, narrate actions (“I’m opening my calendar because…”), and review for improvement.***<br>***- Observe a friend for 15 minutes, using AEIOU to jot notes in each category. Limit questions to simple prompts and avoid interrogation to reduce pressure.***<br>***- Use AEIOU during a remote session: Ask a colleague to share their screen on Zoom or similar; observe and note entries in each AEIOU category, then follow up with a short interview.***<br>***- After a few sessions, reflect on each AEIOU section: Where did observations flow easily? Where did you freeze up or feel awkward? Adjust methods as needed.***<br>***- Celebrate identifying one unexpected behavior (“User always color-codes urgent tasks with red sticky notes”), and treat all imperfections as insights.***<br><br><br><br>### Additional Tips<br><br>- Keep sessions short (≤30 minutes) to avoid overwhelm.<br>- Use consent and ethical guidelines: Explain the purpose, assure privacy, and obtain permission before recording or observing.<br>- Explore guides for backup: The Interaction Design Foundation provides articles on contextual inquiry, and “Contextual Design” by Holtzblatt & Beyer covers the basics (especially chapters 1-3).<br>- Embrace learning loops: After 2-3 AEIOU sessions, review, tweak, and gradually scale up with more varied users to enhance skill and confidence.<br><br>***<br><br>If you share the specific tool or context you wish to explore, these ideas can be refined for maximum relevance.
