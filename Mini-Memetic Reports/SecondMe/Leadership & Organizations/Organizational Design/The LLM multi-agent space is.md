The LLM multi-agent space is rapidly evolving. A very nice survey paper looks at the state of evolution, progress, and resources in the multi-agent space.

It looks in particular at multi-agent domains, environments, enablers, mechanisms for improving capacity, and ecosystem resources:

🌍 Simulated Domains and Environments. LLM-based multi-agents simulate diverse domains including software development, robotics, gaming, societal dynamics, economics, and policymaking. Environments range from sandbox simulations, where agents interact in controlled virtual settings, to physical spaces requiring real-world action, and purely communicative contexts where agents debate or collaborate without external inputs.

🧑‍💼 Agent Profiling and Communication. Agents are profiled based on predefined roles, model-generated traits, or data-derived characteristics. They embody distinct skills and behaviors tailored to tasks, such as programmers in software development or personas in societal simulations. Communication occurs through structured paradigms—cooperative, competitive, or debate-driven—enabled by centralized, decentralized, or shared-message-pool frameworks to foster collaboration and information exchange.

📈 Mechanisms for Capacity Growth. Agents enhance their capabilities via three core mechanisms: (1) Memory, enabling recall of past interactions to refine actions; (2) Self-Evolution, allowing dynamic adjustment of goals and strategies based on feedback; and (3) Dynamic Generation, where new agents are created to address emerging challenges. These mechanisms leverage environmental feedback, inter-agent interactions, and human inputs to continuously improve performance. 

📊 Commonly Used Datasets and Benchmarks. LLM-based multi-agent systems leverage diverse datasets and benchmarks tailored to their application domains:
🛠️ Problem-Solving Scenarios. Benchmarks like HumanEval and MBPP test coding capabilities, while others like RoCoBench and C-WAH evaluate multi-robot planning. 
🌍 World Simulation Scenarios. Datasets such as SOTOPIA and MovieLens-1M explore societal behaviors and user preferences. Gaming simulations utilize frameworks like Werewolf and Avalon to study strategy and interaction, while psychological tests replicate human decision-making scenarios like the Ultimatum Game.

The paper came out in April but still provides a very good frame on current state.
