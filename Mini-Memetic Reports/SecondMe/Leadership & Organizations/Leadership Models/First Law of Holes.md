# First Law of Holes

Created at 2025/06/08 11:10 AM

**Heuristic:** First Law of Holes

**Description:** If you find yourself in a hole, stop digging. That is, when a project or situation is going poorly, the heuristic is to quit making it worse – pause, reassess, change tactics – rather than plow ahead due to pride or sunk costs. In attention terms: recognize a losing game and redirect your focus elsewhere.

**Use Case:** In PR or politics, this is critical – if a spokesperson is caught in a lie, continuing to spin (digging deeper) worsens the reputation damage. Better to stop, apologize, or go silent to prevent further harm. In business, if a product launch is floundering, dumping more money into marketing without fixing core issues is “digging.” Savvy leaders heed this law and pivot instead of doubling down on failure.

Supporting Quote: “Healey’s First Law of Holes: when you’re in one, stop digging.” – Denis Healey (former UK Chancellor, advising politicians on avoiding self-inflicted damage)

**Related Bias:** Escalation of Commitment – the irrational urge to invest more resources in a bad situation because of what’s already invested. The law of holes is the antidote: it bluntly reminds us to halt that cycle. By stopping the digging, you conserve resources and attention for more promising opportunities
