This is an excellent piece in [PwC](https://www.linkedin.com/company/pwc/)’s [strategy+business](https://www.linkedin.com/company/strategy-business/) magazine on organisational decision-making.<br><br>This particularly stood out to me:<br><br>A whopping 58% of CEOs in PwC’s annual global CEO survey say that they determine the quality of a decision by its outcome rather than by its process.<br><br>😲 <br><br>At a certain level, this is rather astonishing. It illustrates how much work there is to do to improve decision quality in so many organisations.<br><br>Decision quality is about the “how”, not the outcome.<br><br>At another level, it isn’t surprising. After all, outcomes do matter and leaders are under intense pressure to deliver them.<br><br>However, here’s a zen koan-like paradox: to deliver them given many of the strategic challenges organisations increasingly face, their / our leaders / ourselves have to “let go” of them (in a sense), and get into the *how* of quality decision-making.<br>The piece does a great job of calling that out.<br><br>It also makes strong recommendations around fostering trust, debate and dissent and leveraging uncertainty.<br><br>It’s great to see this. We need more of this thinking to take root in boardrooms and C-suites, among non-executive directors, and across policymakers around the world.<br><br>[Ben Cattaneo](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ben-cattaneo_strategybusiness-be-a-better-decider-activity-7309578806472638464-DIVy) on LinkedIn
