This lineage diverged from Sub-Saharan populations long before the emergence of modern humans outside Africa, indicating a unique ancestral background that challenges previous assumptions about genetic connections in the region.

- **Insights from the African Humid Period**: During the African Humid Period (14,800 to 5,500 years ago), the Sahara was a lush environment supporting human life. The Takarkori individuals, who lived during this time, were genetically distinct from Sub-Saharan groups, suggesting limited gene flow and a unique adaptation to their environment.

- **Genetic Isolation and Cultural Diffusion**: The Takarkori mummies show evidence of genetic isolation, with minimal contact with Neanderthals and other populations. This isolation likely contributed to the preservation of their distinct genetic traits, while cultural practices, such as farming, spread through cultural diffusion rather than migration.
