## Insight
This report by Deloitte emphasizes the critical role of Indigenous leadership in nature-based climate solutions. It highlights the importance of integrating Indigenous knowledge systems and rights into climate action strategies to achieve equitable and effective outcomes. The report outlines various initiatives, challenges, and opportunities for advancing Indigenous-led climate projects, focusing on policy, economic empowerment, and carbon markets. For anyone involved in climate action, recognizing and supporting Indigenous leadership is not just beneficial but essential for sustainable progress.

**🌍 Indigenous Leadership in Climate Action**
- **Indigenous knowledge systems are vital**: Indigenous Peoples have a long history of sustainable coexistence with their territories, which is crucial for biodiversity protection and climate resilience.
- **Equitable transition is necessary**: Climate equity is linked to recognizing and respecting Indigenous rights, knowledge systems, and cultural connections to the land.
- **Nature-based solutions (NBS) are key**: NBS strategies mitigate climate change by protecting, restoring, and sustainably managing ecosystems, benefiting both people and wildlife.

**📜 Policy and Jurisdiction**
- **Recognize carbon rights and land ownership**: First Nations and other Indigenous governments need support in decision-making, land stewardship, and cultural values.
- **Support Indigenous jurisdiction**: Establishing jurisdiction and control over traditional territories is critical for meaningful reconciliation and advancing NBS projects.
- **Reduce time and uncertainty**: Governments should streamline processes to establish carbon rights and support Indigenous leadership in climate action.

**💼 Economic Empowerment and Sovereignty**
- **Maximize community benefits**: Carbon projects should deliver long-term socioeconomic and cultural values, supporting community well-being and economic independence.
- **Build capacity for understanding**: Corporate Canada, governments, and NGOs need to develop better working relationships with Indigenous partners by understanding their worldviews and laws.
- **Protect Indigenous knowledge and data**: Following the First Nations principles of ownership, control, access, and possession (OCAP) ensures data sovereignty and informed decision-making.

**📊 Protocols and Carbon Markets**
- **Improve protocol timeliness and relevance**: Governments should develop protocols that are timely and relevant for Indigenous projects, embedding UNDRIP principles.
- **Expand access to voluntary markets**: Allowing Indigenous projects to participate in voluntary markets can provide greater opportunities and diversification.
- **Develop Indigenous-led protocols**: Funding Indigenous organizations to create their own protocols and registries can advance reconciliation and attract global investment.

**🔍 Monitoring, Measurement, and Verification**
- **Integrate traditional practices and technology**: MMV programs should combine traditional Indigenous practices with modern technology to ensure credible carbon credits and support project success.
- **Provide long-term job opportunities**: MMV programs can create jobs and support existing stewardship programs, particularly for Indigenous youth.

**🌱 Climate Action with a Seven Generations View**
- **Adopt a long-term perspective**: Decisions should be made based on their impacts on future generations, ensuring sustainable and equitable climate action.
- **Collaborate across sectors**: Governments, NGOs, and Indigenous Peoples need to work together to secure climate-safe and nature-positive relationships for the future.
