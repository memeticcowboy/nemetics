Decision Making and business leadership

🌆 Decision-making is a central part of leadership.
🕰️ Group decision-making processes include shared, collaborative, and democratic styles.
⚡️ Group decision-making doesn't always result in the best outcome.
🧠 Individuals have different decision-making styles, affecting group dynamics.
🤔 Rational decision-makers prioritize logic and data.
🔮 Intuitive decision-makers rely on gut feelings.
🤝 Dependent decision-makers are influenced by others' opinions.
🐌 Avoidant decision-makers struggle with uncertainty and postpone choices

🐌 Avoidant decision-makers dislike uncertainty and ambiguity.
🚀 Spontaneous decision-makers prioritize speed and act impulsively.
📋 Decision-making styles can be assessed with the General Decision-Making Style assessment.
🤔 Individual decision-making styles are reflected in how you respond to assessment statements.
⚖️ A rational decision-maker prioritizes facts and logic in their decision-making.
✨ An intuitive decision-maker heavily relies on their instincts or 'gut feelings'.
🤝 A dependent decision-maker seeks input and approval from others before deciding.
📊 Group decision-making styles can be complex based on the mix of individual members' styles.

💥 Spontaneous decision-making can be useful for time-sensitive problems with accurate knowledge.
🐌 Avoidant decision-making can hinder good decisions, even when knowledge is accurate.
🧠 Rational decision-making can make it harder to reach a collective decision due to high standards.
🎯 Leaders should consider individual decision-making styles when forming decision-making groups.
📉 Avoidant decision-making styles can negatively impact group performance if knowledge is accurate.
🚀 Leaders should recognize the benefits of spontaneous decisions with accurate knowledge.
⚖️ Rational decision-making style can sometimes weaken the benefits of strong cognitive ability.
💡 Leaders can use this research to design training and create environments that improve decision-making
