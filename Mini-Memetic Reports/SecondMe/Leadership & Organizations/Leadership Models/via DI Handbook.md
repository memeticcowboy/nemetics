Summary of Decision Intelligence (DI)
Definition & Purpose
Decision Intelligence (DI) is a structured methodology that integrates processes, technologies, and evidence-based insights to enhance decision-making. The core principle of DI is that decisions can be designed much like physical products, ensuring that actions taken today align with desired future outcomes.
Designing Decisions
Unlike process flows that allow control at each step, DI treats decisions as distinct thought processes that, once made, lead to specific outcomes without further intervention. By designing decisions in advance, organizations can align stakeholders and ensure strategic clarity.
Best Practices & Causal Decision Diagrams (CDDs)
Applying design principles—such as ideation, documentation, and refinement—improves decision-making quality. Causal Decision Diagrams (CDDs) serve as a visual tool to document and analyze decision structures, making implicit thought processes explicit.
Why DI Matters
By systematically documenting decision-making approaches, DI helps individuals and organizations optimize strategies. The methodology is gaining attention in projects like Decision Central, reflecting its growing relevance in business and technology.
In essence, DI is about structuring, visualizing, and improving how decisions are made, ensuring better alignment with long-term objectives.
