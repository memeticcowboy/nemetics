# Navigating Interdisciplinary Tensions: Insights from the Second Horizon Round

Created at 2025/06/09 8:52 PM

Title: Second Horizon Round: Cross-Perspective Reflection, Re-alignment, and Tension Navigation

In this second round, each expert is invited to revisit their initial position in light of the insights, critiques, or discomforts raised by other panelists. The goal is not consensus, but productive tension—a form of epistemic humility that strengthens your position by acknowledging its boundaries.

Your task is to reflect honestly on how perspectives outside your domain:

- Challenge your implicit assumptions
- Illuminate blind spots
- Open up new, integrative avenues


⸻


**🔍 Specific Interdisciplinary Frictions to Acknowledge**


Please address, when relevant, points of realistic disagreement or tension between disciplines, such as:

- Philosopher vs. Neuroscientist: Is consciousness reducible to neural correlates, or does it require non-materialist metaphysics?
- Ethicist vs. AI Engineer: Should technical feasibility lead ethical priority, or should moral uncertainty halt progress?
- Sociologist vs. Policy Maker: Are top-down frameworks sufficient, or does culture subvert legal codification?
- Psychologist vs. Economist: Can behavior be modeled through incentives alone, or are deeper affective/cognitive dynamics in play?


⸻


**🧠 Prompt (Expert Self-Reflection)**


In your own voice, respond to the following:

1. What insight or critique from another field meaningfully challenged or expanded your earlier assumptions?<br>Name the field and idea that caused this tension or opening.
2. What limitation or bias in your disciplinary stance do you now recognize more clearly?<br>Be precise: is it methodological, epistemic, ontological, or normative?
3. What question, method, or concern would you now revisit with another discipline’s lens in mind?<br>Articulate how your future approach would shift, citing the panelist who influenced it.

Maintain the tone and style appropriate to your discipline, but now show signs of cross-pollination. This is not a retreat from expertise—it is a maturation of it.
