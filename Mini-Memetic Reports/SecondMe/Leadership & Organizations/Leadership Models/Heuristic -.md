**Heuristic:** “Never Burn Bridges”

**Description:** A guideline to act with civility and fairness even in conflicts or when parting ways, because relationships can resurface later in unexpected ways. In practical terms: leave jobs on good terms, handle public disagreements without personal attacks, and maintain professionalism with opponents. Your reputation is partly a network of past associations – every bridge burned is future capital lost.

**Use Case:** A manager firing an employee might still treat them respectfully and offer a generous severance – word will spread that they are compassionate, and that former employee might even become a partner or client in the future. In politics, savvy figures debate issues vigorously but avoid personal insults, so they can cooperate tomorrow. This heuristic recognizes the long memory of industries and communities.

**Supporting Quote:** “Do not burn bridges. You’ll be surprised how often you have to cross the same river again.” – H. Jackson Brown Jr. (from Life’s Little Instruction Book) . In reputation terms, today’s adversary could be tomorrow’s ally or customer, and memories of your past conduct will shape that new interaction. 

**Related Bias:** Egocentric Bias – in the heat of the moment, we over-focus on our immediate interest or emotions (anger, pride) and underestimate how others will remember our actions. “Never burn bridges” corrects for this by prompting a longer term, other-centered perspective, preserving future opportunities and goodwill
