# Navigating the Permacrisis

Created at 2024/11/06 8:33 AM

Leading economists Mike Spence and Mohamed El-Erian talk about the “pretty complicated and disorienting environment” we face. 

In this episode of the McKinsey Global Institute’s Forward thinking podcast, co-host Michael Chui talks with A. Michael Spence, dean emeritus of the Stanford Graduate School of Business, and Mohamed El-Erian, president of Queens’ College Cambridge and chief economic advisor at Allianz. Together with former UK Chancellor of the Exchequer and Prime Minister Gordon Brown, they have leveraged decades of experience to explore the question “Is the world in a state of permacrisis?”

## Insight
In this insightful episode of the McKinsey Global Institute's Forward Thinking podcast, Michael Chui and Janet Bush engage with leading economists A. Michael Spence and Mohamed El-Erian. They delve into the concept of 'Permacrisis,' a term coined to describe the ongoing state of global instability and repeated crises. Drawing from their extensive experience, including co-authoring a book with Gordon Brown, they explore the structural changes and challenges facing the global economy. The discussion highlights the erosion of systemic resilience, the need for inclusive and sustainable growth, and the importance of global cooperation. For someone like you, deeply invested in understanding complex societal structures and leveraging technology for growth, this conversation offers valuable insights into the interplay of economic forces and the potential for transformative change. The episode concludes with actionable insights on addressing policy mistakes, enhancing global coordination, and harnessing technological advancements to foster a more resilient and equitable world.

**🎙️Introduction and Background**
- **Introduction of Guests**: The podcast features Nobel laureate Mike Spence and Mohamed El-Erian, who have co-authored a book with Gordon Brown titled 'Permacrisis'. The discussion is based on their experiences and insights into the global economy.[_TIMESTAMP_]('0-51')
- **Personal Backgrounds**: Mike Spence shares his journey from Canada to the US, studying at Princeton and Harvard, and his professional life in Europe. **📚Concept of Permacrisis**
- **Definition and Origin**: The term 'Permacrisis' was coined during discussions among the authors during the pandemic. It refers to the repeated crises and structural changes in the global economy, including technological transformations and geopolitical tensions.[_TIMESTAMP_]('285-399')
- **Purpose of the Book**: The book aims to help people understand and navigate the complex and disorienting global environment. The authors emphasize that the crises are not permanent and can be addressed through decisions, vision, leadership, and coordination.[_TIMESTAMP_]('383-531')

**📈Challenges in Economic Growth**
- **Growth and Productivity Challenges**: The global economy is transitioning to a supply-constrained environment, with structural headwinds like aging populations and declining productivity. The authors highlight the need to focus on tools for improving productivity.[_TIMESTAMP_]('603-802')
- **Financialization and Its Impact**: The growth strategy was hijacked by finance, leading to a loss of focus on productivity. The authors see an opportunity to regain control through technological advancements and labor retraining.[_TIMESTAMP_]('951-1032')

**🔍Analysis of Economic Theories**
- **Transitory Inflation Debate**: The concept of 'transitory' inflation is critiqued as a behavioral concept, not just a time concept. The authors argue that behaviors have changed significantly, impacting price setting and wage behavior.[_TIMESTAMP_]('1360-1474')
- **Understanding Financial Dynamics**: The authors discuss the importance of understanding financial dynamics, as seen in historical crises like the Latin American crisis and the global financial crisis. They emphasize the need for better integration of financial insights into economic management.[_TIMESTAMP_]('1084-1220')

**🌍 Economic Challenges and Mindsets**
- **Supply Chain Disruptions**: The global economy faced significant disruptions due to transitory issues in the supply chain, such as semiconductor shortages and skyrocketing shipping costs. These disruptions were partly due to the shutdowns during the pandemic, affecting industries like the auto sector.[_TIMESTAMP_]('1481-1518')
- **Long-term Economic Trends**: There are secular trends in productivity that are not easily reversible, indicating a semi-permanent shift in the economic landscape. The pandemic and its associated shocks have obscured these trends, making it easier to misinterpret them as temporary issues.[_TIMESTAMP_]('1546-1577')
- **Mindsets and Economic Models**: People's mindsets, akin to parameters in a generative AI model, are slow to change and can lead to false assumptions and policy mistakes. This is compounded by the fact that we are not in a cyclical, mean-reverting world, but one with multiple potential outcomes.[_TIMESTAMP_]('1608-1662')

**📈 Inflation Targeting and Central Bank Credibility**
- **Arbitrary Inflation Targets**: The 2% inflation target originated from New Zealand's experiments in the early 1990s and was adopted by other central banks. It was chosen to avoid the lower bound but is now questioned in a world of inflexible supply.[_TIMESTAMP_]('1662-1724')
- **Central Bank Credibility**: Central banks face challenges in maintaining credibility due to policy errors, communication issues, and forecasting mistakes. This credibility is crucial for effective forward guidance and smooth economic adjustments.[_TIMESTAMP_]('1856-1913')
- **Opportunistic Disinflation**: There is a potential shift towards tolerating slightly higher inflation rates, leveraging opportunistic disinflation to adjust targets over time without losing credibility.[_TIMESTAMP_]('1788-1809')

**🌐 Global Cooperation and Institutional Reform**
- **WTO 2.0 and Multilateral Institutions**: There is a need to reform multilateral institutions like the WTO to reflect the changing global economic landscape. This includes addressing national security concerns and ensuring resilience in global trade.[_TIMESTAMP_]('1920-2070')
- **Globalization Light**: The future of globalization is seen as 'globalization light,' which requires a governance system to ensure cooperative play in trade, avoiding the extremes of ultra-globalization and isolation.[_TIMESTAMP_]('2112-2178')
- **Role of Institutions in Crisis**: Institutions like the WTO, IMF, and World Bank are crucial, especially in crises, to ensure global cooperation. However, reforms are needed to enhance their effectiveness and adapt to new challenges.[_TIMESTAMP_]('2181-2226')

**📊 Inclusive Growth and Sustainability**
- **Inclusive Growth Strategies**: There is a growing focus on inclusive growth, with digital technologies playing a key role in financial inclusion, as seen in India's financial sector. This aligns profitable investments with inclusive growth patterns.[_TIMESTAMP_]('2310-2390')
- **Sustainability Challenges**: Despite progress in inclusive growth, significant challenges remain in achieving sustainability, particularly in reducing carbon emissions while maintaining economic growth.[_TIMESTAMP_]('2451-2517')
- **Successes and Future Directions**: While growth models have lifted billions out of poverty, there is a need to balance growth with inclusivity and sustainability, leveraging technological advancements for better outcomes.[_TIMESTAMP_]('2540-2555')
