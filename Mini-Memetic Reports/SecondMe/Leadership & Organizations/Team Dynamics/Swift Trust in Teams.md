## Insight
Swift trust is a concept introduced by Debra Meyerson and colleagues in 1996, focusing on trust in temporary organizational structures. Unlike traditional trust, which builds over time, swift trust assumes initial trust that is later verified and adjusted. This theory is particularly relevant in today's globalized and technology-driven world where temporary teams are common. Key outcomes include the ability of short-lived teams to achieve high performance and satisfaction despite limited time for traditional trust-building. For those working in or managing temporary teams, fostering an environment of initial trust and verifying it through actions can be crucial for success.

**🔍 Introduction to Swift Trust**
- **Initial Trust Assumption**: Swift trust involves assuming trust at the beginning of a team's formation, which is later verified and adjusted.
- **Relevance in Modern Context**: With globalization and technological advancements, temporary teams are increasingly common, making swift trust a valuable concept.

**💼 Trust in Teams**
- **Impact on Performance**: High levels of trust correlate with positive attitudes, commitment, and satisfaction within teams.
- **Autonomy and Trust**: Trust is linked to higher levels of autonomy in teams, allowing for more independent and effective work.

**📚 Conventional Models of Trust**
- **Trustworthiness and Trust Propensity**: Traditional trust relies on trustworthiness (ability, benevolence, integrity) and trust propensity (willingness to trust).
- **Limitations for Swift Trust**: Traditional antecedents like ability and integrity may not have time to establish in temporary teams, making swift trust more reliant on benevolence.

**⏳ Time-Dependent Trust Models**
- **Deterrence-Based Trust**: Trust based on fear of punishment, ensuring responsibilities are met.
- **Knowledge-Based Trust**: Trust formed through knowledge of the trustee's actions and predictability.
- **Identification-Based Trust**: Trust based on shared goals and values, allowing members to act as agents for one another.

**🏢 Swift Trust in Organizations**
- **Temporary Teams**: Swift trust explains trust development in temporary, nonconventional teams under time constraints.
- **Verification of Trust**: Teams assume trust initially but must verify and adjust trust beliefs through actions.

**🧠 Cognitive Components**
- **Early Trusting Beliefs**: Based on social identities and self-categorizations, promoting early trusting behavior.
- **Self-Reinforcing Beliefs**: Individuals focus on information that validates their trusting beliefs, often ignoring contrary evidence.

**📏 Normative Components**
- **Social Proofs and Fail-Safe Mechanisms**: Normative components provide mechanisms to regulate trust and avoid exaggerated confidence.
- **Importance in Virtual Teams**: Normative actions are crucial for maintaining trust, especially in high uncertainty and virtual contexts.

**🌍 Swift Trust in Different Work Teams**
- **Global Virtual Teams (GVTs)**: Swift trust is critical in GVTs where members are geographically dispersed and rely on electronic communication.
- **Swift Starting Action Teams (STATs)**: STATs are highly independent teams that must quickly establish trust to complete complex, time-pressured projects.

**❗ Criticisms**
- **Fragility of Swift Trust**: Swift trust is fragile and cannot be sustained without subsequent communication.
- **Substitution of Trust**: Some researchers argue that swift trust is a risk management strategy rather than true trust.
