Stochastic processes are fundamental in modeling systems where outcomes are not deterministic but have a degree of unpredictability. For example, in financial markets, stochastic models like the Geometric Brownian Motion are used to describe stock price movements.

*  **Non-Linear and Recursive Thinking**: The inclusion of "Non-Linear Thinking Processes" and "Recursive Thinking Processes" suggests an understanding that systems are not always linear or straightforward. Recursive thinking involves processes that call themselves, allowing for complex problem-solving and adaptation. Non-linear thinking acknowledges that cause-and-effect relationships may not be proportional, and small changes can lead to significant outcomes, a concept often associated with chaos theory and the butterfly effect.

*  **Autopoietic Systems**: The diagram contrasts "Complex Systems (autopoietic)" with "Non-Complex Systems," pointing to the concept of autopoiesis, introduced by biologists Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela. Autopoietic systems are self-producing and self-maintaining, distinguishing them from systems that require external input to sustain themselves. A classic example is a biological cell, which produces its own components and maintains its organization.

*  **Emergence ("Wholes are greater than the sums of their parts")**: The phrase "but wholes are greater than the sums of their parts" encapsulates the concept of emergence, a key property of complex systems. Emergence refers to the appearance of novel properties at a higher level of organization that cannot be predicted from the properties of the individual components. For instance, consciousness is often cited as an emergent property of the brain's complex neural networks.

*  **Feedback Loops**: The diagram mentions "Feedback Loops," which are crucial in systems thinking. Feedback loops can be positive (amplifying change) or negative (dampening change). An example of a positive feedback loop is the spread of misinformation on social media, where each share increases the likelihood of further sharing. A negative feedback loop is a thermostat regulating temperature, where deviations from the set temperature trigger a response to restore balance.
