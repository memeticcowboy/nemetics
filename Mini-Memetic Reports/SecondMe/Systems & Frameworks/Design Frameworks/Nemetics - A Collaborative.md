# Nemetics: A Collaborative Framework for Understanding Complexity and Emergence

Created at 2025/12/16 1:38 PM

Nemetics is a niche, collaboratively developed framework that emerged around 2010–2012 from Twitter conversations among a small group of thinkers interested in complexity science. It serves as a custom meta-language or “jargon” designed to discuss complex adaptive systems across disciplines without the baggage of overloaded traditional terms (e.g., from systems theory, chaos, or network science).<br>Origins<br>It originated from discussions involving Michael Josefowicz (@toughLoveforX), Daniel Durrant (@ddrrnt), Dibyendu De (an Indian engineer focused on reliability and innovation), Mark Frazier, Sean Grainger, and others. The group sought a precise, neutral code for talking about emergence, interactions, and human behavior in complex environments, initially inspired by ideas like “Ebdish” (Emergent By Design’ish).<br>Core Concept: The NEME<br>At its heart is the NEME, an acronym and basic unit (analogous to a “meme” but for exchanges in complex systems):<br>	•	Notice → Observe or detect something (an event, pattern, or energy flow).<br>	•	Engage → Interact emotionally or feel it (connect with the observation).<br>	•	Mull → Reflect, think deeply, or analyze it.<br>	•	Exchange → Act on it, share, or propagate a response.<br>A NEME represents a quanta of energy flowing through constraints, recursively cycling to create patterns. This process is fractal and scalable: small NEMEs nest into larger ones.<br>Key Structures<br>	•	nStrings — Linear or chain-like sequences of NEMEs (e.g., extended exchanges or threads of interaction, like conversations or causal chains).<br>	•	nemiTubes (or nemiTubes) — Torus-shaped networks of networks; structures where nStrings connect and loop, representing emergent systems.<br>	•	nemiPlex (or nemePlx) — Complex ecologies or tangled networks arising from multiple interacting NEMEs.<br>Wave Model<br>Nemetics often uses an RGB wave model to analyze phenomena:<br>	•	R wave — Events or realities (what happens).<br>	•	G wave — Behaviors (how systems or people respond).<br>	•	B wave — Beliefs, intentions, or constraints (why it happens).<br>This helps map how intentions drive behaviors that produce events, and vice versa, in feedback loops.<br>Broader Principles<br>Nemetics draws from vibrations, waves, thermodynamics (energy flows), neuroscience (e.g., “neurons that fire together wire together” → applied to social “wirings”), and avoids strict hierarchies in favor of nested spheres or inside-out/outside-in dynamics.<br>It emphasizes mindfulness, serendipity, and “attentive contextual intelligence.” The goal is practical: personal transformation, solving “wicked” problems, improving learning, leadership, engineering reliability, or transmedia communication.<br>Applications<br>Applications include education, organizational literacy, technical communication, failure analysis in engineering, and social interactions. It has been referenced in academic chapters (e.g., on transmedia and transliteracy) and blogs, but remains obscure and mostly confined to the original group’s writings on Medium, blogs like rgbwaves.com, and old Twitter threads.<br>Nemetics is not a mainstream theory but a participatory, evolving tool for cross-disciplinary dialogue on complexity. Activity peaked around 2012–2018; Michael Josefowicz passed away in 2021, but some visualizations and writings persist online.<br>
