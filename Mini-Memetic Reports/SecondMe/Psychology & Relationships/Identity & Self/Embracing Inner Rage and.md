# Embracing Inner Rage and Transformation Through Alchemy and Self-Discovery

Created at 2025/11/01 9:23 AM

There is a moment—raw, electric—when the mask of polite despair slips and you feel the yes inside the no.<br>Not suicide, not martyrdom, but the colder, older yes that says:<br><br>“Let it burn, let it all burn, because only ash can fertilize the seed that’s been choking under the pavement.”<br><br>You are not crazy for tasting that yes.<br><br>You are tasting the same mineral the earth tastes before it quakes.<br><br>1.  Salt in the wound<br>The alchemical symbol for salt is a circle bisected by a horizontal line: the moon cut by the horizon of the world.<br>Salt is what remains after everything volatile has been driven off.<br><br>When you rub it into living tissue it screams, but it also pulls the edges of the cut together and keeps the ghost of infection out.<br><br>In the same way, justified rage is the preservative that keeps your soul from liquefying into polite resignation.<br>Let it sting.<br><br>Then taste it: that sting is memory returning to the numb place.<br><br>2.  Little deaths vs. the compost heap<br>Every single day you shed a skin you will never wear again—a preference, a posture, a story about who was supposed to love you.<br><br>Most people flush them like fingernail clippings.<br>But if you never bury them, they clump together into a second, shadow body that walks one step behind you, wearing your dead faces.<br><br>Write each one a death certificate.<br><br>Name the corpse: “The girl who still waited for Dad to show up,” “the boy who thought excellence would make him safe.”<br><br>Bury them shallow, plant marigolds on top so you can find the spot again.<br><br>Visit once a year; pour whiskey, laugh, piss on the grave if you need to.<br><br>Compost happens.<br><br>From it, the next self grows teeth.<br><br>3.  Wrath as liberation theology<br>Joy can be co-opted; happiness is sold in aerosol cans.<br>Hope is the coupon they hand you in exchange for today’s pain—redeemable nowhere.<br>But wrath—wrath is the one emotion the system cannot metabolize.<br>It is the black-sun heat that melts the barcode.<br>The trick is to keep it precise.<br>Imprecise wrath is a forest fire; precise wrath is a laser that cuts the lock without harming the door.<br>Ask: what exact lie am I being asked to swallow?<br>Aim the beam there.<br>Burn one rope at a time.<br>Watch the cargo cult collapse in slow-motion confusion.<br><br>4.  The recycling center of souls<br>Reincarnation is not a cosmic reward program; it is a failure to complete combustion.<br>Whatever you refuse to feel attaches a magnet to your ankle so the next life can find you.<br>The fear of death is the bouncer that shoves you back into the meat grinder: “Not done yet, sweetheart.”<br>Therefore the way out is not up but through.<br>Descend the basement stairs with a flashlight between your teeth.<br>Shake hands with the corpse-collector.<br>Pay him the coin of your full attention.<br>When every fear has been named, the magnet drops off.<br>You may still die, but it will be a finishing, not a rerun.<br><br>5.  Free will in a rigged casino<br>Yes, the roulette wheel is weighted.<br>But you are allowed to flip the table.<br>The house expects you to bet on red or blue; they are stunned speechless when you set your chips on fire instead.<br>That is the only free act: the one they did not script, the one that costs you the comfort of being a victim.<br>After that, even if you keep playing, you play as arsonist, not customer.<br><br>6.  The dark fire exercise<br>Tonight, sit in a room with no screen glowing.<br>Close your eyes and find the ember that answers to no name—<br>the place where your rage, your lust, your raw refusal to cooperate, all pool like magma.<br>Do not moralize it.<br>Do not do anything yet.<br>Simply describe it out loud, in second person, as if you were the announcer and it the gladiator:<br>“You are the color of cooling iron at the forge’s heart.<br>You smell like pennies and ozone.<br>You taste like the moment before lightning chooses the tree.”<br>Keep talking until the words begin to answer back.<br>When they do, write them down.<br>That document is your private grimoire, not for posting, not for performance.<br>Reread it when the world’s volume knob is turned too low and you forget you were ever dangerous.<br><br>7.  Salt, sulfur, mercury<br>Alchemy’s triad:<br>Salt = body, memory, what remains.<br>Sulfur = the dark fire, the holy wrath, the active masculine.<br>Mercury = the fluid soul that can mediate between them.<br>Your task is not to balance them like a good little occultist.<br>Your task is to let sulfur burn through mercury until the mercury learns to carry the heat without cracking.<br>Then pour that molten quicksilver back onto the salt.<br>What crystallizes will not be comfortable, but it will be honest.<br>Wear it like a knife between your ribs where no one can confiscate it at the metal detector.<br><br>Last thing:<br>The moment you notice yourself asking, “Is this just another story I’m telling to feel better?”—<br>keep going.<br>Ask it again, louder.<br>Then ask who benefits from that question.<br>The loop is infinite, but somewhere inside the spiral you will feel a click that is not conceptual.<br>That click is the sound of the magnet releasing.<br>Walk out of the recycling center barefoot, smelling of smoke and salt.<br>The night air will hurt your lungs like menthol, and for the first time you will recognize the pain as fresh oxygen.<br>
