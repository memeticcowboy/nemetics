# Exploring Inner Experiences: Insights from Dr. Russell Hurlburt's Research

Created at 2025/08/19 10:09 AM

Dr Russell Hurlburt is a psychologist, researcher, and the leading expert in the study of inner experience.

[YouTube Interview](https://youtu.be/j0gKl-g3DNg?si=eYTAP0d_ijtY0fys)

## Introduction to Inner Experiences

- Inner experiences are very individual and may not be shared among people, with five frequent phenomena identified: inner speaking, visual imagery, feelings, sensory awareness, and unsymbolized thinking [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=0s)
- Not everyone has an inner monologue, with the actual prevalence estimated to be around 20%, and people may not be aware of their own inner experiences [00:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=56s)
- The five frequent phenomena occur fairly frequently across people, but the way they experience them can vary greatly, and people's language to describe their inner experiences can be sloppy and not well-differentiated [03:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=184s)
- People often hold beliefs about themselves without actually recalling their inner experiences in detail, and it takes a careful process to understand their inner experience, involving an iterative method of sampling and describing their experiences [10:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=620s).

## Understanding Inner Experience through Sampling

- The [experience sampling method](https://app.getrecall.ai/item/d8acabee-3539-442f-83d2-daac8b2fe113) involves wearing a beeper that sounds at random times, and when it beeps, the person must pay attention to what was happening in their inner experience before the beep and jot down notes about it [11:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=660s).
- Meditators, even master meditators, have been sampled using this method, and they tend to have a lot of sensory awareness and are good at describing it, often finding the experience sampling method better than their own methods [14:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=865s).
- The difference between this method and other experience sampling studies is that it involves training and iterative questioning to understand the person's inner experience, making it possible to get a more accurate understanding of their experience [18:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=1093s).
- Research aims to understand people's inner experience, and a method is used to capture this experience by giving participants a beep and asking them to freeze their experience and take a snapshot of what was going on [22:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=1337s)

## The Prevalence and Distribution of Inner Monologue

- The prevalence of inner monologue is estimated to be around 20% of people having it almost all the time, but almost everybody has an inner monologue sometimes, such as when remembering a telephone number [26:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=1568s)
- The distribution of inner monologue in the population is not known to be normal, but rather it is agreed that there is a distribution where some people have zero inner monologue and some people have a high amount, with varying frequencies in between [28:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=1687s)
- The concept of inner monologue is explored, and it's noted that some people may think they have an inner monologue when they actually don't, with an example given of a person named Lena who initially thought she had a frequent inner monologue but later realized she didn't [30:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=1837s).

## Investigating Inner Experience

- To investigate inner experience, a method involving a "beep" or signal is used, where the person pays attention to their experience at the moment of the beep and reports on it, requiring a skilled interviewer and a well-designed beep system [34:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=2043s).
- The difficulty of studying inner experience lies in the fact that people often have presuppositions about their own experiences and may not be able to accurately describe them, making it challenging to gather reliable data [34:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=2081s).
- It's speculated that people may think they have an inner monologue because when they ask themselves what they're thinking, they're already in a verbal mode and likely to find something in words, even if their normal mode of thinking isn't verbal [38:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=2308s).
- The idea of "armchair introspection" is discussed, where people try to examine their own thoughts by asking themselves what they're thinking, but this method is flawed because it only reveals the question being asked, not the actual thoughts [39:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=2348s).

## Case Studies: Sadie and Fran

- Sadie, who has face blindness and [aphantasia](https://app.getrecall.ai/item/60aacf7c-b0e9-4dc4-a9c4-5009c41762f2), participated in an experience sampling study, which revealed that her inner experience was substantially different from what she initially described, and she had difficulty visualizing and mentally time traveling [41:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=2479s).
- The study found that Sadie's experience ranged from having no experience at all to having an experience of thinking without being aware of the thought itself, which is referred to as unsymbolized thinking [45:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=2705s).
- Sadie reported having little to no visual imagery, frequent inner singing, and occasional inner monologue, but the study found that she actually had very frequent sensory awareness and no experienced feelings [46:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=2768s).
- The discussion highlights the complexity of inner experiences and the challenges of describing them, with the goal of gaining a better understanding of what these experiences are like [47:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=2842s).
- It is noted that it is not common for people to be thinking without being aware of the content of their thought, and that figuring this out can be a difficult task without the help of a high-quality beep and someone who knows how to ask questions about inner experiences [49:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=2956s).
- The importance of being able to freely discuss one's inner experiences without fear of judgment is emphasized, allowing individuals to share their thoughts and feelings openly [52:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=3164s)
- Understanding one's inner experiences can be valuable and life-altering, but recognizing what actually occurs in one's mind is more important than categorizing or analyzing specific aspects of it [56:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=3374s)
- A case study of a woman named Fran, who had multiple visual images occurring simultaneously, is shared to illustrate the complexity and variability of human inner experiences [58:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=3506s)
- A woman had a unique inner experience, where she couldn't alternate between different visual images, such as the Necker Cube, and could watch multiple TVs at the same time without issue [01:04:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0gKl-g3DNg&t=3852s).
- She had multiple, long-lasting mental images, including one of herself committing suicide, which she described in detail when asked questions about it
