# Understanding the Malignant Trickster Archetype and Its Impact on Self-Sabotage

Created at 2025/05/03 9:04 AM

[Study Notes: The Flooding Self-Sabotaging Trickster - Robert Moore](https://youtu.be/TGAMMPKM0ZI?si=2O0rF5MMvXzrIQl7)
Main Ideas
	•	Malignant Trickster Archetype:
	•	Introduced by Shostrom in “Man the Manipulator.”
	•	Represents a self-sabotaging aspect of the psyche that can undermine personal projects.
	•	Positive Role of the Trickster:
	•	Essential for survival and helps discern manipulation by others.
	•	Enables mental freedom even in oppressive situations.
	•	Self-Sabotage Mechanism:
	•	The trickster can operate on “full automatic,” leading to unintentional self-sabotage.
	•	This occurs when the trickster acts without conscious invocation or guidance.
	•	Personality Disorder:
	•	Discusses the “self-defeating personality disorder” where individuals constantly undermine their own projects.
	•	Dual Nature of the Trickster:
	•	Shadow Side: Critiques and undermines projects.
	•	Conscious Side: Can be well-stewarded to foster creativity and support project building.
Key Concepts
	•	Magician Archetype:
	•	Represents creativity and the potential for building and innovation.
	•	It can also be overly critical, leading to paralysis by analysis (Kierkegaard’s “sickness of infinitude”).
	•	The Throne Metaphor:
	•	The “royal part” of oneself must take charge to direct the trickster and magician.
	•	Importance of decisive action in project development despite potential critiques.
Important Terms
	•	Trickster: An archetype that embodies cunning, humor, and the potential for both creation and destruction in personality.
	•	Self-Sabotage: The act of undermining one’s own efforts, often subconsciously driven by the trickster aspect.
	•	Self-Defeating Personality Disorder: A personality framework characterized by consistent self-sabotage in personal and professional endeavors.
Examples
	•	Imhotep: A historical architect who embodies the positive role of the magician archetype by helping to build significant structures in Egypt.
	•	Royal Decision-Making: Emphasizes that the conscious self must make final decisions and assert control over creative impulses and criticisms.
Summary of Complex Concepts
	•	The trickster in us can be both beneficial and harmful. When channeled positively, it serves to protect and help us navigate power dynamics. However, when it operates unchecked, it leads to a cycle of failed projects and self-defeating behaviors.
