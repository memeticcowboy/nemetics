## Insight
This article delves into the inherent human tendency to judge others, both positively and negatively, and how this affects our mental and physical well-being. It highlights the concept of projection, where we transfer our own life outlook onto others, and discusses the physiological and psychological impacts of living in a judgmental state. The key takeaway is that while judgment is unavoidable, becoming aware of this tendency can help mitigate its negative effects and open the door to greater understanding and joy. If you find yourself constantly judging others, try to become more aware of this habit and consider writing down your judgments to gain a new perspective.

**🧠 Projection and Judgment**
- **Judgment as Projection**: Judging others involves projecting our own life outlook onto them, which can distort our perception of who they truly are.
- **Impact on Awareness**: Judgment causes us to lose awareness of the details of a person’s true nature and circumstances.
- **Physiological Effects**: Living in a judgmental state elevates stress hormones, leading to a state of 'threat physiology' that can harm our health.

**🔄 Personal Insights**
- **Negative Self-Judgment**: Negative self-judgment can become a person's identity, leading to a negative outlook on others and personal life deterioration.
- **Positive Self-Judgment**: Even positive self-judgment can be disruptive, preventing one from experiencing the present moment.
- **Realization**: The author realized that any form of judgment, positive or negative, is still judgment and can be equally disruptive.

**📚 Awareness and Labels**
- **Loss of Awareness**: Labeling someone, positively or negatively, causes a loss of awareness of their true self.
- **Descriptive vs. Disparaging Labels**: Using descriptive labels (e.g., 'a person who drinks too much') rather than disparaging ones (e.g., 'a drunk') can foster better understanding.
- **Impact on Relationships**: Awareness of judgmental nature can improve relationships by fostering better understanding and reducing conflict.

**😊 Joy and Judgment**
- **Judgment and Joy**: It is impossible to experience true joy while in a judgmental state of mind.
- **Survival Mechanism**: Judgment is necessary for survival to assess safety versus danger, making it impossible to stop entirely.
- **Awareness as a Solution**: Becoming aware of the effects of judgment on quality of life and relationships can dissolve judgment and open the door to joy.

**📝 Practical Exercises**
- **Thought Suppression**: Trying to stop being judgmental is a form of thought suppression, which can make us even more judgmental.
- **Writing Exercise**: Writing down judgments and destroying them helps separate from thoughts and reduces their impact.
- **Self-Reflection**: Writing down thoughts about others and comparing them to self-judgments can be enlightening and humbling.

**☮️ Peace and Responsibility**
- **Peace vs. War**: True peace is improbable without taking responsibility for our contribution to collective consciousness.
- **Impact of Anger and Labeling**: Anger and labeling are combative behaviors that disrupt peace.
- **Awareness and Healing**: Becoming aware of judgmental nature and learning to separate from thoughts can positively affect relationships and contribute to collective peace.
