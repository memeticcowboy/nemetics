## Insight


*  **The Rose of Leary Model**: The image depicts the Rose of Leary, a psychological tool used to understand interpersonal behavior. Developed by Timothy Leary in the mid-20th century, this model maps out different behavioral styles along two axes: dominance/submission and hostility/affiliation.

*  **Axes of Interpersonal Behavior**: The model's vertical axis represents dominance (Above) versus submission (Under), while the horizontal axis represents affiliation (Together) versus opposition (Opposed). These axes create four quadrants, each representing a different interpersonal style.

*  **Behavioral Styles**: The eight segments within the circle represent specific behavioral styles: Leading, Competitive, Offensive, Rebellious, Withdrawn, Dependent, Cooperative, and Helping. Each style is a combination of the dominance/submission and affiliation/opposition dimensions.

*  **Interpersonal Dynamics**: The Rose of Leary suggests that interpersonal behavior tends to evoke complementary responses. For example, dominant behavior often invites submissive behavior, and friendly behavior invites more friendly behavior. This concept is known as "reciprocity" or "matching."

*  **Applications in Psychology and Communication**: The Rose of Leary is used in various fields, including psychology, communication, and management, to analyze and improve interpersonal interactions. It can help individuals understand their own behavioral patterns and how they affect others, as well as develop more effective communication strategies.

*  **Timothy Leary's Legacy**: Timothy Leary, the creator of the model, was a controversial figure known for his research on psychedelic drugs and his advocacy for their use. While his work on psychedelics is widely debated, his Rose of Leary model remains a valuable tool for understanding interpersonal dynamics.
