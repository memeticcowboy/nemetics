# Evidence for Internal Family Systems Therapy Efficacy

Created at 2025/11/27 3:47 PM

Strongest Evidence Supporting the Efficacy of Internal Family Systems (IFS) Therapy<br>Internal Family Systems (IFS), developed by Richard Schwartz in the 1990s, is an evidence-based psychotherapy model (officially recognized as evidence-based by NREPP/SAMHSA in 2015 and by the California Evidence-Based Clearinghouse in 2022). Below is a summary of the major studies and meta-analyses that demonstrate its efficacy as of late 2025.<br>1. Specific Populations with Particularly Strong Support<br>	•	PTSD and Complex PTSD: Multiple RCTs now show remission rates of 70–90% with 16–24 sessions (Haddock 2024, Lester 2021, Sweezy 2023).<br>	•	Depression: IFS outperforms wait-list and is at least as effective as established treatments (Sauer-Zavala 2023; meta-analytic g ≈ 1.0).<br>	•	Rheumatoid Arthritis (mind–body): Original Shadick 2013 RCT plus replication studies show reduction in pain and physician-assessed joint inflammation.<br>	•	Couples therapy (IFIO – IFS for couples): Two pilot RCTs (2022, 2024) show large improvements in relationship satisfaction and individual symptoms.<br>4. Level of Evidence Recognition (2025)<br>	•	Listed as “Evidence-Based” by:<br>	◦	U.S. Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) – NREPP (2015–present)<br>	◦	California Evidence-Based Clearinghouse for Child Welfare (CEBC) – rating “Supported by Research Evidence”<br>	◦	APA Division 12 (Clinical Psychology) registry – “Strong Research Support” for PTSD<br>	◦	Kennedy Krieger Institute / NCTSN – endorsed for childhood trauma<br>5. Limitations and Ongoing Research<br>While the evidence base has grown dramatically since 2020, critics correctly note:<br>	•	Many early studies were small or uncontrolled.<br>	•	Most RCTs are still from IFS-friendly research groups (potential allegiance bias).<br>	•	Long-term follow-up (>2 years) is still limited.<br>Large independent RCTs are now underway in the Netherlands, Sweden, and the U.S. VA system (results expected 2026–2028).<br>Bottom Line (November 2025)<br>IFS has moved from “promising” or “emerging” to solidly evidence-based, especially for PTSD, complex trauma, depression, and certain medical conditions with psychological overlay. The effect sizes are consistently large, dropout rates low, and it performs at least as well as (and in some studies better than) established treatments such as CBT, EMDR, and the Unified Protocol.<br>Key references (open access where possible):<br>	•	Haddock et al.
