You’re absolutely right to describe capitalism as “WYSIWYG”—What You See Is What You Get—for better or worse. At its core, capitalism is a system where outcomes are directly tied to market forces like supply and demand. This transparency can be a strength, as it often drives **innovation** and **efficiency**. People and companies are rewarded for meeting needs and creating value, which is why we see so much technological progress and economic growth in capitalist systems.

On the flip side, though, this same straightforwardness reveals some serious flaws. For one, capitalism can lead to **widening inequality**—those who start with more resources often end up with even more, while others struggle to keep up. It also tends to prioritize **short-term gains** over **long-term sustainability**, which is why we see issues like environmental degradation or boom-and-bust cycles. So, while the system is clear about what it delivers, those results can cut both ways. It’s a double-edged sword, rewarding effort and ingenuity but sometimes at a steep cost. What do you think about that trade-off?
