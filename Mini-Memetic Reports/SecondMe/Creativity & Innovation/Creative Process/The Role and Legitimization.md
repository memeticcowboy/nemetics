# The Role and Legitimization of Play in Higher Education Research and Practice

Created at 2025/12/28 5:32 PM

Concise summary

## Perceptions and Objectives of Play in Higher Education

- Playful practices are often viewed with suspicion in Higher Education, with some academics believing they are not "proper" practice due to entrenched rules and conventions.
- Despite this, there are synergies between research and play that make them powerful partners, and many researchers are already using playful practices in their work.
- The use of play in research is often disguised due to negative associations with the term "play", with terms like "creativity" or "participatory engagement" being used instead, and efforts are being made to reclaim and legitimize the term "play" in academic contexts, with researchers like Alison James, Kara, and Miguel Sicart contributing to the discussion.
- The main objectives of "The Value of Play in HE" research are to explore the use of play in higher education, its value systems, and its relationship with playful, creative, and imaginative practice.

## Methodology and Data Collection Approach

- The research uses a mixed-methods approach, including surveys, interviews, and play-based webinars, with participants from diverse disciplines and countries, to gather data on the use of play in higher education.
- The data analysis reveals various themes, including the benefits and drawbacks of play, the importance of context and subjectivity, and the role of play in research practices, such as catalysing ideas, encouraging participation, and informing doctoral research, with references to researchers like Sutton-Smith, Brown, and LEGO Serious Play methodology.

## Themes and Applications of Play in Research

- Respondents use play and playfulness for various reasons, including to critique the status quo, as part of their personal and professional identity, and to re-energize practice.
- Play is also used to recognize the importance of multisensory approaches to research, as liberation, as connection to ideas and others, and as an expression of values.
- Additionally, play is used to make a difference, to recognize the impact of space and place, to nurture intuition and emotional intelligence, and to create a non-judgmental environment that fosters innovative ideas, as discussed by researchers like Gauntlett and Sutton-Smith.

## Theoretical Frameworks and Survey Findings

- Sutton-Smith's Rhetorics of play include seven forms: Progress, Power, Fate, Identity, Self, Imaginary, and Frivolity, each with distinct characteristics and associations with research.
- A survey found that the top values of play in research include fostering creativity and innovation, enjoyable experiences, freedom and experimentation, and self-knowledge and expression, with high agreement rates among respondents.

## Barriers and Risks of Play-Based Research

- Barriers to play in research include dominant paradigms, fear, negative perceptions, and poor planning and facilitation, with some respondents noting that play must be purposeful and ethical to be valued in an academic setting.
- The use of play in research is often perceived as risky or unprofessional, but it can be a valuable approach to investigating complex topics and capturing ineffable experiences.

## Legitimizing Play as a Research Methodology

- Play-based research methods can be evaluated and considered on the basis of their fit with research goals, just like other approaches.
- To legitimize play as a serious research approach, individuals and institutions need to grasp and apply play theory, encourage play-based practices, and challenge negative perceptions and limiting beliefs.
