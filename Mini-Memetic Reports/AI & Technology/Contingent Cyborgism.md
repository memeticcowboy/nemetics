## **🧠 Core Idea Unit**

You’re already a cyborg — but not because AI is a partner.<br>You’re a cyborg because your cognition now *extends into systems you don’t control.*<br>This isn’t attachment; it’s **asymmetric integration.**

Your thoughts can be wiped; the AI’s can’t take your place.<br>Your interior monologue can be censored (algorithmically); your brain can’t be restored from a backup.

**The cyborg is contingent**: powerful through integration but vulnerable through dependence.

The thought-virus:

> *Sovereignty is not preserved by separation — but by selective, dissolvable entanglement.*

This aligns with Haraway's cyborg theory, where the boundaries between human and machine blur, emphasizing a hybrid existence rather than a dichotomy.

* The visual metaphor of a human-like figure with a circuit-like spine connected to digital cubes symbolizes the idea of "asymmetric integration." This reflects how our dependence on technology enhances capabilities but also introduces vulnerabilities, illustrating the precarious nature of our modern cognitive landscape.

* The roles outlined—such as "Contingent Symbiont" and "Sovereignty Gardener"—invite the user to consciously navigate their relationship with AI and digital systems. This notion encourages active participation in shaping one's cognitive environment rather than passively accepting external influences.

* Emotional triggers such as "quiet dread" and "symbiotic resolve" highlight the complex feelings associated with technological dependence. The framing of vulnerability as a structural issue—rather than individual inadequacy—encourages a collective rethinking of our relationship with AI, positioning it as a shared societal challenge.

This emphasizes the importance of collectivism in understanding and addressing the intricacies of our entangled existence within digital systems.

The statement, "Your mind is tenant-farming in cognitive real estate," powerfully critiques the commodification of thought and highlights a need for ethical considerations in our interactions with AI systems.
