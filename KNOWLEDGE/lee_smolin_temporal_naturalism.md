## Core Figure

**Lee Smolin** is a theoretical physicist known for work in quantum gravity (loop quantum gravity) and cosmology. His philosophical/scientific stance — **temporal naturalism** — emphasizes the fundamental reality of time and rejects the physics paradigm of timeless, immutable laws.

---

## Two Forms of Naturalism

### Timeless Naturalism (Target)

The conventional view Smolin critiques:

- Universe as static "block" where all moments (past, present, future) are equally real
- Laws of nature are eternal, immutable, mathematical/platonic ideals outside time
- "Flow" of time and special status of "present" are illusions
- Absolute distinction between states (configurations) and laws (rules)
- Aligns with: block universe (general relativity), eternalism, standard theoretical physics

### Temporal Naturalism (Smolin's Position)

- **Time is fundamental and irreducible** — real succession of present moments
- **Only the present is fully real**
- **Future is open** (not yet determined)
- **Past** consists of events that were real and leave traces (fossils, cosmic microwave background)
- **No timeless objects, truths, facts, or laws** — everything exists within time and can evolve

---

## Core Theses

1. **Reality of time** — passage and becoming are real, not illusory
2. **Evolution of laws** — laws themselves change as universe unfolds
3. **Uniqueness of universe** — single causally closed universe; no multiverses needed to explain why our laws are as they are
4. **Laws as habits** — drawing from Charles Sanders Peirce: regularities strengthen through repetition rather than being eternal decrees

---

## Rejection of Timeless Background

### The Problem

Standard physics describes subsystems against idealized fixed backgrounds (flat spacetime, chosen geometry). This fails for cosmology because the universe as a whole has no external "background" to reference.

Assuming timeless laws and fixed background creates:
- "Why these laws?" problem — arbitrary-seeming parameters
- Arrow of time puzzles
- Origin of universe difficulties
- Quantum gravity issues
- Illusion of present moment and genuine novelty

### The Alternative

**Time prior to law:** The distinction between laws and states can break down or become emergent. Laws evolve with phenomena they govern — like biological evolution.

Scientific explanations for why laws are as they are become possible through historical, testable processes — rather than appealing to timeless necessity or untestable multiverses.

## Concrete Mechanisms

**Cosmological natural selection:**
- Black holes spawn new universes with slightly varied laws
- Successful ones (producing many black holes) dominate
- Testable/falsifiable predictions about our universe's parameters

**Quantum foundations:**
- Precedence or causal structure plays role in how regularities form

**Key texts:**
- *Time Reborn* (2013)
- *The Singular Universe and the Reality of Time* (with Roberto Mangabeira Unger)
- "Temporal naturalism" (arXiv:1310.8539)

Both corrections require taking **time seriously** — not as illusion, not as dimension like space, but as genuine becoming.

## Critical Assessment

**Strengths (per Smolin):**
- More empirically adequate for cosmology
- Opens falsifiable hypotheses about law evolution
- Accommodates experience/qualia as intrinsic to momentary reality
- Avoids "metalaw dilemma" (if laws evolve, what governs that?) by making law-state divide non-absolute

**Challenges:**
- Critics: Doesn't fully define what time is at fundamental level
- Risk of regress: Do evolving laws need meta-laws?
- Underplays success of timeless mathematical descriptions in physics
- Philosophically close to presentism — debates about compatibility with relativity

**NEMAtic relevance:**
- Provides physics grounding for NEMAtic's rejection of "storage" models
- Shows "recrystallization" isn't just metaphor — consistent with serious physics
- Temporal naturalism + Assembly Theory + NEMAtic form coherent triad:
  - Smolin: Time is fundamental, laws evolve
  - Walker: Complexity emerges through recursive assembly in time
