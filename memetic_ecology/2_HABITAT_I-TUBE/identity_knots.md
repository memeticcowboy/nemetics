## What Binds Here

Identity knots are "I am X" claims that have tightened through repetition. Not lies. Not truths. Just... torsions that stabilized.

Examples:
- "I am the Memetic Cowboy"

## Pathology: Frozen ◎

When I-Tube freezes (ε → 0):
- Identity becomes rigid
- "I am X" claims become unfalsifiable
- Ego crystallization
- Self-concept becomes terminal attractor

**Simulation correlates:** flex_i → 0 (perceptual filter frozen) combined with claim_i → 1 (the cut is asserted as identity-defining). This is the pathological combination the State Schema v0.3 specifically introduced these variables to detect. A system can have high claim_i with high flex_i — strong commitment to currently-held distinctions while remaining able to re-cut them. That is healthy. Frozen ◎ is the case where claim has become absolute and revisability has dropped to zero. Identity has become a knot with stiff_m approaching maximum, no matter how shallow or deep its basin.

## Twist Dynamics

Twists in the I-Tube are **self-referential torsions**—moments where the reflexive loop catches on itself. These produce the residues that become identity-binding when tightened.

The I-Tube is the origin point for the SelfMesh.

**Architecture note:** In the simulation architecture, identity knots are the Knot state bundle (S_K) instantiated at the I-Tube layer. The relevant state variables are depth_m (how strongly threads are drawn into "I am X"), stiff_m (how resistant the identity is to deformation), perm_m (whether other threads can pass through without being absorbed into the identity), and revisable_m (the function of stiff_m and perm_m that determines whether the knot can be loosened). The canonical distinction matters here: deep ≠ rigid. A deep identity knot may still be revisable if its stiffness is low and its separatrix permeability is moderate. A shallow identity knot may be pathological if its separatrices are sharp and its stiffness is high. The "tightness" column in the Current Identity Knots table is closer to stiff_m than to depth_m.
