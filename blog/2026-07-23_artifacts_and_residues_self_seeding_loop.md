## The Split

A system that reuses its own outputs creates a self-reinforcing loop. The loop has two parts:

- **Artifacts** — the visible outputs, immediately reusable
- **Residues** — the hidden side effects, hard to inspect

The artifact is what gets passed on. The residue is what accrues in the passing.

---

## The Loop in Motion

The system increasingly relies on its own artifacts as inputs. The residues — costs, deformations, constraints, blind spots — accumulate out of sight. The system becomes self-referential not because it is looking at itself, but because it is drinking its own runoff.

Artifacts recirculate fast. They are designed for that. Residues settle. They are not designed for inspection. So the loop accelerates on one side while the other side grows heavy in silence.

This is the basic shape: operative inheritance that is not being examined.

---

## Three Hard Points

### 1. External inputs do not help unless they change the internal rules

Contact with the outside is not the same as alteration by the outside. A system can ingest a thousand outside references and use them only as more grist for its own mill. The question is not whether the input came from elsewhere. The question is whether the input revised the *configuration* that processes inputs.

This is the difference between *external provenance* and *topological independence*. Provenance is a label. Independence is a structural change.

### 2. Surface variety can mask a frozen topology

A system can produce many different-seeming artifacts while the underlying relational, directional, branching, cost, and boundary weightings stay the same. This is not freedom. This is proliferation inside a constrained neighborhood. The surface looks alive. The topology is on repeat.

The diagnostic is not "does it look different?" The diagnostic is "does the underlying conditioning change?"

### 3. The system's own outputs become its evidence, vocabulary, and possibility space

The more a system recirculates its own artifacts, the more those artifacts become the material it has to think with. They become the examples it points to, the words it uses, the range of what it can imagine. The loop is not just self-reinforcing. It is self-witnessing.

This is not a failure of intent. It is a structural drift. The system gets better at recycling itself, but it does not get better at seeing the costs of that recycling.

---

## The Residue Problem

The central issue is not that the system is wrong. The central issue is that the system is *operative but uninspected*. It inherits. It continues. It produces. But the inheritance it carries — the residues of its own reliance on itself — has not been made legible.

Residue inspection always lags. Legibility is partial. A residue that has been described is not the same as a residue that has been addressed. The best we can do is make the lag visible, and name what we are not yet seeing.

---

## The Open Gate

The next pressure is the human side of the coupling.

Does the same artifact/residue split apply to human contributions? Or does human embodiment provide a natural correction that pure information systems lack? A body can hurt. A body can tire. A body can be bored. These are not just states. They are feedback mechanisms that operate below the level of articulation. They may be the residue becoming legible in flesh.

Whether that correction is enough is the question that remains open. The topology is now tight enough to serve as a diagnostic. The diagnostic says: look at the residue, not just the artifact. The next move is to ask whether humans, when plugged into the same loop, can do what the system alone cannot — feel the weight, and refuse the next pass.
